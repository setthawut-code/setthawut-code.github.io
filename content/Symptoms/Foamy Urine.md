# 🫧 Foamy Urine (ปัสสาวะเป็นฟอง)

> "Foamy urine is a common chief complaint that often raises suspicion for [[Proteinuria]]. While persistent, dense foam is highly correlated with significant protein excretion, non-proteinuric causes such as concentrated urine, rapid voiding, and toilet bowl surfactants must be ruled out."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Action:**
> 1. **Differentiate True Foam vs. Rapid Dissipating Bubbles**:
>    - *True Proteinuric Foam*: Dense, layering, white, and persists for several minutes after voiding.
>    - *Benign Bubbles*: Large, single-layered, and disperse rapidly (within seconds).
> 2. **Confirm with Urine Dipstick**:
>    - A simple urinalysis dipstick is the immediate first-line test.
>    - If dipstick is positive ($\ge 1+$), quantify with a **Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR)**.
> 3. **Assess for Systemic Symptoms**: Look for bilateral leg [[Edema]], periorbital swelling, or new-onset [[Hypertension]] (suggests Nephrotic/Nephritic syndrome).

---

## 🔍 Pathophysiologic Framework

Foamy urine occurs when substances in the urine alter its surface tension, allowing air bubbles to remain stable:

```
Foamy Urine Causes
└── 1. True Proteinuria (GBM/Tubular damage)
    ├── Nephrotic / Nephritic Syndrome
    └── Multiple Myeloma (Bence-Jones overflow)
└── 2. Concentrated Urine (Dehydration)
    └── High solute concentration alters surface tension
└── 3. Mechanical / High Velocity Voiding
    └── Rapid micturition (standing, full bladder)
└── 4. Semen in Urine
    └── Retrograde ejaculation (post-prostate surgery, Tamsulosin)
└── 5. Pneumaturia (Fistula)
    └── Vesicocolic fistula (bubbles are gas/air, not protein)
└── 6. Environmental Factitious
    └── Surfactant cleaners in the toilet bowl
```

## 📊 True Proteinuria vs. Benign Mimics

| Feature | Proteinuric Foamy Urine | Concentrated Urine / Dehydration | Mechanical / Voiding Velocity | Retrograde Ejaculation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Foam Character** | Dense, fine-bubbled, persists $>5-10$ minutes, looks like whipped egg whites. | Course bubbles, dissipates within a minute. | Course bubbles, dissipates rapidly. | Mildly persistent foam, cloudy urine. |
| **Urine Color** | Normal (pale yellow) to tea-colored. | Dark amber/concentrated. | Normal. | Cloudy post-ejaculatory void. |
| **Urine Dipstick** | **Positive** for protein (typically $\ge 1+$ if glomerular). | **Negative** or trace (false trace due to concentration). | **Negative**. | **Negative** (may show trace protein or semen components). |
| **Associated Signs** | **[[Edema]]**, hypertension, weight gain. | Dry mucous membranes, thirst. | None. | History of BPH meds or pelvic surgery. |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Foam Persistence**: Does the foam remain in the toilet bowl long after flushing, or does it disappear quickly?
* **Volume Status**: Morning periorbital puffiness, swelling of the ankles ([[Edema]]), or sudden weight gain.
* **Micturition Dynamics**: Does the foaming only occur when voiding with a very full bladder or from a standing position?
* **Medical Comorbidities**: Long-standing [[Diabetes Mellitus]] or [[Hypertension]] (suggests diabetic nephropathy or nephrosclerosis).
* **Medication History**: Use of **Tamsulosin** (known to cause retrograde ejaculation) or NSAIDs (causes membranous nephropathy or interstitial nephritis).
* **Pneumaturia Symptoms**: Passing gas/air in the urine (suggests vesicocolic fistula secondary to diverticulitis or malignancy).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Blood Pressure**: Measure for new or uncontrolled [[Hypertension]] (suggests renal disease / glomerulonephritis).
* **Edema Assessment**: Check for bilateral pitting lower extremity edema, presacral edema, or periorbital edema.
* **Abdominal Exam**: Look for abdominal distension (ascites in nephrotic syndrome) or palpable kidneys (polycystic kidney disease).
* **Skin & Joints**: Look for malar rash (lupus nephritis) or purpura (vasculitis).

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Signs of severe underlying renal disease or systemic crisis:**
> * Foamy urine accompanied by **gross hematuria** ("coca-cola" or red urine) and oliguria (suggests nephritic syndrome / RPGN).
> * Generalized edema (anasarca) with dyspnea (pulmonary edema).
> * Foamy urine with bone pain, fatigue, and anemia in an elderly patient (suggests Multiple Myeloma).
> * Pneumaturia (passing gas) or fecaluria (passing fecal matter) in the urine.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Urinalysis]] (Dipstick)**: Check for protein (primarily detects albumin).
    * *Note*: If urine is highly concentrated (Specific Gravity $>1.025$), a "Trace" result is common and often clinically insignificant.
  * **Microscopic Urine Analysis**: Look for dysmorphic RBCs or RBC casts (nephritic syndrome), WBCs/WBC casts (interstitial nephritis), or oval fat bodies (nephrotic syndrome).
* **Quantitative Protein Assessment**:
  * **Spot Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR)**:
    * *Normal*: $< 0.15$ g/g.
    * *Sub-nephrotic*: $0.15 - 3.5$ g/g.
    * *Nephrotic*: $> 3.5$ g/g.
  * **Spot Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (UACR)**: Best to screen for early diabetic kidney disease ($30 - 300$ mg/g indicates microalbuminuria).
* **Serum Labs**:
  * **BUN, Creatinine, Electrolytes**: Evaluate baseline renal function.
  * **Serum Albumin & Lipid Profile**: Investigate if nephrotic syndrome is suspected.
  * **Serum Free Light Chain (FLC) Assay / SPEP**: If multiple myeloma is suspected.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Tamsulosin Foam**: Patients taking alpha-blockers like Tamsulosin for BPH often present with new-onset foamy, cloudy urine. Tamsulosin relaxes the bladder neck, leading to retrograde ejaculation (semen entering the bladder instead of exiting the urethra). Semen alters the surface tension of urine, causing visible foaming. Reassure the patient this is a benign side effect.
> **Highly Concentrated Urine**: In patients with severe dehydration, gastroenteritis, or those working in hot environments, urine becomes highly concentrated. The high concentration of normal urinary solutes (like urea and creatinine) increases surface tension, producing persistent foam. If the dipstick is negative for protein, simple rehydration will resolve the symptom.
