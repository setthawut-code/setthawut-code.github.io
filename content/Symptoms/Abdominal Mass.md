# 🤢 Abdominal Mass (ก้อนในท้อง)

> "The clinical evaluation of an abdominal mass is guided by its anatomical location (quadrant) and tissue characteristics. Rule out vascular catastrophes (AAA) and malignancy first."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)**: Look for a **pulsatile, expansile midline/epigastric mass**. *Avoid aggressive deep palpation; a ruptured AAA is rapidly fatal.*
> 2. **Quadrant Localization**: Identify which quadrant the mass occupies to narrow down the organ of origin.
> 3. **Assess for Malignancy**: Hard, fixed, non-tender masses associated with weight loss, night sweats, or bowel habit changes suggest cancer.
> 4. **Differentiate from "Pseudomasses"**: Rule out common culprits like a distended urinary bladder, fecal impaction, or pregnant uterus.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

An abdominal mass is any abnormal localized swelling or growth in the abdominal cavity. It can arise from:
* **Organomegaly**: Enlargement of solid organs (Hepatomegaly, Splenomegaly, Nephromegaly).
* **Neoplastic growth**: Primary carcinomas (colon, stomach, kidney, liver, ovary) or lymphoma.
* **Inflammatory / Infectious**: Abscesses (appendiceal, diverticular, psoas), phlegmons.
* **Vascular dilation**: Aneurysms (AAA).
* **Congenital / Cystic**: Polycystic kidney disease, mesenteric cysts.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Associated Organ-Specific Clues**:
  * *Change in bowel habits, hematochezia, weight loss* $\rightarrow$ Colorectal cancer mass.
  * *Painless jaundice, dark urine, pale stools* $\rightarrow$ Pancreatic head mass or liver mass.
  * *Hematuria, flank pain* $\rightarrow$ Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC) or Polycystic Kidney Disease.
  * *Early satiety, epigastric pain* $\rightarrow$ Gastric cancer or pancreatic pseudocyst.
  * *Fever, chills, severe focal pain* $\rightarrow$ Intra-abdominal abscess (appendiceal/diverticular).
  * *Urinary retention, frequency* $\rightarrow$ Distended bladder (frequently misdiagnosed as pelvic mass).

## 🩺 Physical Examination

### ⏳ Anatomical Quadrants & Mass Localization
> [!TIP]
> **Use the quadrant location to narrow down the anatomical structure:**

| Location | Common Organomegaly | Common Neoplasms / Masses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RUQ** | Hepatomegaly (congestive, hepatitis) | Gallbladder hydrops/cancer, liver tumor (HCC/metastasis). |
| **Epigastric** | None | Gastric cancer, pancreatic pseudocyst/cancer, **AAA** (pulsatile). |
| **LUQ** | **Splenomegaly** (portal HTN, lymphoma, leukemia) | Splenic abscess, gastric mass. |
| **RLQ** | None | **Appendiceal abscess / plastron**, Cecal carcinoma, Crohn's mass. |
| **LLQ** | None | Sigmoid colon cancer, diverticular abscess, fecal impaction. |
| **Flanks** | Nephromegaly (ADPKD, hydronephrosis) | Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC). |
| **Suprapubic** | **Distended bladder**, Pregnant uterus | Ovarian cyst/cancer, Uterine leiomyoma (fibroids). |

---

### 🎨 Mass Characteristics on Palpation:
* **Pulsatility**: *Pulsatile and expansile* (pushes fingers apart) $\rightarrow$ **Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)**.
* **Mobility**:
  * Moves with respiration $\rightarrow$ Liver, spleen, or gallbladder origin (attached to diaphragm).
  * Mobile side-to-side $\rightarrow$ Mesenteric or small bowel origin.
  * Fixed to retroperitoneum $\rightarrow$ Pancreatic, kidney, or retroperitoneal sarcoma.
* **Consistency**:
  * *Soft / Fluctuant*: Cystic, abscess, or fluid-filled loops.
  * *Firm / Rubbery*: Splenomegaly, uterine fibroids, lymphoma.
  * *Hard / Irregular*: Metastatic cancer, primary gastrointestinal carcinomas.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Origin | Benign / Non-Emergent | Dangerous / Can't Miss (Surgical) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vascular** | None | • **Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)** (ruptured/impending) |
| **Neoplastic** | • Uterine leiomyoma (fibroids)<br>• Benign ovarian cyst<br>• Lipoma | • **Colorectal Cancer** (cecal or sigmoid)<br>• **Renal Cell Carcinoma** (RCC)<br>• **Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**<br>• Gastric cancer / Ovarian cancer / Lymphoma |
| **Inflammatory** | • Fecal impaction (pseudomass)<br>• Splenic infarct | • **Appendiceal Abscess / Plastron**<br>• Diverticular abscess / Tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA) |
| **Organomegaly** | • Congestive hepatomegaly<br>• Splenomegaly (portal HTN) | • Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD)<br>• Massive liver metastases |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate surgical/emergency evaluation required if present:**
> * Pulsatile, expansile abdominal mass (suspected AAA).
> * Abdominal mass accompanied by severe abdominal pain, guarding, or rigidity (peritonitis).
> * Mass causing complete [[Bowel Obstruction]] (obstipation, vomiting, severe distension).
> * Hard, fixed supraclavicular node (Virchow's node) or periumbilical nodule (Sister Mary Joseph's nodule $\rightarrow$ metastatic GI cancer).
> * Jaundice, cachexia, and unexplained rapid weight loss.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Look for anemia (GI blood loss) or leukocytosis (abscess/infection).
  * **Renal and Liver Function Tests**: Assess kidney function (GFR for contrast imaging) and LFT markers.
  * **Urine hCG**: Rule out pregnancy in reproductive-age females.
  * **Urinalysis**: Hematuria suggests renal mass (RCC).
* **Imaging (Crucial Second Step)**:
  * **Abdominal Ultrasound**: First-line tool for differentiating solid from cystic masses, mapping pelvic masses (ovary, uterus), and screening for AAA.
  * **CT Abdomen & Pelvis (with IV contrast) (Gold Standard)**: Highly sensitive for mapping the origin, vascularity, and staging of neoplastic masses, and diagnosing intra-abdominal abscesses.
* **Tumor Markers (Supportive)**:
  * CEA (colon cancer), CA19-9 (pancreatic cancer), AFP (HCC), CA-125 (ovarian cancer), PSA (prostate cancer). *Note: Use for monitoring and prognosis, not primary screening (except PSA).*

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm**:
   - For asymptomatic AAA: Monitor size ($<5.5$ cm in men, $<5.0$ cm in women).
   - For symptomatic or rapidly expanding AAA: **Emergency vascular surgery** or Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR).
2. **Intra-Abdominal Abscess**:
   - Interventional Radiology (IR)-guided percutaneous drainage + IV broad-spectrum antibiotics.
3. **Malignant Masses**:
   - Surgical resection if localized; chemotherapy/radiation if advanced or metastatic.
4. **Fecal Impaction / Bladder Distension**:
   - Rectal disimpaction / enemas for impaction.
   - Urinary catheterization (Foley catheter) for distended bladder.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Sister Mary Joseph's Nodule**: A palpable nodule at the umbilicus represents metastasis from an intra-abdominal malignancy, most commonly gastric, pancreatic, or ovarian adenocarcinoma.
> * **The Bladder Trap**: Always ask a patient with a suprapubic mass to void (or insert a Foley catheter) before performing a pelvic ultrasound. A distended bladder can easily mimic a giant ovarian cyst or uterine fibroid on physical exam.
> * **Courvoisier's Law**: In a jaundiced patient, a palpable, non-tender gallbladder (RUQ mass) is unlikely to be caused by gallstones (as gallbladder is chronically fibrosed). It suggests **malignant biliary obstruction** (e.g., pancreatic head cancer).
