# 🦴 Neck Pain (ปวดคอ)

> "Neck pain can arise from local musculoskeletal structures or represent cervical nerve root or spinal cord compression. Distinguishing radiculopathy from myelopathy is critical."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy**:
>    - *Radiculopathy* (Nerve Root): Unilateral shooting arm pain, dermatomal numbness, diminished reflexes.
>    - *Myelopathy* (Spinal Cord): Bilateral hand clumsiness, gait imbalance, **hyperreflexia**, positive **Hoffman's sign**, and positive Babinski sign. *Requires urgent decompression.*
> 2. **Rule Out Meningeal Irritation**: Check for neck stiffness, fever, or headache.
> 3. **C-Spine Immobilization**: Always immobilize the neck in a collar post-trauma until the C-spine is cleared clinically and radiologically.

---

## 🔍 Physiology: Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy

### 📊 Cervical Radiculopathy vs. Myelopathy Table

| Feature | Cervical Radiculopathy (Nerve Root) | Cervical Myelopathy (Spinal Cord) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Laterality** | Typically **unilateral** | Typically **bilateral** |
| **Pain Pattern** | Sharp, shooting pain radiating down arm | Dull neck ache; arm pain may be absent |
| **Hand Function** | Normal (or isolated dermatomal weakness) | **Clumsiness** (difficulty writing, buttoning shirts) |
| **Gait** | Normal | **Ataxic / Spastic** (imbalance, stiff-legged gait) |
| **Reflexes** | **Hyporeflexia** of affected root (e.g., C7) | **Hyperreflexia** below level of lesion |
| **Key Signs** | Positive Spurling's maneuver | Positive **Hoffman's sign**, Babinski sign, clonus |

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character & Radiation of Pain**:
  * *Radicular*: Pain radiating from the neck down the shoulder, arm, or fingers in a specific dermatomal distribution. Often described as burning or electric.
  * *Mechanical*: Localized aching in the neck/shoulders, worsened by prolonged sitting or neck extension; relieved by rest.
* **Myelopathy Clues**: Ask specifically about:
  * Dropping objects frequently.
  * Difficulty with fine motor tasks (buttoning shirts, tying shoelaces, writing).
  * Feeling unstable or "stiff-legged" while walking.
  * New bladder/bowel incontinence.
* **Systemic Clues**: Fever (abscess/meningitis), morning stiffness (rheumatoid arthritis affecting C1-C2 joint), weight loss (malignancy).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Cervical Range of Motion**: Assess flexion, extension, lateral bending, and rotation.
* **Special Bedside Maneuvers**:
  * **Spurling's Maneuver** (Cervical Compression Test): Extend and rotate the patient's head toward the painful side, then apply gentle downward axial pressure. *Positive*: Reproduction of radicular pain down the arm. Highly specific for **cervical radiculopathy**.
  * **Lhermitte's Sign**: Passively flex the patient's neck. *Positive*: A sudden electrical shock-like sensation radiating down the spine or into the limbs. Indicates **cervical demyelination** (MS) or severe spinal cord compression.
  * **Hoffman's Sign**: Flick the distal phalanx of the middle finger. *Positive*: Quick flexion of the thumb and index finger. Indicates **upper motor neuron (cervical cord) compression**.
* **Neurological Exam of Upper Limbs (C5, C6, C7 Localization)**:
  * **C5**: Deltoid/Biceps strength; Biceps reflex; sensory loss over lateral shoulder.
  * **C6**: Wrist extension / Biceps strength; Brachioradialis reflex; sensory loss over lateral forearm/thumb.
  * **C7**: Triceps strength / Wrist flexion; Triceps reflex; sensory loss over middle finger.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical** | • Cervical muscle strain / Sprain<br>• Cervical Spondylosis (osteoarthritis)<br>• Whiplash injury (post-trauma) | None |
| **Neurological** | • **Cervical Radiculopathy** (herniated disc) | • **Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy**<br>• Spinal cord tumor / Syringomyelia |
| **Infectious** | • Retropharyngeal abscess (referred pain) | • **Meningitis** (presents with neck stiffness)<br>• **Spinal Epidural Abscess** (cervical) |
| **Referred / Other** | • Myofascial pain syndrome | • **Coronary Artery Disease** (referred anginal pain)<br>• Pancoast Tumor (apical lung cancer invading C8-T2 roots) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate cervical collar placement and urgent MRI required if present:**
> * **Cervical Myelopathy**: Progressive hand clumsiness, gait ataxia, hyperreflexia, positive Hoffman's sign, or bladder dysfunction.
> * **Spinal Instability (Post-Trauma)**: Severe neck pain following trauma, midline bony tenderness, or paresthesias in multiple limbs.
> * **Spinal Infection**: Fever, chills, IV drug use, with localized cervical percussion tenderness.
> * **Meningismus**: Neck stiffness with fever, headache, photophobia, or altered consciousness ([[Meningitis]]).
> * **Pancoast Tumor**: Neck/shoulder pain radiating to ulnar arm (C8-T1) + **Horner's Syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Cervical Spine X-Ray**: Quick evaluation for fractures, subluxation, osteophyte formation, or disk space narrowing.
  * **Inflammatory Markers (ESR / CRP)**: If infection, malignancy, or polymyalgia rheumatica is suspected.
* **Diagnostic Gold Standard**:
  * **MRI Cervical Spine**: Mandatory for suspected cervical radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, cord compression, spinal infection, or post-traumatic neurological deficits.
  * **CT Cervical Spine**: Preferred modality for detailed bony evaluation post-trauma (fractures).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Mechanical Neck Pain**:
   - Active mobilization (avoid prolonged soft collars; collars worsen muscle atrophy).
   - Short-term NSAIDs, paracetamol, or muscle relaxants. Physical therapy.
2. **Cervical Radiculopathy (HNP)**:
   - Conservative management for $4-6$ weeks (NSAIDs, short course of oral corticosteroids, physical therapy, traction).
   - Surgical decompression (Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion - ACDF) if progressive motor deficit or refractory pain.
3. **Cervical Myelopathy**:
   - Conservative care is generally ineffective for progressive myelopathy. **Refer early to Neurosurgery** for surgical decompression to prevent permanent spinal cord damage.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Rheumatoid Arthritis C1-C2 Subluxation**: In patients with severe Rheumatoid Arthritis, chronic inflammation can destroy the transverse ligament, leading to **atlantoaxial (C1-C2) subluxation**. Passive neck flexion during intubation can cause fatal spinal cord compression. *Always obtain flexion-extension cervical X-rays prior to elective surgery in RA patients.*
> * **Pancoast Tumor Pitfall**: A tumor at the lung apex (Pancoast tumor) can erode into the lower brachial plexus (C8-T1 roots). This presents as neck/shoulder pain radiating down the inner (ulnar) arm, often misdiagnosed as cervical radiculopathy. A key clue is the concurrent presence of **Horner's Syndrome** (due to compression of sympathetic chain).
> * **Meningeal Neck Stiffness Character**: Neck stiffness in meningitis impairs **flexion and extension** (vertical axis), but lateral rotation (side-to-side) is often relatively preserved. In contrast, mechanical neck pain/spasm limits motion in all directions.
