# 🚽 Urinary Frequency & Polyuria (ปัสสาวะบ่อย)

> "The first step in evaluating a patient complaining of 'urinary frequency' is to distinguish increased voiding frequency (storage dysfunction) from true polyuria (urine output >3 L/day)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Frequency vs. Polyuria**:
>    - *Frequency*: Small, frequent voids. Daily volume is normal ($<3$ L/day). Indicates bladder irritation or outlet obstruction (UTI, BPH, OAB).
>    - *Polyuria*: Large, voluminous voids. Daily volume is **$>3$ L/day**. Indicates solute diuresis (Diabetes Mellitus) or water diuresis (Diabetes Insipidus, Primary Polydipsia).
> 2. **Rule Out Diabetes Mellitus**: Check blood glucose for osmotic solute diuresis.
> 3. **Water Deprivation Test**: Indicated for true polyuria with low urine osmolarity to differentiate Central DI, Nephrogenic DI, and Primary Polydipsia.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Urinary Frequency**: Voiding $>7-8$ times during waking hours, or $>1-2$ times at night (nocturia), with normal total daily urine volume.
* **Polyuria**: Exceeding **$>3000$ mL ($>3$ L)** of urine output in 24 hours in adults.
  * **Solute (Osmotic) Diuresis**: Excessive excretion of non-reabsorbed solutes (Glucose, Urea, Sodium) pulling water along. Urine osmolarity is $>300$ mOsm/kg.
  * **Water Diuresis**: Excessive excretion of pure water due to lack of ADH effect. Urine osmolarity is $<250$ mOsm/kg.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### 📊 Frequency vs. Polyuria Differentiator

| Feature | Urinary Frequency (Storage) | Polyuria (Volume) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **24-Hour Urine Volume** | Normal ($<2.5-3.0$ L/day) | **Elevated ($>3.0$ L/day)** |
| **Typical Void Size** | Small | Large, voluminous |
| **Associated Symptoms** | Dysuria, urgency, nocturia, poor stream, hesitancy | Polydipsia (excessive thirst), weight loss |
| **Key Etiologies** | UTI, BPH, OAB, anxiety, cystocele | Diabetes Mellitus, Diabetes Insipidus (DI) |

* **Polyuria Clues**:
  * *Onset*: Abrupt onset of polyuria/polydipsia $\rightarrow$ **Central Diabetes Insipidus** (damage to pituitary stalk).
  * *History*: Head trauma, neurosurgery, or lithium therapy (Nephrogenic DI).
  * *Psychiatric*: History of anxiety or schizophrenia $\rightarrow$ **Primary Polydipsia** (compulsive water drinking).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Hydration Status**: Assess mucous membranes and skin turgor. Patients with Diabetes Insipidus will have intact hydration if they have access to water; they dehydrate rapidly if water is withheld.
* **Abdomen**: Palpate and percuss for a **distended urinary bladder** (suprapubic dullness) $\rightarrow$ chronic retention with overflow.
* **DRE in Men**: Evaluate prostate size, consistency, and tenderness (**BPH** or prostatitis).
* **Neurological**: Check visual fields (pituitary tumor compressing optic chiasm in central DI).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Urinary Frequency (Storage / Irritation) | Polyuria (Solute vs. Water Diuresis) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endocrine / Metabolic** | • Diabetes Mellitus (early mild frequency) | • **Diabetes Mellitus** (glucose solute diuresis)<br>• **Central Diabetes Insipidus** (lack of ADH)<br>• Hypercalcemia / Hypokalemia |
| **Urological** | • **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)**<br>• **Overactive Bladder (OAB)**<br>• Urinary Tract Infection (Cystitis) | • **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus** (ADH resistance; e.g., Lithium-induced) |
| **Psychiatric / Other** | • Anxiety / Psychogenic frequency | • **Primary Polydipsia** (compulsive water drinking) |
| **Dangerous / Can't Miss**| • Bladder / Prostate Cancer (mass effect)<br>• Spinal cord injury (neurogenic bladder) | • Severe uremic diuresis (post-obstructive diuresis)<br>• Pituitary tumor / Craniopharyngioma |

## 📋 The Water Deprivation Test (Polyuria Workup)
Used to evaluate chronic water diuresis (urine osmolarity $<300$ mOsm/kg):
1. **Withhold water** and measure urine osmolarity and body weight hourly.
   - *Urine osmolarity rises to $>600-800$ mOsm/kg* $\rightarrow$ **Primary Polydipsia** (ADH is working).
   - *Urine osmolarity remains low ($<300$ mOsm/kg)* $\rightarrow$ **Diabetes Insipidus** (go to Step 2).
2. **Administer Desmopressin (dAVP)** (synthetic ADH).
   - *Urine osmolarity increases by $>50\%$* $\rightarrow$ **Central DI** (responds to exogenous ADH).
   - *Urine osmolarity increases by $<10\%$* $\rightarrow$ **Nephrogenic DI** (kidneys are resistant to ADH).

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit or investigate urgently if present:**
> * Polyuria in a patient with altered mental status, lethargy, or severe dehydration.
> * Polyuria with profound electrolyte imbalances (severe hypernatremia).
> * Urinary frequency with hematuria and unexplained weight loss (malignancy).
> * Frequency accompanied by saddle anesthesia, leg weakness, or bowel incontinence (cauda equina).
> * Polyuria following a neurosurgical procedure or head trauma (acute central DI).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Fasting Glucose & HbA1c**: Screen for Diabetes Mellitus.
  * **Serum and Urine Osmolarity**: Determine solute vs. water diuresis.
  * **Serum Electrolytes**: Check Sodium (hypernatremia in DI; hyponatremia in primary polydipsia), Potassium, and Calcium (causes nephrogenic DI).
  * **[[Urinalysis]]**: Check for glycosuria, pyuria, or nitrites.
  * **Renal Function (Creatinine/BUN)**.
* **Secondary targeted testing**:
  * **Post-Void Residual (PVR) Volume**: Measured via bedside bladder ultrasound. A PVR **$>100-200$ mL** indicates bladder outlet obstruction or detrusor underactivity.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Diabetes Mellitus**:
   - Glycemic control (insulin or oral hypoglycemics).
2. **Central Diabetes Insipidus**:
   - **Desmopressin (dAVP)** nasal spray or oral tablets.
3. **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus**:
   - Discontinue offending drugs (Lithium).
   - Low-sodium diet + **Thiazide diuretics** (paradoxically reduces urine volume by causing mild volume depletion, enhancing proximal tubule water reabsorption).
4. **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)**:
   - Alpha-blockers (Tamsulosin) to relax bladder neck. 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (Finasteride) to reduce prostate size.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Lithium Defect**: Lithium is the most common cause of acquired **Nephrogenic DI**. It accumulates in the principal cells of the renal collecting duct and inhibits the Aquaporin-2 water channels, making the cells resistant to ADH.
> * **Primary Polydipsia Sodium Clue**: Patients with **Primary Polydipsia** drink excessive water, diluting their blood and presenting with **low-normal or low serum sodium ($Na < 135$ mEq/L)**. In contrast, patients with **Diabetes Insipidus** lose pure water in urine, presenting with **high-normal or elevated sodium ($Na > 145$ mEq/L)**.
> * **Overactive Bladder (OAB)**: Defined by urinary urgency, usually accompanied by frequency and nocturia, with or without urgency incontinence, in the absence of UTI or other local pathology.
