# 🦴 Back Pain (ปวดหลัง)

> "Back pain is one of the most common presentations in clinical practice. The primary goal is to differentiate benign mechanical causes from inflammatory, infectious, or neoplastic spinal disease."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Mechanical vs. Inflammatory**:
>    - *Mechanical*: Worse with movement, relieved by rest. Sharp, acute onset.
>    - *Inflammatory*: Worse with rest, improves with activity. **Morning stiffness $>30$ minutes**. Young onset ($<40$ years).
> 2. **Rule Out Cauda Equina Syndrome**: Look for **Saddle Anesthesia** (numbness in perineum), **new bowel/bladder dysfunction** (urinary retention/incontinence), or **progressive bilateral motor weakness**. *This is a surgical emergency.*
> 3. **Rule Out Spinal Infection / Malignancy**: Fever, night sweats, weight loss, history of cancer, or IV drug use.

---

## 🔍 Physiology: Mechanical vs. Inflammatory

### 📊 Back Pain Differentiation Table

| Feature | Mechanical Back Pain | Inflammatory Back Pain (e.g., Axial SpA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Age at Onset** | Any age (usually $30-90$ years) | **Young onset ($<40$ years)** |
| **Onset** | Abrupt, acute (related to injury) | Gradual, insidious ($>3$ months) |
| **Effect of Exercise** | **Worsens** pain | **Improves** pain |
| **Effect of Rest** | **Relieved** by rest | **No relief** (pain wakes patient in second half of night) |
| **Morning Stiffness** | Brief ($<15-30$ minutes) | **Prolonged ($\ge 30$ minutes)** |
| **Systemic Features** | Absent | Extra-articular: Uveitis, psoriasis, IBD, enthesitis |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character & Radiation of Pain**:
  * *Radicular (Sciatica)*: Sharp, shooting, electric-shock pain radiating down the posterior/lateral leg **below the knee**. Suggests nerve root compression (L4-S1). Worsened by coughing, sneezing, or straining.
  * *Localized dull ache*: Muscle strain, facet arthropathy.
  * *Claudication-like pain (pseudoclaudication)*: Leg pain/numbness provoked by walking, **relieved by sitting down or bending forward** (spinal stenosis / "shopping cart sign").
* **Red Flag Symptoms**: Fever, weight loss (malignancy/infection), saddle anesthesia, urinary retention (cauda equina).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Spine Inspection & Palpation**: Check for spinal curvature (scoliosis/kyphosis). Palpate and percuss the spinous processes. *Localized midline bone tenderness is a key sign of vertebral osteomyelitis, discitis, or compression fracture.*
* **Range of Motion**: Assess flexion, extension, lateral bending.
* **Special Bedside Maneuvers**:
  * **Straight Leg Raise (SLR) Test**: Elevate the extended leg of the supine patient. *Positive test*: Reproduction of radicular pain below the knee between $30-70$ degrees of elevation. Highly sensitive for **L5-S1 disc herniation**.
  * **Crossed SLR Test**: Elevating the unaffected leg reproduces radicular pain in the affected leg. *Highly specific for disc herniation.*
  * **Reverse SLR Test** (Femoral Stretch Test): Patient prone; hyperextend the hip. *Positive*: pain in anterior thigh (L2-L4 radiculopathy).
* **Neurological Exam of Lower Limbs (L4, L5, S1 Localization)**:
  * **L4**: Weakness in knee extension (Quadriceps); diminished patellar reflex; sensory loss over medial lower leg/foot.
  * **L5**: Weakness in great toe extension (EHL) or foot dorsiflexion; no reflex; sensory loss over dorsum of foot/first web space.
  * **S1**: Weakness in foot plantarflexion (Gastrocnemius); diminished Achilles/ankle reflex; sensory loss over lateral foot.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical** | • Lumbar muscle strain<br>• Herniated Nucleus Pulposus (HNP)<br>• Lumbar Spinal Stenosis<br>• Osteoporotic compression fracture | None |
| **Inflammatory** | • **Ankylosing Spondylitis**<br>• Psoriatic Arthritis / Reactive Arthritis | None |
| **Infectious** | None | • **Vertebral Osteomyelitis** / Discitis<br>• **Spinal Epidural Abscess** (IV drug users) |
| **Neoplastic** | None | • **Metastatic Cancer to Spine** (breast, lung, prostate, myeloma)<br>• **Cauda Equina Syndrome** (tumor/disc compression) |
| **Referred / Extra-spinal**| • Nephrolithiasis (flank pain)<br>• Pyelonephritis | • **Ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)**<br>• Acute Pancreatitis |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate MRI and surgical consultation required if present:**
> * **Cauda Equina Syndrome**: New-onset urinary retention or overflow incontinence, fecal incontinence, saddle anesthesia (numbness in perineal/buttock region), or progressive bilateral motor weakness (foot drop).
> * **Spinal Infection**: Fever, chills, IV drug abuse, recent spinal procedure, or indwelling catheters with localized spinal percussion tenderness.
> * **Spinal Malignancy**: History of cancer, unexplained weight loss, age $>50$ years, or severe pain that is constant and worse when lying flat at night.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **Inflammatory Markers (ESR / CRP)**: Elevated in spinal infection, malignancy, and inflammatory back pain.
  * **HLA-B27**: Support diagnosis of Ankylosing Spondylitis (positive in $>90\%$ of patients).
  * **Renal function / Electrolytes / Alkaline Phosphatase**: (ALP elevated in bone metastases or Paget's).
* **Imaging (Crucial)**:
  * **Lumbar Spine X-Ray**: Quick evaluation for fractures, spondylolisthesis, lytic bone lesions, or sacroiliitis (in Ankylosing Spondylitis).
  * **MRI Lumbar Spine (Gold Standard)**: Indicated immediately for any patient with red flags (cauda equina, suspected epidural abscess, discitis, cord compression, or progressive neurologic deficit).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Mechanical Back Pain (uncomplicated)**:
   - *Active recovery*: Avoid bed rest ($>24-48$ hours worsens recovery). Keep active within pain limits.
   - *First-line drugs*: NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen) or Paracetamol short-term. Avoid opioids unless refractory. Muscle relaxants can be added for acute spasms.
2. **Radiculopathy (HNP)**:
   - Conservative management for $4-6$ weeks (NSAIDs, physical therapy) in the absence of progressive neurological deficit.
   - Refer to Neurosurgery for microdiscectomy if cauda equina is present, or if radicular pain is refractory to conservative care.
3. **Inflammatory Back Pain**:
   - First-line: Continuous NSAIDs (very effective).
   - Second-line: Biologics (anti-TNF agents like Adalimumab, or IL-17 inhibitors) if refractory. *Standard DMARDs like Methotrexate are ineffective for axial disease.*

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Saddle Anesthesia Check**: Always check perianal sensation and anal sphincter tone in any patient presenting with bilateral radicular pain or new difficulty voiding. Do not skip the DRE in suspected cauda equina.
> * **The Shopping Cart Sign**: Patients with **Spinal Stenosis** experience leg pain and numbness when walking (pseudoclaudication). Bending forward (like leaning on a shopping cart) increases the diameter of the spinal canal, relieving the compression and the pain. In contrast, vascular claudication is relieved simply by standing still.
> * **Night Pain Warning**: Pain that is constant, does not improve with any position, and is **worse when lying flat at night** is a classic presentation of spinal malignancy (metastatic disease). Benign mechanical pain is usually relieved by lying down.
