# 🫁 Hemoptysis (ไอเป็นเลือด)

> "Hemoptysis is the expectoration of blood originating subglottically from the respiratory tract. Massive hemoptysis is an airway emergency, as asphyxiation is the primary cause of death."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Massive Hemoptysis Alert**: Defined as **$>200-600$ mL of blood within 24 hours** (or any volume causing respiratory distress/hypoxia). **This is an airway emergency.**
> 2. **Immediate Positioning (Massive)**: Position the patient with the **bleeding (bad) lung side down** in the lateral decubitus position. This prevents blood from gravity-draining into and flooding the healthy lung.
> 3. **True Hemoptysis vs. Pseudohemoptysis**: Rule out hematemesis or nasopharyngeal bleeding (epistaxis).
> 4. **Obtain Chest X-Ray & CT Chest** immediately.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Hemoptysis**: Expectoration of blood from the lower respiratory tract (below the vocal cords).
* **Vascular Origins**:
  * **Bronchial Arteries** ($\sim 90\%$ of cases): High-pressure systemic circulation (arising from aorta). *Most massive hemoptysis originates here.*
  * **Pulmonary Arteries** ($\sim 10\%$ of cases): Low-pressure pulmonary circulation.
* **Pseudohemoptysis**: Blood originating from the oral cavity, nasopharynx, or upper GI tract.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### 📊 Hemoptysis vs. Hematemesis Differentiator

| Feature | Hemoptysis (Lungs) | Hematemesis (GI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prodrome** | Coughing, tickling in throat | Nausea, retching, epigastric discomfort |
| **Color** | **Bright red** or pink | Dark red, brown ("coffee-ground"), or black |
| **pH** | **Alkaline** | **Acidic** |
| **Associated Material**| **Frothy sputum, air bubbles** | Food particles, bile |
| **Melena** | Rare (unless blood is swallowed) | **Very common** |

---

### ⏳ Clinical Clues
* **Bleeding Volume**: Dry streaks (bronchitis) vs. cupfuls of pure blood (TB cavern, bronchiectasis, cancer).
* **Infectious Clues**: Fever, productive cough, night sweats, weight loss $\rightarrow$ [[Tuberculosis]] or lung abscess.
* **Malignancy Clues**: Heavy smoking history, chronic progressive weight loss, age $>45$ years $\rightarrow$ Bronchogenic carcinoma.
* **Cardiovascular Clues**: Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, history of rheumatic fever $\rightarrow$ **Mitral Stenosis** (causes pulmonary venous hypertension and rupture of bronchial veins).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Assess for respiratory distress (tachypnea, accessory muscle use, hypoxia) and hypovolemic shock.
* **HEENT**: Inspect the nasal cavity and posterior pharynx to rule out active epistaxis or oral bleeding (pseudohemoptysis).
* **Chest & Lungs**:
  * Localized crackles or wheezing can help localize the side of the bleeding.
* **Cardiovascular**: Listen for the classic **diastolic rumbling murmur** at the apex with an opening snap (**Mitral Stenosis**), or signs of pulmonary hypertension (loud P2).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infectious** | • Acute Bronchitis (most common non-massive)<br>• Bacterial Pneumonia | • **[[Tuberculosis]]** (active or cavernous / Rasmussen's aneurysm)<br>• **Lung Abscess** / Mycetoma (Aspergilloma) |
| **Neoplastic** | None | • **Bronchogenic Carcinoma** (Lung cancer)<br>• Bronchial Adenoma / Metastases |
| **Cardiovascular** | None | • **Mitral Stenosis** (pulmonary venous congestion)<br>• **Pulmonary Embolism (PE) / Infarction**<br>• Pulmonary AV malformation |
| **Immunological** | None | • **Pulmonary-Renal Syndromes** (GPA, Goodpasture's, Lupus pneumonitis) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit to ICU, call ENT/Pulmonology and Interventional Radiology immediately if present:**
> * **Massive Hemoptysis**: $>200-600$ mL of blood in 24 hours.
> * Signs of impending asphyxiation: Severe dyspnea, stridor, accessory muscle use, or hypoxemia ($SpO_2 < 90\%$).
> * Hemodynamic instability / [[Shock]].
> * Hemoptysis in a patient on therapeutic anticoagulants (Warfarin, DOACs).
> * Concurrent hematuria (suspect pulmonary-renal syndrome $\rightarrow$ RPGN risk).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Mandatory first-line test. Look for localized infiltrates (pneumonia/TB), masses (cancer), cavities (TB/abscess), or consolidations.
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT) & CBC**: Assess bleeding risk and baseline hemoglobin.
  * **Sputum AFB Smears & GeneXpert**: Essential if TB suspected.
  * **Urinalysis**: Check for hematuria (pulmonary-renal syndrome check).
* **Diagnostic Standard**:
  * **CT Chest (with IV contrast) / CT Angiography**: Superior to CXR for identifying bronchiectasis, small tumors, and mapping bleeding bronchial arteries.
  * **Bronchoscopy**: Flexible bronchoscopy is used to localize the segment of active bleeding. Rigid bronchoscopy is preferred in massive bleeding for airway clearance and ventilation.

## 🏥 Management Principles

### 1. Massive Hemoptysis (Airway First!)
- **Positioning**: Place the patient in **lateral decubitus, bleeding lung DOWN**.
- **Secure Airway**: Intubate with a large-bore endotracheal tube (size 8.0 or larger) to facilitate suctioning and bronchoscopy. Consider mainstem intubation of the non-bleeding lung.
- **Stop the Bleeding**:
  - *First-line intervention*: **Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE)** via interventional radiology.
  - *Endoscopic*: Cold saline lavage, epinephrine instillation, or balloon tamponade via bronchoscopy.
  - *Surgical*: Segmental lobectomy (refractory cases).

### 2. Non-Massive Hemoptysis
- Treat the underlying cause: Antibiotics for bronchitis/pneumonia; anti-TB therapy; hold anticoagulants/correct coagulopathy.
- Cough suppression (Codeine) to prevent mechanical aggravation of the bleeding site.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Asphyxiation over Hemorrhage**: Patients with massive hemoptysis do not bleed to death (the total blood volume in the lungs is small). They die of **asphyxiation** because blood clots flood the alveoli and block ventilation. Protecting the healthy lung is the most important step.
> * **Rasmussen's Aneurysm**: A rare but classic cause of massive hemoptysis in patients with history of pulmonary tuberculosis. It represents an aneurysm of a pulmonary artery branch inside a chronic tuberculous cavity, which subsequently ruptures.
> * **Goodpasture's Mnemonic**: Goodpasture's syndrome is an autoimmune disease against Type IV collagen (anti-GBM antibodies) affecting the **G**lomerulus (hematuria/nephritic) and **B**ronchial **M**embranes (hemoptysis).
