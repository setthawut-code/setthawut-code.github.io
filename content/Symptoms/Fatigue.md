# 🥱 Fatigue (อ่อนเพลีย ไม่มีแรง)

> "Fatigue is one of the most common yet non-specific symptoms in clinical medicine. The primary challenge is to differentiate organic (physical) from psychogenic (mental) fatigue."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Organic vs. Psychogenic**:
>    - *Organic*: Relieved by rest; worsens with exertion; progressive; specific symptoms point to an organ system.
>    - *Psychogenic*: Not relieved by rest; worse in the morning; fluctuates; associated with depression/stress.
> 2. **Rule Out Endocrine/Metabolic Emergencies**: Check for signs of Adrenal Insufficiency, severe Hypothyroidism (myxedema), or hypercalcemia.
> 3. **Screen for Depression**: Use the PHQ-2 or PHQ-9 screen.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Fatigue is a subjective feeling of tiredness, lack of energy, or exhaustion that interferes with normal physical or mental activities. It is distinct from weakness (objective loss of muscle power) and somnolence (excessive sleepiness/need to sleep).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Organic vs. Psychogenic Fatigue

| Feature | Organic Fatigue | Psychogenic Fatigue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Gradual, progressive | Abrupt, fluctuates with stress |
| **Duration** | Typically persistent ($>1-3$ months) | Intermittent or chronic |
| **Diurnal Variation** | **Worse in evening** / after exertion | **Worse in morning**; improves during day |
| **Effect of Rest** | **Relieved by rest** or sleep | **Not relieved** by sleep (awakes tired) |
| **Exertion Effect** | Decreases physical capacity | Varies; may improve with distraction |
| **Associated Signs** | Weight loss, fever, dyspnea, pallor | Anxiety, depression, sleep disturbances, multiple somatic complaints |

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Document Blood Pressure (hypotension in adrenal insufficiency) and Temperature (fever in chronic infection/malignancy).
* **General Appearance**: Look for psychomotor retardation, flat affect (depression), or cachexia (malignancy).
* **HEENT & Neck**:
  * *Conjunctival pallor* $\rightarrow$ [[Anemia]].
  * *Thyroid palpation*: Goiter or nodules (hypo/hyperthyroidism).
  * *Lymphadenopathy* $\rightarrow$ Malignancy, chronic infection (HIV, TB, EBV).
* **Endocrine Signs**:
  * *Hypothyroidism*: Dry skin, coarse hair, delayed relaxation phase of deep tendon reflexes, periorbital puffiness.
  * *Addison's Disease (Adrenal Insufficiency)*: Hyperpigmentation of palmar creases, oral mucosa, and scars.
* **Neurological**: Perform objective muscle strength testing to rule out true neuromuscular weakness (e.g., Myasthenia Gravis, myopathy).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endocrine / Metabolic** | • Hypothyroidism / Hyperthyroidism<br>• Diabetes Mellitus<br>• Chronic Kidney Disease (Uremia)<br>• Electrolyte imbalance (Hypokalemia, Hypercalcemia) | • **Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's)**<br>• Severe Hypercalcemia (Malignancy-associated)<br>• Myxedema Coma (impending) |
| **Infectious & Inflammatory** | • Chronic hepatitis<br>• Rheumatoid Arthritis / SLE | • **[[Tuberculosis]]**<br>• **Sepsis** (subacute/occult)<br>• **HIV infection** / Infective Endocarditis |
| **Hematologic / Oncologic** | • [[Anemia]] (Iron, B12, or Folate deficiency) | • **Hematologic Malignancies** (Leukemia, Lymphoma, Myeloma)<br>• Solid tumors |
| **Psychogenic / Sleep** | • Major Depressive Disorder<br>• Generalized Anxiety Disorder<br>• Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) | • **Carbon Monoxide poisoning** (chronic low-level exposure) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit or fast-track diagnostic workup if present:**
> * Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or persistent fever (malignancy / TB).
> * Postural hypotension with hyponatremia and hyperkalemia (adrenal crisis).
> * Progressive muscle weakness or bulbar signs (neuromuscular disorder).
> * Acute onset of severe dyspnea or chest pain on exertion.
> * Suicidal ideation in patients with depression.
> * Lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Rule out anemia, leukemia, or chronic infection.
  * **Fasting Blood Glucose / HbA1c**: Rule out Diabetes Mellitus.
  * **TSH & Free T4**: Screen for thyroid disorders.
  * **Electrolytes & Renal Function (BUN/Cr)**: Rule out uremia, hypokalemia, hyponatremia, or hypercalcemia.
  * **Liver Function Tests**: Rule out chronic hepatitis or cirrhosis.
  * **Urinalysis**: Screen for kidney disease or occult infection.
* **Targeted Testing (if clinically indicated)**:
  * **Morning Cortisol (8:00 AM)**: Screen for adrenal insufficiency (if $<3$ mcg/dL, highly suggestive; if $>19$ mcg/dL, rules out). Follow with ACTH stimulation test if intermediate.
  * **Iron Studies**: Ferritin, TIBC (iron deficiency can cause fatigue even before anemia develops).
  * **HIV Serology & chest X-ray**: Screen for HIV and TB.
  * **Polysomnography (Sleep study)**: If history suggests Obstructive Sleep Apnea (snoring, daytime somnolence).

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Etiology-Specific Treatment**:
   - Thyroid replacement (Levothyroxine) for hypothyroidism.
   - Glucocorticoid/mineralocorticoid replacement (Hydrocortisone/Fludrocortisone) for adrenal insufficiency.
   - Iron/vitamin supplementation for nutritional anemia.
2. **Psychogenic Fatigue**:
   - Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and graded exercise therapy are first-line for chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) and fibromyalgia.
   - Pharmacotherapy (SSRIs/SNRIs) if Major Depressive Disorder or anxiety is diagnosed.
3. **Sleep Hygiene**:
   - Optimize sleep patterns, avoid caffeine/stimulants before bed, and manage OSA with CPAP.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Adrenal Insufficiency Diagnostic Trap**: Standard electrolytes show hyponatremia and hyperkalemia in *primary* adrenal insufficiency (Addison's) due to lack of aldosterone. However, in *secondary* adrenal insufficiency (pituitary disease), aldosterone levels are normal, so potassium is typically normal, and only hyponatremia is present.
> * **Non-Anemic Iron Deficiency**: Iron deficiency without anemia (normal Hb/Hct but low ferritin) is a well-documented cause of fatigue. Iron replacement therapy in these patients can significantly improve energy levels.
> * **Weakness vs. Fatigue**: Always ask the patient: "If you had the energy, could you physically lift your arm or stand up?" If yes, it is fatigue. If no, it is objective weakness, pointing to a neuromuscular disorder rather than a systemic cause of fatigue.
