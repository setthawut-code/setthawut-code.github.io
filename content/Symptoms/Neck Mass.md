# 頸 Neck Mass (ก้อนที่คอ)

> "The evaluation of a neck mass is guided by the patient's age and the anatomical location of the mass. In adults $>40$ years, a new persistent neck mass is neoplastic until proven otherwise."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Apply the "Rule of 80" for Adults**: In adults presenting with a non-thyroid neck mass:
>    - $\sim 80\%$ are **neoplastic**.
>    - $\sim 80\%$ of those are **malignant**.
>    - $\sim 80\%$ of those are **metastatic** (mostly squamous cell carcinoma from head/neck primary).
> 2. **Check Anatomical Location**: Midline vs. Lateral.
> 3. **Rule Out Airway Compression**: Check for stridor, dysphagia, or new-onset hoarseness.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

A neck mass is any abnormal swelling or lump in the neck region. It can originate from lymph nodes, thyroid gland, salivary glands, blood vessels, or developmental cysts.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Age of Patient (Crucial Differentiator)**:
  * *Pediatric / Young adult* ($<20$ years): Most masses are **inflammatory** (lymphadenitis) or **congenital** (thyroglossal duct cyst, branchial cleft cyst).
  * *Older adult* ($>40$ years): Most persistent masses are **neoplastic** (metastatic cancer, lymphoma).
* **Duration & Growth**:
  * *Rule of 7s*: Transient masses lasting **7 days** are inflammatory; masses persisting **7 weeks** are neoplastic; masses present for **7 years** are congenital.
* **Pain & Tenderness**:
  * *Tender, painful, warm* $\rightarrow$ Inflammatory/infectious (abscess, lymphadenitis).
  * *Painless, slowly enlarging, firm/hard* $\rightarrow$ Malignancy.
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * Sore throat, dental pain, ear pain $\rightarrow$ Infectious source.
  * Hoarseness of voice, dysphagia, hemoptysis $\rightarrow$ Laryngeal/hypopharyngeal malignancy.
  * Fever, night sweats, weight loss $\rightarrow$ [[Tuberculosis]] or [[Lymphoma]].
  * Heat/cold intolerance, weight change $\rightarrow$ Thyroid disease.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Anatomical Location (Midline vs. Lateral)**:
  * **Midline masses**: Move with swallowing/tongue protrusion?
    * *Moves with swallowing and tongue protrusion* $\rightarrow$ **Thyroglossal Duct Cyst**.
    * *Moves with swallowing but NOT tongue protrusion* $\rightarrow$ **Thyroid Mass** (goiter, nodule).
    * *Does not move with either* $\rightarrow$ Dermoid cyst, submental lymph node.
  * **Lateral masses**:
    * *Anterior to sternocleidomastoid (SCM)* $\rightarrow$ Branchial cleft cyst, lymphadenopathy.
    * *Posterior triangle* $\rightarrow$ Lymphadenopathy (metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma, lymphoma, TB).
    * *Supraclavicular area* $\rightarrow$ Virchow's node (left-sided; GI/pelvic malignancy metastasis) or pulmonary metastasis.

* **Mass Characteristics**:
  * *Soft/fluctuant*: Cystic (congenital, abscess).
  * *Firm/rubbery*: Characteristic of [[Lymphoma]].
  * *Hard, fixed to underlying tissue*: Highly suggestive of **metastatic carcinoma**.
* **Comprehensive Head & Neck Exam**: Perform detailed inspection of the oral cavity, tonsils, tongue, and nasopharynx to search for a primary malignancy.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 Etiology Classification Matrix

| Category | Midline Masses | Lateral Masses |

| **Congenital** | • Thyroglossal duct cyst<br>• Dermoid cyst | • Branchial cleft cyst (anterior to SCM)<br>• Laryngocele / Cystic hygroma |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Investigate aggressively for malignancy if present:**
> * Fixed, hard, non-tender neck mass.
> * Supraclavicular neck mass (any size/character).
> * Mass enlarging progressively for $>3-4$ weeks.
> * New-onset hoarseness of voice, dysphagia, or odynophagia.
> * Unilateral serous otitis media or nasal obstruction.
> * Systemic B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Imaging**:
  * **Neck Ultrasound**: Excellent first step to differentiate solid from cystic masses, map thyroid nodules, and characterize lymph nodes (loss of fatty hilum suggests malignancy).
* **Gold Standard Tissue Diagnosis**:
  * **Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNAB)**: Highly accurate bedside test for thyroid nodules and suspected metastatic/lymph node masses.
  * *Avoid Open Incisional Biopsy*: Do **not** perform an open biopsy of a suspicious lateral neck mass in an adult before doing an FNAB and thorough search for primary head/neck cancer, as this disrupts fascial planes and compromises future curative surgery.
* **Secondary Imaging**:
  * **CT Neck (with contrast)**: Delineates deep neck spaces, evaluates tissue invasion, and identifies head/neck primary tumors.
  * **Chest X-ray**: Rule out primary lung cancer or mediastinal adenopathy.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Inflammatory / Infectious**:
   - Empiric antibiotics for suspected bacterial lymphadenitis. If abscess present: needle aspiration or surgical drainage.
   - Anti-TB therapy (4-drug regimen) for verified Tuberculous lymphadenitis.
2. **Thyroglossal Duct Cyst**:
   - Surgical excision via the **Sistrunk procedure** (removal of the cyst, duct tract, and midline portion of the hyoid bone to prevent recurrence).
3. **Malignant Neck Mass**:
   - Metastatic SCC: Locate primary head/neck tumor (panendoscopy + biopsy) and treat with surgery, radiation, and/or chemotherapy.
   - Thyroid cancer: Thyroidectomy +/- radioactive iodine ablation.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Supraclavicular Rule**: A mass in the supraclavicular fossa is malignant until proven otherwise. A left supraclavicular mass (**Virchow's node** or *Troisier's sign*) indicates intra-abdominal malignancy (stomach, pancreas, colon); a right supraclavicular mass points to mediastinal, lung, or esophageal malignancy.
> * **The Thyroid Swallowing Mechanism**: Thyroid tissue and thyroglossal duct cysts move upward with swallowing because they are enclosed in the pretracheal fascia, which is attached to the larynx and hyoid bone.
> * **Reactive vs. Malignant Lymph Nodes**: Reactive nodes are typically tender, mobile, and small. Malignant nodes are painless, hard, fixed to surrounding tissues, and may matted together.
