# 🩸 Hematuria (ปัสสาวะเป็นเลือด)

> "Hematuria can indicate pathology anywhere from the glomerulus to the urethra. Painless gross hematuria in an adult is malignancy until proven otherwise."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **True Hematuria vs. Pigmenturia**: Confirm presence of RBCs under microscopy. Dipstick (+) without RBCs under microscope indicates myoglobinuria (rhabdomyolysis) or hemoglobinuria.
> 2. **Glomerular vs. Non-glomerular**:
>    - *Glomerular*: Red/brown urine, RBC casts, dysmorphic RBCs, significant proteinuria.
>    - *Non-glomerular*: Red/pink urine, blood clots, normal RBCs, minimal proteinuria.
> 3. **Rule Out Bladder Cancer**: Painless gross hematuria in patients $>35-40$ years or smokers requires urgent cystoscopy and CT Urogram.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Microscopic Hematuria**: $\ge 3$ red blood cells (RBCs) per high-power field (hpf) on microscopic examination of a centrifuged urine sediment.
* **Gross (Macroscopic) Hematuria**: Visually apparent discoloration of urine (pink, red, or brown). As little as 1 mL of blood in 1 liter of urine can produce gross hematuria.

### 📊 True Hematuria vs. Red/Dark Urine Mimics

| Centrifuged Urine Findings | Urine Dipstick for Blood | Microscope Examination | Common Causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Red Sediment** (Normal supernatant) | **Positive** | **$\ge 3$ RBCs/hpf** | True Hematuria (nephrolithiasis, UTI, tumor) |
| **Red Supernatant** | **Positive** | **No RBCs** | Hemoglobinuria (hemolysis) or Myoglobinuria (rhabdomyolysis) |
| **Red Supernatant** | **Negative** | **No RBCs** | Food dyes (beets, blackberries), Drugs (Rifampin, Phenazopyridine) |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Timing & Associated Clues
* **Timing of Hematuria during Voiding**:
  * *Initial hematuria* (beginning of stream): Suggests **urethral** source.
  * *Terminal hematuria* (end of stream): Suggests **bladder neck** or **prostate** source.
  * *Total hematuria* (throughout stream): Suggests **kidney** or **ureter** source.
* **Pain vs. Painless**:
  * *Painful*: UTI (dysuria), Nephrolithiasis (acute colicky flank pain), passage of blood clots.
  * *Painless*: Malignancy (urothelial/bladder cancer, RCC), chronic glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease.
* **Systemic Clues**:
  * Recent upper respiratory tract infection ($1-2$ days ago $\rightarrow$ IgA Nephropathy; $1-3$ weeks ago $\rightarrow$ Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis).
  * Weight loss, anorexia $\rightarrow$ Advanced malignancy.
  * Joint pain, skin rash, oral ulcers $\rightarrow$ Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (Lupus Nephritis).
  * Hemoptysis (coughing blood) $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary-Renal Syndrome (e.g., Goodpasture's disease, GPA).
  * Risk factors for urothelial cancer: Smoking history, occupational exposure to aromatic amines/dyes (rubber, chemical industries), cyclophosphamide use.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Document Blood Pressure. Hypertension is a classic sign of nephritic syndrome / glomerulonephritis. Check for fever (UTI/pyelonephritis, RCC).
* **General**: Look for periorbital or peripheral edema (fluid overload in nephritic/nephrotic syndrome). Check for skin rashes (SLE, IgA vasculitis/Henoch-Schönlein purpura).
* **Abdomen & Flank**:
  * Costovertebral Angle (CVA) tenderness $\rightarrow$ [[Acute Pyelonephritis]] or nephrolithiasis.
  * Palpable abdominal mass $\rightarrow$ Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC) or Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD).
  * Suprapubic tenderness $\rightarrow$ Acute Cystitis.
* **Genitourinary / Rectal**:
  * Digital Rectal Exam (DRE) in men $\rightarrow$ Prostate assessment (BPH, prostatitis, prostate cancer).
  * Inspect urethral meatus for bleeding, trauma, or meatal stenosis.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 Glomerular vs. Non-Glomerular Differentiation

| Parameter | Glomerular Hematuria | Non-Glomerular Hematuria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Urine Color** | Brown, cola-colored, or smoky | Red, pink, or bright red |
| **Blood Clots** | **Absent** (urinary urokinase dissolves clots) | **Present** (highly indicative of non-glomerular) |
| **RBC Morphology** | **Dysmorphic RBCs** or Acanthocytes ($>5\%$) | Monomorphic, normal-appearing RBCs |
| **Proteinuria** | Significant ($>500$ mg/day or $>2+$ on dipstick) | Minimal ($<500$ mg/day) |
| **Casts** | **RBC Casts** or granular casts | Absent |

| Category | Primary Etiologies | Diagnostic Clues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Glomerular** | • IgA Nephropathy<br>• Post-Streptococcal GN (PSGN)<br>• Lupus Nephritis (SLE)<br>• Rapidly Progressive GN (RPGN) | Dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts, hypertension, edema, elevated creatinine. |
| **Non-Glomerular (Upper Tract)** | • Nephrolithiasis (Kidney stones)<br>• Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC)<br>• Pyelonephritis / Renal Abscess | Unilateral flank pain, hematuria with clots, normal RBCs on microscopy, fever. |
| **Non-Glomerular (Lower Tract)** | • Urinary Tract Infection (Cystitis)<br>• Bladder Cancer / Urothelial Cancer<br>• Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)<br>• Urethritis / Prostate Cancer | Dysuria, frequency, painless gross hematuria in older smoker, terminal hematuria, enlarged prostate on DRE. |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate intervention and rapid workup required if present:**
> * Painless gross hematuria in any patient $>35$ years (malignancy check).
> * Hematuria + Hemoptysis (pulmonary-renal syndrome).
> * Acute renal failure (rapidly rising creatinine) + active urine sediment (RBC casts) $\rightarrow$ Impending RPGN.
> * Gross hematuria with large clots causing urinary tract obstruction / acute urinary retention.
> * Hematuria with fever, hypotension, and shock ([[Sepsis]] from urosepsis).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Urinalysis]] & Microscopy**: Confirm $\ge 3$ RBCs/hpf. Check for protein, nitrites, leukocyte esterase, and cast presence.
  * **Urine Phase-Contrast Microscopy**: Visualizes RBC morphology (dysmorphic vs. isomorphic).
  * **Renal Function Tests (Creatinine/BUN)**: Assess baseline GFR.
  * **Urine Culture**: If infection is suspected.
* **Secondary Workup for Non-Glomerular / Gross Hematuria**:
  * **CT Urogram** (with and without IV contrast): Gold standard imaging for evaluating upper tract pathology (stones, renal masses, urothelial tumors of the ureter).
  * **Renal Ultrasound**: Preferred in pregnant patients, children, or those with severe CKD (contraindicated for contrast).
  * **Cystoscopy**: Primary diagnostic tool to evaluate the lower urinary tract (bladder mucosa, urethra). Mandatory for all patients with risk factors for bladder cancer, regardless of imaging findings.
* **Workup for Glomerular Hematuria**:
  * **Quantification of Proteinuria**: Spot Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (UPCR) or 24-hour urine protein.
  * **Immunological Workup**: ANA, dsDNA (SLE), C3/C4 levels (low in PSGN, Lupus, MPGN), ANCA, anti-GBM.
  * **Renal Biopsy**: Indicated if glomerular disease is suspected with significant proteinuria, renal impairment, or progressive deterioration.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Gross Hematuria with Clot Retention**:
   - Insert a large-bore (20-24 Fr) 3-way Foley catheter. Perform manual bladder irrigation to clear clots. Start continuous bladder irrigation (CBI) with sterile normal saline to prevent recurrent clot formation.
2. **Urosepsis**:
   - Start IV broad-spectrum antibiotics immediately after drawing blood/urine cultures.
3. **Nephrolithiasis**:
   - Hydration, analgesia (NSAIDs/opioids), and tamsulosin (alpha-blocker) to facilitate stone passage. Refer to Urology if stone $>10$ mm, refractory pain, or associated with infection.
4. **Glomerular Disease**:
   - Refer to Nephrology. Management may involve blood pressure control (ACE inhibitors or ARBs for antiproteinuric effects) and immunosuppressive therapy (corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Dipstick Trap**: A positive dipstick for blood only detects the pseudoperoxidase activity of hemoglobin. It does **not** replace microscopic examination. Always confirm with microscopy to rule out hemoglobinuria or myoglobinuria.
> * **Painless Gross Hematuria**: Even if the gross hematuria resolves spontaneously, a full diagnostic workup (CT Urogram + Cystoscopy) is still mandatory. Intermittent bleeding is common in bladder cancer.
> * **IgA vs. PSGN Timing**:
>   - *IgA Nephropathy*: "Synpharyngitic" hematuria (occurs *during* or within $1-2$ days of URI).
>   - *Post-Streptococcal GN*: "Post-pharyngitic" hematuria (occurs $1-3$ weeks *after* throat or skin infection; associated with low C3 complement levels).
