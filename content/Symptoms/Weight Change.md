# ⚖️ Weight Change (น้ำหนักเพิ่ม/ลด)

> "Weight change is a sentinel sign of underlying systemic pathology. The clinical focus must distinguish voluntary from involuntary changes, and fat mass accumulation from fluid retention."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Quantify Weight Loss**: Involuntary loss of **$\ge 5\%$ of body weight within $6-12$ months** is clinically significant and warrants evaluation.
> 2. **Differentiate Weight Gain**:
>    - *Rapid onset* (days to weeks): Almost always due to **Fluid Accumulation** ([[Heart Failure]], [[Cirrhosis]], [[Nephrotic Syndrome]]).
>    - *Slow onset* (months): Adipose tissue accumulation or endocrine disorders.
> 3. **Appetite Check**: Differentiate weight loss with *increased* appetite (Hyperthyroidism, DM, Malabsorption) from *decreased* appetite (Cancer, TB, Depression).

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Weight change represents a mismatch between energy intake and energy expenditure, or a significant change in total body water.
* **Involuntary Weight Loss (Wasting)**: Often driven by cytokines (TNF-$\alpha$, IL-1, IL-6) causing cachexia in cancer or chronic infections.
* **Fluid Overload Weight Gain**: Mediated by renal sodium and water retention due to decreased effective arterial blood volume (EABV) or renal failure.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Weight Loss: Appetite Pattern
> [!TIP]
> **Appetite status is the key branching point in weight loss history:**

| Appetite Status        | Possible Etiologies                                                                                                                                          | Diagnostic Clues                                                     |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Increased Appetite** | • [[Hyperthyroidism]]<br>• [[Diabetes Mellitus]]<br>• Malabsorption (Celiac, Chronic Pancreatitis)                                                           | Heat intolerance, tremors, polyuria/polydipsia, chronic steatorrhea. |
| **Decreased Appetite** | • Malignancy (GI, lymphoma)<br>• Chronic Infection ([[Tuberculosis]], HIV)<br>• Psychiatric (Depression, Anorexia)<br>• Advanced Organ Failure (Heart/Renal) | Night sweats, lymphadenopathy, early satiety, flat affect, dyspnea.  |

---

### ⏳ Weight Gain: Speed of Onset
* **Rapid Gain** (fluid): Associated with swelling of legs (pedal edema), abdominal girth increase (ascites), or facial puffiness.
* **Slow Gain** (endocrine/adipose): Look for history of joint pain (decreased mobility), chronic steroid use, cold intolerance (hypothyroidism), or easy bruising (Cushing's).
* **Medication History**: Systemic corticosteroids, insulin, sulfonylureas, atypical antipsychotics (Olanzapine), tricyclic antidepressants.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **General**: Calculate Body Mass Index (BMI). Look for muscle wasting (temporal wasting, prominent clavicles suggesting cachexia).
* **Endocrine & Cushingoid Signs**:
  * *Hypothyroidism*: Dry skin, coarse hair, bradycardia, delayed reflex relaxation.
  * *Cushing's Syndrome*: Moon face, buffalo hump, supraclavicular fat pads, abdominal purple striae ($>1$ cm wide), proximal muscle weakness.
* **Fluid Overload Signs**:
  * *HEENT*: Jugular Venous Distension (JVD).
  * *Lungs*: Coarse crackles (pulmonary edema).
  * *Abdomen*: Ascites (shifting dullness, fluid wave).
  * *Extremities*: Bilateral pitting pedal edema.
* **Malignancy / Infection Signs**:
  * Palpate all lymph node basins (cervical, supraclavicular, axillary, inguinal).
  * Hepatomegaly or splenomegaly.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Weight Loss | Weight Gain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Endocrine / Metabolic** | • [[Diabetes Mellitus]] (Type 1 or poorly controlled Type 2)<br>• [[Hyperthyroidism]] (Thyrotoxicosis)<br>• Adrenal Insufficiency | • [[Hypothyroidism]] (myxedema)<br>• Cushing's Syndrome (hypercortisolism)<br>• Hypothalamic obesity / Growth hormone deficiency |
| **Oncologic & Infectious** | • **Solid Tumors** (GI, lung, renal)<br>• **Hematologic Malignancies** (Lymphoma, Leukemia)<br>• **[[Tuberculosis]]** / HIV | None |
| **Gastrointestinal** | • Malabsorption (Celiac disease)<br>• Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Crohn's)<br>• Chronic pancreatitis | None |
| **Organ Dysfunction (Fluid)**| None | • **[[Heart Failure]]** (Congestive)<br>• **[[Nephrotic Syndrome]]** (Proteinuria)<br>• **[[Cirrhosis]]** (Portal HTN) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent diagnostic workup required if weight loss is accompanied by:**
> * Persistent fever, drenching night sweats (B symptoms $\rightarrow$ Lymphoma / TB).
> * New-onset progressive dysphagia or persistent vomiting.
> * Change in bowel habits, occult blood in stool (GI malignancy).
> * Unilateral cervical or supraclavicular lymph node (Virchow's node $\rightarrow$ gastric cancer).
> * Rapidly progressive dyspnea with orthopnea (fluid overload in heart failure).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Workup for Weight Loss**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Rule out cytopenias or leukocytosis (malignancy, chronic infection).
  * **Basic Chemistries (Electrolytes, Cr, BUN)**: Assess renal function and calcium levels (hypercalcemia in malignancy).
  * **TSH & Free T4**: Screen for hyperthyroidism.
  * **Fasting Glucose & HbA1c**: Screen for Diabetes Mellitus.
  * **Liver Function Tests & Albumin**: Low albumin suggests malabsorption, malnutrition, or liver disease.
  * **ESR & CRP**: Markers of chronic inflammation.
  * **Urinalysis**: Screen for proteinuria or glucosuria.
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Screen for pulmonary TB or primary/metastatic lung cancer.
* **First-line Workup for Fluid Weight Gain**:
  * **Urinalysis**: Check spot UPCR or 24-hr urine protein (proteinuria $>3.5$ g/day $\rightarrow$ [[Nephrotic Syndrome]]).
  * **BNP / NT-proBNP & Echocardiogram**: Assess for Heart Failure.
  * **Liver Ultrasound & LFTs**: Check for Cirrhosis.
* **Targeted Testing for Cushing's** (if suspected):
  * 24-hour urinary free cortisol, late-night salivary cortisol, or overnight 1-mg Dexamethasone Suppression Test.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Involuntary Weight Loss**:
   - Treat the underlying cause (e.g., cancer treatment, anti-TB therapy, thyroid regulation).
   - Nutritional support: High-calorie, high-protein diet, small frequent meals. Avoid prescription of appetite stimulants (Megestrol acetate) unless indicated (has minimal long-term benefit, increases DVT risk).
2. **Fluid Weight Gain**:
   - Limit sodium ($<2$ g/day) and fluid intake.
   - IV/oral Loop diuretics (Furosemide) for rapid volume removal.
3. **Endocrine weight gain**:
   - Levothyroxine titration in hypothyroidism.
   - Surgical excision of ACTH-secreting tumor or adrenalectomy in Cushing's.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Hyperthyroid Elderly Exception**: Elderly patients with hyperthyroidism may not present with classic hyperactive symptoms (tremor, anxiety). Instead, they can present with **"Apathetic Hyperthyroidism"**, characterized by profound weight loss, depression, fatigue, and atrial fibrillation.
> * **Occult Malignancy Screening**: In patients with unexplained involuntary weight loss, do not order random "pan-scans" immediately. Start with a thorough history, physical (including rectal/pelvic exam), basic labs, chest X-ray, and age-appropriate cancer screenings (Mammography, Colonoscopy, Pap smear).
> * **Cushing's Striae**: The striae in Cushing's syndrome are classically wide ($>1$ cm) and deep reddish-purple due to glucocorticoid-induced dermal collagen wasting, which makes the vascular subcutaneous tissue visible. Normal pregnancy/puberty stretch marks are usually narrow and pinkish-white.
