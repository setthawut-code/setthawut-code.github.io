# 🦴 Arthritis (ข้ออักเสบ)

> "Arthritis is defined by objective joint inflammation. The clinical focus requires immediate classification by the number of joints involved (mono vs. poly) and synovial fluid analysis."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Identify Septic Monoarthritis**: An acute **monoarthritis** (single swollen, red, hot, painful joint) with fever is **Septic Arthritis** until proven otherwise. **Perform joint fluid aspiration (arthrocentesis) immediately.**
> 2. **Interpret Synovial Fluid**: Differentiate non-inflammatory (WBC $<2000$), inflammatory (WBC $2000-50,000$), and septic (WBC $>50,000$) fluid profiles.
> 3. **Polyarthritis Localization**: Chronic symmetric polyarthritis affecting small joints $\rightarrow$ Think **Rheumatoid Arthritis**.

---

## 🔍 Syncope & Diagnostic Synovial Fluid Profiles

In any patient with an unexplained joint effusion, **arthrocentesis** is the gold standard diagnostic step:

### 📊 Synovial Fluid Analysis Interpretation Table

| Profile | Color & Clarity | WBC Count ($/\mu\text{L}$) | PMNs (\%) | Gram Stain & Crystals | Key Etiologies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | Clear / Straw-colored | $<200$ | $<25\%$ | Negative | Normal joints |
| **Non-Inflammatory** | Clear / Yellow | $200 - 2,000$ | $<25\%$ | Negative | **Osteoarthritis**, trauma |
| **Inflammatory** | Cloudy / Yellow | **$2,000 - 50,000$** | $\ge 50\%$ | **Crystals present** (MSU/CPPD) | **Gout**, **Pseudogout**, Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE |
| **Septic (Infectious)** | **Purulent / Turbid** | **$>50,000$** | **$\ge 75\%$** | **Often positive Gram stain / Culture** | **Septic Arthritis** (e.g., *S. aureus*, *N. gonorrhoeae*) |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Number of Involved Joints**:
  * **Monoarthritis** (1 joint) $\rightarrow$ Septic arthritis, Gout, Pseudogout, trauma, osteoarthritis flare.
  * **Oligoarthritis** ($2-4$ joints) $\rightarrow$ Reactive arthritis, Psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) arthritis.
  * **Polyarthritis** ($\ge 5$ joints) $\rightarrow$ Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE, systemic sclerosis, viral arthritis (Parvovirus B19, Hepatitis B/C, Dengue).
* **Symmetry**:
  * *Symmetric* (same joints bilaterally) $\rightarrow$ Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE.
  * *Asymmetric* $\rightarrow$ Psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, gout.
* **Duration**: Acute ($<6$ weeks) vs. Chronic ($\ge 6$ weeks).
* **Morning Stiffness**: Morning stiffness lasting $>1$ hour that improves with movement indicates an **inflammatory** etiology (e.g., RA). Stiffness $<30$ minutes worsening with use suggests **mechanical** osteoarthritis.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Signs of Joint Inflammation**:
  * **Swelling**: Bulge sign or patellar tap test for knee effusion.
  * **Warmth & Erythema**: Check relative to adjacent skin. *Note: erythema is common in gout and septic arthritis, but rare in rheumatoid arthritis.*
  * **Range of Motion**: Marked limitation of both active and passive movement.
* **Extra-articular Clues**:
  * *Rheumatoid nodules*: Firm subcutis lumps over extensor surfaces.
  * *Tophi*: Chalky deposits of monosodium urate on ears, fingers, or Achilles tendon $\rightarrow$ **Chronic Tophaceous Gout**.
  * *Skin plaques / Nail pitting / Dactylitis (sausage digit)* $\rightarrow$ Psoriatic Arthritis.
  * *Conjunctivitis / Uveitis / Urethritis* $\rightarrow$ Reactive Arthritis (formerly Reiter's syndrome).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Pattern | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monoarthritis** (Acute) | • Gout / Pseudogout<br>• Trauma / Hemarthrosis<br>• Osteoarthritis flare | • **Septic Arthritis** (Bacterial)<br>• Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI) |
| **Polyarthritis** (Chronic) | • **Osteoarthritis** (mechanical)<br>• Viral arthritis (transient, self-limiting) | • **Rheumatoid Arthritis** (deforming/erosive)<br>• **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**<br>• **Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis** (immune complex arthritis) |

---

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate joint drainage and empiric IV antibiotics if present:**
> * Acute-onset monoarthritis with high fever, chills, and tachycardia.
> * Synovial fluid WBC $>50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with high PMN percentage.
> * Positive Gram stain or culture in synovial fluid.
> * Joint pain with rapidly progressive skin necrosis or crepitus surrounding the joint.
> * Arthritis in a patient with a new-onset cardiac murmur (suspect Infective Endocarditis).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Arthrocentesis & Synovial Fluid Analysis**: Mandatory in any acute monoarthritis or unexplained joint effusion before starting antibiotics. Order cell count with differential, Gram stain, culture, and polarized light microscopy (crystals).
  * **ESR & CRP**: Markers of systemic inflammation.
  * **CBC**: Check for leukocytosis (infection) or thrombocytopenia/leukopenia (SLE).
* **Targeted Serologies**:
  * **Rheumatoid Factor (RF) & Anti-CCP**: (Anti-CCP is $>95\%$ specific for Rheumatoid Arthritis).
  * **ANA**: Screen for SLE.
  * **Uric Acid**: Measure (note: can be falsely normal during acute gout flare).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Plain Joint X-Ray**: Look for periarticular osteopenia, symmetric joint space narrowing, and marginal erosions (RA) vs. asymmetric joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, and osteophytes (OA).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Septic Arthritis (Surgical Emergency)**:
   - Immediate joint decompression (repeated daily needle aspirations, arthroscopy, or open arthrotomy).
   - Start empiric IV antibiotics (Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone) immediately after arthrocentesis. Adjust therapy based on culture results. Total duration: $3-4$ weeks.
2. **Acute Gouty Arthritis**:
   - High-dose NSAIDs (e.g., Indomethacin), oral Colchicine (1.2 mg then 0.6 mg), or oral/intra-articular steroids.
   - Prophylaxis: **Allopurinol** (xanthine oxidase inhibitor). *Do not initiate or discontinue Allopurinol during an acute flare, as sudden serum urate changes prolong the attack.*
3. **Rheumatoid Arthritis**:
   - Initiate Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs (**DMARDs**) early (e.g., **Methotrexate** first line, plus folic acid).
   - Bridge with low-dose corticosteroids or NSAIDs for immediate pain relief until DMARDs take effect ($6-12$ weeks).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Gout Crystals vs. Pseudogout**:
>   - *Gout*: **Monosodium urate (MSU)** crystals. Classically **needle-shaped**, showing **strong negative birefringence** (yellow when parallel to compensator) under polarized light.
>   - *Pseudogout*: **Calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate (CPPD)** crystals. Classically **rhomboid-shaped**, showing **weak positive birefringence** (blue when parallel to compensator).
> * **Pseudogout Chondrocalcinosis**: CPPD deposition (pseudogout) classically presents with **chondrocalcinosis** (linear calcification of articular cartilage/meniscus) on plain joint X-rays, most commonly in the knees and wrists.
> * **Lupus Arthritis is Non-Erosive**: While both RA and SLE cause symmetric polyarthritis, RA arthritis is **erosive** (causes bone destruction and joint space narrowing on X-ray), whereas lupus arthritis is **non-deforming and non-erosive** (Jaccond's arthropathy is due to ligamentous laxity and is reversible).
