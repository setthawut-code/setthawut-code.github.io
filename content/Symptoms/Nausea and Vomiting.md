# 🤢 Nausea and Vomiting (เบื่ออาหาร คลื่นไส้ อาเจียน)

> "Nausea is the subjective sensation of the urge to vomit. Vomiting (emesis) is the oral expulsion of gastrointestinal contents due to coordinated gut and chest wall contractions."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Assess Hydration & Electrolytes**: Look for signs of hypovolemic [[Shock]] and metabolic abnormalities (classic **hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis**).
> 2. **GI vs. Extra-GI Source**:
>    - *GI*: Associated with abdominal pain, distension, or direct food ingestion.
>    - *Extra-GI*: Central nervous system (raised ICP), metabolic (uremia, DKA), vestibular (vertigo), or drugs.
> 3. **Rule Out Obstruction**: Succussion splash, bilious vomitus, and abdominal distension suggest mechanical obstruction.

---

## 🔍 Physiology of Vomiting

Vomiting is coordinated by the **Vomiting Center** in the medulla, which receives inputs from:
1. **Chemoreceptor Trigger Zone (CTZ)** (Area postrema - outside blood-brain barrier): Triggered by drugs (chemo, opiates), toxins, uremia, acidosis.
2. **Vestibular System** (via CN VIII): Triggered by motion (motion sickness, labyrinthitis). Rich in histamine H1 and muscarinic M1 receptors.
3. **Vagal/Splanchnic Afferents** (from GI tract): Triggered by gut distension, mucosal irritation, or localized inflammation. Rich in 5-HT3 receptors.
4. **Cortex / Limbic System**: Triggered by sight, smell, or emotional stress.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Timing & Vomitus Characteristics
* **Timing of Vomiting**:
  * *Early Morning* (before eating): Pregnancy (morning sickness), **Raised Intracranial Pressure** (often without prior nausea), Uremia, or alcohol withdrawal.
  * *During or immediately after meals*: Psychogenic (bulimia) or peptic ulcer disease/gastric outlet obstruction (GOO).
  * *Late postprandial* ($>1-4$ hours after meal): Gastroparesis or mechanical gastric outlet obstruction (undigested food particles).
* **Vomitus Character**:
  * *Bilious (yellow-green)*: Indicates patency of the pylorus; rules out gastric outlet obstruction but suggests obstruction distal to the ampulla of Vater.
  * *Fecaloid (feculent)*: Suggests low intestinal [[Bowel Obstruction]] or gastrocolic fistula.
  * *Coffee-ground / Bright red*: Indicates active upper GI bleeding ([[Hematemesis]]).
  * *Undigested food*: Gastric outlet obstruction, achalasia, or severe gastroparesis.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Hydration Status (Vital)**: Dry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor, sunken eyes, orthostatic hypotension, tachycardia, or oliguria.
* **Abdominal Examination**:
  * *Distension & visible peristalsis* $\rightarrow$ Bowel obstruction.
  * **Succussion Splash**: Auscultate the epigastrium while rocking the patient's hips side-to-side. A splash heard $>3$ hours after a meal indicates **Gastric Outlet Obstruction** or severe gastroparesis.
  * *Guarding, rigidity, rebound tenderness* $\rightarrow$ Peritonitis / acute surgical abdomen.
* **Neurological**: Check for nystagmus (vestibular), focal motor deficits, neck stiffness (meningitis), and papilledema (raised ICP).

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent evaluation or surgical consultation required if present:**
> * Severe, constant abdominal pain (surgical abdomen).
> * Projectile vomiting without nausea, accompanied by headache or focal neurology (raised ICP).
> * Feculent or bilious vomiting with progressive abdominal distension.
> * Hematemesis or coffee-ground emesis.
> * Signs of severe dehydration or hypovolemic [[Shock]].
> * High fever with neck stiffness or altered consciousness.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Electrolyte]] Panel**: Look for hypokalemia, hypochloridemia, and metabolic alkalosis (caused by loss of HCl and potassium from gastric secretions).
  * **Renal Function (BUN/Cr)**: Assess for prerenal AKI (contraction alkalosis) or primary uremia.
  * **Urine hCG**: Mandatory for females of reproductive age.
  * **Amylase & Lipase**: Rule out acute pancreatitis.
  * **Blood Glucose & Ketones**: Screen for DKA.
* **Imaging & Diagnostic Modalities**:
  * **Abdominal X-Ray (Supine and Erect)**: Look for multiple air-fluid levels and dilated bowel loops ([[Bowel Obstruction]]), or free air under the diaphragm (perforation).
  * **CT Abdomen**: High sensitivity for identifying mechanical obstruction, pancreatitis, or abscess.
  * **Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD)**: Diagnostic for PUD, gastric malignancy, and pyloric stricture.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Fluid and Electrolyte Resuscitation**:
   - Give **Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)** with added **Potassium Chloride (KCl)**. *Avoid balanced crystalloids like Ringer's Lactate initially because they contain lactate (which converts to bicarbonate) and will worsen the metabolic alkalosis.*
2. **Nasogastric (NG) Decompression**:
   - Indicated for suspected bowel obstruction, gastric outlet obstruction, or persistent vomiting with severe abdominal distension.
3. **Targeted Antiemetic Selection**:

| Drug Class | Examples | Primary Site of Action | Best Clinical Indication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **5-HT3 Antagonists** | Ondansetron | Visceral vagal afferents, CTZ | Chemotherapy, post-operative, gastroenteritis. *Minimal sedative effect.* |
| **Dopamine Antagonists**| Metoclopramide, Domperidone | CTZ, prokinetic in upper GI tract | Gastroparesis, GERD, uremic vomiting. *Avoid Metoclopramide in bowel obstruction.* |
| **Phenothiazines** | Promethazine, Prochlorperazine | CTZ (D2 receptors), H1 | General nausea, migraine. *Highly sedating.* |
| **Antihistamines** | Dimenhydrinate | Vestibular system (H1, M1) | Motion sickness, vestibular vertigo, labyrinthitis. |

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Contraction Alkalosis**: Loss of gastric acid ($HCl$) leads to bicarbonate excess. Dehydration causes aldosterone activation, which forces the kidney to reabsorb sodium at the expense of hydrogen ($H^+$) and potassium ($K^+$) excretion. This leads to **paradoxical aciduria** (acidic urine in the presence of systemic alkalosis). Correcting volume with Normal Saline is key to turning off this aldosterone axis.
> * **The Silent MI**: Diabetics and elderly patients presenting with isolated nausea, vomiting, and epigastric discomfort may actually be having an **inferior wall acute myocardial infarction** (vagal irritation of the inferior cardiac wall). Always check an ECG.
> * **Ondansetron QTc Prolongation**: Ondansetron can prolong the QT interval. Avoid or monitor closely in patients with baseline long QT, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, or those taking other QTc-prolonging drugs.
