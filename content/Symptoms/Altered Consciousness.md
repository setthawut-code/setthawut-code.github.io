# 🧠 Altered Consciousness (ซึม / ไม่รู้สติ)

> "Altered consciousness is a medical emergency. The diagnostic goal is to determine if the cause is structural/focal (stroke, mass) or diffuse/metabolic, while protecting the airway."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Secure the Airway**: **"GCS $\le 8$, intubate."** Protect the airway from aspiration.
> 2. **Rule Out Hypoglycemia**: Test fingerstick glucose immediately.
> 3. **The "Coma Cocktail" (if etiology is unknown)**:
>    - **IV Dextrose** (50% D/W 50 mL) if hypoglycemic.
>    - **IV Thiamine** 100 mg (give **before** dextrose in alcoholics to prevent Wernicke's).
>    - **IV Naloxone** $0.4-2$ mg if opioid overdose suspected.
> 4. **Locate the Lesion**: Differentiate Structural (asymmetric pupils, focal signs) from Metabolic (reactive pupils, symmetric motor responses).

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Consciousness requires two functional systems: **Arousal** (mediated by the Ascending Reticular Activating System [ARAS] in the brainstem) and **Cognition/Content** (mediated by both cerebral hemispheres).

### 📊 Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) Assessment

| Eye Opening (E) | Verbal Response (V) | Motor Response (M) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| • **4** - Spontaneous<br>• **3** - To sound/voice<br>• **2** - To pressure/pain<br>• **1** - None | • **5** - Oriented<br>• **4** - Confused<br>• **3** - Inappropriate words<br>• **2** - Incomprehensible sounds<br>• **1** - None | • **6** - Obeys commands<br>• **5** - Localizes pain<br>• **4** - Normal flexion (withdrawal)<br>• **3** - Abnormal flexion (**Decorticate**)<br>• **2** - Extension (**Decerebrate**)<br>• **1** - None |

* **Total Score**: $3-15$ ($15$ = fully awake; **$\le 8$** = coma, requiring airway protection).

## 🗣️ Key History Taking: The AEIOU-TIPS Mnemonic

When approaching a patient with altered consciousness, run through this classic checklist:

### 📊 AEIOU-TIPS Diagnostic Matrix

| Letter | Etiology | Key Diagnostic Clues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | **Alcohol** / Acidosis | Breath smell, metabolic acidosis on ABG. |
| **E** | **Endocrine** / Electrolyte / Encephalopathy | Myxedema, adrenal crisis, hyponatremia, hepatic/uremic. |
| **I** | **Insulin** (Hypoglycemia / DKA / HHS) | Fingerstick glucose, ketones, Kussmaul respiration. |
| **O** | **Opiates** / Oxygen deficit (Hypoxia) | Pinpoint pupils, bradypnea ($RR <8$), $SpO_2 <90\%$. |
| **U** | **Uremia** / Renal failure | Asterixis, uremic frost, elevated BUN/Creatinine. |
| **T** | **Trauma** / Tumor / Temperature | Head trauma signs, hypothermia or hyperthermia. |
| **I** | **Infection** ([[Sepsis]] / [[Meningitis]]) | Fever, neck stiffness, hypotension, source of infection. |
| **P** | **Poisoning** / Psychogenic | Medication bottles, toxidrome signs, normal pupillary exam. |
| **S** | **Stroke** / **Seizure** (Post-ictal) | Sudden onset, hemiparesis, Todd's paralysis, lateral tongue bite. |

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Document Temp (infection/heatstroke), BP (hypotension in shock; hypertension + bradycardia + irregular breathing $\rightarrow$ **Cushing's Triad** of raised ICP), and RR.
* **Neurological Examination (The Coma Exam)**:
  * **Pupillary Exam**:
    * *Mid-dilated, fixed, unilateral* $\rightarrow$ Uncal herniation (compressing CN III).
    * *Pinpoint, reactive* $\rightarrow$ Opioid overdose or pontine hemorrhage.
    * *Reactive, symmetric* $\rightarrow$ Suggests metabolic/toxic coma.
  * **Eye Movements**: Test oculocephalic reflex (**"Doll's Eyes"**) if cervical spine cleared. *Normal (intact brainstem)*: eyes move in direction opposite to head rotation. *Abnormal (brainstem lesion)*: eyes remain fixed in midline (like a doll).
  * **Motor Response**:
    * **Decorticate posturing** (flexion of arms, extension of legs) $\rightarrow$ Lesion above the red nucleus (cortical/subcortical).
    * **Decerebrate posturing** (extension and pronation of arms, extension of legs) $\rightarrow$ Lesion in the midbrain/brainstem. *Worse prognosis.*

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Metabolic / Diffuse (Symmetric) | Structural / Focal (Asymmetric) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vascular** | • Anoxic brain injury (post-cardiac arrest) | • **Acute Ischemic Stroke** (basilar artery occlusion)<br>• **Intracerebral / Subarachnoid Hemorrhage** |
| **Trauma / Mass** | None | • **Epidural / Subdural Hematoma**<br>• Brain tumor with herniation / Brain abscess |
| **Infectious** | • Severe [[Sepsis]]-associated encephalopathy | • **[[Meningitis]]** / Encephalitis |
| **Toxic / Metabolic**| • **Hypoglycemia** / DKA / HHS<br>• Hepatic / Uremic Encephalopathy<br>• Hyponatremia / Hypercalcemia<br>• Opioid / Sedative overdose | None |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency intervention required if present:**
> * GCS $\le 8$ (impending loss of airway reflexes).
> * Unilateral dilated, non-reactive pupil (impending uncal herniation).
> * Cushing's Triad (Bradycardia + Hypertension + Irregular respiration $\rightarrow$ imminent brain herniation).
> * Decerebrate posturing.
> * Neck stiffness with fever.
> * Sudden "thunderclap" headache prior to altered consciousness (SAH).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Fingerstick Blood Glucose**: Test immediately at bedside.
  * **[[ABG]] & Lactate**: Assess oxygenation, ventilation ($PaCO_2$ retention causing $CO_2$ narcosis), and acid-base status.
  * **Serum Electrolytes**: Sodium (hyponatremia), Calcium.
  * **Renal and Liver Function, Ammonia**: (Ammonia elevated in hepatic encephalopathy).
  * **Urine Toxicology Screen & Blood Alcohol Level**.
* **Neuroimaging**:
  * **Non-contrast Head CT**: Perform immediately if there is a history of trauma, sudden onset of coma, focal neurological signs, or unexplained coma.
* **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**:
  * Indicated for suspected meningitis or encephalitis *after ruling out midline shift or mass lesion on Head CT* to avoid tonsillar herniation.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Airway Management**:
   - Intubate if GCS $\le 8$, severe respiratory distress, or loss of gag/cough reflexes.
2. **Empirical Coma Cocktail**:
   - **Thiamine 100 mg IV** before Dextrose in malnourished/alcoholic patients (prevents acute Wernicke's encephalopathy).
   - **Dextrose (50% D/W 50 mL IV)** if blood glucose $<70$ mg/dL.
   - **Naloxone** $0.4-2$ mg IV/IM if opioid overdose (respiratory depression + pinpoint pupils) is suspected. Titrate to restore ventilation, not full consciousness (to avoid acute withdrawal).
   - *Avoid Flumazenil* (benzodiazepine antagonist) routinely: can precipitate intractable seizures in patients with chronic benzo use or tricyclic antidepressant co-ingestion.
3. **Management of Raised ICP**:
   - Elevate head of bed to 30 degrees.
   - Hyperventilation (target $PaCO_2$ $30-35$ mmHg) as a short-term bridging measure.
   - IV Osmotic therapy: **Mannitol** ($0.5-1.0$ g/kg IV bolus) or **3% Hypertonic Saline**.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Thiamine Rule**: Never administer IV glucose to an alcoholic or malnourished patient without giving Thiamine first. Glucose metabolism consumes the body's remaining thiamine stores, which can trigger irreversible **Wernicke's Encephalopathy** (triad of ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, and confusion).
> * **CO2 Narcosis**: Patients with chronic respiratory failure (severe COPD) can develop severe hypercapnia ($PaCO_2 > 80-90$ mmHg) leading to $CO_2$ narcosis, presenting as somnolence or coma. Correct with non-invasive ventilation (NIV) or intubation.
> * **Metabolic Pupillary Sparing**: In most metabolic/toxic causes of coma, pupillary light reflexes are preserved (reactive pupils). If you find asymmetric or fixed, non-reactive pupils, suspect a **structural brain lesion** (herniation, brainstem stroke).
