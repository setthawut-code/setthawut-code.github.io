# 🩸 Anemia (โลหิตจาง / ภาวะซีด)

> "Anemia is a laboratory sign, not a diagnosis. The diagnostic approach is structured around cell size (MCV) and bone marrow response (Reticulocyte count)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Assess Severity & Symptoms**: Is the patient hemodynamically stable? Are they experiencing active ischemia (chest pain, dyspnea at rest)?
> 2. **Transfusion Threshold**: Target **$Hb < 7$ g/dL** for general stable patients; **$Hb < 8$ g/dL** for patients with active cardiovascular disease/acute coronary syndrome.
> 3. **Identify Pancytopenia**: Look at WBC and platelet counts. If all three cell lines are depressed, rule out leukemia or aplastic anemia urgently.
> 4. **Review MCV & Reticulocyte Index**: Localize the category immediately.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Anemia is defined as a reduction in the total circulating red blood cell (RBC) mass, leading to decreased oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood.
* **WHO Diagnostic Thresholds**:
  * **Men**: $Hb < 13.0$ g/dL
  * **Women** (non-pregnant): $Hb < 12.0$ g/dL

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Anemia Symptoms**: Fatigue, weakness, generalized malaise, exertional dyspnea, palpitations, dizziness, headache.
* **Bleeding Source Identification**:
  * Gastrointestinal: Melena, hematochezia, hematemesis, history of NSAID use or peptic ulcers.
  * Gynecological: Menorrhagia (heavy menstrual periods).
* **Nutritional History**: Vegan diet (Vitamin B12 deficiency), poor vegetable intake (Folate deficiency), pica (craving ice/clay $\rightarrow$ specific for Iron Deficiency Anemia).
* **Neurological Symptoms**: Paresthesias, numbness, gait imbalance (vibration/proprioception loss in **Vitamin B12 deficiency / subacute combined degeneration**).
* **Family History**: Thalassemia, G6PD deficiency, or bleeding disorders.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Tachycardia, tachypnea, orthostatic hypotension. Look for fever (pancytopenia/infection).
* **Signs of Anemia**:
  * **Pallor**: Inspect palpebral conjunctiva, palmar creases, and nail beds.
  * **Hyperdynamic Circulation**: Tachycardia, wide pulse pressure, and a functional systolic ejection murmur (hemic murmur).
* **Etiology-Specific Signs**:
  * *Koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails)*, *angular cheilitis*, and *glossitis (smooth red tongue)* $\rightarrow$ Iron Deficiency Anemia.
  * *Scleral icterus / Jaundice* + *mild splenomegaly* $\rightarrow$ Hemolytic Anemia.
  * *Loss of vibration and position sensation* (posterior/lateral columns) $\rightarrow$ Vitamin B12 deficiency.
  * *Petechiae, purpura, or ecchymosis* $\rightarrow$ Thrombocytopenia (pancytopenia / marrow infiltration).

---

## 📂 Classification & Differential Diagnosis

Anemia is classified morphologically by Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV):

```text
Anemia
├── Microcytic (MCV < 80)
│   ├── Iron Deficiency
│   ├── Thalassemia trait / disease
│   └── Lead poisoning
├── Normocytic (MCV 80 - 100)
│   ├── Acute blood loss
│   ├── Anemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)
│   └── Hemolytic anemia
└── Macrocytic (MCV > 100)
    ├── Megaloblastic (B12 / Folate deficiency)
    └── Non-megaloblastic (Alcohol, Liver disease, Hypothyroidism)
```

---

### 📊 Reticulocyte Production Index (RPI)
The reticulocyte count reflects the bone marrow's production capacity. Because reticulocytes survive longer in circulation in severe anemia, the raw count must be corrected:

$$\text{Reticulocyte Index (RI)} = \text{Reticulocyte \%} \times \left(\frac{\text{Patient's Hct}}{\text{Normal Hct (45\%)}}\right)$$

$$\text{RPI} = \frac{\text{RI}}{\text{Maturation Factor (usually 2 in anemia)}}$$

* **RPI $> 2\%$ (Hyperproliferative)**: Effective bone marrow response $\rightarrow$ **Hemolysis** or **Acute Blood Loss**.
* **RPI $< 2\%$ (Hypoproliferative)**: Inadequate bone marrow response $\rightarrow$ **Nutritional deficiency** (Iron, B12, Folate), **EPO deficiency** (CKD), or **primary bone marrow disorders** (Aplastic anemia, MDS, leukemia).

### 🧩 Diagnostic Classification Matrix

| Morphological Class | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Microcytic** (MCV $<80$ fL) | • Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)<br>• [[Thalassemia]] trait / Disease | • **Gastrointestinal Malignancy** (cause of occult bleeding in elderly/men)<br>• Lead poisoning |
| **Normocytic** (MCV $80-100$ fL) | • Anemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)<br>• Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)<br>• Early Iron Deficiency | • **Acute severe hemorrhage**<br>• **Aplastic Anemia** / Bone marrow failure<br>• **Acute Leukemia** / Multiple Myeloma |
| **Macrocytic** (MCV $>100$ fL) | • Folate deficiency (alcoholism, malnutrition)<br>• Drug-induced (hydroxyurea, methotrexate)<br>• Liver disease / Hypothyroidism | • **Vitamin B12 deficiency** (risk of irreversible subacute combined degeneration)<br>• Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Initiate emergency stabilization/transfusion if present:**
> * Hemodynamic instability / [[Shock]] due to acute blood loss (tachycardia, hypotension, cool extremities).
> * Anemia accompanied by active ischemic chest pain ([[Acute Coronary Syndrome]]) or severe dyspnea at rest.
> * Pancytopenia (anemia + neutropenia + thrombocytopenia) $\rightarrow$ high risk of fatal infection and hemorrhage.
> * Presence of blast cells or schistocytes (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia like TTP/HUS - medical emergency) on peripheral blood smear.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Workup**:
  * **CBC**: Assess Hb, Hct, MCV, RDW (elevated in IDA, normal in uncomplicated Thalassemia trait), WBC, and Platelet count.
  * **Reticulocyte Count**: To calculate RPI.
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS) (Crucial Step)**:
    * *Microcytic/hypochromic cells, pencil cells* $\rightarrow$ IDA.
    * *Target cells, basophilic stippling* $\rightarrow$ Thalassemia.
    * *Hypersegmented neutrophils* ( $\ge 5$ lobes) $\rightarrow$ Megaloblastic anemia (B12/Folate).
    * *Schistocytes (fragmented RBCs)* $\rightarrow$ Hemolysis (TTP/HUS, DIC).
    * *Spherocytes* $\rightarrow$ Hereditary spherocytosis or Warm AIHA.
* **Secondary targeted tests**:
  * **Iron Studies**:
    * *Iron Deficiency*: Low Ferritin ($<30$ ng/mL is diagnostic), low Serum Iron, high TIBC, low Transferrin Saturation ($<20\%$).
    * *Anemia of Chronic Disease*: Normal/high Ferritin, low Serum Iron, low/normal TIBC.
  * **Vitamin B12 & Folate levels**.
  * **Hemoglobin Electrophoresis**: For diagnosing Thalassemia syndromes.
  * **Hemolysis Workup**: Serum LDH, Haptoglobin (decreased in intravascular hemolysis), unconjugated bilirubin, Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT / Coombs test).
  * **Renal function**: Creatinine/eGFR (assess CKD/EPO deficiency).

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Transfusion Thresholds**:
   - Transfuse Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) for **$Hb < 7$ g/dL** in stable asymptomatic patients (restrictive strategy).
   - Target **$Hb < 8$ g/dL** in patients with pre-existing CAD, active myocardial ischemia, or undergoing orthopedic/cardiac surgery.
   - Transfuse regardless of Hb level if the patient has symptomatic acute hemorrhage, hemodynamic instability, or severe cardiopulmonary symptoms.
2. **Iron Replacement**:
   - *Oral Iron*: Ferrous sulfate/fumarate/gluconate. Take on empty stomach with Vitamin C to enhance absorption. Side effects: dark stools, constipation, metallic taste.
   - *IV Iron* (e.g., Iron Sucrose, Ferric Carboxymaltose): Indicated for oral intolerance, malabsorption (e.g., Celiac, gastric bypass), severe ongoing blood loss, or late-stage CKD.
3. **Vitamin B12 & Folate Replacement**:
   - *B12 Deficiency*: Intramuscular Cyanocobalamin (e.g., 1000 mcg daily for 1 week, then weekly for 1 month, then monthly) if pernicious anemia or malabsorption.
   - *Folate Deficiency*: Folic acid $1-5$ mg PO daily.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Ferritin is an Acute Phase Reactant**: In the presence of systemic inflammation (infection, rheumatologic disease, malignancy), serum ferritin can be falsely elevated. If ferritin is $30-100$ ng/mL but chronic inflammation is present, iron deficiency may still exist. Check transferrin saturation ($<20\%$ supports IDA).
> * **The Thalassemia vs. IDA Differentiator**:
>   - Mentzer Index = $MCV / \text{RBC count}$.
>   - Mentzer Index **$< 13$** $\rightarrow$ Suggests **Thalassemia trait** (microcytosis with high RBC count).
>   - Mentzer Index **$> 13$** $\rightarrow$ Suggests **Iron Deficiency Anemia** (microcytosis with low RBC count).
> * **Avoid Folate Monotherapy in B12 Deficiency**: Never treat macrocytic anemia with folic acid alone without checking B12 levels. Folic acid will correct the hematologic markers of B12 deficiency but will **worsen or fail to prevent the irreversible progressive neurological damage**.
