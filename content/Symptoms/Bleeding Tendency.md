# 🩸 Bleeding Tendency (เลือดออกง่าย)

> "Bleeding tendency represents abnormal susceptibility to hemorrhage. The clinical task is to localize the defect to primary hemostasis (platelets) or secondary hemostasis (coagulation factors)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Classify Hemostasis Defect**:
>    - *Primary (Platelets)*: Mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gum bleed), petechiae, immediate bleeding after trauma.
>    - *Secondary (Coagulation)*: Deep tissue bleeding (hemarthrosis, hematomas), delayed bleeding.
> 2. **Check aPTT and PT/INR**:
>    - *Elevated aPTT only*: Think intrinsic pathway (Hemophilia A/B, heparin).
>    - *Elevated PT only*: Think extrinsic pathway (Early Warfarin, early liver disease, Vit K deficiency).
>    - *Elevated both*: Common pathway defect, advanced liver disease, late Warfarin, or DIC.

---

## 🔍 Physiology: Coagulation Cascade & Hemostasis

Hemostasis occurs in two phases:
1. **Primary Hemostasis**: Platelet plug formation. Platelets bind to subendothelial collagen via **von Willebrand Factor (vWF)** and aggregate.
2. **Secondary Hemostasis**: Activation of coagulation cascade to form a fibrin mesh that stabilizes the platelet plug.

### 📊 Coagulation Pathways and Monitoring Tests

```text
  [ Intrinsic Pathway ] (Factors XII, XI, IX, VIII)  ──> Monitored by: aPTT
                         \
                          ▼
                  [ Common Pathway ] (Factors X, V, II [Prothrombin], I [Fibrinogen])
                          ▲
                         /
  [ Extrinsic Pathway ] (Factor VII + Tissue Factor) ──> Monitored by: PT / INR
```

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Onset & Family History**:
  * *Onset since childhood + family history in males* $\rightarrow$ Congenital factor deficiency (**Hemophilia A or B** - X-linked recessive).
  * *Onset in adulthood* $\rightarrow$ Acquired coagulopathy (liver disease, drugs, Vitamin K deficiency).
  * *Easy bruising + mucosal bleeding in both males and females* $\rightarrow$ **von Willebrand Disease (vWD)** (most common inherited bleeding disorder, autosomal dominant).
* **Medication History**: Warfarin, DOACs, Heparin, Aspirin, Clopidogrel.
* **Comorbidities**: History of chronic alcohol abuse/cirrhosis, end-stage renal disease (uremic platelet dysfunction).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Skin**:
  * *Petechiae* $\rightarrow$ Specifically indicates a **platelet defect** (thrombocytopenia).
  * *Large ecchymoses / subcutaneous hematomas* $\rightarrow$ Coagulation factor deficiency.
* **Joints**: Assess for swollen, deformed joints (**Hemarthrosis** $\rightarrow$ hallmark of severe Hemophilia).
* **Other Signs**:
  * Bleeding from venipuncture/IV sites $\rightarrow$ **DIC** (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation).
  * Stigmata of chronic liver disease (spider nevi, ascites, splenomegaly).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Primary Hemostasis (Platelets / vWF) | Secondary Hemostasis (Coagulation Cascade) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Congenital** | • **von Willebrand Disease (vWD)** (mild bleeding)<br>• Glanzmann's thrombasthenia / Bernard-Soulier | • **Hemophilia A** (Factor VIII deficiency)<br>• **Hemophilia B** (Factor IX / Christmas disease) |
| **Acquired** | • **ITP** (immune thrombocytopenia)<br>• **Leukemia** (marrow infiltration)<br>• Uremic platelet dysfunction (in CKD)<br>• Aspirin / Clopidogrel use | • **Liver Disease / [[Cirrhosis]]**<br>• **Vitamin K Deficiency** (warfarin, malabsorption)<br>• **DIC** (sepsis, trauma, obstetric)<br>• Heparin / DOACs |
| **Dangerous** | • **TTP / HUS** (microangiopathic)<br>• Acute Leukemia | • **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**<br>• Hemophiliac compartment syndrome |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit immediately for emergency stabilization if present:**
> * Active bleeding with hemodynamic instability ([[Shock]]).
> * Bleeding from multiple non-contiguous sites (e.g., IV sites, gums, hematuria $\rightarrow$ suspect **DIC**).
> * Spontaneous joint bleeding (hemarthrosis) causing severe pain and swelling.
> * Bleeding tendency accompanied by high fever, purpura, and shock (urosepsis/meningococcemia).
> * Easy bleeding in a patient with a known history of advanced kidney or liver failure.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[CBC]] & Platelet Count**: Rule out thrombocytopenia.
  * **PT / INR**: Evaluates extrinsic and common pathways (Factors VII, X, V, II, I).
  * **aPTT**: Evaluates intrinsic and common pathways (Factors XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II, I).
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Differentiate true thrombocytopenia from clumping; check for schistocytes (DIC/TTP).
* **Second-line Diagnostic Testing**:
  * **Mixing Study** (if aPTT is prolonged): Mix patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma.
    * *aPTT corrects* $\rightarrow$ **Factor deficiency** (e.g., Hemophilia).
    * *aPTT does not correct* $\rightarrow$ **Factor inhibitor** (e.g., Lupus anticoagulant, acquired Factor VIII inhibitor).
  * **Factor VIII and IX assays**: To diagnose/classify Hemophilia.
  * **vWF Antigen & Ristocetin Cofactor Activity**: To diagnose von Willebrand Disease.
  * **Fibrinogen & D-dimer**: (Low fibrinogen and elevated D-dimer support DIC).

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**:
   - **Treat the underlying cause** (e.g., control infection in sepsis, debride tissue).
   - Supportive: Transfuse platelets (target $>50k$), FFP (if PT/aPTT prolonged), and Cryoprecipitate (if Fibrinogen $<150$ mg/dL) during active hemorrhage.
2. **Hemophilia A & B**:
   - For Hemophilia A: Give **recombinant Factor VIII concentrate** or **Desmopressin (DDAVP)** (for mild cases, releases stored Factor VIII/vWF).
   - For Hemophilia B: Give **recombinant Factor IX concentrate**.
   - *Avoid NSAIDs and intramuscular injections in hemophilia patients.*
3. **Acquired Coagulopathies**:
   - Liver disease/Warfarin: IV Vitamin K. FFP or PCC for active bleeding.
   - Uremic bleeding: IV/SC Desmopressin (DDAVP) $0.3$ mcg/kg or dialysis.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Hemophilia Joint Target**: The knee is the most common site of hemarthrosis in hemophilia, followed by the elbows and ankles. Recurrent bleeding leads to **hemophiliac arthropathy** (chronic joint destruction).
> * **The Mixing Study Rule**: A mixing study uses normal plasma (which contains $100\%$ of all clotting factors). If a patient lacks a factor, adding normal plasma corrects the clot time. If the patient has an antibody (inhibitor) blocking the factors, the normal plasma factors will also be blocked, so the clot time **does not correct**.
> * **vWD and aPTT**: Because von Willebrand Factor serves as a carrier protein that stabilizes **Factor VIII**, severe von Willebrand Disease can present with a **prolonged aPTT** (due to secondary Factor VIII deficiency), mimicking mild Hemophilia A.
