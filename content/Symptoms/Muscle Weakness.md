# 💪 Muscle Weakness (กล้ามเนื้ออ่อนแรง)

> "Muscle weakness is objective loss of motor power. The clinical approach is based on localizing the lesion along the motor pathway from the motor cortex to the muscle."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate UMN vs. LMN**:
>    - *UMN* (Brain/Cord): Hyperreflexia, increased tone, Babinski sign (+).
>    - *LMN* (Root/Nerve/NMJ/Muscle): Hyporeflexia, decreased tone, muscle atrophy.
> 2. **Rule Out Impending Respiratory Failure**: In acute progressive weakness (e.g., GBS, Myasthenic crisis), monitor vital capacity, negative inspiratory force (NIF), and bulbar signs (cough, swallowing).
> 3. **Identify Spinal Cord Compression**: Look for a distinct sensory level, hyperreflexia, and bowel/bladder dysfunction. **This is a neurosurgical emergency.**

---

## 🔍 Physiology: Upper vs. Lower Motor Neurons

### 📊 UMN vs. LMN Differentiation Table

| Feature | Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) | Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site of Lesion** | Brain (cortex, internal capsule) or Spinal Cord | Anterior horn cell, nerve root, plexus, peripheral nerve |
| **Muscle Bulk** | Normal (mild disuse atrophy late) | **Severe wasting / atrophy** |
| **Muscle Tone** | **Spasticity** (velocity-dependent increase) | **Flaccidity** (hypotonia) |
| **Deep Tendon Reflexes** | **Hyperreflexia** (clonus may be present) | **Hyporeflexia** or absent reflexes |
| **Plantar Response** | **Extensor (Babinski sign present: $\uparrow$)** | Flexor (Babinski sign absent: $\downarrow$) |
| **Fasciculations** | Absent | **Present** (muscle twitching) |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Distribution of Weakness**:
  * *Proximal symmetric* (difficulty rising from chair, brushing hair) $\rightarrow$ Myopathy (e.g., polymyositis) or NMJ disorder.
  * *Distal symmetric* (foot drop, tripping, weak grip) $\rightarrow$ Peripheral neuropathy.
  * *Unilateral (hemiplegia)* $\rightarrow$ Brain lesion (Stroke, tumor).
  * *Symmetric ascending* (starts in legs, moves to arms) $\rightarrow$ Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS).
* **Temporal Pattern**:
  * *Sudden onset* (minutes to hours) $\rightarrow$ Stroke or hypokalemic periodic paralysis.
  * *Fluctuating (fatigable, worse at end of day/with muscle use)* $\rightarrow$ Myasthenia Gravis (MG).
* **Associated Clues**:
  * Sphincter dysfunction (urinary retention/incontinence) $\rightarrow$ Spinal cord lesion.
  * Diplopia, ptosis, dysarthria, dysphagia $\rightarrow$ Bulbar/ocular weakness (MG, GBS, or brainstem stroke).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Motor Strength Grading (MRC Scale)**:
  * **Grade 0**: No muscle contraction.
  * **Grade 1**: Flicker or trace of contraction.
  * **Grade 2**: Active movement with gravity eliminated.
  * **Grade 3**: Active movement against gravity.
  * **Grade 4**: Active movement against gravity and resistance.
  * **Grade 5**: Normal strength.
* **Reflexes & Tone**: Check deep tendon reflexes (grade $0$ to $4+$, where $3+$ is brisk and $4+$ is clonus). Check for Babinski sign (scraping lateral sole $\rightarrow$ extension of great toe and fanning of others).
* **Sensory Exam**: Map for a **Sensory Level** (e.g., loss of sensation below the umbilicus, indicating a T10 spinal cord lesion). Check vibration/proprioception (posterior columns).
* **Cranial Nerves**: Assess for ptosis, extraocular movement limits, facial weakness, tongue deviation.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 Localization of LMN Lesions

| Localization | Pathologies | Key Diagnostic Clues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anterior Horn Cell** | ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis) | Mixed UMN and LMN signs in same limb, fasciculations, no sensory loss. |
| **Nerve Root** (Radiculopathy) | Herniated disc, shingles | Asymmetric dermatomal sensory loss + myotomal weakness + radiating pain. |
| **Peripheral Nerve** | **GBS** (acute), diabetic neuropathy (chronic) | Distal "glove-and-stocking" sensory loss, early loss of reflexes. |
| **Neuromuscular Junction** | **Myasthenia Gravis** | Fluctuating weakness, fatiguing ptosis/diplopia, spared reflexes, no sensory loss. |
| **Muscle** (Myopathy) | Polymyositis, drug-induced (statins) | Proximal symmetric weakness, muscle tenderness, elevated CK. |

| Lesion Type | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN)** | • Ischemic Stroke (chronic recovery phase)<br>• Multiple Sclerosis (MS)<br>• Cervical spondylotic myelopathy | • **Acute Stroke** (ischemic/hemorrhagic)<br>• **Spinal Cord Compression** (tumor, epidural abscess)<br>• Brain Abscess / Encephalitis |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit immediately and prepare for airway support if present:**
> * Rapidly ascending weakness (GBS suspect).
> * Bulbar signs: Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), dysarthria (slurred speech), weak cough.
> * Respiratory compromise: Tachypnea, shallow breathing, use of accessory muscles, or **NIF $< -30$ cmH2O** or **FVC $< 20$ mL/kg**.
> * Sudden hemiparesis or facial droop (acute stroke $\rightarrow$ activate stroke fast-track).
> * Acute urinary retention or bowel incontinence + bilateral leg weakness (spinal cord compression/cauda equina).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **Serum Electrolytes**: Check potassium (hypokalemia can cause severe flaccid paralysis / periodic paralysis).
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: Markedly elevated in inflammatory myopathies (polymyositis, rhabdomyolysis).
  * **[[ECG]]**: Look for signs of hypokalemia (U-waves, flat T-waves) or arrhythmia.
  * **Thyroid Panel (TSH / Free T4)**: Thyrotoxicosis can cause proximal myopathy or hypokalemic periodic paralysis.
* **Neuroimaging**:
  * **MRI of Brain or Spinal Cord**: Mandatory for suspected UMN lesions (stroke, demyelination, spinal cord compression).
* **Specialized Studies**:
  * **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**: CSF analysis in suspected GBS shows **albuminocytologic dissociation** (elevated CSF protein with normal/low WBC count).
  * **EMG / NCV (Electromyography/Nerve Conduction Velocity)**: Confirms peripheral neuropathy, radiculopathy, myopathy, or NMJ dysfunction.
  * **AChR Antibodies / Tensilon Test / Ice Pack Test**: Diagnostic for Myasthenia Gravis.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Spinal Cord Compression (Emergency)**:
   - Give **high-dose IV Dexamethasone** (e.g., 10 mg bolus, then 4 mg Q6H) immediately to reduce spinal cord edema. Consult Neurosurgery for urgent decompression.
2. **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)**:
   - Monitor respiratory parameters (FVC/NIF) Q4-Q6 hours.
   - **IVIG** ($0.4$ g/kg/day for 5 days) OR **Plasmapheresis**. *Do not give corticosteroids; they are ineffective in GBS.*
3. **Myasthenic Crisis**:
   - Intubate if respiratory parameters fail. Plasmapheresis or IVIG. Corticosteroids are used but can cause transient worsening initially.
4. **Hypokalemic Periodic Paralysis**:
   - Oral or IV potassium replacement. *Avoid dextrose-containing IV fluids, as insulin shifts potassium intracellularly, worsening the paralysis.*

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Single-Breath Count Test**: A simple bedside test for respiratory muscle strength. Ask the patient to take a deep breath and count out loud as far as they can in a single breath. A count **$<20$** indicates significant respiratory compromise (FVC $< 1.5-2.0$ L) and risk of impending respiratory failure.
> * **Atypical GBS Presentation**: In GBS, deep tendon reflexes are lost early, often before significant weakness develops. A patient with leg weakness but normal reflexes is unlikely to have GBS.
> * **Tensilon vs. Ice Pack Test**: The ice pack test is a safe, non-invasive bedside test for ocular myasthenia. Placing an ice pack over a ptotic eye for 2 minutes improves ptosis by slowing acetylcholinesterase activity in the neuromuscular junction.
