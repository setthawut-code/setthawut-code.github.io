# 🦠 Sepsis & Septic Shock (ภาวะติดเชื้อในกระแสเลือด)

> "Sepsis is a medical emergency. Time-to-antibiotics and aggressive fluid resuscitation are the key determinants of survival."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Surviving Sepsis 1-Hour Bundle (DO IMMEDIATELY):**
> 1. **Measure lactate level**. Re-measure if initial lactate is $>2$ mmol/L.
> 2. **Obtain blood cultures** (minimum 2 sets) before administering antibiotics.
> 3. **Administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics** (e.g., Piperacillin/Tazobactam or Meropenem).
> 4. **Fluid Resuscitation**: Begin rapid administration of **$30$ mL/kg crystalloid** (NSS or Acetar/LR) for SBP $<90$ mmHg, MAP $<65$ mmHg, or Lactate $\ge 4$ mmol/L.
> 5. **Vasopressors**: Start **Norepinephrine** if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation to maintain MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Sepsis is defined by the **Sepsis-3 (2016)** consensus guidelines:
* **Sepsis**: Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. Organ dysfunction is defined as an acute change in total **SOFA score $\ge 2$ points** consequent to the infection.
* **Septic Shock**: A subset of sepsis in which particularly profound circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities are associated with a greater risk of mortality than with sepsis alone.
  * **Clinical criteria**: Sepsis requiring **vasopressors** to maintain **MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg** AND **Serum Lactate $> 2$ mmol/L** despite adequate volume resuscitation.

## 🗣️ Source-Specific History & Physical Exam

The primary clinical task is to identify and control the source of infection:

* **Respiratory Source** ($\sim 50\%$ of cases):
  * *History*: Cough, dyspnea, sputum production.
  * *Physical*: Crackles, bronchial breath sounds, dullness on chest percussion $\rightarrow$ [[Community Acquired Pneumonia]].
* **Genitourinary / Urosepsis** ($\sim 20\%$ of cases):
  * *History*: Dysuria, frequency, urgency, flank pain.
  * *Physical*: Costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness $\rightarrow$ [[Acute Pyelonephritis]].
* **Abdominal Source** ($\sim 15\%$ of cases):
  * *History*: Pain, vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice.
  * *Physical*: Guarding, rebound tenderness $\rightarrow$ Peritonitis, [[Acute Cholangitis]], abscess.
* **Skin / Soft Tissue**:
  * *Physical*: Erythema, warmth, swelling, crepitus (necrotizing fasciitis), pressure ulcers.
* **Line/Device Related**:
  * *Physical*: Erythema or purulence at central line, dialysis catheter, or surgical wound site.

## 🚨 Red Flags for Severe Deterioration

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit to ICU and consult intensivist immediately if present:**
> * Refractory hypotension (MAP $<65$ mmHg despite $30$ mL/kg fluid resuscitation).
> * Lactate $> 4$ mmol/L (indicates profound tissue hypoperfusion).
> * Altered mental status (lethargy, obtundation) $\rightarrow$ signs of poor cerebral perfusion or encephalopathy.
> * Coagulopathy / bleeding from sites $\rightarrow$ Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
> * Severe metabolic acidosis with respiratory fatigue (impending respiratory failure).

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Serum Lactate**: Vital prognostic marker. Guide for resuscitation efficacy (aim to clear lactate by $\ge 10\%$ every 2 hours).
  * **CBC**: Leukocytosis or leukopenia. Thrombocytopenia is a sensitive marker of impending organ dysfunction.
  * **Renal & Liver Function Tests**: Elevated Creatinine (AKI) and Bilirubin (hepatic congestion/dysfunction).
  * **Coagulation Profile**: PT, INR, aPTT (check for DIC).
  * **[[Urinalysis]]**: Pyuria, bacteriuria.
* **Microbiology (Prior to Antibiotics)**:
  * **Blood Culture**: Draw at least 2 sets (one aerobic, one anaerobic) from separate sites.
  * **Urine Culture**.
  * **Sputum Gram stain & culture**.
* **Imaging**:
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Look for infiltrates or effusion.
  * **POCUS (Point-of-Care Ultrasound)**: Evaluate IVC diameter and collapsibility (volume responsiveness), rule out pneumothorax, and assess cardiac contractility.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Fluid Therapy**:
   - Give **$30$ mL/kg** IV crystalloid (balanced crystalloid like Acetar/Lactated Ringer's is preferred over Normal Saline to avoid hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis) within the first 3 hours.
   - Guide further fluids by dynamic measures of volume responsiveness (e.g., passive leg raise, IVC ultrasound) rather than static pressures.
2. **Empiric Antibiotic Selection**:
   - Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics within **60 minutes** of recognition.
   - *Gram-negative + Pseudomonas coverage*: Piperacillin/Tazobactam, Cefepime, or Meropenem.
   - *MRSA coverage*: Add Vancomycin or Linezolid if high-risk (prior MRSA, hemodialysis, indwelling devices).
3. **Source Control**:
   - Essential step! Drain abscesses, debride necrotic tissue, remove infected central lines or urinary catheters, and relieve biliary obstruction (ERCP).
4. **Vasoactive Agents**:
   - First-line: **Norepinephrine** (titrate to maintain MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg).
   - Second-line: Add Vasopressin (up to $0.03$ units/min) to reduce norepinephrine dose.
   - Consider low-dose IV Hydrocortisone ($200$ mg/day) for septic shock patients with refractory hypotension despite fluid and vasopressor therapy.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **qSOFA is a Prognostic Tool, Not Diagnostic**: qSOFA is designed to predict mortality, not to diagnose sepsis. Do not wait for a qSOFA score of $\ge 2$ to start treating suspected infection. If you suspect infection and there is any organ damage, treat as sepsis.
> * **Relative Bradycardia in Tropical Infections**: In febrile patients with shock, the lack of compensatory tachycardia (Faget's sign) should raise suspicion for atypical pathogens like enteric fever (Typhoid), Leptospirosis, or Legionella.
> * **Balanced Crystalloids vs. Normal Saline**: Resuscitating with large volumes of $0.9\%$ NaCl can cause hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, which can worsen renal perfusion. Use balanced solutions (Ringer's Lactate / Acetar) for massive resuscitation.
