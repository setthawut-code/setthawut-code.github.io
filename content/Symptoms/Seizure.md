# 🧠 Seizure (ชัก)

> "A seizure is a transient occurrence of signs/symptoms due to abnormal excessive or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain. The emergency priority is to stop status epilepticus."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Assess Duration**: Any seizure lasting **$>5$ minutes** (or $\ge 2$ seizures without recovery of consciousness) is **Status Epilepticus**. **Initiate the Emergency Protocol immediately.**
> 2. **Check Fingerstick Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
> 3. **Provoked vs. Unprovoked**:
>    - *Provoked*: Caused by immediate systemic insult (hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, alcohol withdrawal, uremia, drug toxicity). *Does not require long-term AEDs.*
>    - *Unprovoked*: Epilepsy (underlying structural/genetic predisposition).
> 4. **Airway Safety**: Roll the patient onto their side (recovery position). **Do not put anything in the patient's mouth.**

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Seizure**: A symptom of transient cerebral neuronal hyperactivity.
* **Epipelsy**: A disease defined by:
  1. At least two unprovoked seizures occurring $>24$ hours apart.
  2. One unprovoked seizure with high probability of recurrence ($>60\%$ over next 10 years).
* **Status Epilepticus (SE)**: Defined operationally at **$t_1 = 5$ minutes** for generalized convulsive SE (when treatment should start to prevent prolonged seizure) and **$t_2 = 30$ minutes** (when irreversible neuronal damage occurs).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **The Three Phases**:
  1. **Aura (Pre-ictal)**: Represents focal seizure onset. Clues: rising epigastric sensation (temporal lobe), olfactory hallucinations (uncinate fit), panic, or flashing lights (occipital).
  2. **Ictal Phase**: Note the character of movements (tonic-clonic, myoclonic, focal twitching), head/eye deviation (typically deviates *away* from the irritative lesion), urinary incontinence, and **lateral tongue biting**.
  3. **Post-ictal Phase**: Characterized by confusion, somnolence, headache, and transient focal weakness (**Todd's Paralysis** - resolves within 24 hours, mimics stroke).
* **Seizure Mimics**:
  * Differentiate from **Syncope** (see detailed table in [[Syncope]]) and **Psychogenic Non-Epileptic Seizures (PNES)** (characterized by closed eyes resisting opening, pelvic thrusting, side-to-side head shaking, lack of post-ictal state, and normal EEG).
* **Triggers**: Sleep deprivation, alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, medication non-compliance, flashing lights (photic stimulation).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Immediate Post-ictal State**: Document GCS, pupillary size and reaction, eye deviation.
* **Trauma Search**: Inspect the mouth for **lateral tongue biting** (high specificity for generalized tonic-clonic seizure). Inspect the head, neck, and shoulders for posterior shoulder dislocation (classic seizure complication).
* **Meningeal Signs & Fever**: Assess for neck stiffness, Kernig's/Brudzinski's signs $\rightarrow$ [[Meningitis]].
* **Neurological**: Look for focal motor/sensory deficits (Todd's paralysis vs. acute stroke/mass lesion).

---

## 🚨 Status Epilepticus Emergency Protocol

If seizure persists $>5$ minutes, proceed stepwise:

```text
 Status Epilepticus Protocol
 
   [ t = 0 - 5 min ]  ──> Airway (O2), IV access, check Glucose.
                                │
   [ t = 5 - 20 min ] ──> Phase 1: Benzodiazepines
                          • IV Lorazepam 4 mg OR IV Diazepam 10 mg (re-dose once if needed)
                          • No IV access: IM Midazolam 10 mg or Rectal Diazepam.
                                │
   [ t = 20 - 40 min ]──> Phase 2: IV Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs)
                          • IV Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (max 4500 mg) OR
                          • IV Valproate 40 mg/kg (max 3000 mg) OR
                          • IV Phenytoin 20 mg/kg (monitor for hypotension/arrhythmia)
                                │
   [ t > 40 min ]     ──> Phase 3: Anesthetic Agents (Refractory SE)
                          • Intubate & administer Propofol, Midazolam infusion, or Ketamine.
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Provoked (Systemic Insult) | Unprovoked / Epilepsy (Structural) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolic** | • **Hypoglycemia** / Hyponatremia<br>• Hypocalcemia / Uremia / Hepatic Encephalopathy | None |
| **Infectious** | • High fever (febrile seizure in kids) | • **[[Meningitis]]** / Encephalitis / Brain Abscess |
| **Structural** | None | • Post-stroke epilepsy / Traumatic brain injury<br>• Brain tumor (glioma, metastasis)<br>• Arteriovenous malformation (AVM) |
| **Toxic / Withdrawal** | • **Alcohol withdrawal** (fits occur $12-48$ hours after last drink)<br>• Benzodiazepine withdrawal<br>• Drug toxicity (tricyclics, tramadol, cocaine) | None |
| **Mimics** | • Vasovagal / Cardiac [[Syncope]] | • Psychogenic Non-Epileptic Seizures (PNES) |

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Fingerstick Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
  * **Electrolytes**: Specifically Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium (hypo-states lower seizure threshold).
  * **Serum Anticonvulsant Levels**: If patient is on chronic AEDs (check compliance/toxicity).
  * **Urine Drug Screen & Blood Alcohol Level**.
* **Diagnostic Modalities**:
  * **Electroencephalogram (EEG)**: The primary diagnostic tool. Look for epileptiform discharges (spikes, sharp waves). *Note: a normal interictal EEG does not rule out epilepsy (sensitivity of single EEG is $30-50\%$).*
  * **Neuroimaging (Head CT vs. MRI)**:
    * *Head CT (non-contrast)*: Indicated in emergency setting for first-ever seizure, trauma, or suspected mass/hemorrhage.
    * *Brain MRI*: Preferred for outpatient workup; superior for detecting mesial temporal sclerosis, low-grade tumors, and cortical dysplasia.
  * **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**: Indicated if febrile, meningismus present, or immunocompromised, *after ruling out mass lesion by head CT*.

## 🏥 Antiepileptic Drug (AED) Selection Guide

Choose based on seizure type, patient comorbidities, and side-effect profile:

| Drug | Seizure Indications | Major Side Effects | Clinical Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Levetiracetam** (Keppra) | Broad-spectrum (Focal & Generalized) | Irritability, depression, somnolence | **Preferred first-line**. No drug interactions; safe in liver disease; renal clearance. |
| **Valproate** (Depakine) | Broad-spectrum (Focal & Generalized) | Weight gain, tremor, hepatotoxicity, thrombocytopenia, **teratogenicity** | Excellent efficacy. **Contraindicated in women of childbearing age** (causes neural tube defects). |
| **Carbamazepine** | Narrow-spectrum (**Focal only**) | Hyponatremia (SIADH), leucopenia, skin rash (Stevens-Johnson) | Can **worsen** generalized absence/myoclonic seizures. Check HLA-B*1502 in Asians before starting. |
| **Phenytoin** (Dilantin) | Focal & Generalized tonic-clonic | Gingival hyperplasia, nystagmus, ataxia, osteoporosis | Zero-order kinetics (unpredictable blood levels). Monitor levels closely. |

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Alcohol Withdrawal Seizures**: Classically occur $12-48$ hours after the reduction or cessation of heavy alcohol intake. They are typically generalized tonic-clonic, often occur in a brief flurry (2-3 seizures), and should be treated with **Benzodiazepines (e.g., Diazepam, Chlordiazepoxide)**, **not** phenytoin.
> * **The HLA-B\*1502 Genetic Test**: In Asian populations (Thai, Chinese), screening for the HLA-B\*1502 allele is mandatory before starting **Carbamazepine**. The presence of this allele is strongly associated with life-threatening **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**.
> * **Prolactin Rise**: Serum prolactin levels rise significantly within $10-20$ minutes of a true generalized tonic-clonic seizure. This can help differentiate true convulsive seizures from PNES or syncope if the diagnosis is ambiguous.
