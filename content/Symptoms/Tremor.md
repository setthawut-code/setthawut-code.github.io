# 🧠 Tremor (สั่น)

> "Tremor is a rhythmic, involuntary, oscillatory movement of a body part. The clinical approach is based on classifying the tremor as resting, postural, or kinetic."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Rest vs. Action Tremor**:
>    - *Rest Tremor*: Present when muscles are fully supported/at rest. Classically unilateral at onset. Think **Parkinson's Disease**.
>    - *Action (Postural/Kinetic) Tremor*: Occurs during voluntary muscle contraction (holding hands out or moving to a target). Think **Essential Tremor** or **Enhanced Physiological Tremor**.
> 2. **Rule Out Wilson's Disease**: Any new-onset tremor in a patient **$<40$ years** requires ruling out Wilson's disease (ceruloplasmin check).
> 3. **Review Medications**: Check for drug-induced tremors (Beta-agonists, Valproate, Lithium, Amiodarone, Neuroleptics).

---

## 🔍 Classification & Physiology

Tremor is generated by alternating or synchronous contractions of antagonistic muscles, regulated by loops between the basal ganglia, cerebellum, and motor cortex.

* **Resting Tremor**: Occurs when the limb is relaxed, fully supported against gravity. Suppressed by voluntary movement. Frequency: $4-6$ Hz.
* **Action Tremor**:
  * **Postural**: Occurs when holding a position against gravity (e.g., extending arms forward).
  * **Kinetic**: Occurs during any voluntary movement (e.g., writing).
  * **Intention (Cerebellar)**: Worsens as the limb approaches a target (dysmetria). Frequency: $<4$ Hz.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Parkinson's Disease vs. Essential Tremor

| Feature | Parkinson's Disease (Resting) | Essential Tremor (Action) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary State** | **Rest** | **Action / Posture** |
| **Symmetry at Onset** | **Asymmetric / Unilateral** (spreads later) | **Symmetric / Bilateral** |
| **Hand Movement** | "Pill-rolling" (pronation-supination) | Flexion-extension |
| **Other Body Parts** | Jaw, tongue, leg | **Head** ("yes-yes" or "no-no" nod), voice |
| **Effect of Alcohol** | No effect | **Significantly improves / temporarily resolves** |
| **Associated Signs** | **Bradykinesia, Rigidity, shuffling gait** | None (isolated tremor) |
| **Family History** | Uncommon | Very common (Autosomal Dominant) |

---

### ⏳ Toxic / Drug Exposure History
* **Enhanced Physiological Tremor**: High frequency ($8-12$ Hz), fine postural tremor. Triggered by anxiety, caffeine, smoking, fever, hyperthyroidism, hypoglycemia.
* **Offending Medications**:
  * *Bronchodilators*: Salbutamol (beta-2 agonist).
  * *Psychiatric*: **Lithium**, Valproic Acid, Tricyclic antidepressants.
  * *Anti-arrhythmics*: Amiodarone.
  * *Dopamine Blockers* (Drug-Induced Parkinsonism): Haloperidol, Metoclopramide.

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Bedside Examination Protocol**:
  1. **Rest**: Observe the patient's hands resting in their lap for 1 minute while distracted (e.g., ask them to count backward). Look for "pill-rolling" tremor.
  2. **Posture**: Ask the patient to stretch their arms straight out in front of them with fingers spread. Look for fine postural tremor.
  3. **Kinetic / Intention**: Perform the **Finger-to-Nose Test**. Watch if the tremor amplitude increases significantly as the finger nears the examiner's hand (intention tremor $\rightarrow$ cerebellar lesion).
  4. **Writing / Drawing**: Have the patient draw an Archimedes spiral. Essential tremor causes jagged lines; Parkinson's causes small, tight lines (micrographia).
* **Search for Parkinsonism**:
  * *Rigidity*: Assess muscle tone (cogwheel rigidity in arms).
  * *Bradykinesia*: Check for finger tapping, hand pronation/supination (slow speed, decreasing amplitude). Masked facies (decreased blinking).
  * *Gait*: Shuffling gait, loss of arm swing, en-bloc turning, postural instability (pull test).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resting** | • **Parkinson's Disease**<br>• Drug-induced Parkinsonism (Metoclopramide, Haloperidol) | • Lewy Body Dementia / Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP) |
| **Postural / Kinetic**| • **Essential Tremor**<br>• **Enhanced Physiological Tremor** (caffeine, anxiety, alcohol withdrawal) | • **Wilson's Disease** (hepatic/neurological decline in young adults)<br>• **Severe Thyrotoxicosis** |
| **Intention** | None | • **Cerebellar Stroke / Tumor**<br>• Multiple Sclerosis (demyelinating plaque) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent diagnostic workup or neuroimaging required if present:**
> * Sudden onset of tremor or rapid progression (suggests vascular/demyelinating etiology).
> * Tremor in a patient **$<40$ years** (suspect Wilson's disease).
> * Intention tremor accompanied by other cerebellar signs: Ataxia (gait instability), dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia, or nystagmus.
> * Tremor accompanied by new-onset cognitive decline, hallucinations, or rapid motor rigidity.
> * Tremor with signs of severe hyperthyroidism (thyroid storm risk) or severe alcohol withdrawal (delirium tremens).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **TSH & Free T4**: Rule out hyperthyroidism.
  * **Electrolytes & Blood Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
  * **Serum Drug Levels**: Check lithium or valproate levels if patient is taking these medications.
* **Workup for Wilson's Disease** (Mandatory in patients $<40$ years with new tremor):
  * **Serum Ceruloplasmin** (characteristically low).
  * **24-hour Urinary Copper Excretion** (characteristically high).
  * **Slit-lamp Eye Exam**: Look for **Kayser-Fleischer (KF) rings** (copper deposition in Descemet's membrane of cornea).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Brain MRI**: Indicated for suspected cerebellar lesions, stroke, multiple sclerosis, or Wilson's disease (MRI shows "giant panda face" sign in midbrain).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Essential Tremor**:
   - First-line pharmacotherapy: **Propranolol** ($40-160$ mg/day - beta-blocker) OR **Primidone** ($50-250$ mg/day - anticonvulsant). *Avoid Propranolol in asthma.*
2. **Parkinson's Disease**:
   - First-line: **Levodopa/Carbidopa** (Dopamine replacement) or Dopamine Agonists (Pramipexole, Ropinirole) depending on patient age and motor complications.
3. **Drug-Induced / Physiological**:
   - Withdraw offending drugs.
   - Limit caffeine, manage stress, treat underlying hyperthyroidism.
4. **Wilson's Disease**:
   - Copper chelators (Penicillamine, Trientine) and zinc acetate (blocks intestinal copper absorption).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Alcohol Differentiator**: Essential tremor is highly sensitive to alcohol. A single alcoholic drink temporarily reduces or completely resolves the tremor for $1-4$ hours. Parkinsonian tremors do not respond to alcohol.
> * **Metoclopramide Warning**: Metoclopramide (Plasil) is a dopamine receptor antagonist widely used for nausea. In elderly patients, prolonged use can cause severe **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism** (resting tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia) that mimics Parkinson's disease. *Always review the drug list.*
> * **Archimedes Spiral Clue**: Drawing an Archimedes spiral: Essential tremor causes oscillations along a single axis (directional); Parkinson's causes a small, tight, micrographic spiral.
