# 👂 Hearing Loss (การได้ยินผิดปกติ)

> "Hearing loss is classified into conductive (sound transmission failure in outer/middle ear) and sensorineural (cochlear or vestibulocochlear nerve pathology)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Conductive vs. Sensorineural (SNHL)**: Use the tuning fork tests (Rinne and Weber) at the bedside.
> 2. **Rule Out Sudden Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SSNHL)**: A unilateral sensorineural loss developing over $<72$ hours is a medical emergency. **Treat with high-dose oral corticosteroids immediately** to prevent permanent deafness.
> 3. **Inspect the Ear Canal**: Always perform otoscopy first to rule out cerumen impaction or foreign body.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Conductive Hearing Loss (CHL)**:
  * Pathology in the external auditory canal, tympanic membrane, or middle ear ossicles. Sound waves cannot physically reach the cochlea.
* **Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL)**:
  * Pathology in the inner ear (cochlea/hair cells), auditory nerve (CN VIII), or central auditory pathways.

---

## 🗣️ Tuning Fork Bedside Evaluation

Perform using a **$512$ Hz tuning fork** (provides the best balance between tone decay and vibratory sensation):

### 📊 Rinne & Weber Interpretation Table

| Test | Procedure | Conductive Hearing Loss (CHL) | Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL) | Normal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rinne Test** | Place fork on mastoid process, then next to ear canal. | **Negative** (Bone conduction $>$ Air conduction: $BC > AC$) | **Positive** (Air conduction $>$ Bone conduction: $AC > BC$) | **Positive** ($AC > BC$) |
| **Weber Test** | Place fork on midline of forehead. | **Lateralizes to AFFECTED (bad) ear** | **Lateralizes to UNAFFECTED (good) ear** | Midline (no lateralization) |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Onset & Progression**:
  * *Sudden unilateral loss* $\rightarrow$ Idiopathic Sudden SNHL, trauma, vascular occlusion.
  * *Gradual bilateral loss* $\rightarrow$ Presbycusis (age-related), chronic noise exposure.
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * Vertigo + Tinnitus + Aural fullness $\rightarrow$ Meniere's disease.
  * Otorrhea (ear discharge) + otalgia (pain) $\rightarrow$ Otitis media or otitis externa.
  * Progressive unilateral SNHL + imbalance $\rightarrow$ Vestibular schwannoma (Acoustic neuroma).
* **Ototoxic Drug Exposure History**:
  * **Aminoglycosides** (Gentamicin, Amikacin): Destroys cochlear hair cells.
  * **Loop Diuretics** (high-dose Furosemide): Alters endolymph electrolyte balance.
  * **Chemotherapeutic agents** (Cisplatin).
  * **Aspirin / Salicylates** (high-dose): Causes reversible tinnitus and mild SNHL.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Otoscopy**:
  * Check external canal: Cerumen (earwax) impaction, foreign body, erythema/edema (otitis externa).
  * Check tympanic membrane (TM): Perforation, fluid/erythema/bulging (acute otitis media), retraction pockets, or whitish mass (cholesteatoma).
* **Neurological Examination**:
  * Examine CN V (corneal reflex) and CN VII (facial expression) function. *Loss of CN V/VII function with CN VIII SNHL suggests a large cerebellopontine angle tumor (acoustic neuroma).*
  * Assess gait and cerebellar signs.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Conductive Hearing Loss (CHL) | Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **External Ear** | • Cerumen impaction (most common)<br>• Otitis externa / Foreign body | None |
| **Middle Ear** | • Tympanic membrane perforation<br>• Acute Otitis Media / Otitis media with effusion<br>• Otosclerosis (ossicle remodeling)<br>• Cholesteatoma | None |
| **Inner Ear / Nerve** | None | • **Presbycusis** (age-related bilateral loss)<br>• **Noise-induced** (damage to hair cells)<br>• **Meniere's Disease** (endolymphatic hydrops)<br>• **Ototoxicity** (aminoglycosides, loop diuretics) |
| **Dangerous / Can't Miss** | • Malignant otitis externa (osteomyelitis of skull base in diabetics) | • **Sudden Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SSNHL)**<br>• **Vestibular Schwannoma (Acoustic Neuroma)**<br>• Temporal bone fracture |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent specialist referral and diagnostic workup required if present:**
> * Unilateral sudden-onset hearing loss ($<72$ hours) without conductive cause.
> * Hearing loss accompanied by focal neurological deficits (cranial neuropathies, ataxia, vertigo).
> * Immunocompromised/diabetic patient with severe ear pain and purulent discharge (malignant otitis externa).
> * Post-traumatic hearing loss, especially if associated with hemotympanum, rhinorrhea/otorrhea (CSF leak), or Battle's sign (basilar skull fracture).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Audiologic Testing**:
  * **Pure Tone Audiometry (PTA)**: Quantifies hearing thresholds for air and bone conduction across frequencies. Differentiates CHL (air-bone gap $>15$ dB) from SNHL.
  * **Tympanometry**: Evaluates middle ear pressure and compliance of tympanic membrane (Type A = normal, Type B = flat due to middle ear effusion, Type C = negative pressure due to Eustachian tube dysfunction).
* **Imaging**:
  * **MRI Brain and IAC (Internal Auditory Canal) with Gadolinium**: Gold standard for diagnosing **Vestibular Schwannoma (Acoustic Neuroma)**. Indicated in any unexplained asymmetric or unilateral SNHL.
  * **CT Temporal Bone**: Indicated for suspected ossicular pathology, cholesteatoma, or trauma.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Idiopathic Sudden SNHL (Medical Emergency)**:
   - Start **high-dose oral Prednisolone** ($1$ mg/kg/day, max $60$ mg/day) for $7-14$ days, followed by a taper. Should be initiated within $2$ weeks of onset.
   - Consider intratympanic steroid injections if oral steroids are contraindicated or fail.
2. **Conductive Causes**:
   - Debris/cerumen removal (lavage or micro-suction - confirm intact TM first).
   - Oral/topical antibiotics for otitis media/externa.
   - Surgical repair (tympanoplasty, myringotomy tube, or ossicular reconstruction) for chronic structural disorders.
3. **Chronic SNHL**:
   - Amplification (hearing aids).
   - Cochlear implantation for severe-to-profound SNHL refractory to hearing aids.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Sudden SNHL is a stroke of the ear**: Do not write off sudden unilateral hearing loss as "Eustachian tube dysfunction" or "fluid in the ear" without performing otoscopy and tuning fork tests. Fluid in the ear causes a *conductive* loss. A normal otoscopy with unilateral SNHL is **Sudden SNHL** until proven otherwise.
> * **Presbycusis Pattern**: Classic age-related hearing loss is **bilateral, symmetric SNHL**, affecting **high frequencies** first. Patients typically complain of difficulty understanding speech in noisy environments (e.g., restaurants) before realizing they have hearing loss.
> * **Acoustic Neuroma Rule**: Any asymmetric or unilateral sensorineural hearing loss is a vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma) until proven otherwise by contrast-enhanced MRI.
