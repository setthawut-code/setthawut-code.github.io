# 💩 Constipation (ท้องผูก)

> "Constipation is a common symptom defined by stool frequency, consistency, and difficulty in defecation. The clinical task is to identify secondary causes (drugs, endocrine, malignancy)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Apply Rome IV Criteria**: Define chronic constipation by $<3$ bowel movements/week, hard/lumpy stools, straining, or a feeling of incomplete evacuation.
> 2. **Rule Out Obstruction / Malignancy**: Any new-onset progressive constipation in an adult **$>50$ years** requires a **Colonoscopy** to rule out Colorectal Cancer.
> 3. **Review Medication List**: Identify offending agents (Opioids, Calcium Channel Blockers, Anticholinergics, Iron supplements).
> 4. **Perform Digital Rectal Exam**: Look for fecal impaction, rectocele, or pelvic floor dyssynergia (failure of sphincter relaxation during straining).

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Chronic functional constipation is defined by the **Rome IV criteria** (symptoms present for $\ge 3$ months in the last 6 months):
* Must include $\ge 2$ of:
  * Straining during $>25\%$ of defecations.
  * Lumpy or hard stools (Bristol Stool Form 1-2) in $>25\%$.
  * Sensation of incomplete evacuation in $>25\%$.
  * Sensation of anorectal obstruction/blockage in $>25\%$.
  * Manual maneuvers to facilitate defecation in $>25\%$.
  * Fewer than 3 spontaneous bowel movements per week.

### Physiological Mechanisms:
1. **Normal-Transit Constipation (NTC)**: Most common. Stool passes at normal rate but patient perceives constipation (linked to IBS-C).
2. **Slow-Transit Constipation (STC)**: Impaired colonic motor activity (colonic inertia).
3. **Defecatory Disorders (Dyssynergia)**: Uncoordinated pelvic floor and anal sphincter relaxation during straining.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Medication History (High-Yield)**:
  * **Opioids** (e.g., Morphine, Tramadol): Direct binding to $\mu$-receptors in the gut increases transit time and reduces secretions.
  * **Calcium Channel Blockers** (especially Verapamil): Relaxes colonic smooth muscle.
  * **Anticholinergics** (Tricyclic antidepressants, antihistamines, ipratropium): Inhibits parasympathetic gut motility.
  * **Oral Iron Supplements**: Classically causes dark, hard stools and constipation.
* **Systemic Clues**:
  * Cold intolerance, dry skin, fatigue $\rightarrow$ [[Hypothyroidism]].
  * Polyuria, polydipsia, muscle weakness $\rightarrow$ Hypercalcemia (antagonizes gut smooth muscle).
* **Dietary & Activity Habits**: Low fiber intake, low fluid intake, sedentary lifestyle.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Abdomen**: Inspect for distension. Palpate for LLQ fullness (fecal impaction in the sigmoid colon) or masses.
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) (Mandatory)**:
  * *Perianal sensation & Anal wink*: Tests CN S2-S4 integrity.
  * *Sphincter Tone*: Assess resting tone and sphincter squeeze.
  * **Push Effort (Dyssynergia test)**: Ask the patient to "push" as if defecating. The examiner should feel the puborectalis muscle relax and the external sphincter open. *Failure to relax indicates pelvic floor dyssynergia.*
  * *Fecal Impaction*: Palpable hard stool in the rectal vault.
  * *Occult blood checking*.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Primary / Functional | Secondary / Organic (Can't Miss) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | • Normal-transit constipation<br>• Slow-transit constipation (colonic inertia)<br>• Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS-C) | • **Colorectal Cancer (CRC)** (obstructive)<br>• **Fecal Impaction** with stercoral ulceration/perforation<br>• Anal fissure / Strictures (pain limits defecation)<br>• Hirschsprung's disease |
| **Neurological** | None | • Parkinson's Disease (autonomic dysfunction)<br>• Spinal Cord Injury / Multiple Sclerosis |
| **Endocrine / Metabolic**| • Inadequate fiber/fluid intake | • **[[Hypothyroidism]]**<br>• **Hypercalcemia** (hyperparathyroidism, malignancy)<br>• Uremia (CKD) |
| **Pharmacological** | • Opioids, CCBs (Verapamil), TCAs, Iron | None |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent surgical/colonoscopy referral required if present:**
> * Age **$>50$ years** with new-onset, progressive constipation.
> * Rectal bleeding (hematochezia) or positive fecal occult blood.
> * Involuntary weight loss ($>5\%$ in 6 months) or fever.
> * Pencil-thin stools or progressive narrowing of stool caliber.
> * Severe abdominal pain, vomiting, or inability to pass gas (impending [[Bowel Obstruction]]).
> * Family history of Colorectal Cancer in first-degree relatives.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Serum Calcium**: Screen for hypercalcemia.
  * **TSH**: Screen for hypothyroidism.
  * **Renal function (Creatinine/BUN)**.
  * **[[CBC]]**: Check for microcytic anemia (occult colon bleeding).
* **Diagnostic Studies (Selected based on clinical picture)**:
  * **Colonoscopy**: Mandatory if red flags are present or age $>50$ with new-onset constipation.
  * **Abdominal X-Ray**: Assess stool burden (fecal load) and rule out bowel obstruction.
  * **Colonic Transit Study (Sitzmarks)**: Patient swallows radiopaque markers; abdominal X-ray at Day 5. If $>20\%$ markers remain, indicates slow-transit constipation.
  * **Anorectal Manometry & Balloon Expulsion Test**: Diagnostic standard for Pelvic Floor Dyssynergia.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Dietary & Lifestyle Modifications**:
   - Gradually increase dietary fiber ($25-30$ g/day) and fluid intake ($1.5-2$ L/day).
   - Increase physical activity. Establish a regular bowel routine (toilet immediately after breakfast/warm drinks to utilize the gastrocolic reflex).
2. **Laxative Selection Table**:

| Class | Mechanism | Examples | Clinical Considerations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bulk-Forming** | Absorbs water, increases stool bulk, stimulates peristalsis. | Psyllium (Metamucil), Methylcellulose | First-line for mild functional constipation. *Ensure adequate fluid intake to avoid impaction.* |
| **Osmotic** | Retains water in lumen by osmotic gradient. | **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)**, Lactulose, Magnesium Hydroxide | **First-line for chronic constipation**. Safe for long-term use. Magnesium is contraindicated in renal failure. |
| **Stimulant** | Direct irritation of enteric nerves, increasing motility. | Senna, Bisacodyl | Best for acute relief or opioid-induced constipation. *Avoid long-term daily use (risk of tolerance/dependency).* |
| **Stool Softeners** | Emulsifies water and fat into stool, softening it. | Docusate Sodium | Good for preventing straining (e.g., post-MI, post-hernia surgery). *Minimal efficacy for severe constipation.* |

3. **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC)**:
   - Always start a stimulant laxative + stool softener alongside opioids. If refractory, use peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonists (PAMORAs, e.g., Methylnaltrexone, Naloxegol), which do not cross the blood-brain barrier and preserve central analgesia.
4. **Fecal Impaction**:
   - Manual disimpaction followed by mineral oil enema or high-volume PEG lavage. Do **not** give oral laxatives (especially stimulants) before the distal impaction is cleared (risks perforation).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Verapamil Effect**: Verapamil is notorious for causing severe constipation, especially in elderly patients. It blocks calcium channels in the enteric smooth muscle, halting peristalsis. Switching to another class of calcium blocker or antihypertensive is curative.
> * **Paradoxical Diarrhea (Encopresis)**: In elderly or bedridden patients, severe fecal impaction can present as "diarrhea". Liquid stool from proximal colon bypasses the hard impacted mass and leaks out of the anus. Always perform a DRE before treating chronic diarrhea in a bedbound patient.
> * **Straining & Cardiovascular Strain**: Defecation straining triggers a Valsalva maneuver, causing a transient spike in blood pressure followed by a drop in cardiac output. This can trigger myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, or syncope, which is why stool softeners are standard post-MI.
