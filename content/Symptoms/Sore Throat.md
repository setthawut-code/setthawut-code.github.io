# 🗣️ Sore Throat (เจ็บคอ)

> "The clinical challenge of sore throat is to differentiate self-limiting viral pharyngitis from bacterial infections requiring antibiotics, and to recognize acute airway emergencies."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Assess Airway Patency**: Look for **stridor**, **drooling**, **inability to swallow**, or a **muffled "hot potato" voice**. If present, assume an upper airway obstruction (Epiglottitis, Peritonsillar/Retropharyngeal abscess) and prepare for airway protection.
> 2. **Calculate Centor Score**: Determines the probability of Group A Beta-Hemolytic Streptococcus (GABHS) pharyngitis and whether empiric antibiotics are indicated.
> 3. **Rule Out Infectious Mononucleosis**: Sore throat + prolonged fever + posterior cervical lymphadenopathy + splenomegaly. Avoid ampicillin/amoxicillin (causes a classic maculopapular rash).

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Sore throat is a subjective sensation of pain, scratchiness, or irritation in the pharynx, typically worsened by swallowing (odynophagia). It is usually caused by inflammation of the pharyngeal mucosa (pharyngitis) or palatine tonsils (tonsillitis).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Symptom Profile**:
  * *Gradual onset, runny nose, cough, hoarseness* $\rightarrow$ Viral pharyngitis.
  * *Abrupt onset, high fever, severe pain, no cough* $\rightarrow$ Streptococcal pharyngitis.
  * *Severe localized throat pain, pain with opening mouth (trismus)* $\rightarrow$ Peritonsillar abscess.
  * *Severe odynophagia but normal-appearing pharynx* $\rightarrow$ Acute Epiglottitis.
* **Systemic Features**:
  * Severe fatigue, prolonged fever, neck swelling $\rightarrow$ Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV).
* **Immunocompromised status**: Risk for Candida esophagitis/pharyngitis, CMV, or HSV.

---

## 🩺 Clinical Scoring: Modified Centor Criteria

Used to evaluate patients with acute pharyngitis for GABHS:

### 📊 Modified Centor Score Table

| Criteria | Points |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Absence of Cough** | $+1$ |
| **Tender Anterior Cervical Lymphadenopathy** | $+1$ |
| **Fever (Temperature $>38^\circ\text{C}$ / $100.4^\circ\text{F}$)** | $+1$ |
| **Tonsillar Exudates or Swelling** | $+1$ |
| **Age Criteria**: <br>• $3-14$ years<br>• $15-44$ years<br>• $\ge 45$ years | <br>$+1$<br>$0$<br>$-1$ |

* **Score $0-1$**: Risk $<10\%$. No diagnostic testing or antibiotics needed.
* **Score $2-3$**: Risk $10-35\%$. Perform Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT) or throat culture; treat only if positive.
* **Score $\ge 4$**: Risk $>50\%$. Consider empiric antibiotic therapy (or RADT/culture).

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Document BP, HR, RR, Temp, and $SpO_2$. Check for tachypnea or stridor.
* **Oropharynx Examination**:
  * *Pharyngeal/Tonsillar erythema and tonsillar hypertrophy*.
  * *Exudates*: Whitish-grey patches (streptococcal tonsillitis, EBV, diphtheria).
  * *Uvular Deviation*: Deviation to the opposite side of swelling indicates **Peritonsillar Abscess (Quinsy)**.
  * *Trismus* (inability to open mouth): Suggests spasm of pterygoid muscles due to deep space neck infection.
* **Neck**:
  * *Lymphadenopathy*: Anterior cervical chain (GABHS) vs. Posterior cervical chain (Infectious Mononucleosis).
  * *Neck swelling / Crepitus* $\rightarrow$ Deep neck space infection (Ludwig's Angina, Retropharyngeal abscess).
* **Skin**: Diffuse erythematous "sandpaper" rash $\rightarrow$ Scarlet fever (streptococcal).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common / Self-Limiting | Dangerous / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infectious** | • **Viral Pharyngitis** (Rhinovirus, Adenovirus)<br>• **GABHS Tonsillitis** (Strep throat)<br>• **Infectious Mononucleosis** (EBV / CMV) | • **Acute Epiglottitis** (supraglottic infection)<br>• **Peritonsillar Abscess (Quinsy)**<br>• **Retropharyngeal Abscess**<br>• **Ludwig's Angina** (submandibular space)<br>• **Diphtheria** (pseudomembranous) |
| **Non-Infectious** | • GERD reflux esophagitis<br>• Post-nasal drip / Allergic rhinitis | • Foreign body inhalation / Esophageal rupture |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency airway management and ENT consult required if present:**
> * Stridor, wheezing, or accessory muscle use.
> * Drooling (indicates inability to swallow secretions due to epiglottic/pharyngeal obstruction).
> * Muffled "hot potato" voice.
> * Trismus (inability to open mouth).
> * Toxic appearance with high fever, neck rigidity, or subcutaneous crepitus.
> * "Tripod" positioning (leaning forward, neck hyperextended, chin thrust outward).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT)**: High specificity ($>95\%$) but moderate sensitivity for GABHS.
  * **Throat Culture**: Gold standard for GABHS. Indicated if RADT is negative in children/adolescents (due to risk of Rheumatic Fever).
* **Laboratory Tests**:
  * **CBC with Differential**: Leukocytosis with atypical lymphocytes ($>10\%$) is highly suggestive of Infectious Mononucleosis.
  * **Monospot Test (Heterophile antibody)**: Rapid diagnostic test for EBV mononucleosis. (May be falsely negative in first week of illness).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Lateral Neck X-ray**: Look for the **"Thumbprint sign"** (swollen epiglottis) in suspected Epiglottitis, or prevertebral space widening in Retropharyngeal abscess.
  * **CT Neck with Contrast**: Gold standard for delineating deep neck space infections (Peritonsillar, retropharyngeal, submandibular abscesses).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Airway Control (Epiglottitis / Ludwig's)**:
   - Do **not** examine the throat of a patient with suspected Epiglottitis with a tongue depressor; this can trigger reflex laryngospasm and complete airway obstruction.
   - Secure the airway in a controlled environment (Operating Room/ICU) with ENT and anesthesiology standby.
2. **GABHS Pharyngitis Antibiotic Therapy**:
   - First-line: **Penicillin V** $500$ mg PO BID-TID for 10 days, or **Amoxicillin** $500$ mg BID / $1000$ mg daily.
   - Penicillin allergy: Macrolides (Erythromycin/Azithromycin) or Cephalosporins.
   - Purpose: Primary goal of treating GABHS is to prevent **Acute Rheumatic Fever** (must treat within 9 days of onset).
3. **Peritonsillar Abscess**:
   - Needle aspiration or Incision & Drainage (I&D) + IV antibiotics (Amoxicillin-Clavulanate or Ampicillin-Sulbactam) + single dose of IV Dexamethasone to reduce swelling.
4. **Symptomatic Relief**:
   - NSAIDs (Ibuprofen) or Paracetamol. Warm saline gargles.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Amoxicillin Mononucleosis Rash**: Giving Amoxicillin or Ampicillin to a patient with EBV Infectious Mononucleosis (misdiagnosed as Strep throat) causes a classic **pruritic maculopapular rash** in $>90\%$ of patients. This is a non-allergic drug reaction.
> * **Sore Throat + Normal Pharynx**: If a patient complains of severe sore throat and pain on swallowing (odynophagia) but the oropharynx looks completely normal on inspection, think **Acute Epiglottitis** or **Esophagitis**.
> * **Rheumatic Fever vs. Post-Streptococcal GN**: Antibiotic therapy for Strep throat prevents Acute Rheumatic Fever, but does **not** prevent the development of Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis.
