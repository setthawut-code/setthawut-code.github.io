# 🩸 Pallor (ซีด)

> "Pallor is the pale appearance of the skin and mucous membranes. The clinical approach must separate chronic anemic pallor from acute vasoconstrictive pallor (shock)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Anemic vs. Vasoconstrictive**:
>    - *Anemic*: Associated with pale **mucous membranes (conjunctiva, oral mucosa)** and **palmar creases**. Indicates true red blood cell mass depletion.
>    - *Vasoconstrictive*: Mucous membranes may be relatively spared. Cold, clammy extremities. Indicates severe sympathetic vasoconstriction due to **[[Shock]]** or pain.
> 2. **Rule Out Shock**: Check for resting tachycardia, hypotension, and poor perfusion.
> 3. **Assess for Active Bleeding**: DRE (melena/hematochezia) and check for hematemesis.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Pallor results from a decrease in oxyhemoglobin content in the dermal and mucosal microcirculation.
* **Anemic Pallor**: Reduction in hemoglobin concentration. *Palmar creases lose their normal pink color and become pale when the hemoglobin level drops below **$7-8$ g/dL**.*
* **Vasoconstrictive Pallor**: Compensatory redirecting of blood flow from the skin to vital organs (brain, heart) during shock or sympathetic stress (hypoglycemia, panic, pheochromocytoma).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Onset**:
  * *Acute onset* $\rightarrow$ Massive hemorrhage, shock, or acute hemolysis.
  * *Chronic progressive* $\rightarrow$ Chronic nutritional anemia, CKD, or thalassemia.
* **Bleeding History**: Melena, hematochezia, menorrhagia, bruising, or recurrent nosebleeds.
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * Fatigue, dyspnea on exertion $\rightarrow$ Chronic [[Anemia]].
  * Sweating, tremors, hunger $\rightarrow$ Hypoglycemia (adrenergic activation).
  * Weight loss, fever, night sweats $\rightarrow$ Malignancy or chronic infection.
* **Family History**: Thalassemia or hemoglobinopathies.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Key Sites for Inspection**:
  * **Conjunctival Pallor**: Pull down the lower eyelid. Compare the pale tarsal conjunctiva with the normal pink appearance.
  * **Palmar Creases**: Stretch the patient's hand. If the creases appear as pale as the surrounding skin, the hemoglobin is likely $<7-8$ g/dL.
  * **Nail Beds & Buccal Mucosa**.
* **Hemodynamics**: Assess for tachycardia, hypotension, or orthostatic drops. *Anemic patients with hyperdynamic circulation may have a wide pulse pressure and a soft systolic flow murmur at the left sternal border.*
* **Abdomen & Skin**:
  * Palpate for splenomegaly (common in thalassemia, hemolysis, portal HTN, leukemia).
  * Palpate for hepatomegaly.
  * Look for petechiae or purpura (platelet deficiency).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Anemic (RBC Mass Depletion) | Non-Anemic (Vasoconstrictive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Microcytic** (MCV $<80$) | • Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)<br>• [[Thalassemia]] disease/trait<br>• Lead poisoning | None |
| **Normocytic** (MCV $80-100$) | • Acute hemorrhage / Hemolysis<br>• Anemia of Chronic Disease (ACD)<br>• Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) | None |
| **Macrocytic** (MCV $>100$) | • Vitamin B12 / Folate deficiency<br>• Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) | None |
| **Vasoconstrictive** | None | • **Hypovolemic / Hemorrhagic [[Shock]]**<br>• **Cardiogenic / Septic Shock**<br>• Hypoglycemia / Panic Attack<br>• Pheochromocytoma |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency resuscitation and transfusion protocols if present:**
> * Pallor accompanied by resting tachycardia ($>100$ bpm), hypotension (SBP $<90$ mmHg), or cold, clammy skin ([[Shock]]).
> * Pallor in a patient with active, ongoing hemorrhage (hematemesis, hematochezia, trauma).
> * Pallor with chest pain (myocardial ischemia) or severe dyspnea at rest.
> * Pallor with fever and diffuse petechiae/purpura (suggests leukemia/aplastic anemia).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Key test. Check Hemoglobin, Hematocrit, MCV, RDW, and WBC/Platelet counts.
  * **Reticulocyte Count**: Determine marrow response (calculate RPI).
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Look for abnormal cell shapes (target cells, hypersegmented neutrophils, schistocytes, blast cells).
  * **Bedside Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia in vasoconstrictive pallor.
* **Secondary targeted testing**:
  * **Iron Studies**: Ferritin, TIBC (if microcytic).
  * **Vitamin B12 & Folate levels** (if macrocytic).
  * **Hemoglobin Electrophoresis** (if suspected thalassemia).
  * **Fecal Occult Blood Test (FOBT) / FIT**: Screen for occult GI bleeding.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Active Hemorrhage / Shock**:
   - Resuscitate with IV crystalloids and activate massive transfusion protocol if hemorrhagic shock.
   - Transfuse Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) to maintain **$Hb \ge 7$ g/dL** (or $\ge 8$ g/dL in active cardiovascular disease).
2. **Chronic Anemia**:
   - Do **not** transfuse stable, asymptomatic patients with $Hb > 7$ g/dL.
   - Treat the underlying cause: Oral/IV iron for IDA; B12/Folate replacement; Erythropoietin-stimulating agents (ESA) for CKD.
3. **Hypoglycemia**:
   - Give 50% Dextrose IV or oral fast-acting glucose.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Palmar Crease Threshold**: Palmar creases are highly vascularized. They do not lose their pink coloration until the hemoglobin concentration drops below **$7-8$ g/dL**. If the creases are pale, the patient is severely anemic.
> * **Carotenemia vs. Jaundice vs. Pallor**:
>   - *Carotenemia*: Yellow-orange skin due to diet (carrots, papayas), but **sclera is completely white**.
>   - *Jaundice*: Yellow skin **AND yellow sclera**.
>   - *Pallor*: Pale, white conjunctiva and skin.
> * **Relative Tachycardia in Anemia**: In chronic anemia, the heart compensates for low oxygen carrying capacity by increasing cardiac output via tachycardia and increased stroke volume. This creates a hyperdynamic, loud, pounding pulse and a systolic flow murmur.
