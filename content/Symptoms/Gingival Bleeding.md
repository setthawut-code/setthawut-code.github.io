# 🦷 Gingival Bleeding (เลือดออกตามไรฟัน)

> "Gingival bleeding is primarily a sign of local periodontal disease, but it can be the presenting symptom of life-threatening systemic hematological disorders."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Local vs. Systemic**:
>    - *Local*: Bleeding only during brushing/flossing; associated with poor oral hygiene, dental plaque, or pregnancy.
>    - *Systemic*: **Spontaneous** gum bleeding; associated with bruising, petechiae, epistaxis, or bleeding from other mucosal sites.
> 2. **Rule Out Acute Leukemia**: Severe bleeding + gingival hypertrophy (swollen gums) + fever + pallor $\rightarrow$ Highly suspicious for **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**.
> 3. **Obtain CBC & Coagulation Profile** immediately for any patient with spontaneous or persistent gum bleeding.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Gingival bleeding occurs due to disruption of vascular endothelium in the gingival tissue. Systemic bleeding disorders worsen local inflammation-induced bleeding:
* **Primary Hemostasis Defect** (Platelets/Vessel Wall): Characterized by immediate, mucosal bleeding (e.g., gum bleeding, nosebleeds) and petechiae.
* **Secondary Hemostasis Defect** (Clotting Factors): Characterized by delayed, deep tissue bleeding (hemarthrosis, hematomas); rarely presents as isolated gum bleeding.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Bleeding Character**:
  * *Triggered (by brushing/hard food)* $\rightarrow$ Likely local gingivitis/periodontitis.
  * *Spontaneous (waking up with blood in mouth)* $\rightarrow$ Severe thrombocytopenia or coagulation defect.
* **Associated Bleeding**: Check for petechiae, easy bruising (ecchymoses), recurrent epistaxis, heavy menses (menorrhagia), or hematuria.
* **Dietary & Social History**: Poor intake of fresh fruits/vegetables $\rightarrow$ **Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency)**. Alcohol abuse $\rightarrow$ Liver cirrhosis (coagulopathy) or nutritional deficiency.
* **Medication History**:
  * Antiplatelets: Aspirin, Clopidogrel.
  * Anticoagulants: Warfarin, Heparin, DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban).
  * Anticonvulsants: Phenytoin (causes gingival hyperplasia, predisposing to bleeding).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Oral Cavity Inspection**:
  * *Gingival Hyperplasia (hypertrophy)*: Overgrowth of gums. Common in AML (monocytic subtype M4/M5), Phenytoin use, or Cyclosporine use.
  * *Plaque & Calculus*: Indicates poor hygiene / local gingivitis.
  * *Mucosal petechiae or purpura* on the palate or buccal mucosa $\rightarrow$ Systemic thrombocytopenia.
* **Skin Exam (Crucial for Systemic Clues)**:
  * **Petechiae** (pinpoint red spots) & **Purpura**: Sign of platelet deficiency or vasculitis.
  * **Perifollicular hemorrhages** & **"Corkscrew" hairs**: Pathognomonic for **Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency)**.
* **Abdomen**: Hepatosplenomegaly (portal hypertension in cirrhosis, or infiltration in leukemia/lymphoma).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 Primary vs. Secondary Hemostasis Differentiator

| Feature | Primary Hemostasis (Platelets) | Secondary Hemostasis (Factors) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gingival Bleeding** | **Common, prominent** | Uncommon |
| **Petechiae** | **Present** (highly specific) | Absent |
| **Ecchymoses (Bruising)** | Small, superficial | Large, deep, palpable (hematomas) |
| **Hemarthrosis / Joint Bleeding** | Rare | **Common** (classic for hemophilia) |
| **Bleeding after minor cuts** | Persistent, immediate | Minimal immediate; delayed recurrence |

### 🧩 Diagnostic Classification Matrix

| Category | Primary Etiologies | Diagnostic Clues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local / Oral** | • Gingivitis (plaque-induced)<br>• Periodontitis (deep tissue bone loss)<br>• Pregnancy-induced gingivitis | Bleeding only during brushing, localized erythema, normal CBC and coagulation profile. |
| **Platelet Disorders** | • **ITP** (Immune Thrombocytopenia)<br>• **Acute Leukemia** (AML/ALL)<br>• Aplastic Anemia / Marrow failure<br>• Uremic platelet dysfunction (in CKD) | Severe thrombocytopenia (Platelets $<20k-50k$), petechiae, splenomegaly, blasts on PBS. |
| **Coagulation / Liver** | • **[[Cirrhosis]]** (impaired factor synthesis)<br>• **Vitamin K Deficiency** (malabsorption, warfarin)<br>• DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coag) | Prolonged PT/INR, history of liver disease or antibiotic use, bleeding from IV sites. |
| **Nutritional / Vascular** | • **Scurvy** (Vitamin C deficiency)<br>• Senile purpura / Steroid-induced | Perifollicular hemorrhages, corkscrew hairs, poor diet, ecchymoses on sun-exposed skin. |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit and perform emergency hematological evaluation if present:**
> * Spontaneous, continuous, or refractory gingival bleeding.
> * Gingival bleeding accompanied by fever, pallor, and weight loss.
> * Marked gingival hypertrophy (swollen, overgrown gums covering teeth).
> * Diffuse petechiae, purpura, or bruising on the trunk and limbs.
> * Co-existing bleeding from other mucosal sites (epistaxis, hematemesis, melena).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[CBC]] with Platelet Count**: Check for severe thrombocytopenia ($<50,000/\mu\text{L}$ risks trauma bleeding; $<10,000/\mu\text{L}$ risks spontaneous bleeding) and leukocytosis/blasts.
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Essential to check for schistocytes (DIC/TTP), blast cells (leukemia), or giant platelets.
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT)**: Check for factor deficiencies (elevated PT in liver disease/warfarin; elevated aPTT in hemophilia/heparin).
* **Targeted Investigations**:
  * **Renal Function Tests**: Evaluate for uremic platelet dysfunction if CKD is present.
  * **Liver Function Tests**: Assess synthetic liver function (Albumin, Bilirubin).
  * **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) level**: If history suggests Scurvy.
  * **Bone Marrow Aspiration & Biopsy**: Indicated if abnormal CBC suggests leukemia, aplastic anemia, or myelodysplasia.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Local Gingival Bleeding**:
   - Refer to Dentist. Dental scaling and root planing to remove plaque and calculus. Chlorhexidine gluconate oral rinse BID.
2. **Severe Thrombocytopenia**:
   - Platelet transfusion (generally indicated if Platelets $<10,000/\mu\text{L}$ to prevent spontaneous hemorrhage, or $<50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with active bleeding).
   - If ITP: High-dose Corticosteroids, IVIG.
3. **Coagulopathy / Liver Disease**:
   - IV Vitamin K ($10$ mg) if due to deficiency/warfarin. Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) or Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) if active life-threatening bleeding.
4. **Scurvy**:
   - Oral Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid) supplementation ($100-500$ mg TID for 1 week, then daily). Symptoms typically resolve rapidly.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Monocytic Leukemia Gum Hypertrophy**: Gingival infiltration by leukemic cells is a classic feature of **Acute Monocytic Leukemia (AML subclass M4 or M5)**. The gums appear swollen, boggy, and bleed easily.
> * **Phenytoin Hyperplasia**: Phenytoin-induced gingival overgrowth occurs in up to $50\%$ of patients. It is worsened by poor oral hygiene. Treatment involves plaque control, drug substitution (e.g., to Valproate), or gingivectomy.
> * **Uremic Bleeding Normal Labs**: In patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD), uremic toxins impair platelet aggregation. The platelet count, PT/INR, and aPTT are usually **completely normal**. Bleeding time was historically used, but clinical history is key. Treat with **DDAVP (Desmopressin)** or dialysis.
