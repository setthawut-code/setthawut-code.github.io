# 🧠 Syncope (วูบ / เป็นลมหมดสติ)

> **Definition**: Syncope is defined as a transient loss of consciousness (TLOC) due to transient global cerebral hypoperfusion. It is characterized by rapid onset, short duration, and spontaneous, complete recovery. 

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Confirm True Syncope**: Distinguish from other states of TLOC (e.g., Seizure, Hypoglycemia, Cataplexy, Psychogenic pseudosyncope).
> 2. **Rule Out Cardiac Syncope**: This is the most dangerous etiology. Look for syncope *during exertion*, *lying flat*, preceded by *palpitations/chest pain*, or associated with *abnormal ECG*.
> 3. **Perform Orthostatic Vital Signs**: Standard test on the ward for suspected orthostasis.
> 4. **Obtain ECG** for all patients presenting with syncope.

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
While reflex syncope is benign, **cardiac syncope** (secondary to arrhythmias or structural heart disease) is associated with up to a 30% one-year mortality rate and a high risk of sudden cardiac death. Immediate diagnosis of high-risk arrhythmias (e.g., complete heart block, ventricular tachycardia) or structural blocks (e.g., critical aortic stenosis) is vital to save the patient's life.

---

## 🔍 Physiology & Pathophysiology
Syncope results from a temporary drop in blood flow to the brain (cerebral hypoperfusion) for as short as **$6-8$ seconds** or a decrease in systemic systolic blood pressure to **$<60$ mmHg**.

### Pathophysiological Categories:
1. **Reflex (Neurally Mediated)**:
   - Inappropriate vasodilation and/or bradycardia triggered by autonomic reflex (e.g., Vasovagal, Situational, Carotid sinus).
2. **Orthostatic Hypotension (OH)**:
   - Blunted vasoconstrictor response to standing leading to blood pooling in lower extremities.
3. **Cardiovascular (Cardiac)**:
   - Primary pump failure due to structural heart disease or arrhythmias.

---

## 🦠 Etiology & Causes
```
Syncope Etiologies
└── 1. Reflex / Neurally Mediated (Benign, vasodilatory)
    ├── Vasovagal: Triggered by standing, pain, fear, heat
    ├── Situational: Micturition, defecation, cough, swallowing
    └── Carotid Sinus Hypersensitivity (shaving, tight collar)
└── 2. Orthostatic Hypotension (Volume / Autonomic failure)
    ├── Volume depletion: Hemorrhage, dehydration, vomiting, diarrhea
    ├── Drug-induced: Vasodilators, diuretics, beta-blockers
    └── Autonomic failure: Parkinson's disease, diabetic neuropathy
└── 3. Cardiac (High Mortality)
    ├── Arrhythmic: Complete Heart Block, Ventricular Tachycardia, Sick Sinus
    └── Structural: Aortic Stenosis, PE, Cardiac Tamponade, HCM
```

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment (Emergency Protocol)

### Airway
* Assess patency. Patients are usually recovering by the time medical staff arrive. If still unconscious, open the airway and verify it is not a coma or status epilepticus.

### Breathing
* Look for normal respiration. Measure $SpO_2$.

### Circulation
* **Obtain an Immediate 12-lead ECG**: This is the single most critical diagnostic test in syncope.
* **Measure Vitals**: Look for profound hypotension (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg) or brady/tachycardia. Secure IV access.
* **Assess for Trauma**: Check the head, neck, and limbs for injuries sustained during the fall (specifically cervical spine injury or intracranial hemorrhage).
* **Orthostatic Vital Signs**: Measure BP and HR after 5 minutes supine, then standing at 1 and 3 minutes.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### Syncope vs. Seizure
Differentiating syncope from seizure is the most common diagnostic dilemma:

| Feature | Syncope | Seizure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trigger / Prodrome** | Warmth, tunnel vision, nausea, pallor, sweating | Aura (smell, taste), flashing lights, panic |
| **Onset** | Gradual over seconds (except cardiac) | Sudden |
| **Limb Movements** | Brief myoclonic jerks ($<15$ seconds, asynchronous) | Tonic-clonic movements ($>1-2$ minutes, synchronous) |
| **Skin Color** | **Pale, ashen, clammy** | Cyanotic or flushed |
| **Urinary Incontinence** | Rare | Common |
| **Post-ictal Phase** | **Immediate** recovery ($<1$ minute), alert | Prolonged confusion, somnolence ($>5-30$ minutes) |
| **Tongue Biting** | None | Common (lateral aspect of tongue) |

### Historical Clues by Category
* **Vasovagal (Common reflex)**: Syncope triggered by prolonged standing, pain, medical procedures, or emotional stress. Preceded by prodrome: nausea, diaphoresis, pallor.
* **Situational reflex**: Syncope occurring during or immediately after micturition, defecation, coughing, or swallowing.
* **Orthostatic**: Syncope occurring within minutes of standing up, associated with dehydration, alcohol, or starting new medications (antihypertensives, diuretics, vasodilators).
* **Cardiac (Dangerous)**: Syncope occurring **during exertion**, **while supine (lying flat)**, preceded by chest pain or palpitations, or sudden onset with no warning/prodrome.

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Orthostatic Vital Signs (Mandatory)**:
  * **Positive test**: A drop in SBP **$\ge 20$ mmHg** or a drop in DBP **$\ge 10$ mmHg** within 3 minutes of standing.
  * *Autonomic failure vs. Hypovolemia*: If HR increases by $>30$ bpm, it suggests hypovolemia/dehydration. If HR does not rise despite BP drop, it suggests autonomic failure (e.g., Parkinson's, Diabetes).
* **Cardiovascular**:
  * Listen for systolic murmur of **Aortic Stenosis** (crescendo-decrescendo, radiating to carotids) or **HCM** (worsens with Valsalva).
  * Check for irregular pulse (e.g., Atrial Fibrillation).
* **Neurological**: Examine for focal neurological deficits. *Note: true syncope does not cause focal deficits; their presence suggests Stroke, TIA, or Todd's paralysis post-seizure.*

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### Syncope Mimics
* **Syncope**: Rapid onset, complete recovery within minutes, no postictal confusion, normal EEG, brief/absent twitching.
* **[[Seizure]] / Convulsion**: Tonic-clonic movements, tongue biting (lateral border), urinary incontinence, **prolonged postictal confusion ($>15-30$ minutes)**.
* **[[Coma]]**: Persistent unarousable state; does not recover spontaneously.
* **Stroke / TIA**: Focal neurologic deficits (hemiparesis, aphasia); consciousness is rarely lost unless brainstem is involved.

### Diagnostic Matrix Table
| Category | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reflex** | • Vasovagal (common faint)<br>• Situational (micturition, cough)<br>• Carotid sinus hypersensitivity | None |
| **Orthostatic** | • Dehydration / Hemorrhage<br>• Medication-induced (diuretics, vasodilators)<br>• Autonomic neuropathy (DM, Parkinson's) | • **Massive internal hemorrhage** (ruptured ectopic, ruptured AAA, GI bleed) |
| **Cardiac** | • Pacemaker malfunction | • **Tachyarrhythmias** (VT, VF, Torsades)<br>• **Bradyarrhythmias** (Sick Sinus, Complete AV block)<br>• **Structural**: Severe Aortic Stenosis, HCM<br>• **Vascular**: Massive [[Pulmonary Embolism]], Aortic Dissection |
| **Mimics** | • Psychogenic pseudosyncope<br>• Vasovagal-like anxiety | • **Generalized Seizure**<br>• Hypoglycemia<br>• Stroke / TIA |

## 🚨 Red Flags & Risk Stratification

```
Syncope Risk Stratification
└── 1. High-Risk Features (ADMIT to telemetry)
    ├── Cardiac history (CHF, CAD, low LVEF < 35%)
    ├── Exertional syncope (occurs during exercise) or while supine
    ├── ECG abnormalities (new conduction block, long QT, ischemia)
    ├── Structural signs (severe murmur of Aortic Stenosis)
    └── Severe anemia or acute blood loss
└── 2. Low-Risk Features (Safe for outpatient follow-up)
    ├── Young age (<40)
    ├── Classical prodrome (warmth, lightheadedness, nausea)
    └── Triggered by standing, pain, micturition, or fear
```

### 📋 San Francisco Syncope Rule (CHESS)
High risk of 30-day serious outcomes if any are present:
* **C** - Congestive Heart Failure history
* **H** - Hematocrit $< 30\%$ (Anemia)
* **E** - ECG abnormal (new changes, non-sinus rhythm)
* **S** - Shortness of breath
* **S** - Systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **12-lead ECG (Mandatory)**: Check for:
  * Bradyarrhythmias (block), tachyarrhythmias.
  * Ischemia/infarction (Q-waves, ST changes).
  * Long QT interval (QTc $>500$ ms $\rightarrow$ risk for Torsades).
  * Brugada pattern (coved ST elevation in V1-V2).
  * WPW syndrome (short PR, delta wave).
  * RV strain / S1Q3T3 (suggesting PE).
* **Bedside Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
* **CBC**: Check for severe anemia (Hematocrit $<30\%$).
* **Electrolytes**: Hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia can predispose to arrhythmias.
* **Troponin**: Check if ischemic cardiac syncope is suspected.
* **Echocardiogram**: Recommended if history of heart disease, cardiac murmur, abnormal ECG, or exertional syncope.
* **Holter / Event Monitor**: For suspected intermittent arrhythmia.
* **Tilt Table Test**: For recurrent unexplained syncope where reflex syncope is suspected but unconfirmed.
* **Carotid Sinus Massage**: Indicated in patients $>40$ years with unexplained syncope. *Contraindicated if carotid bruit or history of stroke/TIA in past 3 months.*

## 🏥 Management Principles

### Immediate Management Protocol
```
Syncope Immediate Management
├── 1. Position Supine (Elevate legs to shift blood to cerebral circulation)
├── 2. ECG Interpretation (Verify absence of high-risk blocks or arrhythmias)
├── 3. Fluid Resuscitation (If orthostatic or hypovolemic: 500-1000 mL crystalloid)
├── 4. Holter / Telemetry Monitoring (For high-risk patients)
└── 5. Treatment of High-Risk Causes
    ├── Complete AV Block / Bradycardia: IV Atropine / Transcutaneous Pacing
    ├── PE: Immediate anticoagulation / thrombolysis
    └── Severe Aortic Stenosis: Avoid vasodilators; consult cardiology
```

### Long-term Management
* **Reflex Syncope**: Reassurance. Educate on avoiding triggers and recognizing prodromes. Use physical counterpressure maneuvers (leg crossing, arm tensing, hand grip) to raise blood pressure during prodrome.
* **Orthostatic Hypotension**: Discontinue offending medications. Ensure fluid/salt repletion. Elevate head of bed ($10-15$ degrees). Use compression garments. Consider Fludrocortisone or Midodrine if refractory.
* **Cardiac Syncope**: Pacemaker for symptomatic bradyarrhythmias/high-degree AV blocks; implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) for VT/VF; surgery for severe aortic stenosis.

---

## 📊 Monitoring
* **Telemetry Monitoring**: Indicated in the ED or hospital for high-risk patients to detect transient heart blocks, pauses ($>3$ seconds), or ventricular tachyarrhythmias.
* **Risk Score Application**: Apply the **San Francisco Syncope Rule (CHESS)** or **Canadian Syncope Risk Score** to determine need for admission.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Exertional Syncope Danger**: Syncope occurring *during* exercise is cardiac (structural or arrhythmic) until proven otherwise. Syncope occurring *after* stopping exercise is often reflex vasovagal due to sudden pooling of blood in dilated skeletal muscle beds once muscle-pumping action stops.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Convulsive Syncope (Seizure Movement Fallacy)**: During a syncopal episode, global cerebral hypoperfusion can cause brief myoclonic jerking or twitching (usually $<15$ seconds). This is frequently misdiagnosed as epilepsy. The key differentiator is the **absence of a postictal phase**: patients with convulsive syncope wake up immediately, fully oriented, whereas seizure patients have prolonged confusion.
* > [!TIP]
  > **The Brugada Sign**: A genetic sodium channelopathy predisposing to sudden cardiac death. Look for a right bundle branch block (RBBB) pattern with **coved ST-elevation in leads V1 and V2** followed by T-wave inversion. These patients require urgent electrophysiology referral and ICD placement.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Carotid Sinus Syncope**: Classically occurs in elderly men while shaving, wearing tight collars, or turning the head, due to hypersensitive carotid baroreceptors.
