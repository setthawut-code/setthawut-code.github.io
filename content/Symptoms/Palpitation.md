# 💓 Palpitation (ใจสั่น)

> "Palpitations represent a subjective awareness of the heartbeat. The primary clinical goal is to distinguish benign sinus tachycardias or extrasystoles from life-threatening arrhythmias."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Arrhythmic Syncope**: Palpitations immediately followed by **[[Syncope]]** or pre-syncope indicate structural heart disease or ventricular tachyarrhythmias. **Admit for telemetry.**
> 2. **Rhythm Characterization**:
>    - *Irregularly irregular* $\rightarrow$ **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)**.
>    - *Abrupt onset and offset* $\rightarrow$ **Paroxysmal SVT** or Ventricular Tachycardia (VT).
>    - *Gradual onset and offset* $\rightarrow$ **Sinus Tachycardia** (anxiety, hyperthyroidism, fever, anemia).
> 3. **Obtain ECG** during active symptoms if possible.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Palpitations are caused by abnormal cardiac mechanics (increased stroke volume, rapid rate, or irregular contractions) stimulating cardiac sensory fibers.
* **Arrhythmic causes**: PACs/PVCs (skipped beats), SVT, AF, Ventricular Tachycardia (VT).
* **High-Output states**: Severe anemia, pregnancy, thyrotoxicosis, arteriovenous fistulas.
* **Psychogenic / Hyperadrenergic**: Anxiety/panic attacks, catecholamine surge.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character of Palpitation**:
  * *"Skipped beats" or "flip-flopping" in chest*: Typically premature atrial or ventricular contractions (PACs/PVCs).
  * *"Rapid, regular drumming"* $\rightarrow$ Sinus tachycardia or SVT.
  * *"Rapid, irregular flip-flopping"* $\rightarrow$ **Atrial Fibrillation** or Atrial Flutter with variable block.
* **Onset & Offset**:
  * *Abrupt, instantaneous* $\rightarrow$ SVT, AF, or VT.
  * *Gradual progression* $\rightarrow$ Sinus tachycardia (exercise, panic, fever).
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * *Palpitations + Syncope* $\rightarrow$ High risk of **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)** or complete heart block.
  * *Palpitations + Chest Pain* $\rightarrow$ Myocardial ischemia.
  * *Palpitations + Dyspnea* $\rightarrow$ Impending **[[Heart Failure]]** or pulmonary congestion.
* **Exposures**: Caffeine, alcohol ("holiday heart" triggering AF), sympathomimetics (pseudoephedrine), thyroxine overdose.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Pulse & Rhythm (Crucial)**:
  * **Irregularly irregular** $\rightarrow$ Atrial Fibrillation.
  * *Regular tachycardia* (HR $>150$ bpm) $\rightarrow$ SVT (e.g., AVNRT) or Atrial Flutter.
* **Jugular Venous Pulse (JVP)**: Look for **"Cannon a-waves"** (giant pulsations in neck veins caused by right atrium contracting against a closed tricuspid valve). Indicates AV dissociation, typical of **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)** or complete heart block.
* **Cardiovascular**:
  * Listen for systolic click and late systolic murmur of **Mitral Valve Prolapse** (MVP).
  * Murmur of Aortic Stenosis (can cause exertional ventricular arrhythmias).
* **Thyroid & Tremor**: Palpate thyroid for goiter; check for fine tremor (hyperthyroidism).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Origin | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiac (Arrhythmic)** | • PACs / PVCs (ectopic beats)<br>• Sinus Tachycardia<br>• Paroxysmal SVT (AVNRT/AVRT) | • **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)** / Ventricular Fibrillation<br>• **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)** with rapid ventricular response<br>• Sick Sinus Syndrome / Mobitz II / Complete AV block |
| **Cardiac (Structural)** | • Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP) | • **Severe Aortic Stenosis** / Mitral Regurgitation<br>• Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit for continuous telemetry monitoring and urgent cardiology consult if present:**
> * Palpitations accompanied by syncope, near-syncope, or orthostatic dizziness.
> * Palpitations causing chest pain or progressive dyspnea (pulmonary congestion).
> * History of previous myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, or heart failure (EF $<35\%$).
> * Family history of premature sudden cardiac death ($<50$ years).
> * **Abnormal resting ECG** (old Q-waves, long QTc, delta wave, or Brugada pattern).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[ECG]] (Mandatory)**: If patient has active symptoms, obtain immediate ECG. Otherwise, check resting ECG for clues:
    * Short PR + delta wave $\rightarrow$ **WPW Syndrome** (pre-excitation).
    * Prolonged QTc ($>500$ ms) $\rightarrow$ risk for Torsades de Pointes.
    * Pathological Q waves $\rightarrow$ previous MI.
    * Left ventricular hypertrophy $\rightarrow$ aortic stenosis, hypertension, or HCM.
  * **Serum Electrolytes**: Check **Potassium and Magnesium** (hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia trigger arrhythmias).
  * **TSH & Free T4**: Screen for hyperthyroidism.
  * **[[CBC]]**: Rule out severe anemia.
* **Outpatient Rhythm Monitoring** (if ECG normal but recurrent symptoms):
  * **Holter Monitor (24-48 hours)**: Best if symptoms occur daily.
  * **Event Recorder (2-4 weeks)**: Best for weekly symptoms.
  * **Patch/Loop Recorder (months)**: Best for infrequent symptoms.
* **Echocardiography**: Recommended if structural heart disease, murmur, or abnormal ECG is present.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Unstable Arrhythmia** (Hypotension, shock, chest pain, or pulmonary edema):
   - **Perform Immediate Direct Current (DC) Cardioversion** (synchronized for tachyarrhythmias with pulses; unsynchronized defibrillation for VF/pulseless VT).
2. **Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)**:
   - Try vagal maneuvers (modified Valsalva, carotid sinus massage).
   - If unsuccessful: **IV Adenosine** ($6$ mg rapid flush; if fails, give $12$ mg). *Warn the patient about transient flushing/chest pressure.*
3. **Atrial Fibrillation**:
   - Rate control: Beta-blockers (Metoprolol) or CCBs (Diltiazem).
   - Anticoagulation: Calculate $CHA_2DS_2-VASc$ score to determine stroke risk and initiate oral anticoagulants (DOACs or Warfarin).
4. **Ventricular Ectopy (PVCs)**:
   - Beta-blockers if highly symptomatic. Avoid triggers (caffeine, nicotine).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Cannon a-waves in the Neck**: In Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), the atria and ventricles beat independently (AV dissociation). When the right atrium contracts while the tricuspid valve is closed by ventricular systole, blood is forced back into the jugular veins, creating massive, intermittent **cannon a-waves** in the neck.
> * **The "Holiday Heart" Syndrome**: Acute consumption of large amounts of alcohol in otherwise healthy individuals can trigger paroxysmal **Atrial Fibrillation**, typically resolving spontaneously within 24 hours.
> * **Adenosine Administration**: Adenosine has an extremely short half-life ($<10$ seconds). It must be given via a large bore IV in the antecubital fossa as a rapid bolus, immediately followed by a rapid saline flush.
