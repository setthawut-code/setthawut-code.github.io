# 🦴 Arthralgia (ปวดข้อ)

> "Arthralgia is joint pain. The key clinical task is to differentiate arthralgia from arthritis (true joint inflammation), and determine if the pain is articular or extra-articular."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Arthralgia vs. Arthritis**:
>    - *Arthralgia*: Joint pain **without** physical signs of inflammation (no swelling, warmth, or redness).
>    - *Arthritis*: Joint pain **with** objective signs of inflammation (swelling, warmth, erythema, joint effusion).
> 2. **Rule Out Septic Arthritis**: Any acute **monoarthritis** (single swollen, red, hot joint) with fever is Septic Arthritis until proven otherwise. **Perform joint aspiration (arthrocentesis) immediately.**
> 3. **Articular vs. Extra-articular**:
>    - *Articular*: Pain is deep, diffuse, and limits **both active and passive** range of motion.
>    - *Extra-articular* (tendon/bursa): Pain is localized and limits **active** movement but spares passive movement.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Arthralgia**: Subjective sensation of joint pain.
* **Articular structures**: Synovium, synovial fluid, articular cartilage, intra-articular ligaments, joint capsule, and juxta-articular bone.
* **Extra-articular structures**: Tendons, entheses, ligaments, bursae, overlying skin, and muscle.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Articular vs. Extra-Articular Localization**:
  * *Articular*: Pain is diffuse, deep, worse with all movement (passive and active), and accompanied by morning stiffness or joint swelling.
  * *Extra-Articular*: Pain is superficial, localized, worse with specific movements (active contraction of muscle/tendon), and rarely causes morning stiffness.
* **Chronology & Stiffness**:
  * *Inflammatory joint pain* (RA, lupus): Pain is worse in the morning, accompanied by **morning stiffness lasting $>1$ hour**, and **improves with activity**.
  * *Non-inflammatory joint pain* (Osteoarthritis): Pain is worse at the end of the day/after use, accompanied by brief morning stiffness ($<30$ minutes), and **worsens with activity**.
* **Pattern of Joint Involvement**:
  * *Monoarticular* (1 joint) $\rightarrow$ Gout, septic arthritis, trauma.
  * *Oligoarticular* ($2-4$ joints) $\rightarrow$ Reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, osteoarthritis.
  * *Polyarticular* ($\ge 5$ joints) $\rightarrow$ Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Joint Inspection & Palpation**:
  * Look for swelling (joint effusion vs. bony enlargement), redness, or deformity (rheumatoid nodules, Heberden's/Bouchard's nodes in osteoarthritis).
  * Palpate for **local warmth** and **joint line tenderness**.
* **Range of Motion (Bedside Differentiator)**:
  * Passively move the joint through its range of motion.
  * *Articular pathology*: Pain and limitation present in **both active and passive** movements.
  * *Extra-articular pathology* (e.g., rotator cuff tendinitis): Pain is elicited during **active** arm abduction but is relatively painless during **passive** abduction by the examiner.
* **Extra-articular Systemic Exam**: Check for skin rashes (malar rash in SLE, scaly plaques in psoriasis), eye redness (uveitis in Ankylosing Spondylitis/reactive arthritis), oral ulcers (lupus/Behçet's).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Articular (Joint Cavity) | Extra-Articular (Tendon, Bursa, Muscle) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inflammatory** | • **Rheumatoid Arthritis** (symmetric polyarthritis)<br>• **SLE / Lupus**<br>• Gout / Pseudogout (monoarthritis) | • Tenosynovitis (e.g., De Quervain's)<br>• Bursitis (olecranon, prepatellar)<br>• Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR)<br>• Enthesitis (Achilles tendonitis) |
| **Non-Inflammatory**| • **Osteoarthritis** (mechanical wear)<br>• Internal derangement (meniscal tear) | • Tendinitis (Rotator cuff, patellar)<br>• Fibromyalgia (widespread trigger points)<br>• Sprains / Strains |
| **Dangerous / Can't Miss**| • **Septic Arthritis** (bacterial joint infection)<br>• **Acute Rheumatic Fever** | • Necrotizing fasciitis (overlying skin/fascia)<br>• Osteomyelitis (adjacent bone infection) |

---

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent joint aspiration and specialized workup required if present:**
> * Acute onset of a single, swollen, hot, and painful joint (Monoarthritis) $\rightarrow$ Suspect **Septic Arthritis**.
> * Joint pain accompanied by high fever, chills, and signs of systemic toxicity ([[Sepsis]]).
> * Joint pain following significant trauma with joint instability or inability to bear weight.
> * Joint pain accompanied by progressive systemic symptoms (unexplained weight loss, skin rashes, uveitis).
> * Unexplained acute joint pain in an immunocompromised patient.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **ESR & CRP**: Markers of systemic inflammation (highly elevated in inflammatory arthritis, normal/mildly elevated in osteoarthritis).
  * **Uric Acid**: (Note: uric acid can be normal during an acute gout flare, but helps in long-term monitoring).
  * **Rheumatologic Panel**: Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP (specific for Rheumatoid Arthritis), ANA (lupus screen).
* **Synovial Fluid Analysis (Arthrocentesis) (Gold Standard for Swollen Joint)**:
  * Mandatory in any acute unexplained joint effusion to differentiate:
    * *Infectious*: Purulent, WBC $>50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with $>75\%$ PMNs, positive Gram stain/culture.
    * *Inflammatory*: Cloudy/yellow, WBC $2,000-50,000/\mu\text{L}$, presence of crystals (monosodium urate in gout; CPPD in pseudogout).
    * *Non-inflammatory*: Clear/yellow, WBC $<2,000/\mu\text{L}$ (osteoarthritis).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Plain X-Ray**: Assess for joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, osteophytes (osteoarthritis) vs. periarticular osteopenia and marginal erosions (rheumatoid arthritis).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Septic Arthritis**:
   - Immediate joint drainage (needle aspiration, arthroscopy, or open arthrotomy) + empiric IV antibiotics (e.g., Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin) for $3-4$ weeks.
2. **Gout / Pseudogout Flare**:
   - First-line: NSAIDs (Indomethacin/Naproxen), **Colchicine** ($1.2$ mg followed by $0.6$ mg 1 hour later), or oral/intra-articular Corticosteroids. *Do not start or stop Allopurinol during an acute flare, as sudden uric acid fluctuations prolong the attack.*
3. **Osteoarthritis**:
   - Non-pharmacological: Weight loss, low-impact exercise, physical therapy.
   - Pharmacological: Topical NSAIDs (Diclofenac gel), oral Paracetamol, short-term oral NSAIDs. Intra-articular steroid injections.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Septic Joint Rule**: In clinical practice, any acute red, hot, swollen joint is considered **Septic Arthritis** until proven otherwise by synovial fluid aspiration. Never start antibiotics before aspirating the joint.
> * **Gout Uric Acid Trap**: Up to $30\%$ of patients have a normal serum uric acid level during an acute gout flare because uric acid deposits into the joint space, lowering circulating levels. A normal uric acid does not rule out acute gout.
> * **Morning Stiffness Threshold**: Morning stiffness lasting **$>1$ hour** that is relieved by warm showers or physical activity is a highly sensitive screening parameter for inflammatory joint diseases (RA, lupus) rather than mechanical osteoarthritis (stiffness $<30$ minutes, worse with use).
