# 🚽 Dysuria (ปัสสาวะแสบขัด)

> "Dysuria is painful or burning urination, indicating mucosal irritation of the urethra or bladder. The clinical priority is to rule out ascending upper tract infection (pyelonephritis) and urosepsis."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Pyelonephritis (Upper UTI)**: Look for **fever, chills, flank pain, and costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness**. *Requires IV antibiotics and imaging to rule out obstruction.*
> 2. **Vaginitis/Urethritis vs. Cystitis**:
>    - *Cystitis*: Associated with urinary frequency, urgency, and suprapubic pain.
>    - *Urethritis / Vaginitis*: Associated with vaginal or urethral discharge, external dysuria (pain as urine touches inflamed labia/meatus), and lack of frequency/urgency.
> 3. **The Male Dysuria Rule**: Dysuria in men is always considered **complicated**. Rule out prostatitis, urethritis (STIs), or bladder outlet obstruction.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Dysuria is a subjective sensation of pain, burning, or discomfort during micturition.
* **Internal Dysuria**: Pain felt inside the body at the start or throughout urination, indicating bladder neck or urethral inflammation.
* **External Dysuria**: Pain felt at the end of stream as urine passes over inflamed external genitalia/labia (common in vaginitis, herpes).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Urinary Patterns**:
  * *Abrupt onset of dysuria + frequency + urgency + suprapubic pain* $\rightarrow$ **Acute Cystitis** (lower UTI).
  * *Gradual onset, vaginal discharge, pruritus, dyspareunia* $\rightarrow$ **Vaginitis** (Candida, Trichomonas, Bacterial Vaginosis).
  * *Urethral discharge, history of new sexual partner* $\rightarrow$ **Urethritis** (STIs: *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, *Chlamydia trachomatis*).
  * *Perineal or pelvic pain, dysuria, pain during ejaculation in a male* $\rightarrow$ **Acute Prostatitis**.
* **Host Risk Factors**: Pregnancy, diabetes, immunocompromised status, indwelling Foley catheter.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Document Temperature. Fever points to **[[Acute Pyelonephritis]]**, acute prostatitis, or systemic urosepsis.
* **Abdomen & Flank**:
  * **Costovertebral Angle (CVA) Tenderness**: Pain elicited by gentle percussion over the 12th rib/vertebral angle $\rightarrow$ highly indicative of **Pyelonephritis**.
  * **Suprapubic Tenderness**: Elicited by palpation over the bladder area $\rightarrow$ Acute Cystitis.
* **Genital & Rectal Examination**:
  * Speculum/pelvic exam in women if vaginal symptoms are present.
  * **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) in Men**: Perform gently. *A warm, swollen, exquisitely tender, boggy prostate indicates **Acute Prostatitis**. Avoid vigorous prostate massage, as this can precipitate bacteremia and septic shock.*

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common / Uncomplicated | Dangerous / Complicated (Can't Miss) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infectious (Urinary)** | • **Acute Cystitis** (uncomplicated in young healthy females)<br>• Urethritis (Chlamydia/Gonorrhea) | • **[[Acute Pyelonephritis]]** (kidney infection)<br>• **Acute Bacterial Prostatitis** (men)<br>• Renal / Perinephric Abscess |
| **Infectious (Genital)** | • Vaginitis (Candidiasis, Trichomoniasis)<br>• Genital Herpes simplex | • Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) |
| **Non-Infectious** | • Interstitial Cystitis (bladder pain syndrome)<br>• Atrophic vaginitis (postmenopausal) | • Bladder / Urethral Cancer<br>• Nephrolithiasis passing through urethra |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit for IV antibiotics and imaging if present:**
> * Dysuria accompanied by high fever, rigors, flank pain, or CVA tenderness (Pyelonephritis).
> * Hemodynamic instability, hypotension, or signs of [[Shock]] (urosepsis).
> * Dysuria in any male patient (complicated UTI/prostatitis risk).
> * Dysuria in a pregnant patient (high risk of ascending infection/preterm labor).
> * Acute urinary retention or inability to void (obstruction).
> * Immunocompromised state (DM, chemotherapy, transplant).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Urinalysis]] (Dipstick & Microscopy)**:
    * *Leukocyte Esterase & Nitrites*: High specificity for Enterobacteriaceae (e.g., E. coli). *Note: Nitrite can be false negative if urine is not in bladder for $>4$ hours or caused by non-nitrite producing bacteria (Enterococcus, Saprophyticus).*
    * *Microscopy*: **Pyuria** ($\ge 5-10$ WBCs/hpf) confirms inflammation. Check for hematuria and bacteriuria.
  * **Urine Culture & Sensitivity**: Gold standard. Mandated for all complicated UTIs, males, pregnant women, recurrent cases, or suspected pyelonephritis.
* **Specialized Testing**:
  * **Urethral/Vaginal Swabs & NAAT (PCR)**: For Chlamydia and Gonorrhea if discharge is present or urethritis suspected.
  * **Renal Ultrasound / CT Abdomen-Pelvis**: Indicated in pyelonephritis if fever persists after 72 hours of antibiotics, to rule out renal abscess or urinary tract obstruction.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Uncomplicated Cystitis**:
   - First-line: **Nitrofurantoin** $100$ mg PO BID for 5 days, or **Fosfomycin** 3 g PO single dose, or **TMP-SMX** $160/800$ mg PO BID for 3 days. *Avoid Nitrofurantoin if GFR $<30$ mL/min.*
2. **[[Acute Pyelonephritis]]**:
   - Mild/Outpatient: Oral **Ciprofloxacin** $500$ mg BID for 7 days or Levofloxacin.
   - Severe/Inpatient: IV third-generation Cephalosporin (**Ceftriaxone** 1-2 g daily) or Piperacillin/Tazobactam.
3. **Acute Prostatitis**:
   - Long-term antibiotics required due to poor prostate tissue penetration. Give oral Ciprofloxacin or TMP-SMX for **$4-6$ weeks**.
4. **Urethritis (STIs)**:
   - Treat Gonorrhea: Ceftriaxone $500$ mg IM single dose.
   - Treat Chlamydia: Doxycycline $100$ mg PO BID for 7 days (or Azithromycin 1 g single dose). *Always treat both partners.*

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Complicated vs. Uncomplicated UTI**: An uncomplicated UTI only occurs in **healthy, non-pregnant, premenopausal females** with anatomically normal urinary tracts. Any UTI in a male, pregnant woman, diabetic, immunocompromised patient, or those with catheters/stones is **complicated** and requires a urine culture and longer treatment course.
> * **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in Pregnancy**: While asymptomatic bacteriuria (positive urine culture without dysuria/frequency) is left untreated in general patients, it **must always be treated in pregnant women**. Untreated, up to $30-40\%$ of pregnant women will develop acute pyelonephritis, risking preterm labor.
> * **Sterile Pyuria**: The presence of WBCs in the urine (pyuria) but with negative standard urine cultures is termed sterile pyuria. Always consider **genitourinary tuberculosis**, *Chlamydia trachomatis*, or interstitial cystitis.
