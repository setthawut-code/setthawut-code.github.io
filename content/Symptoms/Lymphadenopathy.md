# 🔍 Lymphadenopathy (ต่อมน้ำเหลืองโต)

> "Lymphadenopathy represents enlargement of one or more lymph nodes. The clinical approach is based on local vs. systemic localization, and node characteristics."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Localized vs. Generalized**:
>    - *Localized*: Single region. Look for local infection, drainage site pathology, or localized malignancy.
>    - *Generalized*: **$\ge 2$ non-contiguous regions**. Think systemic infection (HIV, TB, EBV), autoimmune disease (SLE), or hematologic malignancy ([[Lymphoma]], leukemia).
> 2. **Rule Out Supraclavicular Node**: Any supraclavicular node (Virchow's node) is highly suspicious for malignant metastasis.
> 3. **Indication for Biopsy**: Painless, firm/hard, fixed node $>2$ cm persisting for $>4-6$ weeks without clear infectious cause.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Lymphadenopathy is defined as abnormal size, number, or consistency of lymph nodes.
* **Size Thresholds**:
  * Normal lymph nodes are generally **$<1$ cm** in diameter.
  * Exceptions: Epitrochlear nodes ($>0.5$ cm is abnormal), inguinal nodes ($>1.5$ cm is abnormal).
* **Pathophysiology**:
  1. *Antigenic stimulation*: Proliferation of intrinsic lymphocytes and macrophages (reactive hyperplasia).
  2. *Infiltration by inflammatory cells*: Lymphadenitis.
  3. *Infiltration by neoplastic cells*: Primary lymph node malignancy ([[Lymphoma]]) or metastatic cells.
  4. *Infiltration by metabolite-laden macrophages*: Storage diseases.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Localized vs. Systemic**:
  * Check for fever, night sweats, weight loss $\rightarrow$ Systemic (B symptoms of [[Lymphoma]] or active [[Tuberculosis]]).
* **Duration & Evolution**:
  * *Acute* ($<2$ weeks): Highly suggestive of bacterial/viral infection.
  * *Chronic* ($>4-6$ weeks): Chronic infection (TB, fungal), autoimmune, or malignancy.
* **Exposures**:
  * Cat scratch/bite $\rightarrow$ Cat scratch disease (*Bartonella henselae*).
  * Raw meat or cat feces ingestion $\rightarrow$ Toxoplasmosis.
  * High-risk sexual behavior / IV drug use $\rightarrow$ Acute HIV syndrome, Syphilis, CMV.
  * Travel to endemic areas $\rightarrow$ Histoplasmosis, Coccidioidomycosis.
* **Medication History (High-Yield)**:
  * **Phenytoin** (classic cause of drug-induced lymphadenopathy / pseudolymphoma).
  * Carbamazepine, Allopurinol, Atenolol.

## 🩺 Physical Examination

Evaluate every node group systematically:

### 📊 Anatomical Regions & Clinical Matches

| Node Location | Drainage Area | Common Pathologies |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cervical** | Scalp, face, oral cavity, pharynx | Upper respiratory infections, dental abscess, EBV, TB (Scrofula), head/neck cancer. |
| **Supraclavicular** | Mediastinum, lungs, abdomen | **Left (Virchow's node)**: GI, pelvic, or renal cancer.<br>**Right**: Lung or esophageal cancer. |
| **Axillary** | Breast, upper extremity, thoracic wall | Breast cancer, local skin infection, cat-scratch disease. |
| **Epitrochlear** | Ulnar aspect of hand/forearm | Secondary Syphilis, local skin infection, sarcoidosis, lymphoma. |
| **Inguinal** | Lower extremity, external genitalia, perineum | Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs), lower limb infections, melanoma. |

---

### 🎨 Node Characteristics:
* **Tenderness**: Warm, tender, mobile nodes indicate **inflammatory/infectious** processes. Pain is caused by rapid stretching of the node capsule.
* **Consistency**:
  * *Soft/Fluctuant*: Abscess or suppurative lymphadenitis.
  * *Firm, rubbery*: Highly characteristic of **[[Lymphoma]]**.
  * *Hard, stony*: Suggests **metastatic carcinoma**.
* **Mobility**: Mobile (normal/infection) vs. **Fixed** (adherent to adjacent tissues/skin due to tumor invasion or chronic inflammation).
* **Matted nodes** (grouped and connected): Tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, or advanced cancer.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Type | Common / Benign | Dangerous / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Localized** | • Reactive hyperplasia (viral/URI)<br>• Bacterial lymphadenitis<br>• Cat-scratch disease | • **Metastatic Squamous Cell Carcinoma** (cervical)<br>• **Breast Cancer** (axillary)<br>• Head and Neck cancers |
| **Generalized** | • Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV / CMV)<br>• Secondary Syphilis<br>• Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) | • **[[Lymphoma]]** (Hodgkin & Non-Hodgkin)<br>• **Acute/Chronic Leukemias** (CLL, ALL)<br>• **[[Tuberculosis]]** (disseminated)<br>• **Acute HIV Infection** |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Proceed to urgent tissue biopsy if any red flags are present:**
> * Supraclavicular lymphadenopathy (any size, age, or character).
> * Painless, hard, fixed, or matted lymph nodes.
> * Node diameter **$>2$ cm**.
> * Lymphadenopathy persisting **$>4-6$ weeks** without explanation.
> * Presence of systemic B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss).
> * Unexplained splenomegaly or cytopenias on CBC.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **[[CBC]] & Peripheral Blood Smear**: Check for atypical lymphocytes (EBV), blast cells (leukemia), or cytopenias.
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Assess for mediastinal or hilar adenopathy (sarcoidosis, lymphoma, TB).
  * **Serologies**: HIV, EBV, CMV, Syphilis (VDRL/RPR).
* **Tissue Diagnosis (Gold Standard)**:
  * **Excisional Lymph Node Biopsy**: **This is the diagnostic standard.** Provides intact node architecture necessary for lymphoma grading. *Excise the largest, most suspicious node (cervical/axillary preferred over inguinal due to chronic low-grade inflammation in inguinal nodes).*
  * **FNA (Fine Needle Aspiration)**: Useful for confirming metastasis of known primary carcinomas, but **insufficient** for primary lymphoma diagnosis due to loss of architecture.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Infectious Lymphadenitis**:
   - Direct antibiotic therapy targeting the primary source (e.g., Dicloxacillin or Cephalexin for staph/strep).
2. **Autoimmune / Rheumatic**:
   - Treat the underlying disease (corticosteroids/immunosuppressants for SLE/sarcoidosis).
3. **Malignancy (Leukemia/Lymphoma)**:
   - Refer to Hematology/Oncology for chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or radiation.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Inguinal Trapped Lymph Node**: Avoid biopsying inguinal lymph nodes if possible. Chronic minor trauma and skin infections of the lower limbs lead to chronic fibrotic enlargement of inguinal nodes, making them non-diagnostic.
> * **Drug-Induced Pseudolymphoma**: Phenytoin can cause a benign syndrome of lymphadenopathy, fever, and hepatosplenomegaly that mimics lymphoma. The syndrome typically resolves within weeks after discontinuing the drug.
> * **Epitrochlear Node Rule**: Epitrochlear nodes are rarely palpable. If they are $>0.5$ cm, always consider secondary syphilis, lymphoma, or sarcoidosis.
