# 🫁 Cyanosis (เขียวคล้ำ)

> "Cyanosis is a bluish discoloration of the skin and mucous membranes. The clinical path depends on distinguishing central from peripheral cyanosis, and ruling out abnormal hemoglobins."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Central vs. Peripheral**:
>    - *Central*: Affects both **mucous membranes (tongue, lips)** and extremities. Indicates systemic hypoxemia or shunt.
>    - *Peripheral*: Affects only **extremities (fingers, toes)**; mucous membranes are spared. Indicates local vasoconstriction or low cardiac output.
> 2. **Rule Out Methemoglobinemia**: If the patient is cyanotic with low $SpO_2$ ($85\%$) but has a **normal arterial oxygen tension ($PaO_2$) on ABG**, check for drug exposure and methemoglobin levels.
> 3. **Rule Out Upper Airway Obstruction**: Stridor with cyanosis is a life-threatening emergency.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Cyanosis becomes visible when the concentration of **deoxygenated hemoglobin** in capillary blood exceeds **$5$ g/dL** (or $SaO_2$ falls below $\sim 85\%$).
* *Anemic Patients*: Patients with severe anemia ($Hb < 7$ g/dL) may not manifest cyanosis even when critically hypoxic because they cannot generate $5$ g/dL of deoxygenated hemoglobin.
* *Polycythemic Patients*: Patients with elevated red blood cell counts can manifest cyanosis at relatively mild levels of hypoxia.

## 🗣️ Key History & Physical Examination

### 📊 Central vs. Peripheral Cyanosis Differentiator

| Feature | Central Cyanosis | Peripheral Cyanosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mucous Membranes** (Tongue, oral mucosa) | **Cyanotic / Blue** | **Normal / Pink** (spared) |
| **Extremity Temperature** | Warm (unless concurrent shock) | **Cold, clammy** |
| **Cause** | Systemic desaturation, right-to-left shunt, pulmonary disease. | Local vasoconstriction, cold exposure, low cardiac output. |
| **Effect of local warming** | No change | **Improves / resolves** |
| **Effect of $100\%$ Oxygen** | Variable (poor response if shunt) | No change |
| **Nail Clubbing** | **Present** in chronic cases | Absent |

---

### ⏳ Historical Clues & Exposures
* **Onset & Duration**:
  * *Since birth / early childhood* $\rightarrow$ Congenital cyanotic heart disease (Tetralogy of Fallot, transposition).
  * *Acute onset* $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, foreign body airway obstruction.
* **Drug Exposure (Methemoglobinemia triggers)**:
  * **Dapsone** (used for PCP prophylaxis / leprosy).
  * Topical local anesthetics (Benzocaine spray used for endoscopy).
  * Nitrites / Nitroglycerin infusion.
  * Sulfa drugs.

| Category | Common Causes | Dangerous Causes / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central** (Pulmonary) | • Severe pneumonia / ARDS<br>• Acute [[Asthma Exacerbation]]<br>• Severe [[COPD Exacerbation]] | • **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)**<br>• **[[Tension Pneumothorax]]**<br>• Acute Upper Airway Obstruction (stridor) |
| **Central** (Cardiac) | • Advanced congestive [[Heart Failure]] | • **Congenital Heart Disease (R-to-L Shunt)** (Eisenmenger's syndrome) |
| **Peripheral** | • Cold exposure (normal physiological)<br>• Raynaud's phenomenon | • **Cardiogenic / Septic [[Shock]]** (low output)<br>• Severe Peripheral Arterial Disease (critical limb ischemia) |
| **Abnormal Hemoglobin**| None | • **Methemoglobinemia** (drug-induced)<br>• Sulfhemoglobinemia |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency airway and oxygen resuscitation if present:**
> * Cyanosis accompanied by respiratory distress, stridor, or accessory muscle use.
> * Sudden onset of unilateral chest pain and absent breath sounds ([[Tension Pneumothorax]]).
> * Cyanosis associated with hemodynamic instability or signs of refractory [[Shock]] (MAP $<65$ mmHg).
> * Cyanosis that fails to respond to high-flow $100\%$ oxygen therapy (suspect right-to-left cardiac shunt or methemoglobinemia).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Pulse Oximetry ($SpO_2$)**: Simple screening tool. *Note: Classic methemoglobinemia causes the pulse oximeter to read $\sim 85\%$ regardless of the actual oxygenation.*
  * **[[ABG]] (Arterial Blood Gas)**: Measures true $PaO_2$ and $SaO_2$ (co-oximetry).
    * *The "Oxygen Saturation Gap"*: A difference of $>5\%$ between calculated $SaO_2$ on ABG and measured $SaO_2$ via co-oximetry is highly suggestive of **Methemoglobinemia** or Carbon Monoxide poisoning.
  * **[[CBC]]**: Assess hemoglobin levels (rule out severe anemia; identify polycythemia).
* **Diagnostic Imaging**:
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Assess for pneumonia, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, or pulmonary edema.
  * **Echocardiography**: If cardiac etiology or right-to-left shunt is suspected (bubble study to confirm shunt).
* **Specialized Tests**:
  * **Methemoglobin level** (co-oximetry). *Classic "chocolate-brown" color of arterial blood when drawn.*

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Airway & Oxygenation**:
   - Deliver high-flow oxygen via non-rebreather mask, HFNC, or mechanical ventilation.
   - For tension pneumothorax: Immediate needle decompression.
2. **Methemoglobinemia Treatment**:
   - Discontinue the offending drug (e.g., Dapsone).
   - If methemoglobin level $>20-30\%$ or patient is symptomatic: Administer **Methylene Blue** $1-2$ mg/kg IV over 5 minutes. *Methylene blue acts as an electron acceptor to reduce methemoglobin back to hemoglobin.*
   - **CONTRAINDICATION**: Avoid Methylene Blue in patients with **G6PD deficiency** (can cause severe acute hemolytic anemia). Treat G6PD patients with high-dose ascorbic acid (Vitamin C).
3. **Cardiogenic / Septic Shock**:
   - Resuscitate with fluids, vasopressors, or inotropes to restore peripheral tissue perfusion.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Chocolate Blood Test**: In patients with drug-induced **Methemoglobinemia**, the arterial blood drawn for ABG appears characteristically **chocolate-brown** in color and does not turn bright red when exposed to $100\%$ oxygen.
> * **The Anemia Paradox**: A severely anemic patient (e.g., $Hb = 5$ g/dL) who is critically hypoxic will appear pale but **not** cyanotic because they do not have enough total hemoglobin to produce the $5$ g/dL of deoxygenated hemoglobin needed to cause the blue color.
> * **Eisenmenger's Syndrome**: Reversal of a long-standing left-to-right shunt (VSD, ASD, PDA) to a right-to-left shunt due to progressive pulmonary hypertension. Presents with central cyanosis and severe clubbing in a young adult.
