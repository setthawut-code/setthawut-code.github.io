# 🫁 Dyspnea (เหนื่อยง่าย / หายใจลำบาก)

> "Dyspnea is the subjective sensation of breathing discomfort that consists of qualitatively distinct sensations that vary in intensity."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach (3 Steps):**
> 1. **Triage (ABCDE)**: Look for accessory muscle use, stridor, tripod position, or altered mental status. Determine if emergency intubation, NIV, needle decompression for [[Pneumothorax]], or IM Epinephrine for [[Anaphylaxis]] is needed.
> 2. **Temporal Pattern**: Is it *Acute* (sudden onset - minutes/hours) or *Chronic* (progression - weeks/months)?
> 3. **System Localization**: Is it **Pulmonary**, **Cardiovascular**, **Systemic/Metabolic**, or **Neuromuscular**?

---

## 🔍 Definition & Classifications

* **Acute dyspnea**: Developing over hours to days.
* **Chronic dyspnea**: Persisting for $> 4-8$ weeks.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Temporal & Positional Patterns
> [!TIP]
> **Position-dependent dyspnea offers strong diagnostic clues:**

| Pattern | Definition | Suspected Diagnosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orthopnea** | Dyspnea when lying flat | [[Heart Failure]], diaphragmatic paralysis, severe COPD |
| **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND)** | Sudden awakening at night gasping for air | Highly specific for [[Heart Failure]] / [[Acute Pulmonary Edema]] |
| **Platypnea** | Dyspnea when upright, relieved by lying flat | Hepatopulmonary syndrome, right-to-left shunts (e.g., PFO) |
| **Trepopnea** | Dyspnea in lateral decubitus, relieved by opposite side | Unilateral lung/pleural disease, unilateral bronchus obstruction |

### 🧩 Associated Symptoms
* Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain $\rightarrow$ [[Community Acquired Pneumonia]].
* Unilateral leg swelling, sudden pleuritic chest pain $\rightarrow$ DVT leading to [[Pulmonary Embolism]].
* Chest pain radiating to left arm/jaw, diaphoresis $\rightarrow$ [[Acute Coronary Syndrome]] (anginal equivalent).
* Weight loss, night sweats, chronic cough $\rightarrow$ [[Tuberculosis]] or malignancy.
* Rash, facial swelling, allergen exposure $\rightarrow$ [[Anaphylaxis]].
* Progressive generalized weakness $\rightarrow$ Neuromuscular etiology (e.g., myasthenia gravis, GBS).

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**:
  * *Tachypnea (RR >20/min)*: Sensitive marker of respiratory distress.
  * *Tachycardia*: Sympathetic drive, hypoxia, or primary arrhythmia.
  * *Hypotension*: Signs of obstructive, cardiogenic, or distributive [[Shock]].
  * *Pulsus Paradoxus* (SBP drop $>10$ mmHg on inspiration): Severe asthma/COPD, cardiac tamponade, massive PE.
* **General Inspection**:
  * *Accessory muscle use / Tripod position / Pursed-lip breathing*: Indication of high work of breathing.
  * *Cyanosis*: Central cyanosis (tongue/lips) indicates severe hypoxemia ($SaO_2 < 85\%$).
  * *Inability to speak in full sentences*: Critical marker of distress.
* **Neck**:
  * *Tracheal Deviation*: Away from affected side in [[Tension Pneumothorax]] or massive effusion; toward side in massive atelectasis.
  * *JVD*: Elevated in [[Heart Failure]], cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, massive PE.
* **Lungs**:
  * *Palpation*: Tactile fremitus increased in consolidation; decreased in effusion/pneumothorax.
  * *Percussion*: Dullness (consolidation, [[Pleural Effusion]]) vs. Hyperresonance ([[Pneumothorax]], emphysema).
  * *Auscultation*: Crackles (pulmonary edema, pneumonia, ILD) vs. Wheezing (asthma, COPD) vs. Decreased/absent breath sounds (effusion, pneumothorax, silent chest).
* **Heart & Extremities**:
  * Displaced PMI, S3/S4 gallop (systolic/diastolic heart failure), new murmurs.
  * Pitting pedal edema (HF, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis).
  * Unilateral calf swelling/tenderness (DVT).
  * Clubbing (bronchogenic carcinoma, ILD, bronchiectasis; *absent in uncomplicated asthma/COPD*).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| System | Common Causes | Dangerous / Can't Miss Causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pulmonary** | • [[Asthma Exacerbation]]<br>• [[COPD Exacerbation]]<br>• [[Community Acquired Pneumonia]]<br>• [[Pleural Effusion]]<br>• Spontaneous [[Pneumothorax]] | • **[[Pulmonary Embolism]]**<br>• **[[Tension Pneumothorax]]**<br>• Acute Upper Airway Obstruction (epiglottitis, foreign body, angioedema) |
| **Cardiovascular** | • [[Heart Failure]]<br>• Arrhythmia (e.g., rapid AF) | • **[[Acute Coronary Syndrome]]** (STEMI / NSTEMI)<br>• **[[Cardiac Tamponade]]** |
| **Systemic / Other** | • [[Anemia]]<br>• Hyperventilation / Panic attack | • **[[Anaphylaxis]]**<br>• **[[Septic Shock]]** (hyperpnea due to metabolic acidosis)<br>• **Myasthenic Crisis** / Guillain-Barré syndrome |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Perform immediate resuscitation and call for help if present:**
> * Stridor, grunting, or severe upper airway swelling
> * Accessory muscle use, tripod position, or gasping
> * Altered mental status (agitation, lethargy, somnolence $\rightarrow$ signs of $CO_2$ narcosis or severe hypoxia)
> * $SpO_2 < 90\%$ on room air
> * Hemodynamic instability / [[Shock]] (systolic BP $<90$ mmHg, cold clammy skin)
> * "Silent chest" in severe distress (no air entry)
> * Asymmetrical chest expansion with unilateral absent breath sounds

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Bedside Tests**:
  * **[[ECG]]**: ST changes, T-wave inversion (ischemia), S1Q3T3 pattern or right axis deviation (PE/right heart strain).
  * **Lung Ultrasound / POCUS**: BLUE protocol to check for lung sliding (rules out pneumothorax), B-lines (pulmonary edema), consolidation, effusion, or RV strain.
* **Laboratory Tests**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Assess for anemia or leukocytosis.
  * **[[ABG]]**: Assess pH, $PaO_2$, $PaCO_2$, $HCO_3^-$, and A-a gradient. Differentiates:
    * *Type 1 (Hypoxemic)*: $PaO_2 < 60$ mmHg, normal/low $PaCO_2$.
    * *Type 2 (Hypercapnic)*: $PaCO_2 > 45$ mmHg.
    * *Metabolic Acidosis*: Low pH, low $HCO_3^-$ with compensatory hyperpnea (Kussmaul).
  * **Troponin**: Rule out MI.
  * **BNP / NT-proBNP**: High negative predictive value to rule out heart failure.
  * **D-dimer**: To rule out PE in patients with low-to-moderate Wells score/PERC.
* **Imaging**:
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Assess for pneumonia, edema, pneumothorax, effusion, hyperinflation.
  * **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**: Confirms [[Pulmonary Embolism]].

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Oxygenation Targets**:
   - Normal target: **$SpO_2$ 92–96%** (nasal cannula, simple mask, non-rebreather, or HFNC).
   - Hypercapnic risk (COPD, obesity-hypoventilation): **$SpO_2$ 88–92%** (to prevent worsening $CO_2$ retention).
   - Use Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure in COPD or cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
2. **Immediate Interventions**:
   - **Tension Pneumothorax**: Emergency needle decompression followed by chest tube.
   - **Anaphylaxis**: IM Epinephrine ($0.3$ mg $1:1000$).
   - **Airway Compromise**: Intubate.
3. **Directed Medical Therapy**:
   - *[[Heart Failure]] / Pulmonary Edema*: IV loop diuretics (Furosemide) + IV vasodilators.
   - *Asthma/COPD exacerbation*: Nebulized SABA/SAMA + systemic corticosteroids + antibiotics (COPD).
   - *Pneumonia*: Empiric antibiotics + hydration.
   - *Pulmonary Embolism*: Therapeutic anticoagulation (e.g., LMWH, UFH); thrombolytics if unstable.
   - *Anemia*: PRBC transfusion if symptomatic or Hb $< 7$ g/dL.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Silent Chest Trap**: A patient in severe distress without wheezing is *worse* than one who is wheezing. Minimal air movement means they are close to respiratory arrest.
> * **Normal CXR with Severe Dyspnea**: Always think **PE**, severe **Metabolic Acidosis** (hyperpnea), **Carbon Monoxide poisoning**, early **Asthma**, or **Anxiety/Panic attack**.
> * **CO2 Retention Mechanism**: Over-oxygenating a COPD patient causes $CO_2$ retention mainly via **reversal of hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction** (worsening $V/Q$ mismatch) and the **Haldane effect**, not just a decrease in respiratory drive.
> * **Dyspnea is Subjective**: Never ignore a patient complaining of dyspnea even if vital signs and $SpO_2$ are normal.
