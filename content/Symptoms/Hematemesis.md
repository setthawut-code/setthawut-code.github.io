# 🩸 Hematemesis (อาเจียนเป็นเลือด)

> "Hematemesis indicates active Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (UGIB) originating proximal to the ligament of Treitz. Stabilizing the airway and circulation precedes diagnostic endoscopy."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Airway Protection First**: Massive hematemesis carries a high risk of aspiration. Intubate early if altered mental status or ongoing massive hematemesis.
> 2. **Establish Large-Bore IV Access**: Insert two 14-16G peripheral catheters. Start crystalloid resuscitation.
> 3. **Variceal vs. Non-variceal**:
>    - *Variceal (Cirrhosis history)*: **Give IV Octreotide + IV Prophylactic Antibiotic (Ceftriaxone)** immediately.
>    - *Non-variceal (PUD/NSAID history)*: **Give IV High-dose PPI** (Esomeprazole/Pantoprazole).
> 4. **Target Transfusion**: Restrictive target of **$Hb > 7$ g/dL** (except in active ischemic CAD where target is $>8$ g/dL). Over-transfusion in variceal bleed increases portal pressure and triggers rebleeding.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Hematemesis**: Vomiting of bright red blood or "coffee-ground" material (representing blood altered by gastric acid to form acid hematin).
* **Upper GI Bleed Boundary**: Anatomically defined by the **Ligament of Treitz** (suspensory muscle of the duodenum).
* **BUN-to-Creatinine Ratio**: Typically elevated ($>30:1$) in UGIB due to absorption of blood proteins in the small intestine (renal hypoperfusion also contributes).

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Bleeding Presentation**:
  * *Retching/vomiting repeatedly BEFORE the onset of hematemesis* $\rightarrow$ **Mallory-Weiss Tear** (mucosal tear at the gastroesophageal junction).
  * *Painless massive bright red vomiting* $\rightarrow$ Esophageal/gastric varices.
  * *Epigastric pain, history of PUD, or chronic NSAID/Aspirin use* $\rightarrow$ Peptic Ulcer Disease.
* **Risk Factors**:
  * Alcohol abuse, history of hepatitis B/C $\rightarrow$ [[Cirrhosis]] / portal hypertension.
  * Weight loss, early satiety, smoking $\rightarrow$ Gastric cancer.

## 📋 Clinical Risk Stratification: Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS)

Used in the ER/ward to identify low-risk patients who can be safely managed as outpatients without early endoscopy:

### 📊 Glasgow-Blatchford Score (GBS) Criteria

| Parameter | Finding | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BUN** | $6.5-7.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{2}$ \| $8.0-9.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{3}$ \| $10.0-24.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{4}$ \| $\ge 25.0 \rightarrow \mathbf{6}$ | Up to $6$ |
| **Hemoglobin** | *Men*: $12.0-12.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{1}$ \| $10.0-11.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{3}$ \| $<10.0 \rightarrow \mathbf{6}$<br>*Women*: $10.0-11.9 \rightarrow \mathbf{1}$ \| $<10.0 \rightarrow \mathbf{6}$ | Up to $6$ |
| **Systolic BP** | $100-109 \rightarrow \mathbf{1}$ \| $90-99 \rightarrow \mathbf{3}$ \| $<90 \rightarrow \mathbf{5}$ | Up to $5$ |
| **Other Markers**| HR $\ge 100$ bpm $\rightarrow \mathbf{1}$ \| Presenting with Melena $\rightarrow \mathbf{1}$ \| Syncope $\rightarrow \mathbf{2}$ | Up to $4$ |
| **Comorbidities**| Hepatic disease $\rightarrow \mathbf{2}$ \| Heart failure $\rightarrow \mathbf{2}$ | Up to $4$ |

* **Score = 0**: Extremely low risk. Eligible for outpatient discharge.
* **Score $\ge 1$**: Requires hospital admission and inpatient endoscopy.

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Hemodynamics**: Assess for orthostatic changes (early volume loss) or resting tachycardia/hypotension (suggests $>15-30\%$ volume loss $\rightarrow$ [[Shock]]).
* **Stigmata of Portal Hypertension / Cirrhosis**: Spider nevi, palmar erythema, ascites, caput medusae, splenomegaly, jaundice.
* **Abdominal**: Epigastric tenderness, palpable masses.
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Essential to check for **melena** (black, tarry stools, confirming upper tract transit of blood).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Origin | Common / Non-Emergent | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variceal** | None | • **Esophageal Varices** (ruptured)<br>• **Gastric Varices** / Portal hypertensive gastropathy |
| **Non-Variceal** | • Mallory-Weiss tear (usually self-limiting)<br>• Erosive gastritis / Duodenitis<br>• Gastric antral vascular ectasia | • **Peptic Ulcer Disease** (bleeding gastroduodenal artery)<br>• **Aortoenteric Fistula** (in patients with previous aortic graft surgery)<br>• **Dieulafoy Lesion** (dilated submucosal arteriole)<br>• Gastric Adenocarcinoma |

---

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate resuscitation and emergency endoscopy/surgery alert if present:**
> * Hemodynamic instability / [[Shock]] unresponsive to initial fluid boluses.
> * Ongoing active vomiting of bright red blood.
> * Altered consciousness with loss of airway protective reflexes (aspiration risk).
> * Known history of previous aortic graft surgery (suspect aortoenteric fistula).
> * Refractory bleeding despite primary endoscopic intervention.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **CBC**: Check baseline hemoglobin (note: in acute bleeding, Hb may initially be normal until hemodilution occurs).
  * **BUN & Creatinine**: Look for elevated BUN-to-creatinine ratio ($>30:1$).
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT)**: Check for coagulopathy (especially in cirrhotic or anticoagulated patients).
  * **Type & Crossmatch**: Prepare $\ge 2-4$ units of PRBC.
* **Diagnostic Standard**:
  * **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)**: Should be performed within **24 hours** of presentation (within 12 hours for suspected variceal bleeding). Both diagnostic and therapeutic (clips, epinephrine injection, band ligation).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

### 1. General Resuscitation
- Two large-bore IVs. Fast infusion of crystalloids. Target MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg.
- Transfusion target: **$Hb \ge 7$ g/dL** (restrictive policy). *In variceal bleed, target is strictly $7-8$ g/dL; over-transfusion increases portal pressure and mortality.* Correct INR to $<2.5$ and platelets to $>50,000/\mu\text{L}$ in active bleed.

### 2. Pharmacotherapy
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPI)**: Start IV bolus (Esomeprazole 80 mg) followed by continuous infusion (8 mg/hr) or 40 mg IV BID. Reduces gastric acidity, stabilizing clot formation.
- **Vasoactive therapy (Variceal suspect)**: **Octreotide** (50 mcg IV bolus, then 50 mcg/hr infusion) or Terlipressin. Causes splanchnic vasoconstriction, lowering portal pressure.
- **Prophylactic Antibiotics (Variceal)**: **Ceftriaxone** 1 g IV daily for 7 days. Reduces bacterial translocation, infectious risk, and mortality.

### 3. Endoscopic / Interventional Therapy
- *Variceal*: Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL / banding) first-line.
- *Non-variceal*: Dual endoscopic therapy (e.g., Epinephrine injection + thermal coagulation or hemoclip).
- *Refractory*: TIPS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt) for variceal; Angiographic embolization or surgery for non-variceal.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **BUN/Creatinine Ratio Key**: In a patient presenting with anemia or syncope of unknown origin, an elevated BUN/Creatinine ratio $>30:1$ in the absence of renal failure is a strong clue pointing to occult upper GI bleeding.
> * **Prophylactic Antibiotics Rule**: Every cirrhotic patient with gastrointestinal bleeding must receive prophylactic antibiotics (Ceftriaxone). Infection is a major trigger for variceal bleeding and worsens survival.
> * **Aortoenteric Fistula**: Any patient with a history of an abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) repair who presents with a "herald" self-limiting gastrointestinal bleed followed by massive hematemesis has an aortoenteric fistula until proven otherwise. Do not wait; consult vascular surgery immediately.
