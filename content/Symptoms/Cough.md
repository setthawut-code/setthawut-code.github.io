# 🫁 Cough (ไอ)

> "Cough is a vital protective airway reflex. The diagnostic approach is structured around duration: acute (<3 weeks), subacute (3–8 weeks), or chronic (>8 weeks)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Establish Temporal Pattern**:
>    - *Acute* ($<3$ weeks): Usually viral URI, acute bronchitis, or pneumonia.
>    - *Subacute* ($3-8$ weeks): Post-infectious cough (e.g., Pertussis, mycoplasma).
>    - *Chronic* ($>8$ weeks): **Rule out the "Chronic Cough Triad"** (UACS, Asthma, GERD) if chest X-ray is normal.
> 2. **Check Medication List**: Stop **ACE inhibitors** immediately (common cause of dry cough).
> 3. **Rule Out TB**: In endemic areas, any cough $>2$ weeks with weight loss/fever requires sputum AFB smears.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Acute Cough**: $<3$ weeks.
* **Subacute Cough**: $3-8$ weeks.
* **Chronic Cough**: $>8$ weeks.
* **Physiology**: Triggered by mechanical or chemical stimulation of cough receptors (vagal afferents) in the larynx, trachea, and large bronchi.

---

## 🗣️ Key History & The Chronic Cough Triad

If a non-smoking patient with a normal chest X-ray has a chronic cough, and is not taking an ACE inhibitor, $>90\%$ of cases are caused by:

### 📊 The Chronic Cough Triad Table

| Cause | Key Historical Clues | Bedside Diagnostic Trials |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS)** (Post-nasal drip) | Nasal discharge, throat-clearing sensation. Cobblestoning of posterior pharynx on exam. | Antihistamine-decongestant trial. |
| **Cough Variant Asthma** | Nocturnal cough, triggered by exercise, cold air, or allergens. No classic wheezing. | Inhaled SABA (Albuterol) or ICS trial. |
| **GERD (Gastroesophageal Reflux)** | Heartburn, acid regurgitation, cough worsens when lying flat or after meals. | High-dose Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) trial for $4-8$ weeks. |

* **Drug-Induced Cough**: **ACE Inhibitors** (e.g., Enalapril) cause a dry, hacking cough in up to $15\%$ of patients due to accumulation of bradykinin/substance P. *The cough can start weeks to months after drug initiation and takes $1-4$ weeks to resolve after discontinuation.*

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **HEENT**: Nasal mucosa (pale/boggy in allergies), **"Cobblestoning"** of the posterior pharyngeal wall (UACS/post-nasal drip).
* **Chest & Lungs**:
  * *Expiratory Wheeze* $\rightarrow$ Asthma, COPD.
  * *Coarse Crackles* $\rightarrow$ Pneumonia, bronchiectasis, heart failure.
  * *Fine, dry "velcro" crackles* $\rightarrow$ Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD).
  * *Decreased/absent breath sounds* $\rightarrow$ Pleural effusion.
* **Cardiovascular**: Elevated JVP, S3 gallop, pedal edema $\rightarrow$ Congestive heart failure (causing pulmonary congestion).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Duration | Common / Benign | Dangerous / Can't Miss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute** (<3 weeks) | • Viral Upper Respiratory Infection (URI)<br>• Acute Bronchitis<br>• Environmental irritants | • **[[Community Acquired Pneumonia]]** (CAP)<br>• **Acute Pulmonary Edema** / Heart Failure<br>• Pulmonary Embolism (PE) |
| **Subacute** (3-8 weeks) | • **Post-infectious cough** (hyperreactive airway)<br>• Pertussis (whooping cough) | • Exacerbation of undiagnosed underlying lung disease |
| **Chronic** (>8 weeks) | • **Upper Airway Cough Syndrome** (post-nasal drip)<br>• **Cough Variant Asthma**<br>• **GERD**<br>• ACE-inhibitor induced cough | • **[[Tuberculosis]]** (especially if endemic)<br>• **Bronchogenic Carcinoma** (lung cancer)<br>• Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)<br>• Bronchiectasis |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent Chest CT and specialist evaluation required if present:**
> * Hemoptysis (coughing blood).
> * Chronic cough in a heavy smoker or patient $>45$ years with a new-onset pattern.
> * Constitutional symptoms: Unexplained weight loss, drenching night sweats, chronic fever.
> * Progressive dyspnea on exertion or at rest.
> * Dysphonia / hoarseness of voice (laryngeal nerve compression by mediastinal tumor).
> * Unilateral crackles or localized wheeze (rules out generalized asthma, suggests endobronchial obstruction).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]] (CXR)**: Mandatory for any chronic cough ($>8$ weeks) to rule out parenchymal disease (TB, pneumonia), lung cancer, or pleural effusion.
  * **Sputum AFB Smear & GeneXpert**: If TB is clinically suspected (cough $>2$ weeks + B symptoms in endemic region).
* **Diagnostic Modalities (for normal CXR chronic cough)**:
  * **Spirometry & Bronchodilator Reversibility**: Diagnose asthma (FEV1 increases by $\ge 12\%$ and $\ge 200$ mL post-SABA).
  * **Methacholine Challenge Test**: High negative predictive value to rule out asthma.
  * **High-Resolution CT (HRCT) of Chest**: If ILD or bronchiectasis is suspected.
  * **Barium swallow / 24-hr pH monitoring**: For refractory suspected GERD.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Etiology-Directed Treatment**:
   - *UACS*: First-generation antihistamine (Chlorpheniramine) + decongestant (Pseudoephedrine) or nasal steroids.
   - *Asthma*: Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) + Long-Acting Beta-Agonists (LABA).
   - *GERD*: PPI (Omeprazole/Esomeprazole) + lifestyle modifications (elevate head of bed, avoid eating within 3 hours of sleep).
   - *ACEI Cough*: Switch to an Angiotensin Receptor Blocker (ARB) (does not affect bradykinin).
2. **Infectious (CAP)**:
   - Empiric antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin or Macrolide/Doxycycline for atypical coverage).
3. **Symptomatic Cough Suppressants**:
   - Dextromethorphan or Codeine. *Avoid in productive coughs (retaining secretions worsens infection).*

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The ACE Inhibitor Cough Switch**: Swapping an ACE inhibitor to another drug of the same class (like another ACEI) will not help, as the cough is a class effect. Switch to an **ARB (e.g., Losartan, Valsartan)**. The cough may take up to 4 weeks to fully resolve after stopping the ACEI.
> * **GERD Without Heartburn**: Up to $75\%$ of patients with chronic cough caused by GERD have **"silent GERD"** (no classic heartburn or acid regurgitation). The cough is triggered by micro-aspiration of acid or a vagally mediated esophageal-tracheobronchial reflex.
> * **Pertussis in Adults**: Post-infectious subacute cough in adults presenting as paroxysmal coughing fits followed by vomiting (post-tussive emesis) is classically **Pertussis** (Whooping Cough), due to waning vaccine immunity.
