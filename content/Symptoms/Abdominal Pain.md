# 🤢 Abdominal Pain (ปวดท้อง)

> "In acute abdominal pain, the primary objective is to differentiate conditions requiring emergent surgery from those managed medically."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Assess for Peritonitis**: Check for involuntary guarding, rigidity, and rebound tenderness.
> 2. **Surgical vs. Medical**: Determine if the patient has a "surgical abdomen" (perforation, obstruction, peritonitis, hemorrhage).
> 3. **Rule Out Mimics**: Always consider extra-abdominal causes (e.g., inferior wall MI, DKA, pneumonia).
> 4. **Always perform a pregnancy test (Urine hCG)** in females of childbearing age presenting with abdominal pain.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Visceral Pain**:
  * Caused by stretching, distension, or contraction of hollow organs.
  * Felt in the midline due to bilateral symmetric innervation.
  * *Foregut* (stomach to second part of duodenum) $\rightarrow$ Epigastric pain.
  * *Midgut* (third part of duodenum to proximal 2/3 of transverse colon) $\rightarrow$ Periumbilical pain.
  * *Hindgut* (distal 1/3 of transverse colon to rectum) $\rightarrow$ Suprapubic pain.
* **Somatic (Parietal) Pain**:
  * Caused by inflammation/irritation of the parietal peritoneum.
  * Sharp, intense, localized to the site of the stimulus. Worsens with coughing or movement.
* **Referred Pain**:
  * Felt at a site distant from the diseased organ due to shared neurosegments (e.g., right shoulder pain in cholecystitis via the phrenic nerve C3-C5).

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Anatomical Quadrants & Diagnoses
> [!TIP]
> **Use the location of pain to localize the organ of origin:**

| Quadrant | Common Etiologies | Can't Miss Diagnoses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RUQ** | Biliary colic, [[Acute Cholecystitis]], hepatitis, liver abscess | [[Acute Cholangitis]], subphrenic abscess |
| **Epigastric** | PUD, GERD, gastroparesis | Acute Pancreatitis, [[Acute Coronary Syndrome]] (inferior MI), Ruptured AAA |
| **LUQ** | Gastritis, splenic infarction, splenomegaly | Ruptured spleen, splenic abscess |
| **RLQ** | [[Appendicitis]], cecal diverticulitis, mesenteric adenitis | Ectopic pregnancy rupture, Ovarian torsion, Ileocecal intussusception |
| **LLQ** | Diverticulitis, colitis | Ectopic pregnancy, Ovarian torsion, Ischemic colitis |
| **Suprapubic** | Cystitis, pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), dysmenorrhea | Ruptured ectopic pregnancy, Uterine perforation |
| **Diffuse** | Gastroenteritis, IBS, constipation | Peritonitis, Bowel Obstruction, Mesenteric Ischemia, DKA |

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **General**: Look for patient position. Patients with peritonitis lie completely still (movement hurts); patients with renal colic writhe in pain and cannot find a comfortable position.
* **Vital Signs**: Tachycardia and hypotension indicate [[Shock]] (septic, hemorrhagic, or neurogenic). High fever indicates infection.
* **Abdominal Inspection**: Scars, distension, hernia orifices, Cullen's sign (periumbilical ecchymosis) or Grey Turner's sign (flank ecchymosis) in severe retroperitoneal hemorrhage (pancreatitis, ruptured AAA).
* **Auscultation**: Absent bowel sounds $\rightarrow$ paralytic ileus or peritonitis; high-pitched hyperactive "tinkling" sounds $\rightarrow$ mechanical [[Bowel Obstruction]].
* **Palpation**:
  * *Involuntary Guarding*: Reflex spasm of abdominal wall muscles during palpation (cannot be voluntarily relaxed).
  * *Rigidity*: Board-like abdomen representing diffuse parietal peritoneal irritation.
  * *Rebound Tenderness*: Increased pain when the examiner's hand is released suddenly.
* **Special Signs**:
  * **Murphy's Sign**: Pain and inspiratory arrest on deep palpation of RUQ during inspiration $\rightarrow$ [[Acute Cholecystitis]].
  * **McBurney's Point Tenderness**: Maximal tenderness 1/3 of the distance from the ASIS to the umbilicus $\rightarrow$ [[Appendicitis]].
  * **Rovsing's Sign**: Palpation of LLQ causes pain in the RLQ $\rightarrow$ [[Appendicitis]].
  * **Psoas Sign**: RLQ pain with extension of the right hip $\rightarrow$ Retrocecal [[Appendicitis]].
  * **Obturator Sign**: RLQ pain with internal rotation of the flexed right hip $\rightarrow$ Pelvic [[Appendicitis]].

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate surgical/emergency consultation if present:**
> * Rigidity, guarding, or rebound tenderness (Peritoneal signs)
> * Hemodynamic instability / [[Shock]] (SBP $<90$ mmHg, tachycardia, cold extremities)
> * Pulsatile abdominal mass (suspected AAA)
> * Sudden onset of "worst abdominal pain of life" (perforation or vascular catastrophe)
> * High fever with jaundice and altered mental status ([[Acute Cholangitis]])
> * Severe abdominal distension with vomiting and inability to pass flatus or stool
> * Gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis, melena, or hematochezia)

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Laboratory Workup**:
  * **CBC**: Leukocytosis with left shift (inflammation/infection), drop in hemoglobin (active bleeding).
  * **Amylase & Lipase**: Lipase is more sensitive and specific for Acute Pancreatitis (diagnostic if $>3\times$ upper limit of normal).
  * **LFTs & Coagulation**: Bilirubin, ALP, AST, ALT, PT/INR to evaluate hepatobiliary etiology.
  * **Urinalysis**: Pyuria/bacteriuria (UTI/pyelonephritis), hematuria (nephrolithiasis).
  * **Urine hCG**: Mandated in all reproductive-age females.
  * **Lactate & ABG**: Elevated lactate suggests tissue ischemia (e.g., Mesenteric Ischemia, severe sepsis).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Erect Chest X-ray**: Look for pneumoperitoneum (free air under the diaphragm) indicating bowel perforation.
  * **Abdominal Ultrasound**: First-line for RUQ (gallstones, cholecystitis), pelvic pain (ovarian/uterine origin), and suspected appendicitis in children/pregnant patients.
  * **CT Abdomen & Pelvis (with IV contrast)**: Gold standard for most acute abdominal pain etiologies (diverticulitis, appendicitis, bowel obstruction, pancreatitis, bowel ischemia).

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Keep Nil Per Os (NPO)**: Restrict oral intake until a surgical cause is ruled out or definitive plan is made.
2. **Fluid Resuscitation**: Provide IV crystalloids (NSS or Acetar/LR) based on hydration status and markers of tissue perfusion.
3. **Nasogastric (NG) Tube Decompression**: Indicated for patients with persistent vomiting, severe abdominal distension, or suspected bowel obstruction.
4. **Analgesia**: *Myth debunked:* Do not withhold analgesics (e.g., IV opioids) from patients with acute abdominal pain; adequate pain control does not mask physical findings or delay diagnosis.
5. **Empiric Antibiotics**: Start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g., Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole, or Piperacillin/Tazobactam) if peritonitis, sepsis, cholangitis, or intra-abdominal abscess is suspected.
6. **Early Surgical Consultation**: Call surgery team immediately for suspected perforation, obstruction, ischemic bowel, or hemodynamically unstable AAA.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Pain Out of Proportion**: Severe, excruciating abdominal pain in a patient with a relatively soft, non-tender abdominal exam is the classic hallmark of **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**, especially in patients with a history of atrial fibrillation or vascular disease.
> * **The Silent Perforation**: Elderly patients or those on systemic corticosteroids may present with bowel perforation but show minimal fever, leukocytosis, or classic peritoneal signs. A high index of suspicion is required.
> * **Appendicitis Pain Migration**: The classic migration of pain from the periumbilical region (visceral pain, T10 dermatome) to the right lower quadrant (somatic pain, localized peritoneal inflammation) occurs over $12-24$ hours.
> * **Extra-Abdominal Mimics**: Always order an ECG in elderly or diabetic patients presenting with isolated epigastric pain to rule out an inferior myocardial infarction.
