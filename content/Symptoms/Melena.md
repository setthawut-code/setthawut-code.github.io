# 💩 Melena (อุจจาระดำ)

> "Melena is the passage of black, tarry, foul-smelling stools. It is the classic presentation of Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding (UGIB) degraded by gastric acid and intestinal bacteria."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **True Melena vs. Mimics**: Check the stool character. True melena is **black, sticky/tarry, and has a distinct foul odor**. Iron supplements, bismuth, and charcoal cause dark stools but they are **non-sticky and odorless**.
> 2. **Rule Out Shock**: Check for resting tachycardia, hypotension, or orthostatic dizziness.
> 3. **BUN/Creatinine Clue**: An elevated BUN-to-creatinine ratio **$>30:1$** strongly points to an upper GI source of blood.
> 4. **Diagnostic Endoscopy**: Schedule an Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD) within 24 hours.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Melena**: Stool blackened by the oxidation of iron in hemoglobin (forming hematin) by gastric acid and intestinal bacteria.
* **Volume & Transit Requirements**:
  * Typically requires at least **$50-100$ mL** of blood in the upper tract.
  * Blood must remain in the GI tract for at least **$10-14$ hours** to be degraded into melena.
* **Anatomical Origin**: Usually proximal to the ligament of Treitz, but can originate from the cecum or right colon if transit time is slow.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ True Melena vs. Mimics Table

| Feature | True Melena | Mimics (Iron, Bismuth, Charcoal) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Color** | Shiny, jet-black | Dull black, dark green, or grey |
| **Consistency** | **Sticky, tarry, paste-like** | Normal paste or dry/hard |
| **Odor** | **Extremely foul, characteristic** | Normal stool odor |
| **Fecal Occult Blood** | **Positive** | **Negative** (unless concurrent bleed) |

---

### ⏳ Clinical Risk Clues
* **Gastroduodenal Acid History**: Epigastric pain, history of H. pylori, or heavy use of NSAIDs/Aspirin $\rightarrow$ Peptic Ulcer Disease.
* **Alcohol / Liver Disease History**: Jaundice, ascites, history of varices $\rightarrow$ Variceal hemorrhage.
* **Retching**: History of violent vomiting followed by melena $\rightarrow$ Mallory-Weiss tear.
* **Diet & Meds**: Direct questioning about iron supplements, Pepto-Bismol (bismuth), activated charcoal, or eating large amounts of black licorice/blueberries.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**: Assess for hypovolemic [[Shock]]. A pulse $>100$ bpm or SBP $<90$ mmHg indicates massive bleeding ($>20-30\%$ volume loss).
* **General & Skin**: Check for conjunctival pallor (anemia), jaundice, spider angiomas, palmar erythema (cirrhosis).
* **Abdomen**: Epigastric tenderness, palpable masses, or ascites.
* **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) (Mandatory)**:
  * Inspect the finger glove under direct light to confirm the presence of black, sticky, tarry material.
  * Perform a fecal occult blood test if stool color is borderline.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Origin | Common / Non-Emergent | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper GI** (Proximal to Treitz) | • Peptic Ulcer Disease (gastric/duodenal)<br>• Mallory-Weiss tear<br>• Gastritis / Esophagitis | • **Esophageal / Gastric Varices** (ruptured)<br>• **Aortoenteric Fistula** (vascular emergency)<br>• **Dieulafoy Lesion**<br>• Gastric cancer |
| **Lower GI / Small Bowel** | • Meckel's Diverticulum (painless melena in young adult) | • Right-sided Colon Cancer (slow transit allows melena formation)<br>• Small bowel angiodysplasia / tumor |
| **Extra-GI Mimics** | • Swallowed blood (epistaxis, oral trauma) | None |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent resuscitation and immediate GI endoscopy referral required if present:**
> * Hemodynamic instability (SBP $<90$ mmHg, tachycardia, cold extremities) or syncope.
> * Melena associated with active vomiting of bright red blood ([[Hematemesis]]).
> * Melena in a patient with a history of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) repair (suspect aortoenteric fistula).
> * Severe respiratory distress or active ischemic chest pain.
> * Signs of advanced cirrhosis with altered mental status.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screen**:
  * **[[CBC]]**: Baseline hematocrit/hemoglobin (may be normal in first few hours of acute bleed before hemodilution).
  * **BUN & Creatinine**: Classically shows **BUN/Cr ratio $>30:1$** due to blood breakdown products being absorbed in the small intestine.
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT)**: Assess for coagulopathy (especially in cirrhotic/warfarinized patients).
  * **Type & Crossmatch**: Prepare blood products.
* **Diagnostic Standard**:
  * **Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD)**: Perform within **24 hours** (or $<12$ hours if suspected variceal bleeding) to localize and treat the source.
  * **Colonoscopy**: Indicated if EGD is negative and patient continues to pass dark/bloody stools to check for right-sided colon pathology.
  * **CT Angiography / Tagged RBC scan**: If both scopes are negative but patient continues to bleed actively.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Vascular Resuscitation**:
   - Establish two large-bore IVs. Infuse crystalloids.
   - Transfusion target: **$Hb \ge 7$ g/dL** (restrictive strategy). *Avoid over-transfusion in variceal bleed ($>8$ g/dL) to prevent portal pressure surge.*
2. **Peptic Ulcer Disease (Suspected Non-Variceal)**:
   - Give IV PPI (Esomeprazole/Pantoprazole 80 mg bolus, then 8 mg/hr infusion or 40 mg IV BID).
   - Endoscopic therapy: Hemoclips, thermal coagulation, or epinephrine injection for high-risk ulcers (Forrest Class Ia, Ib, IIa, IIb).
3. **Variceal Hemorrhage (Suspected)**:
   - Give **IV Octreotide** (50 mcg bolus, then 50 mcg/hr infusion).
   - Give **IV Ceftriaxone** 1 g daily for infection prophylaxis.
   - Plan urgent EGD for band ligation.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **BUN/Cr Diagnostic Utility**: In a stable patient presenting with dark stools, a BUN/Cr ratio of $>30:1$ has a high positive predictive value for an upper GI bleed, whereas a ratio of $<20:1$ suggests a lower GI bleed.
> * **The Iron Mimic**: Always ask a patient who complains of "black stools" if they are taking iron supplements. Iron changes stool color to a greenish-black but does **not** cause the sticky, tarry consistency or the characteristic foul smell of true melena.
> * **Right-Sided Colon Cancer**: While colon cancer classically causes hematochezia or occult blood, a tumor in the cecum or ascending colon can present as melena if the transit time is slow enough to allow mucosal blood degradation.
