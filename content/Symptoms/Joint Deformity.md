# 🦴 Joint Deformity (ข้อผิดรูป)

> "Joint deformity represents chronic structural alteration of a joint due to bone, cartilage, or ligamentous destruction. The diagnostic path contrasts osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate RA vs. OA Hand Deformities**:
>    - *Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)*: Symmetric involvement of **MCP and PIP joints** (spares DIPs). Ulnar deviation, Swan-neck, Boutonniere deformities. Soft, boggy joint swelling.
>    - *Osteoarthritis (OA)*: Asymmetric involvement of **DIP and PIP joints** (spares MCPs). Hard, bony nodes (**Heberden's and Bouchard's nodes**).
> 2. **Correctable vs. Fixed**: Determine if the deformity can be manually corrected (suggests tendon laxity, e.g., Jaccoud's in SLE) or is fixed/ankylosed.
> 3. **Assess Functional Impairment**: Check for severe limitations in daily activities (gripping, writing).

---

## 🔍 Pathophysiology of Deformity

Joint deformities occur via three primary pathways:
1. **Inflammatory Pannus Destruction**: Proliferation of synovial tissue (pannus) in **Rheumatoid Arthritis** erodes cartilage, bone, and adjacent ligaments, causing subluxation and tendon rupture.
2. **Mechanical Mechanical Wear & Repair**: Loss of articular cartilage in **Osteoarthritis** leads to bone-on-bone contact, subchondral bone remodeling, and new bone formation (**osteophytes**).
3. **Periarticular Urate Deposition**: Massive uric acid crystal deposition (**Tophi**) in chronic gout destroys bone and joint architecture.
4. **Ligamentous Laxity**: Chronic tendon/ligament inflammation without bone erosion causes correctable subluxation (**Jaccoud's Arthropathy** in SLE).

## 🩺 Physical Examination & Hand Comparison

Evaluate hand joints systematically, as they provide the clearest diagnostic clues:

### 📊 RA vs. OA Hand Deformity Comparison Table

| Feature | Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) | Osteoarthritis (OA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Symmetry** | **Symmetric** bilateral | Asymmetric |
| **DIP Joints** | **Spared** | **Heberden's Nodes** (bony osteophytes) |
| **PIP Joints** | **Boutonniere & Swan-neck** (soft swelling) | **Bouchard's Nodes** (bony osteophytes) |
| **MCP Joints** | **Ulnar Deviation**, subluxation | Spared |
| **Wrist** | Radial deviation, "piano-key" ulnar head | Spared |
| **Joint Feel** | **Soft, boggy, spongy** (active synovitis) | **Hard, bony** (osteophytes) |

* **Classic RA Deformities Defined**:
  * **Swan-Neck**: Hyperextension of PIP joint with flexion of DIP joint.
  * **Boutonniere**: Flexion of PIP joint with hyperextension of DIP joint.
  * **Ulnar Deviation**: Subluxation of MCP joints causing fingers to drift toward the ulnar side.
  * **Piano-key sign**: Hypermobility of the ulnar head due to destruction of the distal radioulnar joint.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Inflammatory / Erosive | Non-Inflammatory / Chronic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rheumatologic** | • **Rheumatoid Arthritis** (chronic erosive)<br>• Psoriatic Arthritis (arthritis mutilans - "telescoping fingers") | • **Jaccoud's Arthropathy** (reversible deformities in SLE)<br>• Chronic Tophaceous Gout (destructive tophi) |
| **Mechanical** | None | • **Primary Osteoarthritis** (nodal)<br>• Neuropathic Joint (Charcot joint in diabetes) |
| **Dangerous / Can't Miss**| • **Atlantoaxial (C1-C2) Subluxation** (RA cervical complication) | • Joint destruction from untreated Septic Arthritis |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent surgical or specialist evaluation required if present:**
> * Rheumatoid arthritis patient with new-onset neck pain, radiating arm pain, or paresthesias/clumsiness (suspect **Atlantoaxial Subluxation** compressing the spinal cord).
> * Acute joint locking (complete inability to flex or extend the joint) due to loose bodies or meniscal displacement.
> * Rapidly progressive joint deformity accompanied by fever and systemic toxicity.
> * Sensory loss or painless joint destruction in a diabetic patient (suspect Charcot neuropathic arthropathy).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Radiographs**:
  * **Plain X-Rays of Hands and Wrists**: The primary diagnostic tool.
    * *RA findings*: Periarticular osteopenia, symmetric joint space narrowing, and marginal bone erosions.
    * *OA findings*: Asymmetric joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, subchondral cysts, and osteophytes.
    * *Psoriatic Arthritis*: **"Pencil-in-cup" deformity** of the DIP joints.
    * *Gout*: **"Punched-out" lytic lesions** with overhanging margins (Rat-bite erosions).
* **Laboratory Panels**:
  * **Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP**: (Anti-CCP is highly specific for aggressive, erosive RA).
  * **ANA**: Screen for SLE (if correctable deformities are present).
  * **Serum Uric Acid**: If chronic tophaceous gout is suspected.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Rheumatoid Arthritis**:
   - Start **DMARDs (Methotrexate, Sulfasalazine, Leflunomide)** immediately upon diagnosis to slow down joint destruction and prevent future deformities.
   - Introduce biologic agents (anti-TNF, anti-IL-6) early if DMARDs fail to achieve remission.
   - Use orthotics, splints, and joint-protection techniques. Surgical options include synovectomy, tendon repair, or total joint arthroplasty (replacement).
2. **Osteoarthritis**:
   - Physical therapy, muscle strengthening (e.g., quadriceps strengthening for knee OA to stabilize joint).
   - Joint replacement (Total Hip/Knee Arthroplasty) for end-stage OA with severe pain and functional limitation.
3. **Jaccoud's Arthropathy (SLE)**:
   - Physical therapy, tendon strengthening, and orthotic splinting. Surgical repair is rarely indicated as bones are not eroded.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Erupting Tophi Mimic**: Gouty tophi can ulcerate and extrude a thick, white, chalky, toothpaste-like substance (monosodium urate crystals). This is frequently misdiagnosed as purulent drainage from septic arthritis or cellulitis. Polarized microscopy is diagnostic.
> * **The Reversible Deformity Test**: To differentiate **Jaccoud's Arthropathy** (SLE) from **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: ask the patient to place their hands flat on a table. Jaccoud's deformities will completely straighten out and disappear; RA deformities are fixed and cannot be flattened.
> * **Cervical Subluxation Warning**: Any patient with longstanding, deforming Rheumatoid Arthritis must have **flexion-extension cervical spine X-rays** before undergoing any surgery requiring general anesthesia. Intubation flexion can displace C1 on C2 and cause fatal spinal cord transection.
