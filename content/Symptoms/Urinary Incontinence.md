# 🚽 Urinary Incontinence (กลั้นปัสสาวะไม่ได้)

> "Urinary incontinence is the involuntary leakage of urine. The clinical approach is based on classifying the type (stress, urge, overflow) and ruling out transient, reversible causes."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Reversible Causes (DIAPPERS)**: Assess for delirium, UTI, medications, or fecal impaction.
> 2. **Differentiate the Big 3 Types**:
>    - *Stress*: Leakage with **coughing, laughing, sneezing, or lifting**. Normal post-void residual (PVR).
>    - *Urge*: Sudden, **uncontrollable urge to void** followed by immediate leakage. Normal PVR.
>    - *Overflow*: **Constant dribbling**, hesitancy, weak stream. **Elevated PVR ($>200$ mL)**.
> 3. **Obtain Post-Void Residual (PVR)** to rule out overflow incontinence due to obstruction.

---

## 🔍 Physiology: Types & Reversible Causes

### 📊 Urinary Incontinence Comparison Table

| Feature | Stress Incontinence | Urge Incontinence | Overflow Incontinence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathophysiology** | Urethral hypermobility or intrinsic sphincter deficiency. | Detrusor muscle overactivity (hyperreflexia). | Detrusor underactivity (atonic bladder) or outlet obstruction. |
| **Triggers** | Coughing, sneezing, laughing, exercise. | Sudden urge, cold water, hearing running water. | None (constant dribbling). |
| **Post-Void Residual**| **Normal** ($<50-100$ mL) | **Normal** ($<50-100$ mL) | **Markedly elevated** ($>200-400$ mL) |
| **First-line Therapy** | Pelvic floor muscle training (Kegel exercises). | Bladder training, Anticholinergics, or Mirabegron. | Catheterization, treat obstruction (BPH medication/surgery). |

---

### ⏳ Reversible Causes: The DIAPPERS Mnemonic
Always screen for these transient triggers before diagnosing chronic incontinence:
* **D** - **Delirium** or acute confusion.
* **I** - **Infection** (symptomatic UTI).
* **A** - **Atrophic urethritis / vaginitis** (postmenopausal estrogen deficiency).
* **P** - **Pharmaceuticals** (Diuretics, Anticholinergics, Calcium Channel Blockers, Alpha-blockers).
* **P** - **Psychological** disorders (e.g., severe depression).
* **E** - **Excessive urine output** (Diabetes Mellitus, hypercalcemia, heart failure fluid mobilization).
* **R** - **Restricted mobility** (unable to reach toilet in time $\rightarrow$ Functional Incontinence).
* **S** - **Stool impaction** (fecal mass compresses bladder neck, causing retention/overflow).

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character of Leakage**:
  * *Leakage only during physical exertion, coughing, or sneezing* $\rightarrow$ **Stress Incontinence**.
  * *Involuntary loss of urine accompanied by or immediately preceded by urgency* $\rightarrow$ **Urge Incontinence**.
  * *Constant dribbling, difficulty initiating stream, weak flow, sensation of incomplete emptying* $\rightarrow$ **Overflow Incontinence**.
* **Medication History**: Diuretics (increased volume), Sedatives (decreased awareness), CCBs (detrusor underactivity), Alpha-blockers (sphincter relaxation).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Abdomen**: Palpate and percuss for a **distended urinary bladder** (highly suggestive of overflow incontinence / urinary retention).
* **Neurological Examination**:
  * Assess sacral nerve roots (**S2-S4**) which control micturition.
  * Check **Perianal Sensation** and **Anal Wink Reflex**.
  * Check **Bulbocavernosus Reflex** (squeezing clitoris/glans penis causes contraction of anal sphincter).
* **Pelvic Examination in Women**: Look for pelvic organ prolapse (cystocele, rectocele, uterine prolapse) and signs of vaginal atrophy (pale, dry mucosa).
* **DRE in Men**: Evaluate for prostate enlargement (**BPH**).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Stress Incontinence | Urge / Overflow Incontinence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anatomical** | • Urethral hypermobility (post-childbirth)<br>• Pelvic organ prolapse (cystocele) | • **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)** (obstruction $\rightarrow$ overflow)<br>• Urethral stricture |
| **Neurological** | None | • **Neurogenic Bladder** (spinal cord injury, MS, neuropathy $\rightarrow$ overflow)<br>• Stroke / Dementia (loss of cortical inhibition $\rightarrow$ urge) |
| **Functional** | None | • Severe osteoarthritis / dementia (unable to reach toilet $\rightarrow$ Functional) |
| **Dangerous / Can't Miss**| None | • **Spinal Cord / Cauda Equina Compression**<br>• Bladder Neck Cancer |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate neurological/neurosurgical evaluation required if present:**
> * Acute onset of urinary incontinence accompanied by **saddle anesthesia** (numbness in perineal region).
> * Incontinence associated with bilateral lower extremity weakness or foot drop.
> * Acute urinary retention with overflow following spinal trauma.
> * Incontinence accompanied by hematuria and pelvic pain (malignancy).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[Urinalysis]]**: Rule out UTI (leukocyte esterase, nitrites) and hematuria.
  * **Renal Function (Creatinine / BUN)**: Assess for renal impairment due to chronic obstructive uropathy.
* **Diagnostic Bedside Standard**:
  * **Post-Void Residual (PVR) Volume**: Measure via bladder ultrasound or straight catheterization immediately after the patient voids.
    * *PVR $<50$ mL*: Normal.
    * *PVR $50-100$ mL*: Borderline.
    * *PVR $>200$ mL*: Abnormal, diagnostic of **Overflow Incontinence** / urinary retention.
* **Specialized Studies**:
  * **Urodynamic Studies**: Evaluates bladder pressure and flow rates; indicated if diagnosis is unclear or surgery is planned.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Stress Incontinence**:
   - **Pelvic Floor Muscle Training (Kegel Exercises)**: First-line. Strengthens levator ani and pelvic floor.
   - Topical estrogen for postmenopausal vaginal atrophy.
   - Surgical: Mid-urethral sling (Tension-free Vaginal Tape / TVT) is highly effective.
2. **Urge Incontinence**:
   - Bladder training (timed voiding) and dietary modifications (avoid caffeine/alcohol).
   - Pharmacotherapy:
     - **Anticholinergics** (Oxybutynin, Solifenacin): Relaxes detrusor. *Side effects: dry mouth, constipation, confusion (avoid in elderly).*
     - **Beta-3 Agonist (Mirabegron)**: Relaxes detrusor without anticholinergic side effects. *Avoid in uncontrolled severe hypertension.*
3. **Overflow Incontinence**:
   - Relieve obstruction: Alpha-blockers (Tamsulosin) or 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (Finasteride) for BPH.
   - Intermittent clean self-catheterization (CIC) or indwelling Foley catheter for atonic bladder (detrusor underactivity).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Elderly Anticholinergic Danger**: Anticholinergics (e.g., Oxybutynin) are highly effective for urge incontinence but are on the **Beers List** of medications to avoid in the elderly. They can cause **acute delirium, worsening dementia, dry mouth, constipation**, and can precipitate **acute urinary retention** or acute glaucoma. Mirabegron is preferred.
> **Fecal Impaction Retention**: A hard, impacted fecal mass in the rectum physically compresses the bladder neck and urethra, causing urinary retention and subsequent overflow incontinence. Always treat constipation in patients with new-onset incontinence.
