# 🦴 Myalgia (ปวดเมื่อยกล้ามเนื้อ)

> "Myalgia is muscle pain. Systemic diffuse myalgia usually indicates infectious, inflammatory, endocrine, or toxic/drug-induced etiologies, whereas localized pain points to trauma."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Rhabdomyolysis**: Look for **severe diffuse muscle pain + tea-colored (dark) urine + oliguria**. **Check Creatine Kinase (CK) immediately.**
> 2. **Differentiate Pain vs. Weakness**: Perform objective muscle strength testing to verify if the patient has muscle pain (myalgia) or actual motor loss (myopathy/weakness).
> 3. **Review Drug List**: Check for statin use, corticosteroids, or colchicine.
> 4. **Infectious Screen**: Acute onset diffuse myalgia + fever $\rightarrow$ Think **[[Dengue]]**, Influenza, Leptospirosis, or Malaria.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Myalgia represents irritation of muscle nociceptors by chemical mediators (prostaglandins, cytokines, lactic acid) or direct mechanical injury.
* **Myositis**: Myalgia accompanied by histologic or laboratory evidence of muscle inflammation (elevated CK).
* **Rhabdomyolysis**: Severe muscle breakdown leading to release of cell contents (myoglobin, potassium, phosphate) into circulation, risking acute tubular necrosis.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Onset & Duration**:
  * *Acute diffuse onset with fever*: Classic for viral infections ([[Dengue]], Influenza) or atypical bacteria ([[Leptospirosis]] $\rightarrow$ characteristically severe calf pain).
  * *Gradual subacute onset*: Endocrine (hypothyroidism), rheumatological (polymyositis), or medication-induced.
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * *Tea-colored (dark brown) urine* $\rightarrow$ Myoglobinuria (Rhabdomyolysis).
  * *Proximal muscle weakness* (difficulty rising, combing hair) $\rightarrow$ Polymyositis / Dermatomyositis.
  * *Dry skin, weight gain, constipation* $\rightarrow$ [[Hypothyroidism]].
* **Medication History (High-Yield)**:
  * **Statins** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors): Can cause a spectrum from mild myalgia to severe rhabdomyolysis. Risk increases with drug interactions (fibrates, macrolides, azole antifungals).
  * **Corticosteroids**: Chronic use causes steroid myopathy (painless proximal weakness/myalgia).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Muscle Palpation & Tenderness**:
  * Assess for localized vs. diffuse muscle tenderness. *Severe calf tenderness is a key sign of Leptospirosis.*
  * Look for muscle swelling or compartment syndrome signs in severe trauma/rhabdomyolysis.
* **Strength Testing**: Verify muscle power (MRC scale $0-5$). Pain can cause poor effort, but try to distinguish from true neurogenic or primary myopathic weakness.
* **Skin Examination (Dermatomyositis clues)**:
  * *Heliotrope rash*: Purplish eruption on the upper eyelids, often with edema.
  * *Gottron's papules*: Violet-colored, flat papules over the dorsal metacarpophalangeal (MCP) or interphalangeal joints.
  * *Shawl sign*: Erythema over the upper chest, neck, and shoulders.

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit immediately for aggressive IV hydration if present:**
> * Tea-colored or dark brown urine.
> * Creatine Kinase (CK) levels **$>5\times$ upper limit of normal** ($>1000$ U/L) or $>5000-10000$ U/L (imminent AKI risk).
> * Muscle pain associated with objective, progressive proximal muscle weakness.
> * Muscle pain with swelling, tense compartments, or severe paresthesias (compartment syndrome).
> * High fever with jaundice, conjunctival suffusion, and oliguria ([[Leptospirosis]]).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Serum Creatine Kinase (CK)**: The most sensitive indicator of muscle injury. Normal is $<200$ U/L.
  * **[[Urinalysis]]**: A positive urine dipstick for "blood" but with **no RBCs** under microscopy indicates **myoglobinuria** (rhabdomyolysis).
  * **Electrolytes & Renal Function (BUN/Cr)**: Check potassium (hyperkalemia from muscle breakdown) and creatinine (AKI).
  * **TSH**: Screen for hypothyroidism.
* **Secondary targeted testing**:
  * **ANA and Myositis-Specific Antibodies** (Jo-1, Mi-2): If polymyositis or dermatomyositis is suspected.
  * **ESR & CRP**: (ESR is markedly elevated in Polymyalgia Rheumatica).
  * **Muscle Biopsy**: Definitive diagnosis for inflammatory myopathies.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Rhabdomyolysis**:
   - **Aggressive IV hydration** with Normal Saline ($200-500$ mL/hour) to maintain urine output $>200-300$ mL/hour. Target is to flush myoglobin from renal tubules.
   - Monitor and correct hyperkalemia immediately (calcium gluconate, insulin/dextrose). *Avoid calcium replacement for hypocalcemia unless symptomatic, as calcium will deposit in damaged muscle and release later, causing rebound hypercalcemia.*
2. **Statin-Induced Myopathy**:
   - Hold the statin. Check CK level. If CK normal, statin can be restarted at a lower dose or switched to a different class after symptoms resolve. If CK $>10\times$ ULN, discontinue statin permanently.
3. **Inflammatory Myopathies (Polymyositis)**:
   - High-dose systemic Corticosteroids (Prednisolone $1$ mg/kg/day) + steroid-sparing agents (Methotrexate, Azathioprine).
4. **Polymyalgia Rheumatica**:
   - Responds dramatically to low-dose Prednisolone ($15-20$ mg/day).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Leptospira Calf Pain**: While many viral infections cause generalized myalgia, severe, exquisite tenderness localized specifically to the **gastrocnemius (calf) muscles** accompanied by conjunctival suffusion (red eyes without discharge) is the classic clinical triad for **Leptospirosis**.
> * **The Dipstick blood vs. RBC discrepancy**: The urine dipstick uses a chemical reaction that cannot differentiate between hemoglobin, myoglobin, or intact RBCs. If the dipstick is strongly positive for "blood" but the lab report shows $0-1$ RBCs on microscopy, you are dealing with either hemolysis (hemoglobinuria) or rhabdomyolysis (myoglobinuria).
> * **Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR) vs. Polymyositis**:
>   - *PMR*: Presents with **pain and stiffness** in the shoulder and pelvic girdles, normal muscle strength, and very high ESR.
>   - *Polymyositis*: Presents with **objective muscle weakness** (proximal), minimal pain, and very high CK.
