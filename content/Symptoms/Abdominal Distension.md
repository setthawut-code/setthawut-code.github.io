# 🤰 Abdominal Distension (ท้องอืด / ท้องโต)

> "Abdominal distension is a common clinical presentation characterized by increased intra-abdominal pressure and visible swelling. The clinical approach focuses on distinguishing gaseous distension, bowel obstruction, and fluid accumulation (ascites)."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Action:**
> 1. **Differentiate Fluid vs. Gas vs. Obstruction**:
>    - *Fluid (Ascites)*: Shifting dullness, fluid wave.
>    - *Gas/Obstruction*: Tympanic percussion, high-pitched or absent bowel sounds, nausea/vomiting, obstipation.
> 2. **Calculate Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient (SAAG)**:
>    - SAAG = Serum Albumin - Ascitic Fluid Albumin.
>    - **SAAG $\ge 1.1$ g/dL**: Portal Hypertension (e.g., Cirrhosis, Heart Failure).
>    - **SAAG $< 1.1$ g/dL**: Non-portal Hypertension (e.g., Peritoneal Carcinomatosis, TB).
> 3. **Assess for SBP (Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis)**: If fever, abdominal pain, or altered consciousness occurs in a cirrhotic patient with ascites, perform diagnostic paracentesis immediately.

---

## 🔍 Pathophysiologic Framework

To remember the major causes of abdominal distension, use the classic **"5 F's" Mnemonic**:

```
Abdominal Distension (5 F's)
└── 1. Fluid (Ascites)
    ├── High SAAG (Portal HTN: Cirrhosis, CHF)
    └── Low SAAG (Peritoneal: Cancer, TB)
└── 2. Flatus (Gaseous distension)
    └── Ileus, Small/Large Bowel Obstruction
└── 3. Feces (Obstipation / Impaction)
    └── Megacolon, Severe Constipation
└── 4. Fat (Adiposity / Obesity)
    └── Increased subcutaneous or visceral fat
└── 5. Fatal Tumor (Intra-abdominal mass)
    └── Ovarian, Gastric, Colorectal malignancy
```

## 📊 SAAG (Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient) Interpretation

The SAAG is the most reliable tool to classify ascites, replacing the older transudate/exudate classification:

$$\text{SAAG} = \text{Serum Albumin} - \text{Ascitic Fluid Albumin}$$

| High SAAG ($\ge 1.1$ g/dL) <br>*(Portal Hypertension present)* | Low SAAG ($< 1.1$ g/dL) <br>*(Portal Hypertension absent)* |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hepatic Cirrhosis** (most common) | **Peritoneal Carcinomatosis** (malignancy) |
| **Heart Failure** (Congestive Heart Failure) | **Tuberculous Peritonitis** |
| **Budd-Chiari Syndrome** (hepatic vein thrombosis) | **Nephrotic Syndrome** |
| Portal Vein Thrombosis | Pancreatic or Biliary Ascites |
| Constrictive Pericarditis | Bowel Obstruction or Infarction |

*Note: In Heart Failure (high SAAG), the Ascitic Total Protein is typically high ($> 2.5$ g/dL), whereas in Hepatic Cirrhosis, the Ascitic Total Protein is low ($< 2.5$ g/dL).*

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Onset and Progression**: Gradual swelling over months (Cirrhosis, tumor) vs. acute distension over hours (Bowel obstruction, mesenteric ischemia).
* **Associated Symptoms**:
  * Vomiting, inability to pass gas or stool ([[Constipation]], obstipation) $\rightarrow$ Bowel obstruction.
  * Fever, abdominal pain, confusion $\rightarrow$ **[[Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis]] (SBP)**.
  * Dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea $\rightarrow$ **Heart Failure** with cardiac ascites.
* **Risk Factors**: Alcohol abuse, chronic hepatitis B/C infection (Cirrhosis risk), family history of malignancy.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Inspection**: Look for abdominal contour, everted umbilicus, and caput medusae (dilated abdominal wall veins radiating from umbilicus $\rightarrow$ Portal Hypertension).
* **Percussion**:
  * *Tympanic*: Gaseous distension (ileus, obstruction).
  * *Dullness in flanks*: Suggests fluid. Perform **Shifting Dullness** test (sensitivity 80%, specificity 90% for detecting ascites $>1500$ mL).
* **Palpation**: Check for fluid wave (for tense ascites) and evaluate for underlying organs or masses ([[Abdominal Mass]]).
* **Auscultation**: Check bowel sounds.
  * *High-pitched, rushing, tinkling*: Mechanical obstruction.
  * *Silent / Absent*: Paralytic ileus or peritonitis.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | High SAAG ($\ge 1.1$ g/dL) | Low SAAG ($< 1.1$ g/dL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal / Hepatic** | • **Hepatic Cirrhosis**<br>• Budd-Chiari Syndrome<br>• Portal vein thrombosis | • **Peritoneal Carcinomatosis**<br>• **Tuberculous Peritonitis**<br>• Pancreatic ascites |
| **Cardiovascular** | • **Congestive Heart Failure**<br>• Constrictive Pericarditis | None |
| **Renal / Other** | • Myxedema | • **Nephrotic Syndrome** (severe [[Edema]]) |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency intervention required if:**
> * Distension with **severe abdominal pain, guarding, and rebound tenderness** (suggests bowel perforation or peritonitis).
> * High fever, hypotension, or altered mental status in a patient with ascites (suggests sepsis/SBP).
> * Feculent vomiting or complete obstipation (indicates strangulated or complete bowel obstruction).
> * Rapidly progressive abdominal distension associated with severe shortness of breath ([[Dyspnea]] / respiratory compromise).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Diagnostic Paracentesis**: Mandatory for all new-onset ascites or suspected SBP.
  * **Cell Count & Differential**: **PMN count $\ge 250$ cells/$\mu$L** is diagnostic of **SBP** (treat immediately with IV Cefotaxime).
  * **Albumin**: To calculate SAAG.
  * **Total Protein**: Helps differentiate cirrhosis ($<2.5$ g/dL) from cardiac ascites ($>2.5$ g/dL).
  * **Amylase**: Elevated in pancreatic ascites.
  * **Cytology**: Check for malignant cells.
  * **Gram stain & Culture** (inoculate directly into blood culture bottles at bedside).
* **Imaging Studies**:
  * **Abdominal Ultrasound**: Most sensitive tool to confirm minimal ascites (detects as little as 100 mL of fluid), portal vein patency, and cirrhosis features.
  * **Abdominal X-ray (erect/supine)**: Look for dilated bowel loops and air-fluid levels (bowel obstruction) or free air under diaphragm (perforation).

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Cirrhotic Ascites**:
   - **Sodium Restriction**: Limit dietary sodium to $< 2000$ mg/day ($88$ mEq/day).
   - **Diuretics**: Dual therapy with **Spironolactone** (aldosterone antagonist) and **Furosemide** (loop diuretic).
     - *Starting ratio*: Spironolactone 100 mg to Furosemide 40 mg PO daily. This ratio maintains normokalemia. Max dose is 400 mg Spironolactone / 160 mg Furosemide.
2. **Large-Volume Paracentesis (LVP)**:
   - Indicated for tense, symptomatic ascites causing respiratory distress.
   - **Albumin Replacement**: If $> 5$ liters of fluid is removed, administer **IV Albumin (6-8 g per liter of fluid removed)** to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction (which leads to renal impairment and hyponatremia).
3. **Bowel Obstruction**:
   - Nil per os (NPO), intravenous fluids, and **nasogastric (NG) tube decompression**. Consult general surgery immediately.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) PMN Rule**: Do not wait for culture results to treat SBP. If the polymorphonuclear (PMN) neutrophil count in the ascitic fluid is **$\ge 250$ cells/mm³**, initiate empiric antibiotic therapy immediately (typically IV Cefotaxime 2 g every 8 hours) to prevent septic shock and mortality.
> **Avoid Diuretics in High Creatinine**: Discontinue diuretics if the patient develops acute kidney injury (Creatinine increases $> 100\%$ or $> 2.0$ mg/dL), severe hyponatremia (Sodium $< 120$ mEq/L), or hepatic encephalopathy.
