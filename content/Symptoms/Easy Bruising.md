# 🩸 Easy Bruising (จุดเลือดออก / รอยช้ำง่าย)

> "Easy bruising represents an increased susceptibility to cutaneous hemorrhage (ecchymosis or purpura). The clinical focus is to differentiate vascular, platelet, or coagulation abnormalities."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Quantify & Localize Bruises**: Bruises on extensor surfaces of limbs (shin, forearm) are common in active individuals. Bruises on the **trunk, back, face, or upper arms** without severe trauma suggest pathology or abuse.
> 2. **Petechiae vs. Ecchymosis**:
>    - *Petechiae* (pinpoint $<3$ mm red spots): Suggest **Thrombocytopenia** (platelet deficiency).
>    - *Ecchymoses* ($>1$ cm flat bruises): Suggest **Coagulation Factor Deficiency** or vascular wall fragility.
> 3. **Review Medications**: Check for NSAIDs, Aspirin, Warfarin, DOACs, or chronic corticosteroid use.
> 4. **Obtain CBC with Platelets & Coagulation Profile (PT/aPTT)** immediately.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Petechiae**: Flat, non-blanching pinpoint lesions **$<3$ mm** in size. Caused by red cells extravasating from capillaries due to platelet deficiency.
* **Purpura**: Raised or flat, non-blanching lesions **$3-10$ mm** in size. *Palpable purpura is the hallmark of cutaneous vasculitis.*
* **Ecchymosis (Bruise)**: Superficial skin bleeding **$>10$ mm** ($>1$ cm) in size, transitioning through color changes (purple $\rightarrow$ green $\rightarrow$ yellow) as hemoglobin degrades.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character & Severity**:
  * *Spontaneous bruising* without trauma vs. *minor bump* causing massive bruise.
  * History of mucosal bleeding: Nosebleeds (epistaxis), bleeding gums ([[Gingival Bleeding]]), or heavy menses.
* **Medications & Supplements**:
  * Antiplatelets (Aspirin, Clopidogrel).
  * Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Rivaroxaban, Apixaban, LMWH).
  * Systemic or topical corticosteroids (causes dermal collagen thinning).
  * Herbal supplements (Ginkgo biloba, high-dose Vitamin E, garlic).
* **Systemic Clues**:
  * Joint pain, dry eyes/mouth, fever $\rightarrow$ Connective tissue disease (SLE, Sjögren's).
  * Heavy alcohol intake $\rightarrow$ Alcoholic liver disease (impaired clotting factor synthesis).
  * Poor nutrition $\rightarrow$ Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Lesion Characterization**:
  * Check if purpura is **palpable** (rules out simple thrombocytopenia; indicates **vasculitis**, e.g., IgA Vasculitis / Henoch-Schönlein Purpura).
  * Look for **Senile Purpura**: Dark purple ecchymoses strictly on the extensor surfaces of the forearms and hands in elderly patients (due to age-related dermal collagen atrophy).
  * Check for **Perifollicular hemorrhages** and **corkscrew hairs** (pathognomonic for Scurvy).
* **Abdomen**: Palpate for splenomegaly (sequestration of platelets) or hepatomegaly.
* **Joints**: Assess for swelling, warmth, or deformity. *Hemarthrosis (bleeding into joints) is typical of severe coagulation factor deficiency (hemophilia), not platelet disorders.*

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Admit for urgent hematology consult if present:**
> * Easy bruising accompanied by fever, weight loss, and pallor (malignancy).
> * Diffuse **palpable purpura** on lower extremities.
> * Bruises on the trunk, back, or neck without history of trauma.
> * Bruising accompanied by spontaneous bleeding from other mucosal sites (hemoptysis, hematochezia).
> * Extreme thrombocytopenia (Platelets $<10,000/\mu\text{L}$ risks spontaneous intracranial hemorrhage).
> * Multiple joint bleedings (hemarthrosis) or deep muscle hematomas.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[CBC]] with Platelet Count**: Evaluate for thrombocytopenia.
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Essential to rule out pseudothrombocytopenia (platelet clumping), blast cells (leukemia), or schistocytes (TTP/HUS, DIC).
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT)**: Assess extrinsic (PT) and intrinsic (aPTT) pathways.
* **Secondary targeted testing**:
  * **Liver Function Tests**: Assess synthetic capacity.
  * **Renal Function Tests**: Evaluate for uremic platelet dysfunction.
  * **Skin Biopsy**: If palpable purpura is present (suspected vasculitis $\rightarrow$ leukocytoclastic vasculitis).
  * **Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid) level**: If clinical signs of Scurvy.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Thrombocytopenia (Platelets $<50,000/\mu\text{L}$)**:
   - Transfuse platelets if count $<10,000/\mu\text{L}$ (spontaneous bleeding risk) or if active bleeding.
   - If ITP suspected: Avoid platelet transfusion unless life-threatening; treat with IVIG and high-dose steroids.
2. **Coagulopathy / Anticoagulant Reversal**:
   - Warfarin overdose: Give oral/IV Vitamin K. For active bleeding, give 4-factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) or FFP.
   - DOAC-associated bleed: Specific antidotes (Idarucizumab for Dabigatran; Andexanet Alfa for Rivaroxaban/Apixaban) or PCC.
3. **Steroid / Senile Purpura**:
   - Limit/taper systemic steroids if possible. Reassure elderly patients (senile purpura is benign).
4. **Scurvy**:
   - Vitamin C replacement therapy.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Pseudothrombocytopenia**: A common lab artifact where platelets clump in the collection tube containing EDTA anticoagulant. The automated counter reads this as a low platelet count. A peripheral blood smear will instantly show the clumps, confirming normal platelet mass. Repeat drawing with a heparin or citrate tube.
> * **Non-Blanching Purpura**: To differentiate purpura from simple erythema/rash: perform the **Glass Slide Test (Diascopy)**. Press a clear glass slide firmly over the red spots. If the spots turn pale/disappear (blanch), it is simple vasodilation (erythema). If they do not fade, it is purpura (extravasated blood).
> * **Steroid Purpura**: Long-term corticosteroid use inhibits collagen synthesis, causing atrophy of dermal connective tissues supporting the capillaries. Minor shear stress on the skin causes local capillary rupture and bruising.
