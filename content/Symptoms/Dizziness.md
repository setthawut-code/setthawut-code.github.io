# 🌀 Dizziness and Vertigo (เวียนศีรษะ)

> "Dizziness is a broad term encompassing four distinct clinical sensations: **Vertigo** (false sensation of motion, spinning), **Presyncope** (feeling of impending faint), **Disequilibrium** (imbalance while walking), and **Ill-defined lightheadedness**. Differentiating these and ruling out life-threatening central causes (e.g., posterior stroke) is the primary clinical priority."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Action:**
> 1. **Classify the Dizziness**:
>    - *Vertigo*: "Room is spinning" $\rightarrow$ Vestibular system.
>    - *Presyncope*: "Going to pass out" $\rightarrow$ Cardiovascular / perfusion.
>    - *Disequilibrium*: "Off-balance, legs only" $\rightarrow$ Neurological / proprioceptive.
> 2. **Rule Out Central Vertigo (HINTS Exam)**:
>    - Perform in patients with continuous, active vertigo and nystagmus to rule out **cerebellar/brainstem stroke**.
>    - *Central signs*: Normal Head Impulse, direction-changing Nystagmus, vertical skew deviation.
> 3. **Rule Out Orthostasis**: Check orthostatic blood pressure in presyncope.

## 🔍 Diagnostic Framework

```
Dizziness Classification
└── 1. Vertigo (Spinning)
    ├── Peripheral (Inner ear: BPPV, Vestibular Neuritis)
    └── Central (Brainstem/Cerebellum: Stroke, Malignancy)
└── 2. Presyncope (Fainting feeling)
    ├── Orthostatic Hypotension
    └── Cardiac Arrhythmia
└── 3. Disequilibrium (Imbalance)
    ├── Peripheral Neuropathy
    └── Cerebellar Degeneration
└── 4. Ill-defined Lightheadedness
    └── Anxiety / Hyperventilation
```

## 📊 Central vs. Peripheral Vertigo

Differentiating central causes (requiring urgent neuroimaging) from peripheral causes:

| Feature | Peripheral Vertigo | Central Vertigo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Sudden, severe spinning | Gradual, milder spinning |
| **Nystagmus** | Horizontal-rotatory, fatigable, suppressed by visual fixation. Never vertical. | Direction-changing, non-fatigable, not suppressed by fixation. Can be purely vertical/rotatory. |
| **Neurologic Deficits**| Absent | Present (diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, dysphonia, dysmetria - **"5 D's"**). |
| **Auditory Symptoms** | Tinnitus or [[Hearing Loss]] may be present. | Absent (rare, except in AICA stroke). |
| **Compensation** | Rapid (days to weeks) | Slow / incomplete |
| **HINTS Exam** | Abnormal Head Impulse, Unidirectional Nystagmus, No Skew Deviation. | **Normal Head Impulse, Direction-changing Nystagmus, Skew Deviation present.** |

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character of Dizziness**: Is it a spinning sensation (vertigo), feeling lightheaded/faint (presyncope), or feeling unsteady on feet (disequilibrium)?
* **Timing & Duration**:
  * *Seconds to minutes* $\rightarrow$ **Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)**.
  * *Hours to days* $\rightarrow$ **Meniere Disease** (associated with hearing loss/tinnitus), vestibular migraine.
  * *Days to weeks* $\rightarrow$ **Vestibular Neuritis** (isolated) or **Labyrinthitis** (with hearing loss).
* **Triggers**: Is it provoked by head movements (BPPV) or standing up (orthostatic hypotension)?
* **Neurologic Symptoms**: Diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, focal weakness, or numbness (indicates Central/Stroke).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Nystagmus Examination**: Observe direction, latency, and fatigability.
* **HINTS Exam** (Highly sensitive for central stroke in acute vestibular syndrome):
  * **H**ead **I**mpulse: Rapid horizontal head rotation while patient fixes gaze on examiner's nose.
    * *Peripheral*: Catch-up saccade (delayed correction) when head is turned to the affected side.
    * *Central*: Normal test (no catch-up saccade; the brainstem reflex is intact, but cerebellar stroke is present).
  * **N**ystagmus: Observe gaze in primary, left, and right directions.
    * *Peripheral*: Unidirectional (beats in same direction regardless of gaze).
    * *Central*: Direction-changing nystagmus (beats left on left gaze, right on right gaze).
  * **T**est of **S**kew: Alternate cover test of the eyes.
    * *Peripheral*: Normal (eyes remain aligned).
    * *Central*: Vertical misalignment (skew deviation) seen when covering/uncovering eyes.
* ** Dix-Hallpike Maneuver**: Turn head 45 degrees and rapidly lower patient to supine with head extended 20 degrees.
  * *Diagnostic for BPPV*: Produces latency (2-10 sec), geotropic rotatory-upbeating nystagmus, and fatigability.
* **Orthostatic Vitals**: BP/HR measured lying down, then standing at 1 and 3 minutes. (Drop of $>20$ mmHg systolic or $>10$ mmHg diastolic confirms orthostatic hypotension).

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Common / Peripheral | Dangerous / Central |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vertigo** | • **BPPV** (canalithiasis)<br>• Vestibular Neuritis<br>• Meniere Disease | • **Cerebellar / Brainstem Stroke**<br>• Vertebrobasilar Insufficiency<br>• Cerebellar Hemorrhage |
| **Presyncope** | • Dehydration<br>• Vasovagal response | • **Cardiac Arrhythmia** (AV block, VT)<br>• Aortic Stenosis |
| **Disequilibrium**| • Diabetic [[Muscle Weakness]] / Neuropathy<br>• Aging / Presbyastasis | • **Cervical Myelopathy**<br>• Cerebellar Tumor |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate neurology referral and Brain MRI (DWI) required if:**
> * Acute dizziness with any of the **5 D's**: **D**iplopia, **D**ysarthria, **D**ysphagia, **D**ysphonia, **D**ysmetria (ataxia).
> * **New-onset severe headache** or neck pain preceding vertigo (suggests vertebral artery dissection).
> * Vertical nystagmus (beats straight up or down).
> * Inability to stand or walk independently (severe truncal ataxia, typical of cerebellar stroke).
> * **Normal Head Impulse Test** in a patient with continuous vertigo and nystagmus (Central Stroke!).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Screening Tests**:
  * **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Check for arrhythmias, QT prolongation, or heart block in presyncope.
  * **Bedside blood glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
  * **Hemoglobin**: Rule out severe [[Anemia]] causing presyncope/pallor.
* **Neuroimaging**:
  * **Brain MRI with DWI**: Gold standard to detect posterior circulation stroke (often missed by CT scan in the first 24 hours).
* **Audiometry**: Indicated in patients with chronic dizziness and hearing loss to evaluate for Meniere disease or Acoustic Neuroma.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Symptomatic Management (Peripheral Vertigo)**:
   - Vestibular suppressants: Antihistamines (Dimenhydrinate 50 mg IV/PO every 6-8h) or Benzodiazepines (Diazepam). *Use only in the acute phase ($<3$ days) as they delay long-term vestibular compensation.*
2. **Positional Vertigo (BPPV)**:
   - **Epley Maneuver**: Canalith repositioning maneuver. Highly curative for posterior canal BPPV ($>90\%$ success rate).
3. **Meniere Disease**:
   - Low-salt diet ($<2$ g/day), avoid caffeine/alcohol. Thiazide diuretics if refractory.
4. **Central Vertigo / Stroke**:
   - Manage as acute ischemic stroke or intracranial hemorrhage. Initiate antiplatelets (Aspirin/Clopidogrel) or coordinate stroke thrombolysis (tPA) if within time window.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **"HINTS beats MRI"**: In the first 24 to 48 hours of an acute vestibular syndrome, a structured **HINTS exam** performed by an experienced clinician is *more sensitive* than a brain MRI for detecting a posterior stroke. A negative MRI does not rule out cerebellar stroke in the ultra-acute phase.
> **Dimenhydrinate Caution in Elderly**: Dimenhydrinate (Dramamine) has strong anticholinergic properties. Avoid prolonged use in elderly patients due to risks of urinary retention, acute delirium, falls, and worsening of dementia.
