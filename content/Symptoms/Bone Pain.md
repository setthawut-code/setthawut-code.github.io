# 🦴 Bone Pain (ปวดกระดูก)

> "Bone pain is characterized as a deep, boring, poorly localized ache that is typically worse at night or during weight-bearing. The priority is to rule out metastatic disease and pathologic fracture."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Rule Out Metastatic Disease**: In older adults with localized bone pain, suspect metastasis (from **Breast, Lung, Prostate, Renal, Thyroid** cancers) or **Multiple Myeloma**.
> 2. **Identify Pathologic Fracture Risk**: Assess if the pain is acute and exacerbated by minor movement/weight-bearing in osteoporotic or cancer patients.
> 3. **Search for Hypercalcemia**: Look for constipation, polyuria, altered mental status, and short QT on ECG.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Bone pain arises from nociceptors located in the **periosteum** and the **bone marrow**. The calcified bone cortex itself has no sensory innervation.
* **Mechanism**: Pain is triggered by mechanical stretch of the periosteum (tumor expansion, fracture, osteomyelitis) or chemical mediators of inflammation.

---

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

* **Character of Pain**:
  * *Deep, boring, aching, worse at night (unrelated to movement)* $\rightarrow$ Classic for bone malignancy/metastasis.
  * *Sharp, acute onset following minor movement or trauma* $\rightarrow$ Pathologic fracture.
  * *Diffuse, generalized bone pain + muscle weakness* $\rightarrow$ Osteomalacia (severe Vitamin D deficiency).
* **Associated Clues**:
  * Prior history of cancer (breast, lung, prostate, kidney, thyroid).
  * Fever, chills, swelling $\rightarrow$ **Osteomyelitis** (bone infection) or abscess.
  * Weight loss, fatigue, bone pain $\rightarrow$ Multiple Myeloma or metastatic disease.
  * Polyuria, polydipsia, confusion, constipation $\rightarrow$ Hypercalcemia.

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Midline and Focal Percussion**:
  * Tap gently over the spine (vertebral percussion tenderness) $\rightarrow$ vertebral compression fracture, metastasis, or discitis/osteomyelitis.
  * Palpate and apply gentle pressure over the symptomatic bone (e.g., tibia, femur). *Localized, point bone tenderness is a key sign of fracture or localized pathology.*
* **Joint Exam**: Check range of motion of adjacent joints. In pure bone pain, **joint range of motion is preserved and painless** (differentiates bone pain from arthritis).
* **Neurological**: Look for signs of nerve root or spinal cord compression (weakness, hyperreflexia, sensory loss below a level) due to vertebral collapse.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 Common Primary Cancers Metastatic to Bone (Mnemonic: Pb KTL)
* **P**rostate (classically osteoblastic/sclerotic lesions)
* **B**reast (mixed lytic and blastic lesions)
* **K**idney / Renal (purely osteolytic)
* **T**hroid (purely osteolytic)
* **L**ung (purely osteolytic)

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Urgent neuroimaging/orthopedic consultation required if present:**
> * Localized bone pain that is constant and progressively worse at night, preventing sleep.
> * Localized bone pain in a patient with a known history of cancer.
> * Sudden, severe pain in a weight-bearing bone after minimal trauma (pathologic fracture).
> * Spine pain accompanied by neurological deficits (sensory level, leg weakness, sphincter dysfunction $\rightarrow$ spinal cord compression).
> * Localized bone pain with high fever, redness, and swelling (osteomyelitis).

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Laboratory Workup**:
  * **Serum Calcium & Phosphate**: Elevated calcium in bone metastases or multiple myeloma. Low calcium and low phosphate in osteomalacia.
  * **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)**: Elevated in states of high bone turnover (Paget's disease, bone metastases, healing fractures).
  * **Renal Function (BUN/Cr)**: Assess for renal impairment (common in Multiple Myeloma due to light chain cast nephropathy or hypercalcemia).
  * **Multiple Myeloma Screen**: **Serum and Urine Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP / UPEP)** to look for a monoclonal spike (M-protein); Free Light Chain (FLC) assay.
  * **CBC**: Check for cytopenias (marrow infiltration).
* **Imaging Modalities**:
  * **Plain X-Ray**: First-line imaging. Look for osteolytic (punched-out) or osteoblastic (sclerotic) lesions, periosteal reaction, or cortical disruption (fracture).
  * **Bone Scan (Technetium-99m)**: Highly sensitive for detecting osteoblastic activity (metastases, fractures, osteomyelitis). *Note: Bone scan can be falsely normal in pure lytic processes like Multiple Myeloma.*
  * **CT & MRI**: MRI is the gold standard for evaluating spinal cord compression, early osteomyelitis, and marrow infiltration.
  * **Skeletal Survey**: Series of plain X-rays (skull, spine, pelvis, long bones) – the standard imaging for **Multiple Myeloma**.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Bone Metastases**:
   - Pain control: WHO analgesic ladder (NSAIDs, opioids).
   - Bone-modifying agents: **Bisphosphonates** (Zoledronic acid) or **Denosumab** (RANKL inhibitor) to reduce skeletal-related events (fractures, spinal compression) and treat hypercalcemia.
   - Radiation therapy: Highly effective for localized bone pain from metastases.
2. **Pathologic Fractures**:
   - Surgical stabilization (intramedullary nailing, joint arthroplasty) followed by radiation.
3. **Osteomyelitis**:
   - Long-term IV antibiotics (usually $4-6$ weeks) targeted to bone culture results. Surgical debridement if necrotic bone (sequestrum) is present.
4. **Multiple Myeloma**:
   - Chemotherapy (e.g., Bortezomib, Lenalidomide, Dexamethasone) and stem-cell transplant.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **The Myeloma Bone Scan Pitfall**: Multiple Myeloma lesions are purely osteolytic and do not stimulate osteoblasts. Because Technetium-99m bone scans rely on osteoblastic activity to show "hot spots," **bone scans can be falsely negative in Multiple Myeloma**. Always use a **Skeletal Survey (plain X-rays)** or CT/MRI instead.
> * **Prostate Bone Metastases**: Unlike most solid tumors which cause destructive lytic bone lesions, prostate cancer metastases are classically **osteoblastic (sclerotic)**, presenting on X-ray as dense, white, sclerotic bone lesions.
> * **Osteomalacia vs. Osteoporosis**:
>   - *Osteoporosis*: Normal bone mineralization, decreased bone mass. Silent until a fracture occurs.
>   - *Osteomalacia*: Defective bone mineralization (usually due to severe Vitamin D deficiency). Presents with **widespread bone pain, bone tenderness, and proximal muscle weakness**.
