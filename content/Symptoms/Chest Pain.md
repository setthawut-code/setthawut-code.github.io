# 💔 Chest Pain (เจ็บแน่นหน้าอก)

> "When dealing with chest pain, the priority is to rule out life-threatening causes first."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Identify Life Threats**: Assess for signs of cardiogenic [[Shock]], respiratory distress, or severe hypoxemia.
> 2. **Rule Out the "Big 5"**:
>    - [[Acute Coronary Syndrome]] (ACS)
>    - [[Aortic Dissection]]
>    - [[Pulmonary Embolism]] (PE)
>    - [[Tension Pneumothorax]]
>    - Boerhaave Syndrome (Esophageal Rupture)
> 3. **Obtain ECG & Troponin immediately** for any acute non-traumatic chest pain.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

* **Visceral Pain**:
  * Originates from internal organs (myocardium, pericardium, esophagus).
  * Fibers: Unmyelinated C fibers (slow, diffuse, poorly localized).
  * Character: Dull, squeezing, heavy, pressure-like. May radiate due to shared dermatomes (C8-T4).
* **Somatic Pain**:
  * Originates from dermis, parietal pleura, pericardium, peritoneum, skeletal muscle.
  * Fibers: Myelinated A-delta fibers (fast, sharp, well-localized).
  * Character: Sharp, stabbing, positional, or pleuritic.

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Pain Characters & Clues

| Pain Feature | Possible Causes | Diagnostic Clue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Crushing, substernal pressure** | [[Acute Coronary Syndrome]] | Radiates to left arm/jaw, Levine's sign, worsens with exertion, relieved by NTG. |
| **Sudden, tearing/ripping to back** | [[Aortic Dissection]] | Migrating pain, maximal intensity at onset, associated with severe hypertension. |
| **Pleuritic (sharp, worse with deep breath)** | [[Pneumothorax]], PE, Pericarditis, CAP | Relieved by sitting up/leaning forward in Pericarditis. |
| **Burning, epigastric/retrosternal** | GERD, Esophageal spasm | Relieved by antacids or warm water (spasm), related to food intake. |
| **Sharp, localized chest wall pain** | Costochondritis, rib fracture | Reproducible by palpation over costochondral joints. |

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Vital Signs**:
  * *Hypertension* $\rightarrow$ Risk factor for ACS, trigger/finding in [[Aortic Dissection]].
  * *Hypotension / Shock* $\rightarrow$ Massively compromised cardiac output (cardiogenic shock in ACS, obstructive shock in tension pneumothorax, tamponade, or massive PE).
  * *Unequal BP in arms (difference >20 mmHg)* $\rightarrow$ Strong indicator of [[Aortic Dissection]].
  * *Tachypnea / Hypoxemia* $\rightarrow$ Suggests pulmonary origin (PE, pneumothorax, pneumonia).
* **Inspection**:
  * Look for vesicular skin lesions along a dermatome (Herpes Zoster).
  * Surgical scars (previous CABG/sternotomy).
* **Neck**: JVD present in right-sided heart failure (secondary to PE, RV infarction) or cardiac tamponade.
* **Chest/Lungs**:
  * *Unilateral decreased breath sounds + hyperresonance* $\rightarrow$ [[Pneumothorax]].
  * *Consolidation signs (crackles, bronchial breath sounds)* $\rightarrow$ [[Community Acquired Pneumonia]].
  * *Localized chest wall tenderness* $\rightarrow$ Costochondritis (Tietze syndrome if swelling present).
* **Cardiovascular**:
  * *Pericardial friction rub* $\rightarrow$ Pericarditis (three-phase scratchy sound).
  * *New murmur*: Diastolic murmur of aortic regurgitation in aortic dissection; systolic murmur of acute mitral regurgitation or VSD in post-MI complications.
  * *S3 or S4 gallop*: Suggests ischemic heart dysfunction.
* **Extremities**: Asymmetric leg swelling/calf tenderness suggests DVT $\rightarrow$ [[Pulmonary Embolism]].

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Origin | Common / Benign | Life-Threatening (Must Rule Out) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiac** | • Stable Angina<br>• Pericarditis / Myocarditis | • **[[Acute Coronary Syndrome]]** (STEMI / NSTEMI / UA)<br>• Arrhythmia-induced ischemia |
| **Vascular** | • Stable pulmonary hypertension | • **[[Aortic Dissection]]**<br>• **[[Pulmonary Embolism]]** |
| **Pulmonary** | • Pleurisy / Pleural effusion<br>• Mild bronchitis | • **[[Tension Pneumothorax]]**<br>• Boerhaave Syndrome (Esophageal rupture) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | • GERD<br>• Esophageal spasm / Esophagitis<br>• Peptic Ulcer Disease | • Acute Pancreatitis (can present with atypical chest/upper abdominal pain) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | • Costochondritis<br>• Rib fracture / Muscle strain | None |
| **Psychogenic** | • Panic attack / Anxiety hyperventilation | None |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency protocols if present:**
> * Crushing retrosternal chest pain $>20$ minutes
> * Sudden "tearing" or "ripping" pain radiating to back
> * Hemodynamic instability (SBP $<90$ mmHg, signs of shock)
> * Asymmetrical blood pressure in upper extremities ($>20$ mmHg difference) or absent peripheral pulses
> * Altered level of consciousness
> * Respiratory distress, accessory muscle use, or $SpO_2 < 90\%$ on room air
> * New-onset diastolic murmur (aortic regurgitation)

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **[[ECG]] (within 10 minutes of presentation)**: Look for ST elevations (STEMI), ST depressions/T-wave inversions (NSTEMI/ischemia), PR depressions/diffuse concave ST elevations (Pericarditis), or S1Q3T3/Right axis deviation (PE).
  * **Serum [[Troponin]]**: High-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn) T or I at 0 and 1-3 hours to rule in/out acute MI.
  * **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: Assess for widened mediastinum (aortic dissection), pneumothorax (pleural line), consolidation (pneumonia), or free air under diaphragm (Boerhaave/perforation).
* **Targeted Diagnostic Imaging**:
  * **CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)**: If PE suspected (high Wells score / positive D-dimer).
  * **CT Aortogram**: Gold standard for verifying [[Aortic Dissection]].
  * **Echocardiography (TTE/TEE)**: Assess regional wall motion abnormalities (ACS), aortic flap (dissection), pericardial effusion/tamponade, or RV strain.

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Suspected ACS Protocol**:
   - Oxygen (only if $SpO_2 < 90\%$), **Aspirin** $160-325$ mg chewed immediately, sublingual Nitroglycerin (avoid in RV infarction/phosphodiesterase inhibitor use).
   - If STEMI: Immediate reperfusion therapy $\rightarrow$ Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) within $90$ minutes, or Fibrinolysis within $30$ minutes of door-to-needle time if PCI is unavailable.
2. **[[Aortic Dissection]] Management**:
   - Target Heart Rate $<60$ bpm and SBP $100-120$ mmHg using IV short-acting beta-blockers (e.g., Esmolol, Labetalol). *Control heart rate before lowering blood pressure to avoid reflex tachycardia.*
   - Stanford Type A (ascending aorta): Emergency surgery. Stanford Type B (descending only): Medical management unless complications occur.
3. **[[Pulmonary Embolism]]**:
   - Start anticoagulation immediately if clinical suspicion is high (e.g., LMWH or Unfractionated Heparin). Systemic thrombolysis if hemodynamically unstable (massive PE).
4. **[[Tension Pneumothorax]]**:
   - Immediate needle decompression followed by thoracostomy tube placement.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **Anginal Equivalents**: Elderly, diabetic, and female patients frequently present with atypical symptoms instead of chest pain—such as isolated dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, or syncope. Always perform ECG.
> * **Nitroglycerin Trap**: Relief of chest pain after sublingual Nitroglycerin is **not** diagnostic of CAD; esophageal spasm and gastrointestinal disorders also show improvement with nitrates.
> * **Aortic Dissection + Ischemia**: Aortic dissection can propagate retrograde and dissect the coronary ostia (most commonly the right coronary artery), presenting as an acute inferior STEMI. Giving antiplatelets and anticoagulants/thrombolytics here is fatal. Always check bilateral pulses and BP first.
> * **Wellens' Syndrome**: T-wave inversion or biphasic T-waves in V2-V3 in a pain-free state indicates critical proximal LAD stenosis. *Do not perform stress tests on these patients; refer directly to angiography.*
