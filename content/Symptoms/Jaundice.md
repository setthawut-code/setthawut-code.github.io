# 🟡 Jaundice (ดีซ่าน / ตัวเหลืองตาเหลือง)

> "Jaundice is the visual manifestation of hyperbilirubinemia. The diagnostic path starts by classifying the bilirubin elevation as conjugated or unconjugated."

---

## 🎯 30-Second Rounding Guide
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Immediate Ward Approach:**
> 1. **Differentiate Bilirubin Fractions**:
>    - *Unconjugated Hyperbilirubinemia*: Think hemolysis or Gilbert's syndrome.
>    - *Conjugated Hyperbilirubinemia*: Hepatocellular injury vs. Biliary obstruction.
> 2. **Rule Out Biliary Obstruction (Cholestasis)**: Look for dilated bile ducts on RUQ ultrasound.
> 3. **Assess Liver Function**: Check PT/INR, serum albumin, and mental status (to rule out acute liver failure).
> 4. **Emergency Check**: Charcot's triad (Fever + Jaundice + RUQ pain) $\rightarrow$ [[Acute Cholangitis]]. Treat immediately.

---

## 🔍 Definition & Physiology

Jaundice (icterus) occurs when serum total bilirubin exceeds **$2.5-3.0$ mg/dL**. It is best detected in the sclera (scleral icterus) due to high elastin content which has high affinity for bilirubin.

```text
[ Heme Breakdown ]
       │
       ▼
Unconjugated Bilirubin (bound to albumin)
       │
       ▼ (Liver: UGT1A1 enzyme conjugation)
Conjugated Bilirubin
       │
       ▼ (Excreted into bile)
Gut Urobilinogen
 ├── Stercobilin (excreted in stool; pale stools if blocked)
 └── Urobilin (excreted in urine; dark urine if conjugated)
```

## 🗣️ Key History Taking

### ⏳ Symptom Clues & Exposures
* **Urine & Stool Color**:
  * *Dark (tea-colored) urine* + *Pale (clay-colored) stools*: Direct indicator of **Conjugated Hyperbilirubinemia** (biliary obstruction).
  * *Normal urine color*: Suggests **Unconjugated Hyperbilirubinemia** (unconjugated bilirubin is bound to albumin and not filtered by kidneys).
* **Pruritus (itching)**: Suggests bile acid deposition in skin due to prolonged cholestasis.
* **Pain vs. Painless**:
  * *Painful jaundice*: Choledocholithiasis, acute hepatitis, liver abscess.
  * *Painless progressive jaundice*: Malignant biliary obstruction (e.g., pancreatic head cancer, cholangiocarcinoma).
* **Toxic/Drug Exposure**: Acetaminophen, herbal remedies, high-risk medications (amoxicillin/clavulanate, anti-TB drugs, statins).
* **Infectious Risk Factors**: Travel (Hepatitis A/E), blood transfusion, IV drug use, tattoos, sexual contact (Hepatitis B/C).

---

## 🩺 Physical Examination

* **Scleral Icterus & Skin**: Check under natural light. Look for excoriations from scratching (pruritus).
* **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease (Cirrhosis)**:
  * Spider angiomata, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, testicular atrophy, caput medusae, ascites, Dupuytren's contracture, asterixis (flapping tremor indicating encephalopathy).
* **Abdominal Palpation**:
  * *Tender hepatomegaly*: Acute hepatitis, congestive hepatopathy.
  * *Splenomegaly*: Portal hypertension (cirrhosis) or hemolysis.
  * **Courvoisier's Sign**: A palpably enlarged, non-tender gallbladder in a jaundiced patient suggests malignant biliary obstruction (e.g., pancreatic cancer), rather than gallstones.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

### 📊 LFT Interpretation Guide
Use liver function tests to classify direct hyperbilirubinemia:

| Pattern | AST & ALT | ALP & GGT | Suspected Site / Etiology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hepatocellular** | **Marked elevation** ($>10\times$ ULN) | Mild elevation ($<2-3\times$ ULN) | Hepatocyte injury (viral, toxic, alcoholic, ischemic hepatitis) |
| **Cholestatic** | Mild elevation ($<5\times$ ULN) | **Marked elevation** ($>3\times$ ULN) | Biliary obstruction or intrahepatic cholestasis |

---

### 🧩 Diagnostic Classification Matrix

| Category | Pathophysiology | Key Etiologies | Useful Diagnostic Clues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-hepatic** | Excess unconjugated bilirubin production | • Hemolysis (autoimmune, microangiopathic, G6PD)<br>• Gilbert's syndrome | Elevated reticulocytes, low haptoglobin, direct Coombs test, normal LFTs. |
| **Intra-hepatic** | Hepatocellular dysfunction or intrahepatic cholestasis | • Acute Viral Hepatitis (A, B, C, E)<br>• Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)<br>• [[Cirrhosis]] / Alcoholic liver disease<br>• Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) | Elevated AST/ALT, positive viral serology, drug history, ultrasound showing no bile duct dilation. |
| **Post-hepatic** | Obstruction of extrahepatic bile ducts | • Choledocholithiasis<br>• Pancreatic head adenocarcinoma<br>• Cholangiocarcinoma | Dilated bile ducts on ultrasound, elevated ALP and direct bilirubin, clay-colored stools. |

## 🚨 Red Flags

> [!CAUTION]
> **Immediate emergency intervention required if present:**
> * **Acute Liver Failure (ALF)**: Altered mental status (hepatic encephalopathy) + Coagulopathy (PT/INR $\ge 1.5$) in a patient without pre-existing cirrhosis.
> * **[[Acute Cholangitis]]**: Charcot's triad (Fever + Jaundice + RUQ pain) or Reynolds' pentad (adding Hypotension + Altered mental status).
> * **Severe bleeding**: Hematemesis, melena, or bleeding from venipuncture sites (DIC / variceal rupture).
> * Profound cachexia and rapid weight loss (malignancy).

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Laboratory Workup**:
  * **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: Bilirubin (total and fractionated), AST, ALT, ALP, GGT, Albumin.
  * **Coagulation Profile**: PT/INR (key marker of liver synthetic function; does not correct with Vitamin K in severe parenchymal disease).
  * **CBC & Hemolytic Workup**: Complete blood count, peripheral blood smear (schistocytes, spherocytes), reticulocyte count, LDH, haptoglobin if unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia.
  * **Urine Analysis**: Detects bilirubinuria (only conjugated bilirubin enters urine).
* **Imaging (Crucial Second Step)**:
  * **Abdominal Ultrasound (RUQ)**: First-line imaging. Evaluates for presence of biliary tree dilation (extrahepatic vs. intrahepatic cholestasis) and gallstones.
  * **MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography)**: Non-invasive evaluation of biliary tree anatomy; highly sensitive for stones and strictures.
  * **CT Abdomen**: Better for detecting pancreatic masses, liver metastases, or cirrhosis complications.
* **Specialized Studies**:
  * **ERCP (Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography)**: Invasive therapeutic procedure for stone extraction, stenting of malignant strictures, or tissue brushing.
  * **Viral Hepatitis Panel**: Anti-HAV IgM, HBsAg, anti-HBc IgM, anti-HCV, anti-HEV IgM.

---

## 🏥 Management Principles

1. **Biliary Obstruction**:
   - Keep NPO, consult GI/Surgery.
   - For [[Acute Cholangitis]] or obstructive choledocholithiasis: Urgent biliary decompression via **ERCP** or percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) + IV antibiotics.
2. **Acute Liver Failure**:
   - Admit to ICU. Monitor blood glucose (risk of severe hypoglycemia) and neurological status. Avoid sedatives. Consider N-acetylcysteine (NAC) for both acetaminophen and non-acetaminophen etiologies. Early transplant consultation.
3. **Hepatic Encephalopathy**:
   - Oral or rectal **Lactulose** (titrate to $2-3$ soft stools/day) + oral **Rifaximin** to reduce ammonia-producing gut bacteria.
4. **Pruritus Relief**:
   - Cholestyramine (bile acid sequestrant) first line.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> * **INR vs. Albumin**: In acute liver injury, albumin is often normal because it has a long half-life ($20$ days), whereas PT/INR elevates quickly due to short half-life of Factor VII ($4-6$ hours). INR is the most sensitive marker for acute hepatic synthetic dysfunction.
> * **Gilbert's Syndrome**: A benign genetic defect in bilirubin conjugation. Presents as mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia (total bilirubin $< 4$ mg/dL) triggered by fasting, stress, dehydration, or illness. *Reassure the patient; no treatment is necessary.*
> * **Dark Urine Clue**: Bilirubinuria (tea-colored urine) is only caused by conjugated bilirubin. If a patient is yellow but has normal-colored urine, the cause is unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia or carotenemia (yellow skin but normal sclera).
