# 🦴 Systemic Lupus Erythematosus / SLE (โรคพุ่มพวง / เอสแอลอี)

> **Definition**: Chronic, multisystem autoimmune connective tissue disease characterized by loss of self-tolerance, production of pathogenic autoantibodies (predominantly antinuclear antibodies), and immune complex deposition in tissues, triggering classical complement activation and widespread inflammatory organ damage.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology & Risk Factors

* **Gender**: Female-to-male ratio **$9:1$** during childbearing years ($15-45$). Ratio $2:1$ prepuberty/postmenopause (estrogen role).
* **Age**: Peak onset $15-45$ years.
* **Race**: Greater prevalence, severity, and mortality in non-Caucasians (African Americans, Hispanics, Asians).
* **Genetic Factors**:
  * **HLA associations**: HLA-DR2 and HLA-DR3.
  * **Complement deficiencies**: Early complement component mutations (**C1q, C2, C4**) associated with $>90\%$ risk of developing SLE (due to failure of clearing apoptotic debris).
* **Environmental Triggers**:
  * **UV Light (UVB)**: Induces keratinocyte apoptosis, alters DNA structure to increase immunogenicity, and triggers systemic flares.
  * **Drugs**: Hydralazine, Procainamide, Isoniazid, Minocycline (causes Drug-Induced Lupus).
  * **Infections**: Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) triggers autoreactivity via molecular mimicry.
  * **Estrogen**: Enhances T-cell and B-cell activation (evidenced by flares during pregnancy or oral contraceptive use).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology

Type III (Immune complex) and Type II (autoantibody-mediated) hypersensitivity mechanisms:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ENVIRONMENTAL TRIGGERS (UV, Drugs) + GENETIC BIAS     │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ▼
      Abnormal/Excessive Apoptosis + Impaired Clearance
                            │
                            ▼
    Prolonged Exposure of Intracellular Nuclear Antigens
    (dsDNA, nucleosomes, histones, Sm, Ro, La)
                            │
                            ▼
     Autoreactive B-Cell Activation & Autoantibody (ANA)
     Production (driven by IFN-alpha from plasmacytoid DCs)
                            │
                            ▼
        Circulating & Tissue Immune Complex Formation
                            │
                            ▼
  Deposition in Glomeruli, Joints, Dermal-Epidermal Junction
                            │
                            ▼
  Classical Complement Activation (C3/C4 consumption) &
  Leukocyte Recruitment $\rightarrow$ Severe Tissue Destruction
```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

Highly variable; "the great imitator" of rheumatology.

### 1. Mucocutaneous Features ($80–90\%$)
* **Malar Rash (Butterfly Rash)**: Flat or raised erythematous rash over cheeks and nasal bridge. **Characteristically spares the nasolabial folds** (distinguishes from rosacea, seborrheic dermatitis). Photosensitive, resolves without scarring.
* **Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE)**: Erythematous raised coin-shaped plaques with adherent scale, follicular plugging, and central scarring/atrophy. Causes **scarring alopecia** (permanent hair loss).
* **Subacute Cutaneous Lupus (SCLE)**: Annular/polycyclic or psoriasiform rash in sun-exposed areas. Sparing of face, resolves **without scarring** but leaves pigment changes. Strongly linked to **Anti-SSA/Ro**.
* **Painless Oral/Nasal Ulcers**: Typically on hard palate or nasal septum.
* **Non-scarring Alopecia**: Diffuse hair thinning or fragile frontal hairs ("lupus hair").

### 2. Musculoskeletal Features ($90\%$)
* **Lupus Arthritis**: Symmetrical, migratory polyarthritis affecting small joints (MCP, PIP, wrists, knees). Intermittent, pain disproportionate to swelling.
* **Jaccoud's Arthropathy**: Reversible, non-erosive joint deformities (ulnar drift, swan-neck, boutonniere) due to chronic tendonitis and ligamentous laxity rather than bone erosions. Deformities **can be manually corrected** by the patient (distinguishes from Rheumatoid Arthritis).
* **Avascular Necrosis (AVN)**: High propensity in femoral head. Risk multiplied by high-dose corticosteroids.

### 3. Renal Features: Lupus Nephritis (LN, $50\%$)
Significant determinant of prognosis. Presents with hematuria, proteinuria, cellular casts (RBC casts), or full-blown nephrotic/nephritic syndrome.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Renal Biopsy Requirement**: **Mandatory in any SLE patient with unexplained proteinuria ($>0.5$ g/day) or active urine sediment (hematuria, RBC casts)**. Essential for staging and guiding therapy.

#### ISN/RPS Pathology Classification:
* **Class I**: Minimal mesangial LN (normal light microscopy, deposits on immunofluorescence).
* **Class II**: Mesangial proliferative LN (mesangial hypercellularity).
* **Class III**: Focal proliferative LN ($<50\%$ of glomeruli involved).
* **Class IV**: Diffuse proliferative LN ($\ge 50\%$ of glomeruli involved). **Most common and most severe**. Subendothelial immune complex deposits produce **"wire loop" lesions** on light microscopy. Requires aggressive immunosuppression.
* **Class V**: Membranous LN. Subepithelial immune complex deposits. Presents with severe **nephrotic syndrome** (hyperlipidemia, hypoalbuminemia, anasarca).
* **Class VI**: Advanced sclerosing LN ($>90\%$ globally sclerosed glomeruli). Chronic kidney disease; requires dialysis/transplant.

* *Telescoped Urinary Sediment*: Co-existence of RBCs, WBCs, oval fat bodies, and multiple types of casts (RBC, WBC, granular, waxy) in active nephritis.

### 4. Cardiovascular & Pulmonary Features
* **Serositis**: **Pleuritis** (pleural effusion: exudative, low complement) and **Pericarditis** (pericardial effusion, risk of tamponade). Presents as pleuritic/positional chest pain.
* **Libman-Sacks Endocarditis**: Sterile, verrucous endocarditis characterized by small, fibrous vegetations on **both sides** (atrial and ventricular surfaces) of heart valves (mitral valve most common), predisposing to embolic strokes and secondary infective endocarditis.
* **Accelerated Atherosclerosis**: Major cause of late-stage mortality.
* **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH)**: Rare, catastrophic complication. Presents as dyspnea, hemoptysis, drop in hemoglobin, and bilateral alveolar infiltrates. High mortality.
* **Shrinking Lung Syndrome**: Restrictive lung defect due to diaphragmatic dysfunction/paralysis. Elevated diaphragms on chest X-ray.

### 5. Neuropsychiatric Features (NPSLE, $10-20\%$)
* Mediated by vasculopathy, autoantibodies, or cytokine-mediated neurotoxicity.
* Presents as **lupus psychosis**, seizures, stroke, cognitive dysfunction, transverse myelitis, or aseptic meningitis.

### 6. Hematologic Features
* **Anemia of Chronic Disease** (most common).
* **Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)**: Warm IgG, Coombs-positive spherocytosis.
* **Leukopenia** (WBC $<4,000/\mu\text{L}$) and **Lymphopenia** (lymphocytes $<1,500/\mu\text{L}$).
* **Thrombocytopenia** (platelets $<100,000/\mu\text{L}$).
* *Evans Syndrome*: Co-existence of AIHA and ITP.

## 📂 Autoantibody Profiles & Diagnostic Associations

| Autoantibody | Sensitivity | Specificity | Clinical Associations / Pearls |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **ANA** | **$>99\%$** | $65\%$ | • **Best screening test**; negative rules out SLE.<br>• Titer $\ge 1:80$ on HEp-2 cells required as EULAR/ACR entry criterion. |
| **Anti-dsDNA** | $70\%$ | **$>97\%$** | • Highly specific.<br>• **Titers correlate with disease activity, especially Lupus Nephritis flares**.<br>• Consumes complement when active. |
| **Anti-Smith (Anti-Sm)** | $25-30\%$ | **$>99\%$** | • Highly specific; diagnostics hallmark.<br>• Target is core proteins of small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs).<br>• **Does not correlate with disease activity**. |
| **Anti-SSA (Ro)**<br>**Anti-SSB (La)** | $30-50\%$ | Low | • Associated with photosensitivity, subacute cutaneous lupus (SCLE).<br>• Associated with **Sjögren syndrome**.<br>• **Neonatal Lupus / Congenital Complete Heart Block** risk. |
| **Anti-Histone** | $95\%$ | Low | • Diagnostic for **Drug-Induced Lupus (DIL)**.<br>• Positive in $95\%$ of DIL, but only $50\%$ of systemic SLE. |
| **Anti-Ribosomal P** | Low | High | • Associated with **neuropsychiatric lupus** (specifically lupus psychosis). |
| **Anti-U1-RNP** | Low | Low | • High titers diagnostic for **Mixed Connective Tissue Disease (MCTD)**. |
| **Antiphospholipid (aPL)** | $30-40\%$ | Low | • Lupus anticoagulant (LA), anti-cardiolipin (aCL), anti-$\beta_2$-gpI.<br>• Predisposes to hypercoagulable state (**Antiphospholipid Syndrome**). |

## 📋 2019 EULAR / ACR Classification Criteria

Requires meeting the **Entry Criterion**, then scoring **$\ge 10$ points** from clinical and immunological domains (at least one clinical domain must be present).

* **Entry Criterion**: ANA titer $\ge 1:80$ on HEp-2 cells (or equivalent positive test).
* **Clinical Domains & Points**:
  * **Constitutional**: Fever (2 pts).
  * **Cutaneous**: Non-scarring alopecia (2 pts), Oral ulcers (2 pts), Subacute cutaneous OR discoid lupus (4 pts), Acute cutaneous lupus/malar rash (6 pts).
  * **Joint**: Joint involvement $\ge 2$ joints with synovitis or tenderness and morning stiffness $\ge 30$ mins (6 pts).
  * **Neurologic**: Delirium (2 pts), Psychosis (3 pts), Seizures (5 pts).
  * **Serosal**: Pleural/pericardial effusion or acute pericarditis (5 pts), Acute pleural/pericardial rub or chest pain (6 pts).
  * **Hematologic**: Leukopenia (3 pts), Thrombocytopenia (4 pts), Autoimmune hemolysis (4 pts).
  * **Renal**: Proteinuria $>0.5$ g/24h (4 pts), Biopsy Class II or V LN (8 pts), **Biopsy Class III or IV LN (10 pts)**.
* **Immunological Domains & Points**:
  * **Antiphospholipid Antibodies**: Anti-cardiolipin, anti-$\beta_2$-gpI, or Lupus Anticoagulant (2 pts).
  * **Complement Proteins**: Low C3 OR low C4 (3 pts), **Low C3 AND low C4** (4 pts).
  * **SLE-Specific Antibodies**: **Anti-dsDNA OR Anti-Sm** (6 pts).

## 🏥 Management

Treatments are tailored to disease severity and specific organ involvement.

### 1. General & Baseline Therapy (All Patients)
* **Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ)**: **5 mg/kg/day PO** (Max 400 mg/day).
  * *Mechanism*: Concentrates in lysosomes, increases pH, inhibits antigen presentation, and blocks Toll-like receptor (TLR-7 and TLR-9) signaling.
  * *Benefit*: Prevents flares, reduces thrombotic risk, limits organ damage accumulation, and improves survival.
  * *Monitoring*: Baseline and annual ophthalmology exams (SD-OCT, visual fields) starting after 5 years of use to screen for **bull's-eye maculopathy** (retinal toxicity).
* **Sun Protection**: Daily application of broad-spectrum SPF $\ge 30$ sunscreen, UV-blocking clothing, and sun avoidance.

### 2. Mild and Moderate SLE
* **Mild (Arthralgias, Mild Rash)**: NSAIDs, topical steroids, and low-dose oral Prednisolone ($\le 7.5-10$ mg/day).
* **Moderate (Serositis, Cutaneous flares, Cytopenias)**: Moderate-dose Prednisolone ($0.5$ mg/kg/day) bridged with steroid-sparing agents: **Methotrexate** (10-25 mg/week PO/SC) or **Azathioprine** (1.5-2.5 mg/kg/day PO).

### 3. Severe Organ-Threatening SLE & Lupus Nephritis (Class III / IV)

#### Phase 1: Induction Therapy (Goal: Remission)
1. **Glucocorticoids**: IV Methylprednisolone pulse ($500–1000$ mg IV daily for 3 days), followed by high-dose oral Prednisolone ($0.5-1.0$ mg/kg/day) tapered over 3-6 months.
2. **Immunosuppressive Options**:
   * **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**: 2.0–3.0 g/day PO.
     * *Mechanism*: Reversibly inhibits IMPDH (inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase) $\rightarrow$ selectively blocks de novo purine synthesis in B and T lymphocytes, halting proliferation.
     * *First-line* for Class III/IV, particularly in patients of African or Hispanic descent.
   * **Cyclophosphamide (CYC)**: Low-dose IV (Euro-Lupus protocol: 500 mg IV every 2 weeks for 6 doses) OR High-dose IV (NIH protocol: 500-1000 mg/$m^2$ monthly for 6 months).
     * *Mechanism*: Alkylating agent crosslinking DNA.
     * *Toxicity Management*: Co-administer **heavy IV hydration** and **Mesna** (2-mercaptoethane sulfonate; binds toxic metabolite **acrolein** to prevent **hemorrhagic cystitis** and bladder cancer).
     * *Ovarian Protection*: Co-administer GnRH agonist (e.g., Leuprolide) to protect ovaries from premature failure/infertility.

#### Phase 2: Maintenance Therapy (Goal: Prevent Flares)
* Continue **MMF** (1.0–2.0 g/day PO) OR **Azathioprine** (2.0 mg/kg/day PO) combined with lowest effective dose of Prednisolone.
* Duration: Minimum 3–5 years before attempting gradual taper/withdrawal.

#### Targeted Biologics
* **Belimumab**: Monoclonal antibody targeting **BLyS (B-lymphocyte stimulator) / BAFF**. Inhibits B-cell survival. Approved for active systemic SLE and active lupus nephritis.
* **Anifrolumab**: Monoclonal antibody targeting **Type I Interferon Receptor (IFNAR1)**. Approved for moderate-to-severe systemic SLE.
* **Rituximab**: Chimeric anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Used off-label for refractory LN or severe autoimmune cytopenias.

## 🤰 Pregnancy in SLE

* **Planning**: Conceive only when disease is in **remission for $\ge 6$ months** (especially lupus nephritis).
* **Risks**: High risk of maternal flares, preeclampsia, spontaneous abortion, intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), and preterm birth.
* **Congenital Heart Block**: Associated with **maternal Anti-SSA/Ro and Anti-SSB/La** antibodies. Antibodies cross placenta and cause inflammatory fibrosis of the fetal AV node. Permanent, requires pacemaker at birth. Screen with fetal echocardiography weekly from weeks 16 to 26.
* **Medication Safety in Pregnancy**:
  * **Safe**: Hydroxychloroquine (essential to continue; prevents flares during pregnancy), low-dose Prednisolone, Azathioprine, and Calcineurin inhibitors (Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus).
  * **Contraindicated (Teratogenic)**: MMF (highly teratogenic, causes craniofacial/limb defects), Cyclophosphamide, Methotrexate, Leflunomide, ACE inhibitors.

### Lupus Nephritis Flare vs. Preeclampsia Differentiation

| Feature | Lupus Nephritis Flare | Preeclampsia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Any trimester | Almost always $>20$ weeks gestation |
| **Systemic Signs** | Fever, malar rash, arthritis, active serositis | Headache, visual changes, RUQ pain (HELLP) |
| **Complement (C3, C4)** | **Low** (complement consumption) | Normal or elevated (acute phase reactant) |
| **Anti-dsDNA** | **Rising titers** | Stable / negative |
| **Urine Sediment** | **Active**: RBCs, dysmorphic RBCs, RBC/WBC casts | **Inactive**: pure proteinuria, no casts/hematuria |
| **Serum Uric Acid** | Normal | **Elevated** |

## 💊 Drug-Induced Lupus (DIL)

* **Definition**: A lupus-like syndrome temporally related to drug exposure, resolving after drug withdrawal.
* **High-Risk Offending Drugs (SHIPP)**:
  * **S**ulfonamides.
  * **H**ydralazine (highest risk; risk correlates with slow-acetylator status).
  * **I**soniazid.
  * **P**rocainamide (highest rate of antibody induction).
  * **P**henytoin.
  * *Others: Minocycline, TNF-alpha inhibitors (etanercept, infliximab).*
* **Immunological Markers**:
  * **Anti-Histone Antibodies**: **Positive in $>95\%$** of cases.
  * ANA positive.
  * Anti-dsDNA and Anti-Sm are typically **negative**. Complement levels are normal.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Fever, myalgia, arthralgia, arthritis, pleuritis, pericarditis.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Organ Sparing**: Drug-Induced Lupus characteristically **spares the kidneys (nephritis) and the central nervous system (CNS)**. If active nephritis or CNS signs are present, suspect idiopathic SLE, not DIL.

## 🩸 Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) in SLE

* **Definition**: Systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by venous/arterial thrombosis or pregnancy morbidity in the presence of persistent antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL).
* **Clinical**:
  * **Vascular Thrombosis**: Deep vein thrombosis (most common), pulmonary embolism, arterial stroke, myocardial infarction.
  * **Pregnancy Morbidity**: $\ge 3$ consecutive spontaneous abortions before week 10, $\ge 1$ unexplained fetal death at $\ge 10$ weeks, or $\ge 1$ premature birth before week 34 due to severe preeclampsia or placental insufficiency.
  * **Other**: **Livedo reticularis** (lace-like purplish skin discoloration), thrombocytopenia, cardiac valve thickening.
* **Lab Diagnosis**: Requires $\ge 1$ clinical criterion + $\ge 1$ lab criterion positive on **two separate occasions at least 12 weeks apart**:
  * **Lupus Anticoagulant (LA)**: Prolongs phospholipid-dependent clotting tests (aPTT, dRVVT). Paradoxically **prolongs aPTT in vitro**, but causes **thrombosis in vivo**. Mixing study with normal plasma does not correct clotting time (indicates presence of inhibitor), but corrects when excess phospholipid is added.
  * **Anti-cardiolipin (aCL)** IgG/IgM (medium-high titer).
  * **Anti-$\beta_2$-glycoprotein I** IgG/IgM (medium-high titer).
* **Management**:
  * **Acute thrombosis**: Therapeutic Heparin bridged to **Warfarin** (long-term).
  * **Target INR**: **$2.0–3.0$** for venous thrombosis; **$3.0$** (or combination therapy) for arterial thrombosis or recurrent events on therapeutic Warfarin.
  * **Obstetric APS**: Prophylactic/therapeutic LMWH + low-dose Aspirin (81 mg) daily throughout pregnancy.
  * > [!CAUTION]
    > **DOAC Warning**: Direct Oral Anticoagulants (Rivaroxaban, Apixaban, Dabigatran) are **contraindicated** in triple-positive APS patients due to high rates of recurrent thrombosis compared to Warfarin.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Complement and dsDNA Flare Monitoring**: Active lupus nephritis consumes complement. An impending flare is heralded by a **rapid rise in anti-dsDNA titers** matching a **drop in serum C3 and C4** levels.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Acrolein and Hemorrhagic Cystitis**: Cyclophosphamide's metabolite acrolein accumulates in the bladder, causing necrotizing cystitis and hematuria. Always enforce aggressive hydration and administer **Mesna** to neutralize acrolein.
* > [!TIP]
  > **False-Positive Syphilis**: Antiphospholipid antibodies (specifically anticardiolipin) bind cardiolipin, which is the antigen used in the VDRL/RPR screening tests for syphilis. SLE patients with aPL will have a **false-positive VDRL/RPR**, but a **negative FTA-ABS/TPPA** (specific treponemal tests).
