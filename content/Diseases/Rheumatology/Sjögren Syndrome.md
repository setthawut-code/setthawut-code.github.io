# 🦴 Sjögren Syndrome (กลุ่มอาการโจเกรน / โรคตาแห้ง-ปากแห้ง)

> **Definition**: Sjögren Syndrome is a chronic, systemic autoimmune disease characterized by progressive lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine glands, primarily the lacrimal and salivary glands. This leads to glandular dysfunction, manifesting as dry eyes (**xerophthalmia**) and dry mouth (**xerostomia**), collectively known as **sicca syndrome**. It can occur as a primary condition or secondary to other connective tissue diseases (most commonly Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE, or Systemic Sclerosis).

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Gender Ratio**: Extremely female-predominant, with a **$9:1$** female-to-male ratio.
* **Age of Onset**: Commonly diagnosed in middle-aged women, peaking between ages 40 and 60.
* **Malignancy Risk**: Associated with a **44-fold increased risk** of developing **Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma** (specifically marginal zone B-cell lymphoma / MALT lymphoma of the parotid gland).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **Target**: Exocrine glands (salivary, lacrimal, sebaceous, mucous).
2. **Infiltration**: Chronic activation of B-cells and T-helper cells (primarily CD4+ T-cells) leads to infiltration of the salivary and lacrimal glandular parenchyma.
3. **Destruction & Atrophy**: Infiltrating cells form focal aggregates, releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as IFN-$\alpha$, TNF-$\alpha$, and IL-6) that drive acinar cell apoptosis and progressive ductal destruction, replacing glandular tissue with fibrous tissue.
4. **B-Cell Hyperactivation**: Chronic stimulation of B-cells leads to hypergammaglobulinemia, autoantibody production (RF, anti-SSA/Ro, anti-SSB/La), and oligoclonal B-cell expansion, which can transition into malignant B-cell lymphoma.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Glandular / Sicca Symptoms
* **Xerophthalmia (Dry Eyes)**: Sensation of sand, grit, or gravel in the eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca). Symptoms include burning, redness, itching, and photophobia.
* **Xerostomia (Dry Mouth)**: Difficulty swallowing dry foods (requires drinking fluids to swallow), difficulty speaking continuously, altered taste, accelerated dental decay (rampant caries), and recurrent oral candidiasis (thrush) due to loss of protective saliva.
* **Salivary Gland Enlargement**: Bilateral, painless, recurrent or persistent swelling of the **parotid glands** or submandibular glands.
* **Other Sicca Features**: Dry skin, dry nasal passages, dry trachea (causing chronic dry cough), and dyspareunia (due to loss of vaginal lubrication).

### 2. Extra-glandular Manifestations (Systemic)
* **Musculoskeletal**: Symmetric polyarthralgias or mild, non-erosive arthritis affecting small joints.
* **Cutaneous**: Palpable purpura (indicating cutaneous leukocytoclastic vasculitis, associated with hypergammaglobulinemia) or Raynaud's phenomenon.
* **Renal**: **Interstitial Nephritis** (leading to distal **Renal Tubular Acidosis / Type 1 RTA**, presenting as hypokalemia and metabolic acidosis) or glomerulonephritis (rare, immune complex-mediated).
* **Neurological**: Sensory peripheral neuropathy or mononeuritis multiplex.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Drug-Induced Sicca**: Anticholinergics, tricyclic antidepressants, antihistamines, beta-blockers, and diuretics. (Review medication list first).
* **Age-Related Sicca (Senile Xerostomia)**: Atrophy of exocrine glands in elderly patients. Biopsy shows no lymphocytic infiltrates.
* **Chronic Infections**: **Hepatitis C**, **HIV** (can present with parotid gland enlargement and dry mouth, mimicking Sjögren's), and **Tuberculosis**.
* **Sarcoidosis**: Can infiltrate salivary glands and mimic sicca, but biopsy will show non-caseating granulomas.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

### 1. Objective Dryness Assessment
* **Schirmer's I Test**: Assesses tear production. A strip of filter paper is placed in the lower conjunctival sac.
  * **Positive**: **$\le 5$ mm of moisture after 5 minutes** in one or both eyes.
* **Ocular Surface Staining**: Fluorescein or Lissamine Green dye shows corneal epithelial damage (keratoconjunctivitis sicca).
* **Unstimulated Salivary Flow**: A collection of total saliva spit over 15 minutes.
  * **Positive**: **$< 0.1$ mL/minute**.

### 2. Laboratory (Serology)
* **Antinuclear Antibody (ANA)**: Positive in $>80\%$ of patients.
* **Anti-SSA/Ro**: Positive in $70-80\%$ of primary cases. (Associated with neonatal lupus/congenital heart block in pregnant patients).
* **Anti-SSB/La**: Positive in $40-50\%$ of cases. Almost always co-exists with anti-SSA.
* **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**: Positive in $>60\%$ of patients, even in primary Sjögren's.
* **Hypergammaglobulinemia**: Elevated IgG, which can lead to a high erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR).

### 3. Minor Salivary Gland Biopsy (Lip Biopsy - Diagnostic Gold Standard)
* Done by making a small incision in the inner lower lip to harvest 4-6 minor salivary glands.
* **Diagnostic Finding**: **Focus Score $\ge 1$**: Defined as an aggregate of **$\ge 50$ lymphocytes** per $4\text{ mm}^2$ of glandular tissue.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
### 2016 ACR / EULAR Classification Criteria (Requires score $\ge 4 / 9$)
* Minor salivary gland biopsy with focus score $\ge 1$ (3 points).
* Positive Anti-SSA/Ro antibody (3 points).
* Ocular staining score $\ge 5$ in at least one eye (1 point).
* Schirmer's test $\le 5$ mm/5 mins in at least one eye (1 point).
* Unstimulated salivary flow rate $\le 0.1$ mL/min (1 point).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Xerophthalmia (Dry Eyes)
* Avoid dry, drafty environments.
* **Preservative-free artificial tears** during the day and lubricating ointments at night.
* Topical anti-inflammatory drops (e.g., Cyclosporine or Lifitegrast drops) to stimulate tear production.
* Punctal plugs (surgical occlusion of tear ducts) to retain tears.

### 2. Xerostomia (Dry Mouth)
* Constant sips of water; avoid sugary, acidic foods and caffeine.
* Sugar-free gum or lozenges to stimulate saliva.
* **Fluoride therapy** and strict dental hygiene to prevent rampant caries.
* **Secretagogues (Muscarinic Agonists)**: **Pilocarpine** (5 mg PO tid/qid) or **Cevimeline** (30 mg PO tid). Side effects include sweating, flushing, and urinary frequency.

### 3. Systemic / Extra-glandular disease
* **Hydroxychloroquine**: Effective for managing arthralgias and mild arthritis.
* **Systemic Corticosteroids** or immunosuppressants (Methotrexate, Azathioprine) for severe vasculitis, nephritis, or lung disease.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Neonatal Lupus Risk**: Anti-SSA/Ro and Anti-SSB/La autoantibodies can cross the placenta. In pregnant patients with Sjögren's or SLE, these antibodies target the fetal cardiac conduction system, predisposing the fetus to **congenital complete heart block** (detectable by fetal echocardiography at 16-26 weeks).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Lymphoma Surveillance**: If a patient with longstanding Sjögren's syndrome develops **persistent, unilateral parotid gland enlargement**, **unexplained weight loss**, **new-onset lymphadenopathy**, or a **drop in serum IgG/RF levels**, immediately obtain a biopsy. These are red flags for transformation into B-cell lymphoma.
