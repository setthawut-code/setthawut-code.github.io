## type: disease

# Definition
Reactive Arthritis (ReA), formerly known as Reiter's syndrome, is a sterile, inflammatory, asymmetric oligoarthritis that develops 1 to 4 weeks following an extra-articular infection, typically of the gastrointestinal (GI) or genitourinary (GU) tract. It is classified under the **seronegative spondyloarthropathies**.

# Epidemiology
* **Incidence**: Most commonly affects young adults aged 20 to 40.
* **Gender Ratio**: 
  * Post-GU (venereal) form: Predominantly male ($9:1$ male-to-female ratio).
  * Post-GI (enteric) form: Equal distribution ($1:1$ ratio).
* **Genetic Association**: Strong association with the **HLA-B27** allele. About 30-50% of all ReA patients are HLA-B27 positive, rising to over 80% in patients with chronic, severe, or recurrent disease.

# Etiology
ReA is triggered by specific mucosal pathogens:
* **Genitourinary Pathogens**:
  * ***Chlamydia trachomatis*** (the most common cause overall).
  * *Ureaplasma urealyticum*.
* **Gastrointestinal Pathogens**:
  * ***Salmonella enterica*** (various serovars).
  * ***Shigella*** (*S. flexneri*, *S. dysenteriae*).
  * ***Yersinia*** (*Y. enterocolitica*, *Y. pseudotuberculosis*).
  * ***Campylobacter jejuni***.
  * *Clostridioides difficile*.

# Pathophysiology
1. **Mucosal Invasion**: The pathogen invades the GU or GI mucosal lining.
2. **Antigen Trafficking**: Instead of being cleared completely, bacterial antigens (e.g., lipopolysaccharide, outer membrane proteins) are processed by macrophages and dendritic cells and traffic via the bloodstream to the joints.
3. **Synovial Inflammation**: The joint space itself remains sterile (no viable replicating bacteria can be cultured from the joint, hence "reactive"), but bacterial fragments stimulate a local T-cell-mediated immune response.
4. **Molecular Mimicry**: In HLA-B27 positive individuals, CD8+ cytotoxic T-cells recognize self-antigens in the synovium that structurally resemble the bacterial peptides. This cross-reactivity triggers a massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (specifically **TNF-$\alpha$ and IL-17/IL-23**), leading to synovitis, enthesitis, and extra-articular manifestations.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Onset**: 1–4 weeks following an episode of urethritis, cervicitis, or acute infectious diarrhea.
* **Arthritis**:
  * **Asymmetric, oligoarticular** (involving $\le 4$ joints).
  * Predominantly affects **large, weight-bearing joints of the lower extremities** (knees, ankles, subtalar joints).
* **Enthesitis**: Inflammation at the site of tendon or ligament insertion into bone. Most commonly presents as **Achilles tendonitis** or **plantar fasciitis** (heel pain).
* **Dactylitis ("Sausage Digit")**: Diffuse swelling of an entire finger or toe due to simultaneous inflammation of the joints and surrounding flexor tendons.
* **Inflammatory Back Pain**: Occurs in $50\%$ of patients due to sacroiliitis or spondylitis.

## Extra-articular Manifestations
* **Ocular**:
  * **Conjunctivitis**: Typically bilateral, mild, and self-limiting; occurs early.
  * **Acute Anterior Uveitis**: Unilateral, painful red eye with photophobia, blurred vision, and ciliary flush. Requires urgent ophthalmology referral.
* **Genitourinary**:
  * **Urethritis** / **Cervicitis**: Sterile, presenting as dysuria or vaginal/urethral discharge.
  * **Circinate Balanitis**: Painless, shallow, serpiginous ulcerations on the glans penis (more common in uncircumcised males).
* **Cutaneous & Mucosal**:
  * **Keratoderma Blennorrhagicum**: Hyperkeratotic, erythematous, papulovesicular skin lesions on the soles of the feet and palms of the hands (clinically indistinguishable from pustular psoriasis).
  * **Painless Oral Ulcers**: Located on the tongue or hard palate.
  * **Nail Changes**: Dystrophy and subungual hyperkeratosis.

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Infectious Arthritis]] (Septic Arthritis)**: The most critical differential. Septic arthritis is usually monoarticular, shows extremely high synovial WBC count ($>50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with $>75\%$ PMNs), and has a **positive** Gram stain or culture. ReA is sterile.
* **Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI)**: Presents with migratory tenosynovitis, purpuric skin lesions, and arthritis. Differentiated by positive NAAT for *N. gonorrhoeae* in GU tract, joint fluid, or blood.
* **[[Gout]] / [[Pseudogout]]**: Crystal arthropathies. Confirmed by identifying monosodium urate or calcium pyrophosphate crystals in the synovial fluid.
* **Acute Rheumatic Fever**: Post-streptococcal (group A) autoimmune disease. Affects younger populations, presenting with migratory polyarthritis, carditis, chorea, and elevated ASO titer.

# Investigations

## Synovial Fluid Analysis
* **Appearance**: Turbid, yellow.
* **WBC Count**: Elevated ($2,000 - 50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with PMN predominance), indicating an inflammatory profile.
* **Microbiology**: **Gram stain and cultures are negative** (rules out septic arthritis).

## Laboratory
* **Inflammatory Markers**: Elevated ESR and CRP.
* **HLA-B27**: Positive in $30-50\%$ of cases (supports diagnosis and portends chronicity).
* **Pathogen Detection**:
  * **Genitourinary**: Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT) for *Chlamydia trachomatis* on a first-catch urine specimen or cervical swab (mandatory even if asymptomatic).
  * **Gastrointestinal**: Stool culture and PCR for enteric pathogens (only useful if diarrhea is active).
* **Serology**: Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP are **negative** (hence "seronegative").

## Imaging
* **Plain Radiography**:
  * In acute phase: Soft tissue swelling, periarticular osteopenia.
  * In chronic phase: Asymmetric **sacroiliitis**, heel spurs (due to Achilles or plantar enthesitis), and bulky, asymmetric **syndesmophytes** along the spine.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
ReA is a clinical diagnosis. The diagnostic suspicion is established by the presence of:
1. Acute asymmetric oligoarthritis, predominantly of the lower extremities.
2. Clinical or laboratory evidence of a preceding GU or GI infection within the past 4 weeks.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* **First-line Therapy (NSAIDs)**:
  * High-dose oral NSAIDs (e.g., **Indomethacin 50 mg PO tid** or **Naproxen 500 mg PO bid**) for at least 2-4 weeks.
* **Intra-articular Corticosteroid Injection**:
  * Highly effective for patients with persistent, severe mono- or oligoarthritis when infection has been completely ruled out.
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**:
  * Oral Prednisolone (20–40 mg daily) is reserved for severe polyarthritis, severe enthesitis, or constitutional symptoms unresponsive to NSAIDs.
* **Antimicrobial Therapy**:
  * Indicated **only** if there is active *Chlamydia trachomatis* infection identified on NAAT (e.g., **Doxycycline 100 mg PO bid for 7 days**). Antibiotic therapy does not shorten or cure the post-infectious arthritis itself.

## Definitive / Chronic Management (For symptoms $>3-6$ months)
* **First-line DMARD**: **Sulfasalazine (2-3 g/day)** is the preferred choice for chronic peripheral arthritis.
* **Second-line DMARD**: Methotrexate (7.5-25 mg weekly) or Azathioprine.
* **Biologic Therapy**: TNF-$\alpha$ inhibitors (e.g., **Etanercept**, **Adalimumab**) are indicated for severe, chronic ReA refractory to NSAIDs and conventional DMARDs.

---

# Complications
* **Chronic Arthritis**: About 15-20% of patients progress to a chronic, destructive arthropathy or ankylosing spondylitis-like disease.
* **Ocular Impairment**: Recurrent anterior uveitis can lead to cataracts, glaucoma, and permanent vision loss if untreated.
* **Cardiovascular**: Rare cases of aortic regurgitation and conduction blocks in chronic, severe HLA-B27 positive disease.

# Prognosis
* Excellent for the majority of patients; $60-80\%$ experience complete resolution within 3 to 6 months.
* Chronic or recurrent symptoms occur in $20-40\%$ of cases, particularly in those who are HLA-B27 positive.

# Clinical Pearls
> [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Antibiotic Trap**: Do not prescribe antibiotics to treat Reactive Arthritis secondary to *gastrointestinal* infections (e.g., *Salmonella*, *Shigella*). By the time arthritis develops, the enteric pathogen has already been cleared, and antibiotics do not alter the course of the joint inflammation. Only treat active *Chlamydia* GU infections.

>[!WARNING]
  > **Uveitis Urgency**: Conjunctivitis in ReA is benign and self-limiting, but **anterior uveitis** is a medical emergency that can cause permanent blindness. Check for photophobia, pain, and a ciliary flush (redness concentrated around the cornea) and refer immediately to ophthalmology.
