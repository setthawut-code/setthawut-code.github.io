# 🦴 Polymyositis (โรคกล้ามเนื้ออักเสบหลายมัด)

> **Definition**: Polymyositis (PM) is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory myopathy characterized by subacute, progressive, symmetric proximal muscle weakness in the **absence** of cutaneous involvement. It is driven by CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell-mediated destruction of myofibers.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Gender Ratio**: Twice as common in females ($2:1$ female-to-male ratio).
* **Age of Onset**: Primarily affects adults between the ages of 40 and 60. (Rarely diagnosed in childhood).
* **Malignancy Risk**: Associated with an increased risk of internal malignancy, though the association is significantly less pronounced than in Dermatomyositis.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
PM is characterized by a cell-mediated autoimmune attack directly targeting the endomysial space:
1. **Aberrant Antigen Expression**: Healthy skeletal muscle fibers aberrantly express **MHC Class I** molecules on their surface (normal muscle fibers do not express MHC Class I).
2. **T-Cell Recognition**: Antigen-specific **CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocytes** recognize self-antigens presented by these MHC Class I molecules on myofibers.
3. **Endomysial Infiltration**: CD8+ T-cells and macrophages infiltrate the **endomysium** (the connective tissue surrounding individual muscle fibers).
4. **Myofiber Necrosis**: Cytotoxic T-cells release perforin and granzymes, directly leading to necrosis, degeneration, and regeneration of myofibers.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│     ABERRANT EXPRESSION OF MHC-I ON SKELETAL MYOFIBERS │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ▼
          CD8+ Cytotoxic T-Cell Antigen Recognition
                            │
                            ▼
     T-Cell & Macrophage Infiltration of the Endomysium
                            │ (CD8+ cells surround healthy fibers)
                            ▼
      Perforin & Granzyme Mediated Myofiber Necrosis
```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Muscle Weakness
* **Symmetric, Proximal Weakness**: Progressive, symmetric weakness affecting the shoulder girdle (difficulty lifting objects, brushing hair) and hip girdle (difficulty climbing stairs, rising from a low chair without using arms).
* **Rate of Progression**: Typically develops subacutely over weeks to months.
* **Striated Upper Esophageal Muscle Involvement**: Presents as dysphagia, dysarthria, and an increased risk of aspiration.
* **Normal Neuro Exam**: Sensation and deep tendon reflexes are characteristically **normal** (distinguishes it from neuropathy/myasthenia).

### 2. Lack of Rashes
* **Clinical Distinction**: PM is defined by the **absence** of the pathognomonic rashes of Dermatomyositis (no Heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules, or photosensitive erythema).

### 3. Systemic Manifestations
* **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**: Occurs in $30-40\%$ of patients. Strongly associated with **Anti-Jo-1** antibodies, causing progressive dyspnea and dry cough.
* **Polyarthritis**: Symmetric, non-erosive inflammatory arthritis of small joints, mimicking RA.
* **Raynaud's Phenomenon**.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Dermatomyositis]] (DM)**: Distinguished by pathognomonic cutaneous manifestations (Heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules) and a perivascular CD4+ complement-mediated capillary pathology on biopsy.
* **Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)**: Most common in older males ($>50$ years). Characterized by **asymmetric** weakness, predominantly involving **distal** muscles (finger flexors causing poor grip) and quadriceps. Biopsy shows rimmed vacuoles. Resistant to steroids.
* **Drug-Induced Myopathy (Statins)**: Myalgias, elevated CK, resolves after stopping statins.
* **Glucocorticoid-Induced Myopathy**: Proximal weakness but **muscle enzymes (CK) are completely normal** and there is no inflammation on biopsy.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **Muscle Enzymes**:
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: Elevated **$5 - 50$ times** the upper limit of normal during active disease.
  * **Aldolase**, **AST, ALT, and LDH**: Also elevated.
* **Myositis-Specific Antibodies (MSAs)**:
  * **Anti-Jo-1** (Anti-synthetase): Associated with **Antisynthetase Syndrome** (myositis, ILD, mechanic's hands, fever, Raynaud's, arthritis).
  * **Anti-Signal Recognition Particle (Anti-SRP)**: Strongly associated with acute, severe necrotizing autoimmune myopathy (NAM) with cardiac involvement and a poor response to steroids.

### Imaging & Diagnostics
* **Muscle MRI (T2/STIR)**: Shows hyperintensity (muscle edema) in active disease. Used to guide muscle biopsy.
* **Electromyography (EMG)**: Shows a myopathic triad (short-duration, small-amplitude MUAPs; increased insertional activity/fibrillations; high-frequency repetitive discharges).

### Muscle Biopsy (Diagnostic Gold Standard)
* Key finding: **Endomysial inflammatory infiltrates** consisting predominantly of **CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocytes** surrounding and invading healthy and necrotic muscle fibers.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Corticosteroid Therapy
* **First-line**: High-dose oral **Prednisolone (1 mg/kg/day)**. If bulbar or respiratory muscles are involved, start with IV Methylprednisolone pulse (1 g IV daily for 3 days). 
* *Taper*: Maintain high dose for 4-6 weeks until muscle strength improves and CK levels normalize, then taper slowly over 9-12 months.

### 2. Steroid-Sparing Agents (Conventional DMARDs)
* Started early to reduce steroid side effects.
  * **Methotrexate** or **Azathioprine** (preferred if the patient has co-existing interstitial lung disease or liver disease).

### 3. Refractory Disease
* **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**.
* **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**: Preferred for active myositis-associated ILD.
* **Rituximab**.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **MHC Class I Staining**: In histopathology, healthy muscle fibers do not express MHC Class I. The diffuse, upregulation and expression of MHC Class I on the sarcolemma of myofibers is a highly sensitive and specific diagnostic marker for polymyositis and dermatomyositis, even in areas without prominent inflammatory infiltrates.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The Steroid CK Rule**: In a patient on high-dose steroids who develops worsening weakness:
  > * If **CK is elevated** $\rightarrow$ disease flare (increase steroids).
  > * If **CK is normal** $\rightarrow$ steroid myopathy (taper steroids and begin physical therapy).
