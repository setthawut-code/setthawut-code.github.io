# 🦴 Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR / โรคปวดกล้ามเนื้อข้อต่อหลากที่)

> **Definition**: Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR) is a chronic, systemic inflammatory disorder of older adults characterized by proximal aching and morning stiffness in the shoulder girdle, pelvic girdle (hips), and neck. It is highly associated with **Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA)**.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Age Limit**: Almost **exclusively affects patients age $>50$ years**, with the average age of onset being 70 years.
* **Gender Ratio**: Twice as common in females ($2:1$ female-to-male ratio).
* **Ethnic Predominance**: Most common in Caucasians of Northern European descent.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
Unlike inflammatory myositis (which targets muscle fibers), PMR targets proximal synovial structures and surrounding bursae:
* **Synovial Inflammation**: Characterized by subclinical synovitis, tenosynovitis, and bilateral **subacromial / subdeltoid bursitis** (shoulders) and **trochanteric bursitis** (hips).
* **Immune Dysregulation**: Driven by dendritic cell activation, recruiting macrophages and T-cells, which release high levels of **IL-6** (the primary driver of the marked systemic inflammatory response).
* **Genetic Link**: Strong association with **HLA-DR4** alleles.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Symptoms
* **Proximal Aching & Pain**: Symmetric pain affecting the neck, shoulders, and hip girdles.
* **Morning Stiffness (The Hallmark)**: Severe, symmetric morning stiffness lasting **$>45$ minutes** (often hours). Stiffness worsens after periods of physical inactivity (the "gel phenomenon").
* **Constitutional Symptoms**: Low-grade fever, malaise, fatigue, anorexia, and weight loss.

### 2. Physical Examination
* **Normal Muscle Strength**: There is **no true muscle weakness** (manual muscle testing is normal once pain is controlled). Weakness on initial exam is due to pain inhibition.
* **Range of Motion**: Active range of motion of the shoulders and hips is limited by pain. Passive range of motion is relatively preserved.
* **Mild Synovitis**: Transient, mild asymmetric peripheral arthritis (e.g., knee or wrist swelling) can occur in $20\%$ of patients.

### 3. Connection to Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA)
* **PMR to GCA**: Approximately **$15 - 20\%$** of patients with PMR will develop GCA.
* **GCA to PMR**: Up to **$40 - 60\%$** of patients with GCA have co-existing symptoms of PMR.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **GCA Screen Requirement**: All patients diagnosed with PMR must be screened at every visit for symptoms of GCA: new headache, jaw claudication (pain when chewing), scalp tenderness, visual changes (diplopia, loss of vision), and prominent temporal arteries.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Polymyositis (PM)**: Differentiated by **true muscle weakness** on exam, marked elevation of **Creatine Kinase (CK)**, and endomysial inflammation on biopsy (PMR has normal CK and no muscle pathology).
* **Late-Onset Rheumatoid Arthritis (LORA)**: Can present similarly with symmetric shoulder pain and high ESR. However, LORA has positive RF/anti-CCP, DIP/MCP synovitis, and can lead to marginal bone erosions.
* **Fibromyalgia**: Widespread chronic pain in younger patients, but **inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP) are completely normal** and there is no morning stiffness improvement with steroids.
* **Rotator Cuff Pathology / Shoulder Osteoarthritis**: Pain is typically unilateral and localized, with normal inflammatory markers.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **Inflammatory Markers**: Characteristically **markedly elevated ESR (often $>40-50\text{ mm/hour}$)** and elevated CRP.
* **Muscle Enzymes**: **Creatine Kinase (CK) and Aldolase are completely normal** (essential to differentiate from myositis).
* **Serology**: Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP are **negative**.
* **CBC**: Normocytic normochromic anemia (anemia of chronic disease) and thrombocytosis.

### Imaging (Joint Ultrasound / MRI)
* Shows bilateral **subacromial / subdeltoid bursitis** and bicipital tenosynovitis (shoulders) and trochanteric bursitis (hips). Highly useful to confirm proximal periarticular inflammation.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
### 2012 ACR / EULAR Classification Criteria (Requires score $\ge 4 / 6$ without ultrasound)
* Age $\ge 50$ years.
* Bilateral shoulder pain.
* Elevated ESR and/or CRP.
* *Plus clinical points*:
  * Morning stiffness $>45$ minutes (2 points).
  * Hip pain or limited mobility (1 point).
  * Absence of Rheumatoid Factor and anti-CCP (2 points).
  * Absence of other peripheral joint involvement (1 point).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Low-Dose Corticosteroid Therapy (The Standard of Care)
* **First-line**: **Prednisolone 12.5 - 15 mg PO daily** as a single dose.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Diagnostic Steroid Response**: PMR features a **dramatic, rapid clinical response** to low-dose steroids. Pain and stiffness should improve by $>70\%$ within **24 - 48 hours** (and inflammatory markers normalize within 2-4 weeks). If the patient does not show a dramatic response within 3-4 days, the diagnosis of PMR is highly questionable; consider alternative diagnoses.
* **Taper Protocol**: Once symptoms resolve and ESR/CRP normalize, taper Prednisolone slowly (e.g., reduce by 2.5 mg every 4 weeks to 10 mg/day, then by 1 mg every 4 weeks) over **1 to 2 years** to avoid relapse.

### 2. Steroid-Sparing Agents
* If a patient has multiple relapses or cannot taper steroids due to toxicity (e.g., severe osteoporosis, uncontrolled diabetes), add **Methotrexate (7.5-15 mg PO once weekly)**.

### 3. Osteoporosis Prophylaxis
* Because patients require steroid therapy for $\ge 1-2$ years, co-prescribe **Calcium (1,000-1,200 mg daily)** and **Vitamin D3 (800-1,000 IU daily)**. Obtain a baseline DEXA scan.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Weakness vs. Stiffness**: PMR causes severe morning stiffness, making it hard for patients to roll out of bed or lift their arms. However, if you support the arm and test the deltoid, the **muscle strength itself is normal**. True weakness indicates myositis, thyroid dysfunction, or neuropathy.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Visual Emergency Warning**: If a patient with PMR develops a new, severe headache, jaw claudication, or transient visual loss (**amaurosis fugax**), assume **Giant Cell Arteritis**. Immediately escalate Prednisolone to **40-60 mg PO daily** (do not wait for a temporal artery biopsy) to prevent permanent blindness.
