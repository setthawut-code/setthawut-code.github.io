# 🦴 Osteoarthritis / OA (โรคข้อเสื่อม)

> **Definition**: Osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic, progressive, non-inflammatory degenerative joint disease characterized by breakdown of articular cartilage, subchondral bone remodeling, osteophyte formation, and joint space narrowing. It is the most common joint disorder worldwide.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Increases with age; present in $>80\%$ of individuals $>65$ years (radiographical evidence).
* **Gender Ratio**: Female predominance ($2:1$), especially post-menopausal.
* **Risk Factors**: 
  * **Obesity** (most modifiable risk factor for knee OA).
  * Aging, female sex, genetics (strong for hand OA).
  * Prior joint injury (post-traumatic OA), occupational repetitive mechanical stress, joint laxity.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
Mechanical stress + chondrocyte dysfunction lead to joint degeneration:
1. **Chondrocyte Injury**: Chronic mechanical stress damages chondrocytes, causing release of inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, TNF-alpha) and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs).
2. **Articular Cartilage Loss**: Increased enzymatic degradation of proteoglycans and collagen exceeds synthesis, leading to cartilage thinning, fibrillation, and fissuring.
3. **Subchondral Bone Remodeling**: Denuded subchondral bone undergoes friction-induced micro-fractures, leading to:
   * **Subchondral Sclerosis**: Osteoblast activation increases bone density (eburnation).
   * **Subchondral Cysts**: Synovial fluid forced through cartilage micro-cracks into bone.
   * **Osteophyte Formation**: Bony spurs at joint margins due to neo-vascularization and chondroid metaplasia.
4. **Secondary Synovitis**: Cartilage fragments shed into joint space, triggering low-grade synovial inflammation.

```
│           CHRONIC MECHANICAL STRESS / LOAD             │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ▼

                            │

```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Pain Pattern**: **Mechanical pain** (worse with joint use/weight-bearing, relieved by rest). Contrasts with inflammatory pain (RA) which is worse with rest/morning and improves with activity.
* **Stiffness**: Morning stiffness or inactivity stiffness (gelling) lasting **$<30$ minutes** (RA stiffness is $>1$ hour).
* **Joints Involved**: Typically asymmetric, weight-bearing, or high-use joints:
  * **Knee**: Most common symptomatic site. Pain on stairs, crepitus on movement.
  * **Hip**: Groin pain radiating to anterior thigh or knee, restricted internal rotation.
  * **Hands**:
    * **Heberden's Nodes**: Bony enlargement of **DIP** joints.
    * **Bouchard's Nodes**: Bony enlargement of **PIP** joints.
    * **First CMC Joint**: Thumb base involvement causing "squaring" of hand.
    * *Spars MCP joints* (unlike RA).
  * **Spine**: Cervical and lumbar facet joint osteoarthritis (leads to spondylosis).
* **Physical Exam**: Crepitus (grating sensation), bony hypertrophy, cool joint effusions (no warmth/erythema), restricted range of motion, joint instability. **No systemic symptoms** (no fever, fatigue, weight loss).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] (RA)**: Symmetrical, inflammatory pain (better with activity), morning stiffness $>1$ hour, involves **MCP and wrists** (spares DIP), positive RF/anti-CCP, systemic symptoms.
* **[[Pseudogout]] (CPPD)**: Can present as chronic pyrophosphate arthropathy mimicking OA, but shows chondrocalcinosis on X-ray and rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals in joint fluid.
* **[[Infectious Arthritis]]**: Acute, severe monoarticular pain, warmth, redness, fever. Must tap joint if septic joint suspected.
* **[[Psoriatic Arthritis]] (PsA)**: DIP joint involvement, but associated with psoriatic plaques, dactylitis ("sausage digit"), nail pitting, and "pencil-in-cup" deformity on X-ray.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
**Primarily a clinical diagnosis.** Investigations rule out other pathologies or confirm staging.

### 🩻 Plain Radiography (Gold Standard Imaging)
Classic findings represented by the mnemonic **LOSS**:
1. **L - Loss of Joint Space**: Characteristically **asymmetric/uneven** (contrasts with symmetric/uniform narrowing in RA).
2. **O - Osteophytes**: Bony projections at joint margins.
3. **S - Subchondral Sclerosis**: Increased bone density (bright white appearance) directly beneath joint space.
4. **S - Subchondral Cysts**: Fluid-filled lucencies in subchondral bone.

### Laboratory
* **ESR & CRP**: Normal (rules out systemic inflammatory processes).
* **Rheumatoid Factor (RF) & Anti-CCP**: Negative.
* **Synovial Fluid Analysis**: Non-inflammatory profile:
  * Clear, yellow, high viscosity.
  * WBC count **$<2,000/\mu\text{L}$** ($>75\%$ mononuclear cells; RA/gout/septic have $>2,000$ to $>50,000$ with PMN predominance).
  * Negative for crystals and cultures.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Non-Pharmacological (First-Line & Cornerstone)
* **Weight Loss**: Essential for knee/hip OA (reduces mechanical force; 5% weight loss reduces pain).
* **Exercise**: Low-impact aerobic exercise (swimming, cycling) and quadriceps strengthening to stabilize the knee.
* **Physical Therapy**: Joint protection techniques, assistive devices (e.g., cane held in the **contralateral** hand to reduce joint loading).

### 2. Pharmacological Therapy
* **Topical NSAIDs** (e.g., Diclofenac 1% gel): **Preferred first-line** for knee and hand OA. Excellent local efficacy, minimal systemic absorption ($<10\%$), low GI/renal risk.
* **Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)**: Mild efficacy. Safe for patients with contraindications to NSAIDs. Limit to **$<3\text{ g/day}$** to avoid hepatotoxicity.
* **Oral NSAIDs** (e.g., Celecoxib 100-200 mg PO bid, Naproxen 250-500 mg PO bid): Most effective for pain but carry risks:
  > [!WARNING]
  > **NSAID Safety in Elderly**: Oral NSAIDs must be used with caution in elderly patients. They can precipitate GI bleeding, acute kidney injury (AKI), hypertension, fluid retention, and cardiovascular events. Co-prescribe a Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) like Omeprazole for GI protection, and use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
* **Intra-articular Corticosteroid Injection**: Useful for acute, severe flares of knee/hip OA. Provides rapid relief (lasts 1-3 months).
  * *Constraint*: Limit to $\le 3-4$ injections per year (risk of accelerated cartilage damage and infection).
* **Duloxetine** (SNRI): Approved for chronic musculoskeletal pain. Useful in patients with multi-joint OA or contraindications to NSAIDs.

### 3. Surgical Management
* **Total Joint Arthroplasty** (Total Knee/Hip Replacement): Indicated for patients with end-stage OA, severe refractory pain, and major functional impairment who have failed conservative treatment.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **DIP vs. MCP Sparing Rule**: 
  > * **Osteoarthritis** classically involves the **DIP (Heberden's)** and **PIP (Bouchard's)** joints, but **spares the MCP joints** (except 1st CMC at thumb base). 
  > * **Rheumatoid Arthritis** classically involves the **MCP** and **PIP** joints, but **spares the DIP joints**.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Contralateral Cane Rule**: Teach the patient to hold the cane in the hand **opposite (contralateral)** to the painful knee/hip. When walking, the cane is moved forward simultaneously with the painful limb, shifting weight onto the cane and reducing the joint loading by up to 60%.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The Osteophyte Trap**: Presence of osteophytes on X-ray does not mean they are the cause of pain. Many elderly have radiographic OA but are asymptomatic. Treat the patient, not the X-ray.
