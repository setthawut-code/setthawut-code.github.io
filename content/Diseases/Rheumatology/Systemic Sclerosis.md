# 🦴 Systemic Sclerosis / Scleroderma (โรคหนังแข็ง)

> **Definition**: Systemic Sclerosis (SSc) is a chronic, multi-system autoimmune connective tissue disease characterized by vascular dysfunction (obliterative microangiopathy), immune activation, and progressive tissue fibrosis affecting the skin and multiple internal organs (lungs, gastrointestinal tract, kidneys, and heart).

---

## 📊 Classification & Subtypes

SSc is divided into two major subsets based on the extent of skin involvement:

| Feature | Limited Cutaneous SSc (lcSSc) | Diffuse Cutaneous SSc (dcSSc) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin Thickening** | Restricted to **distal** extremities (below elbows/knees) and the face/neck. | Extends **proximal** to elbows/knees, involving the chest, abdomen, and trunk. |
| **Skin Onset** | Gradual, slow progression. | Rapid, acute onset and progression. |
| **Raynaud's** | Precedes skin thickening by years or decades. | Occurs concurrently with or shortly after skin thickening. |
| **Clinical Association** | **CREST Syndrome** (Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia). | Early, progressive internal organ damage (ILD, Renal Crisis, Myocardial fibrosis). |
| **Pulmonary Danger** | **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** (isolated, late-onset). | **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)** (early and progressive). |
| **Autoantibody** | **Anti-Centromere** ($>60-80\%$ specific). | **Anti-Scl-70** (anti-topoisomerase I) and **Anti-RNA Polymerase III**. |

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
The pathology consists of a triad of vascular injury, cellular/humoral autoinflammation, and extensive fibrosis:
1. **Vascular Injury**: Endothelial cell damage (triggered by unknown environmental insults) leads to a loss of vasodilators (Nitric Oxide, Prostacyclin) and an excess of vasoconstrictors (Endothelin-1). This leads to chronic vasoconstriction (Raynaud's) and proliferative obliterative vasculopathy (smooth muscle cell hyperplasia), causing tissue ischemia.
2. **Immune Activation**: Infiltrating T-cells (Th2 biased) and macrophages release pro-fibrotic cytokines, primarily **TGF-$\beta$** (Transforming Growth Factor beta), **IL-4**, and **PDGF** (Platelet-Derived Growth Factor).
3. **Fibrosis**: TGF-$\beta$ and PDGF stimulate fibroblasts to transition into highly active **myofibroblasts**. Myofibroblasts produce excessive amounts of extracellular matrix proteins, mainly **Collagen Types I and III**, which deposit in skin and organ parenchymal tissues, destroying architecture.

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Cutaneous Features
* **Sclerodactyly**: Thickened, tight, non-pitting skin of the fingers. Early stage presents as "puffy hands"; late stage presents as shiny, taut skin with loss of normal skin folds, leading to flexion contractures of the MCP/PIP joints.
* **Microstomia**: Tightening of the skin around the mouth, leading to a small mouth opening (decreased inter-incisor distance) and radial furrowing (wrinkles) around the lips.
* **Calcinosis Cutis**: Deposition of white, chalky calcium crystals in the subcutaneous tissue, often over pressure points (fingertips, elbows), which can ulcerate and extrude calcium.
* **Telangiectasias**: Dilated, macular capillary loops on the face, mucosal membranes, and hands.

### 2. Vascular Features
* **Raynaud's Phenomenon**: Present in $>95\%$ of SSc. Triphasic color changes in response to cold or stress:
  1. **White** (Pallor due to sudden digital arterial spasm/ischemia).
  2. **Blue** (Cyanosis due to venous deoxygenation under tissue hypoxia).
  3. **Red** (Rubor due to reactive hyperemia and reperfusion).
* **Digital Ulcers**: Painful ischemic ulcers on the fingertips due to obliterative vasculopathy.
* **Nailfold Capillaroscopy**: Shows capillary loop enlargement, giant capillaries, and capillary dropout (loss of microvessels).

### 3. Pulmonary Manifestations (The #1 Cause of Death)
* **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**: Highly associated with **dcSSc and anti-Scl-70**. Presents with progressive exertional dyspnea, dry cough, and fine velcro-like crackles at lung bases. HRCT scan typically reveals a Non-Specific Interstitial Pneumonia (NSIP) pattern.
* **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)**: Highly associated with **lcSSc and anti-Centromere**. Remodeling of pulmonary arterioles leads to isolated PAH (without pulmonary fibrosis). Presents as progressive dyspnea, loud $P_2$, and right heart failure.

### 4. Renal Manifestations (Scleroderma Renal Crisis / SRC)
* A medical emergency presenting as sudden onset of **malignant hypertension** (BP $> 180/110$ mmHg), **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**, and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) with thrombocytopenia.
* Highly associated with **anti-RNA Polymerase III** antibodies.
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **The Steroid Trigger**: High-dose systemic corticosteroids (Prednisolone $> 15$ mg/day) are the strongest trigger for Scleroderma Renal Crisis. Avoid systemic steroids in dcSSc unless absolutely mandatory, and monitor BP closely.

### 5. Gastrointestinal Manifestations
* **Esophagus**: Atrophy of smooth muscle in the lower $2/3$ of the esophagus leads to lower esophageal sphincter (LES) incompetence, presenting as severe GERD, dysphagia, and esophageal stricture.
* **Small Bowel**: Dysmotility leads to pseudo-obstruction and **Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO)**, causing severe malabsorption, bloating, and diarrhea.
* **Stomach**: **GAVE** (Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia / "Watermelon Stomach"): Dilated ectatic vessels in the gastric antrum, causing chronic occult GI bleeding.

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Eosinophilic Fasciitis**: Skin thickening but **spares the fingers and face**; peripheral blood eosinophilia and normal capillaroscopy.
* **Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis (NSF)**: Widespread skin thickening in patients with end-stage renal disease exposed to gadolinium-based contrast agents. Spares the face.

---

## 🧪 Investigations
* **Antinuclear Antibody (ANA)**: Positive in $>90-95\%$ of patients (speckled or nucleolar pattern).
* **Specific Autoantibodies**:
  * **Anti-Centromere**: Associated with Limited SSc, CREST, and PAH. Good prognosis.
  * **Anti-Scl-70** (Anti-topoisomerase I): Associated with Diffuse SSc and severe ILD. Poor prognosis.
  * **Anti-RNA Polymerase III**: Associated with Diffuse SSc, rapid skin progression, and high risk of Scleroderma Renal Crisis.
* **Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs)**:
  * *ILD*: Restrictive pattern (decreased FVC, decreased TLC, decreased DLCO).
  * *PAH*: Isolated decrease in DLCO with normal lung volumes. An **FVC / DLCO ratio $> 1.6$** is highly suggestive of underlying PAH.
* **Echocardiogram**: Screening tool for PAH.
* **Right Heart Catheterization**: Gold standard to diagnose PAH (mPAP $\ge 20$ mmHg with pulmonary capillary wedge pressure $\le 15$ mmHg).
* **High-Resolution CT (HRCT) of Chest**: Gold standard to diagnose ILD (shows ground-glass opacities, reticulation, traction bronchiectasis).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Systemic Fibrosis / ILD
* **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**: First-line therapy for SSc-ILD (slows decline in FVC).
* **Cyclophosphamide**: Reserved for rapidly progressive ILD.
* **Nintedanib**: Tyrosine kinase inhibitor (anti-fibrotic) approved to slow lung function decline.

### 2. Scleroderma Renal Crisis (SRC) Management
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Emergency ACE Inhibitor Protocol**: The treatment of choice is immediate, aggressive initiation of short-acting **ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Captopril)**. Do **not** withhold or delay Captopril even if serum creatinine is rising; ACE inhibitors reverse the renal vasoconstriction and are life-saving. Beta-blockers are contraindicated.

### 3. Symptomatic / Organ-Specific Management
* **Raynaud's & Digital Ulcers**:
  * Avoid cold exposure.
  * **Calcium Channel Blockers** (e.g., Nifedipine, Amlodipine) are first-line.
  * PDE5 inhibitors (e.g., Sildenafil) or topical nitrates for refractory ulcers.
* **Pulmonary Hypertension**: Endothelin receptor antagonists (Bosentan, Macitentan), PDE5 inhibitors (Sildenafil), or prostacyclin analogues.
* **Gastrointestinal**: High-dose PPIs for GERD; prokinetics (Metoclopramide) for gastroparesis; rotating antibiotics (Rifaximin, Metronidazole) for SIBO.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Captopril Rule**: In Scleroderma Renal Crisis, Captopril is the gold standard because it reverses the hyper-reninemic state. If the patient's creatinine increases by $30-50\%$ after starting Captopril, **do not stop the drug**. Continue to titrate Captopril to control blood pressure; renal function will stabilize once perfusion is restored.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Steroid Warning**: If a patient with diffuse systemic sclerosis presents with joint pain, **do not prescribe systemic Prednisolone $> 15$ mg/day**. High-dose steroids are a potent trigger for Scleroderma Renal Crisis. Use NSAIDs or low-dose steroids with extreme caution while monitoring daily home BP.
