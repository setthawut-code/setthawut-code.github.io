# 🦴 Psoriatic Arthritis (โรคข้ออักเสบสะเก็ดเงิน)

> **Definition**: Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA) is a chronic, systemic, inflammatory seronegative spondyloarthritis associated with skin psoriasis. It is characterized by highly variable joint involvement (ranging from mild oligoarthritis to destructive arthritis mutilans and axial spine disease), prominent **dactylitis**, **enthesitis**, and **nail dystrophy**.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Occurs in approximately **$5 - 30\%$** of patients with cutaneous psoriasis. 
* **Onset Order**: In $70\%$ of cases, skin psoriasis precedes arthritis (often by 8-10 years). In $15-20\%$, arthritis precedes skin lesions, and in $10\%$ they occur simultaneously.
* **Gender Ratio**: Equal female-to-male ratio ($1:1$), unlike RA which is female-predominant.
* **Age of Onset**: Typically develops between ages 30 and 50.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & The IL-23/IL-17 Axis
PsA features an inflammatory cascade driven by the innate and adaptive immune systems at both the **synovium** and **entheses**:
1. **Trigger**: Environmental stress (mechanical microtrauma at entheses or streptococcal infection) in HLA-susceptible hosts activates dendritic cells and macrophages in the skin, gut, or joints.
2. **Cytokine Cascade**: Activated cells secrete **IL-23**, which stimulates local tissue-resident immune cells (such as $\gamma\delta$ T-cells and ILC3s) to produce **IL-17**, **TNF-$\alpha$**, and **IL-22**.
3. **Joint & Bone Remodeling**:
   * **Synovitis**: Synovial lining hyperplasia and intense vascularity.
   * **Enthesitis**: Inflammation at ligamentous insertions.
   * **RANKL Pathway**: Driven by IL-17 and TNF-$\alpha$, osteoclast precursors are activated, leading to bone resorption and marginal erosions.
   * **Osteoblast Pathway**: Mediated by IL-22, new bone formation (**periostitis** and **syndesmophytes**) occurs adjacent to erosions, leading to a pathognomonic mix of joint destruction and bone proliferation.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Joint Involvement Patterns (Moll and Wright Criteria)
* **Asymmetric Oligoarthritis ($~30\%$)**: Affects $<5$ joints, typically large joints of the lower extremities and a few random small joints of the hands/feet.
* **Symmetric Polyarthritis ($~30-40\%$)**: Symmetrical polyarthritis affecting small and large joints, mimicking Rheumatoid Arthritis. Differentiated by DIP involvement, absence of RF/anti-CCP, and presence of psoriasis.
* **Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) Joint Predominant ($~5-10\%$)**: Involves the DIP joints of the hands and feet. Characteristically associated with adjacent **nail dystrophy**.
* **Arthritis Mutilans ($<5\%$)**: Extremely severe, osteolytic, destructive arthritis. Causes resorption of phalangeal bone, leading to **"telescoping digits"** (skin folds accordion-like over shortened bone; also called **"opera glass hand"**).
* **Axial Spondyloarthritis ($~20-40\%$)**: Sacroiliitis and inflammatory back pain, often asymmetric.

### 2. Prominent Core Features
* **Dactylitis ("Sausage Digit")**: Seen in $30-40\%$ of patients. Diffuse, uniform swelling of an entire finger or toe due to concurrent synovitis, flexor tenosynovitis, and soft tissue/entheseal edema.
* **Enthesitis**: Most commonly Achilles tendonitis and plantar fasciitis.
* **Nail Dystrophy**: Present in $>80\%$ of patients with PsA (compared to only $40\%$ of those with uncomplicated skin psoriasis). Look for:
  * **Nail pitting** (punctate depressions in the nail plate).
  * **Onycholysis** (separation of nail from bed).
  * **Subungual hyperkeratosis** and **oil drop sign** (yellow-brown discoloration).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Rheumatoid Arthritis]]**: Symmetric, spares DIP joints, no dactylitis or psoriasis; positive RF and anti-CCP.
* **[[Osteoarthritis]]**: Involves DIP joints (Heberden's nodes) and PIP joints (Bouchard's nodes), but pain is mechanical (worse with activity), morning stiffness is short ($<30$ minutes), and X-rays show osteophytes rather than erosions/periostitis.
* **Gouty Arthritis**: Tophi can mimic dactylitis or nodules. Synovial fluid shows monosodium urate crystals.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **Serology**: Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP are **characteristically negative**.
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ESR and CRP are elevated in only $50\%$ of active cases.
* **Uric Acid**: May be elevated due to high epidermal cell turnover in severe skin psoriasis (predisposes to secondary gout).

### Imaging (X-Ray Findings)
* **DIP Joint Involvement**: Marginal erosions combined with new bone formation (periostitis).
* **Pencil-in-Cup Deformity**: Severe osteolysis of the distal head of the phalanx (pencil) with expansion/cup-like remodeling of the adjacent proximal phalangeal base.
* **Acro-osteolysis**: Resorption of the terminal tufts of the distal phalanges.
* **Asymmetric Sacroiliitis** and asymmetric, bulky, non-marginal syndesmophytes.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
### CASPAR Classification Criteria (Requires score $\ge 3$ points)
* Active psoriasis (2 points) or history of psoriasis (1 point) or family history of psoriasis (1 point).
* Psoriatic nail dystrophy (1 point).
* Negative Rheumatoid Factor (1 point).
* Current or history of dactylitis (1 point).
* Radiographic evidence of juxta-articular new bone formation (1 point).

---

## 🏥 Management

### Mild Disease (Oligoarthritis, no erosions)
* **NSAIDs (First-line)**: Full-dose continuous NSAID therapy (e.g., Naproxen, Celecoxib).
* **Intra-articular Steroid Injections** for single active joints.

### Moderate-to-Severe Disease (Polyarticular, erosions, or skin disease)
* **Conventional synthetic DMARDs (First-line for peripheral joints)**:
  * **Methotrexate** (7.5-25 mg PO once weekly + Folic acid): Treats both peripheral joints and skin psoriasis.
  * **Leflunomide** or **Sulfasalazine**: Alternatives.
  * *Note: Conventional DMARDs are completely ineffective for axial spine disease.*
* **Biologic & Targeted DMARDs**:
  * **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors** (e.g., Adalimumab, Etanercept): Highly effective for joints, spine, and skin.
  * **IL-17 Inhibitors** (e.g., **Secukinumab**): Highly effective for joints, spine, and skin. *(Contraindicated in active Crohn's/UC)*.
  * **IL-23 Inhibitors** (e.g., Guselkumab) and **IL-12/23 Inhibitors** (e.g., Ustekinumab): Excellent for skin and peripheral joints, but **ineffective for axial spine symptoms**.
  * **PDE4 Inhibitors** (Apremilast) or **JAK Inhibitors** (Tofacitinib).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **DIP Joint Rule**: DIP joint arthritis in the presence of nail pitting is the signature of Psoriatic Arthritis. If you see a patient with suspected osteoarthritis of the DIP joints but they have prominent nail dystrophy and prolonged morning stiffness, look closely at their scalp and extensor surfaces for hidden psoriatic plaques.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Telescoping Digits**: The "pencil-in-cup" deformity can lead to severe shortening and flail joints in the fingers, known as arthritis mutilans. On physical exam, the fingers can be pulled out to their normal length and then collapsed back like a telescope.
