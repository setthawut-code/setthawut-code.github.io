# 🦴 Behçet Disease (โรคเบห์เช็ต)

> **Definition**: Behçet Disease is a chronic, relapsing, multi-system inflammatory vascular disorder characterized by the classic clinical triad of recurrent oral aphthous ulcers, recurrent genital ulcers, and ocular inflammation (uveitis). It is unique because it can cause vasculitis affecting blood vessels of all sizes (small, medium, and large) on both the arterial and venous sides.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Geographical Distribution**: Highest prevalence along the ancient **"Silk Road"** (stretching from the Mediterranean basin, Middle East, and Turkey to East Asia).
* **Age of Onset**: Typically develops in young adults between the ages of 20 and 40.
* **Genetic Association**: Strongest genetic link is with the **HLA-B51** allele (present in up to $50-80\%$ of patients from endemic areas).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
The pathology features neutrophilic hyperreactivity, endothelial activation, and systemic vasculitis:
1. **Neutrophilic Infiltration**: T-cell activation (Th1, Th17) and overexpression of cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-$\alpha$) drive **neutrophilic hyperreactivity**. Neutrophils migrate rapidly to tissues and blood vessels.
2. **Vasculitis**: Perivascular infiltration of neutrophils and mononuclear cells leads to destruction of the elastic lamina and vessel walls.
3. **Thrombosis & Aneurysm**:
   * *Venous*: Endothelial inflammation promotes platelet adhesion and venous thrombosis.
   * *Arterial*: Destruction of the arterial wall media leads to weakening, causing **aneurysm formation** (especially pulmonary and abdominal aorta) and rupture.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. The Core Triad
* **Recurrent Oral Ulcers**: The hallmark and almost universal ($>98\%$) presenting feature. Painful, round aphthous-like ulcers on the lips, tongue, or buccal mucosa. They must recur **at least 3 times in a 12-month period** and typically heal without scarring within 1-2 weeks.
* **Recurrent Genital Ulcers**: Highly specific ($>90\%$). Painful, morphologically similar to oral ulcers, located on the scrotum/vulva. Unlike oral ulcers, genital ulcers are deeper and **frequently heal with scarring**.
* **Ocular Involvement**: Occurs in $50-70\%$ of patients (more common and severe in young males). Can cause:
  * **Anterior Uveitis**: Classic presentation with a **hypopyon** (a visible layer of white inflammatory cells/pus in the anterior chamber of the eye).
  * **Posterior Uveitis & Retinal Vasculitis**: Presents with painless visual loss, floaters, and retinal hemorrhages. Can lead to irreversible blindness if untreated.

### 2. Other Systemic Features
* **Cutaneous Lesions**: **Erythema Nodosum** (tender nodules on shins), pseudofolliculitis, and acneiform papulopustular lesions.
* **Pathergy Reaction**: Characterized by skin hyperreactivity. A minor skin prick with a sterile 20-gauge needle triggers the formation of a sterile erythematous papule or pustule ($>2$ mm) at the site within 24-48 hours.
* **Vascular Manifestations**:
  * **Venous (Most common)**: Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) in the lower extremities, superficial thrombophlebitis, or dural venous sinus thrombosis.
  * **Arterial (Life-threatening)**: **Pulmonary Artery Aneurysms** are highly characteristic. Rupture leads to catastrophic hemoptysis.
* **Gastrointestinal**: Mucosal ulceration predominantly in the **ileocecal region**, causing abdominal pain, bleeding, and bowel perforation (mimics Crohn's disease).
* **Neurological (Neuro-Behçet)**: Parenchymal brainstem lesions, aseptic meningitis, or cranial nerve palsies.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Crohn's Disease**: Can present with oral aphthous ulcers, ileocecal ulceration, and uveitis. Differentiated by absence of genital ulcers/pathergy, and histopathology showing non-caseating granulomas (Behçet ulcers show neutrophilic vasculitis).
* **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**: Oral ulcers are common in SLE, but they are typically **painless** and associated with lupus serology (ANA, anti-dsDNA) and malar rash.
* **Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV)**: Causes painful oral and genital ulcers, but is typically vesicular initially, has positive viral PCR, and does not cause systemic vasculitis or pathergy.

---

## 🧪 Investigations
* **Pathergy Test**: Performed by inserting a sterile needle obliquely into the forearm skin. Positive if a pustule or papule develops at 24-48 hours.
* **Serology**: Autoantibodies (ANA, ANCA, RF) are **characteristically negative**.
* **HLA-B51 Genotyping**: Supportive, but not diagnostic on its own.
* **Imaging**: CT Angiography of the chest/abdomen to evaluate for pulmonary artery aneurysms or aortic aneurysms in patients with hemoptysis or vascular symptoms.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
### International Study Group Criteria (Oral ulcers + $\ge 2$ features)
* **Recurrent Oral Ulceration**: Aphthous or herpetiform ulcers observed by clinician or patient, recurring at least 3 times in 12 months.
* **Plus any 2 of the following**:
  1. Recurrent genital ulceration (showing scars).
  2. Eye lesions (anterior/posterior uveitis, retinal vasculitis).
  3. Skin lesions (erythema nodosum, pseudofolliculitis, papulopustular lesions).
  4. Positive pathergy test.

---

## 🏥 Management

### Mucocutaneous & Joint Disease
* **Colchicine (The Drug of Choice)**: **0.6 - 1.2 mg PO daily**. Highly effective in preventing recurrent oral and genital ulcers, erythema nodosum, and arthralgias.
* Topical corticosteroid gels for active oral/genital ulcers.

### Severe / Ocular / Vascular Disease
* **Systemic Corticosteroids** (Prednisone 1 mg/kg/day) for acute flares of uveitis, gastrointestinal ulcers, or CNS vasculitis.
* **Azathioprine**: Standard first-line immunosuppressant to maintain ocular remission and prevent blindness.
* **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors (Infliximab, Adalimumab)**: Indicated for severe, refractory panuveitis, GI ulcers, or Neuro-Behçet's.
* **Vascular Aneurysm/Thrombosis**: High-dose corticosteroids + Cyclophosphamide.
  * > [!CAUTION]
    > **Anticoagulation Trap**: Anticoagulation is controversial in Behçet's venous thrombosis. Because venous clots in Behçet's are highly adherent to the inflamed endothelium (rarely embolize) and because patients have a high risk of co-existing pulmonary artery aneurysms, starting anticoagulants can trigger fatal pulmonary hemorrhage. Prioritize immunosuppression to resolve the vascular inflammation.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Scarring Clue**: Oral ulcers in Behçet's heal completely without leaving structural changes. Genital ulcers, however, are deeper and almost always leave **visible, hypopigmented scars**. Checking for scrotum/vulva scarring is a high-yield physical exam technique when oral ulcers are the only active symptom.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Hemoptysis Alert**: In any patient with known or suspected Behçet's disease who presents with hemoptysis, immediately order a **CT chest angiogram**. This is a potential pulmonary artery aneurysm rupture; starting anticoagulants or failing to perform embolization can be fatal.
