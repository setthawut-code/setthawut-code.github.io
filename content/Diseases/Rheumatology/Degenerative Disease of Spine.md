# 🦴 Degenerative Disease of Spine (โรคกระดูกสันหลังเสื่อม)

> **Definition**: Degenerative spine disease refers to age-related degenerative changes affecting the intervertebral discs, facet joints, ligamentum flavum, and vertebral bodies. It manifests as cervical or lumbar spondylosis, Herniated Nucleus Pulposus (HNP), Spinal Stenosis, and Spondylolisthesis.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Common; affects $>80\%$ of individuals $>50$ years (radiographically).
* **Risk Factors**: 
  * Aging, obesity, genetic predisposition.
  * Occupational repetitive strain (heavy lifting, bending), prior spinal trauma.
  * **Smoking** (accelerates disc degeneration by decreasing blood supply).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
Degenerative cascade involving mechanical and biochemical changes:
1. **Disc Degeneration (Intervertebral Osteochondrosis)**: Nucleus pulposus loses proteoglycans and water content (dehydration/desiccation) $\rightarrow$ disc height decreases $\rightarrow$ mechanical load shifts to annulus fibrosus and facet joints.
2. **Herniated Nucleus Pulposus (HNP)**: Tears in weakened annulus fibrosus allow gel-like nucleus pulposus to herniate (usually posterolaterally) $\rightarrow$ direct mechanical compression of adjacent nerve root + chemical irritation from inflammatory mediators (e.g., TNF-alpha).
3. **Facet Joint Arthropathy & Spondylosis**: Increased load on posterior facet joints causes cartilage wear, osteophyte formation, and joint capsule stretching.
4. **Spinal Stenosis**: Hypertrophy of facet joints + vertebral osteophytes + **hypertrophy/buckling of the Ligamentum Flavum** + disc bulging $\rightarrow$ narrowing of spinal canal/neural foramina $\rightarrow$ ischemia and compression of nerve roots/cauda equina.

## 🔍 Clinical Syndromes

### 1. Axial Spine Pain (Spondylosis)
* Localized neck or low back pain without radiation. 
* Pain worsened by prolonged sitting (discogenic) or spinal extension/rotation (facetogenic).

### 2. Radiculopathy (Compressed Nerve Root)
* **Radicular Pain**: Sharp, burning, electric-shock pain radiating down a specific dermatome. Accompanied by paresthesias, sensory loss, motor weakness, and reduced deep tendon reflexes.
* **Lumbar Radiculopathy**:
  * **L5 Nerve Root (L4-L5 HNP)**: Lateral calf/dorsum of foot sensory loss. **Weakness of ankle dorsiflexion and big toe extension (Extensor Hallucis Longus)**. Normal reflexes. Can cause foot drop.
  * **S1 Nerve Root (L5-S1 HNP)**: Sole and lateral border of foot sensory loss. **Weakness of plantarflexion (Gastrocnemius)**. **Lost/decreased Achilles tendon reflex**.
* **Cervical Radiculopathy**:
  * **C6 Nerve Root**: Lateral forearm and thumb sensory loss. Weakness of biceps/brachioradialis. Lost **brachioradialis reflex**.
  * **C7 Nerve Root**: Middle finger sensory loss. Weakness of triceps. Lost **triceps reflex**.
* **Key Radiculopathy Exams**:
  * **Straight Leg Raise (SLR)**: Passive hip flexion with knee extended. Positive if pain radiates below the knee at **$30 - 70^\circ$**. *High sensitivity (90%) for L4-S1 radiculopathy.*
  * **Crossed (Contralateral) SLR**: Raising unaffected leg reproduces radicular pain in affected leg. *High specificity (90%) for disc herniation.*
  * **Spurling's Test**: Neck extension and lateral rotation/axial compression reproduces ipsilateral radicular arm pain. *Specific for cervical radiculopathy.*

### 3. Lumbar Spinal Stenosis
* **Neurogenic Claudication**: Bilateral buttock, thigh, or calf pain, numbness, or heaviness precipitated by walking or standing.
* **Shopping Cart Sign**: Symptoms **relieved by lumbar flexion** (sitting or bending forward) because flexion increases the spinal canal cross-sectional area.
* **Differentiating Claudication**:

| Feature | Neurogenic Claudication | Vascular Claudication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathology** | Spinal canal narrowing (ischemia of nerve roots) | Peripheral Arterial Disease (skeletal muscle ischemia) |
| **Relieving factors** | **Lumbar flexion** (sitting, leaning forward) | **Standing still** / stopping activity |
| **Triggers** | Walking, prolonged standing | Walking only (not standing still) |
| **Pulses / Skin** | Normal peripheral pulses | Decreased/absent pulses, cool skin, hair loss |
| **Shopping Cart** | Relieves pain | No effect |

### 4. Cauda Equina Syndrome (Emergency)
* Massive central disc herniation compressing cauda equina nerve roots.
* **Red Flags**:
  * **Saddle anesthesia** (sensory loss in perineum, buttocks, inner thighs).
  * **Bowel or bladder dysfunction** (urinary retention, overflow incontinence, loss of anal sphincter tone).
  * Progressive or bilateral motor weakness (e.g., bilateral foot drop).

## 🔬 Investigations
* **MRI Spine (Gold Standard)**: Best for visualizing soft tissues. Demonstrates disc herniation, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, spinal canal stenosis, and nerve root/spinal cord compression.
* **Plain X-Ray**: Shows disc space narrowing, osteophytes, and facet arthropathy. Rules out fractures, lytic lesions, or spondylolisthesis. Cannot diagnose HNP or stenosis.
* **CT Myelography**: Alternative when MRI is contraindicated (e.g., pacemakers, metallic implants).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Conservative Management (First-Line)
* **Reassurance & Activity**: Avoid bed rest $>2$ days (worsens chronic back pain). Encourage continuation of activity as tolerated.
* **Physical Therapy**: Core stabilization, McKenzie extension exercises (for lumbar stenosis) or flexion exercises.
* **Pharmacotherapy**:
  * **NSAIDs** (e.g., Celecoxib, Naproxen): First-line for acute back pain/axial pain.
  * **Neuropathic Agents**: **Gabapentin** ($300 - 1800\text{ mg/day}$) or **Pregabalin** ($75 - 300\text{ mg/day}$) for radicular pain.
  * **Muscle Relaxants** (e.g., Eperisone 50 mg tid, Tolperisone 50-150 mg tid): Short-term (3-7 days) for acute muscle spasms.
  * **Corticosteroids**: Short-course oral Prednisolone taper for acute severe radicular pain (moderate evidence).

### 2. Interventional Management
* **Epidural Steroid Injection (ESI)**: Targeted delivery of steroids to the epidural space for patients with radicular pain refractory to oral medications.

### 3. Surgical Management
* **Indications**:
  1. **Cauda Equina Syndrome** (Requires **emergent surgical decompression within 24-48 hours**).
  2. Progressive or disabling motor deficit (e.g., worsening foot drop).
  3. Intractable pain failing $>6 - 12$ weeks of conservative therapy.
* **Procedures**:
  * **Discectomy / Microdiscectomy**: Excision of herniated disc fragment.
  * **Laminectomy**: Removal of lamina to decompress the spinal canal in stenosis.
  * **Spinal Fusion**: Stabilizing segments (indicated in spondylolisthesis or mechanical instability).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Cauda Equina Emergency**: Never delay evaluation of a patient with back pain presenting with new-onset urinary retention or saddle anesthesia. Immediate MRI and urgent neurosurgical consultation are mandatory. Delay $>48$ hours can lead to permanent bladder/bowel incontinence and paraplegia.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The SLR Confusion**: A positive SLR must reproduce **radicular pain** radiating below the knee (pain in back of thigh or calf). Localized low back pain during SLR is NOT a positive test.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Radiculopathy Reflex Rule**: 
  > * Achilles reflex is mediated by **S1** nerve root.
  > * Patellar reflex is mediated by **L4** nerve root.
  > * L5 compression does NOT alter patellar or Achilles reflexes (normal reflexes).
