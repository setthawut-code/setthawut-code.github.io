# 🦴 Enteropathic Arthritis (โรคข้ออักเสบร่วมกับโรคลำไส้อักเสบเรื้อรัง)

> **Definition**: Enteropathic Arthritis is a form of chronic, inflammatory, seronegative spondyloarthritis associated with Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)—Crohn's Disease and Ulcerative Colitis. It manifests as either peripheral arthritis (divided into Type 1 and Type 2), axial spondyloarthritis (sacroiliitis/spondylitis), enthesitis, or dactylitis.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Affects approximately **$10 - 20\%$** of patients with Crohn's disease or Ulcerative Colitis.
* **Gender Ratio**: Equal gender distribution ($1:1$).
* **Clinical Timing**: Joint symptoms can appear years before, concurrently with, or years after the onset of gastrointestinal symptoms.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & The Gut-Joint Axis
The disease highlights the **gut-joint axis** where subclinical gut inflammation drives joint pathology:
1. **Mucosal Barrier Disruption**: Chronic intestinal inflammation in Crohn's or UC leads to increased mucosal permeability, allowing bacterial antigens to translocate into the lamina propria.
2. **Immune Activation**: Gut dendritic cells and macrophages process these antigens and secrete **IL-23**, activating T-helper cells (Th17) and innate lymphoid cells.
3. **Lymphocyte Homing**: Activated gut lymphocytes upregulate homing receptors (like $\alpha_4\beta_7$ integrin). These cells traffic through the circulation and home to the joints and entheses, which express matching adhesion molecules (MAdCAM-1).
4. **Local Inflammation**: Infiltrating cells secrete **TNF-$\alpha$** and **IL-17**, driving synovitis, enthesitis, and bone erosion.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Peripheral Arthritis Patterns
* **Type 1 (Pauciarticular / Oligoarticular) ($~60-70\%$)**:
  * **Joints**: Affects $<5$ joints, typically large, lower-extremity joints (knees, ankles, hips) in an asymmetric pattern.
  * **Onset**: Acute, self-limiting (attacks last $<10$ weeks).
  * **Bowel Link**: **Flares mirror the activity of the bowel disease.** A flare of UC or Crohn's is accompanied by joint swelling; successful treatment of the bowel inflammation resolves the Type 1 arthritis.
* **Type 2 (Polyarticular) ($~30-40\%$)**:
  * **Joints**: Affects $\ge 5$ joints symmetrically, involving small joints of the hands (MCP, PIP) and wrists, mimicking Rheumatoid Arthritis.
  * **Onset**: Chronic, persisting for months to years.
  * **Bowel Link**: **Runs completely independent of bowel disease activity.** Treating colonic inflammation does not resolve Type 2 arthritis.

### 2. Axial Arthritis (Spondyloarthritis)
* Clinically indistinguishable from **Ankylosing Spondylitis**. Presents as inflammatory back pain, night pain, and limited spinal mobility.
* **Bowel Link**: **Runs completely independent of bowel disease activity.**
* **Genetics**: Strongly associated with **HLA-B27** (up to $50-70\%$ of patients with axial enteropathic arthritis are HLA-B27 positive, compared to only $5-10\%$ of those with isolated peripheral enteropathic arthritis).

### 3. Extra-articular Features (Shared with IBD)
* **Cutaneous**: **Erythema Nodosum** (painful, red nodules on shins, mirrors bowel activity) and **Pyoderma Gangrenosum** (rapidly progressive, painful skin ulcers with purpuric, undermined borders; runs independent of bowel activity).
* **Mucosal**: Painless aphthous oral ulcers.
* **Ocular**: Acute anterior uveitis or episcleritis.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Rheumatoid Arthritis]]**: Symmetric polyarthritis with positive RF/anti-CCP and no bowel symptoms.
* **[[Reactive Arthritis]]**: Sterile arthritis following a *transient* enteric infection (*Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter*), whereas Enteropathic Arthritis is associated with chronic, relapsing IBD.
* **Celiac Disease**: Can present with arthralgias and osteopenia; resolves with a gluten-free diet.

---

## 🧪 Investigations
* **Serology**: Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP are **characteristically negative**.
* **Inflammatory Markers**: ESR and CRP are elevated, reflecting both active bowel and joint inflammation.
* **Stool Tests**: Fecal Calprotectin is elevated (highly sensitive marker for active bowel inflammation).
* **Endoscopy & Biopsy**: Colonoscopy with terminal ileoscopy and biopsies to confirm and monitor Crohn's disease or Ulcerative Colitis.
* **Imaging (SI joints & Spine)**: Sacroiliitis on X-ray or bone marrow edema on MRI (confirming axial disease).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 🚨 Critical Cautions in Prescribing
* **Avoid Standard NSAIDs**: Classic NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Indomethacin) **can precipitate severe flares of IBD** by disrupting mucosal prostaglandin synthesis. Selective COX-2 inhibitors (e.g., Celecoxib) may be used in short courses with caution.
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **IL-17 Inhibitors (Secukinumab) are Strictly Contraindicated**: IL-17 is a key cytokine for maintaining gut mucosal epithelial barrier integrity and host defense. Blocking IL-17 can disrupt this barrier, leading to severe, new-onset, or life-threatening flares of Crohn's or UC.

### First-line Medical Management
* **Sulfasalazine (DOC for peripheral arthritis)**: Acts as a dual agent. The 5-ASA component treats active colonic inflammation, while the sulfapyridine component treats peripheral joint synovitis.
* **Corticosteroids**: Systemic or local steroid injections can bridge joint and bowel flares.

### Biologic Therapy (For refractory peripheral or axial disease)
* **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors (Monoclonal Antibodies)**:
  * **Infliximab** or **Adalimumab** are highly effective as they treat both active IBD and axial/peripheral arthritis.
  * *Note*: **Etanercept is ineffective** for enteropathic arthritis because it is a decoy receptor that does not treat active bowel disease.
* **IL-12/23 Inhibitors (Ustekinumab)**: Highly effective for Crohn's/UC and peripheral enteropathic arthritis. Ineffective for axial spondyloarthritis.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Monoclonal Antibody Rule**: If a patient with active Crohn's disease and ankylosing-like spondylitis fails NSAIDs, **do not prescribe Etanercept**. Use **Infliximab** or **Adalimumab**. Etanercept lacks efficacy in Crohn's disease and can lead to bowel relapse.
* > [!TIP]
  > **EN vs. PG Bowel Mirroring**: Erythema Nodosum (EN) on the shins is highly correlated with active bowel disease; treating the gut flare will heal the shins. Pyoderma Gangrenosum (PG), however, runs an independent course and requires aggressive systemic immunosuppression regardless of bowel state.
