# 🦴 Fibromyalgia (โรคไฟโบรมัยอัลเจีย)

> **Definition**: Fibromyalgia is a chronic, widespread musculoskeletal pain disorder characterized by fatigue, non-restorative sleep, cognitive dysfunction ("fibro fog"), and somatic symptoms. It is driven by **central sensitization** (neurological amplification of pain signals) rather than peripheral tissue inflammation or joint destruction.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Affects approximately $2-8\%$ of the general population.
* **Gender Ratio**: Significantly female-dominated ($2-9:1$).
* **Age of Onset**: Most commonly presents in middle age (30-50 years), but can occur at any age.
* **Comorbidities**: Highly associated with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), migraine, interstitial cystitis, and anxiety/depression.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
Pathology lies in the **central nervous system**, not the joints or muscles:
1. **Central Sensitization**: Increased excitability of nociceptive neurons in the central nervous system.
2. **Sensory Amplification**: 
   * **Hyperalgesia**: Exaggerated pain response to painful stimuli.
   * **Allodynia**: Perception of pain from normally non-painful stimuli (e.g., light touch).
3. **Neurotransmitter Imbalance**: 
   * Elevated levels of excitatory neurotransmitters (Substance P, glutamate) in CSF.
   * Decreased levels of inhibitory neurotransmitters (serotonin, norepinephrine) in descending pain pathways.
4. **HPA Axis & Sleep Dysfunction**: Impaired hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis and disruption of slow-wave (Phase 4) non-REM sleep (causing persistent fatigue).

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               CENTRAL SENSITIZATION OF CNS             │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ▼
          Inadequate Descending Pain Inhibition
          (Low Serotonin & Norepinephrine levels)
                            │
                            ▼
         Amplification of Pain Signals in Spinal Cord
                            │
      ┌─────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
      ▼                                            ▼
Allodynia (Pain from touch)            Hyperalgesia (Severe pain response)
```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Widespread Pain**: Diffuse, chronic, aching pain affecting **both sides** of the body, **above and below** the waist, and the **axial skeleton** for **$\ge 3$ months**.
* **Persistent Fatigue**: Severe, present even upon waking (non-restorative/unrefreshing sleep).
* **Cognitive Dysfunction ("Fibro Fog")**: Memory lapses, difficulty concentrating, mental cloudiness.
* **Somatic / Psychiatric Symptoms**: 
  * Tension headaches or migraines.
  * Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) (abdominal pain, bloating, diarrhea/constipation).
  * Paresthesias (numbness/tingling without objective neurological deficits).
  * Mood disturbances (anxiety, depression).
* **Physical Exam**: Widespread subjective tenderness to palpation. **No objective signs of inflammation** (no joint swelling, warmth, redness, or muscle atrophy). Normal motor strength, sensation, and reflexes.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Myofascial Pain Syndrome]] (MPS)**: Characterized by **localized** muscle pain with **trigger points** (palpation causes referred pain in a specific zone) and taut bands. Fibromyalgia features widespread pain, **tender points** (local pain only), and prominent systemic symptoms (fatigue, sleep problems).
* **[[Polymyalgia Rheumatica]] (PMR)**: Occurs in patients **$>50$ years** old. Presents with pelvic and shoulder girdle pain/stiffness. Marked by **highly elevated ESR and CRP** and rapid improvement with low-dose steroids (Prednisolone 15 mg/day).
* **Hypothyroidism**: Presents with fatigue, weight gain, and diffuse myalgias. Excluded by checking **TSH** levels.
* **[[Polymyositis]] / [[Dermatomyositis]]**: Presents with objective proximal muscle weakness (difficulty climbing stairs, combing hair) and **elevated muscle enzymes (CK, Aldolase)**. Fibromyalgia features subjective pain but normal strength and normal CK.
* **[[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] (RA)**: Polyarticular inflammatory joint swelling, morning stiffness $>1$ hour, positive RF/anti-CCP.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
**Fibromyalgia is a clinical diagnosis. Investigations serve only to rule out other organic diseases.**

* **Routine Labs (Must be normal in Fibromyalgia)**:
  * **CBC**: Normal.
  * **ESR & CRP**: Normal (rules out inflammatory/rheumatologic diseases).
  * **TSH**: Normal (rules out hypothyroidism).
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: Normal (rules out myositis).
  * **Calcium, Phosphate, Vitamin D**: Normal (rules out metabolic bone disease/osteomalacia).
* **Autoantibody Screen** (ANA, RF): Negative (unless patient has comorbid SLE or RA).

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
### 2016 ACR Diagnostic Criteria
Diagnosis requires meeting **all** the following:
1. **Widespread Pain Index (WPI)** $\ge 7$ and **Symptom Severity Scale (SSS)** score $\ge 5$ **OR** WPI $3-6$ and SSS score $\ge 9$.
   * *WPI*: List of 19 body areas where pain was felt in the past week.
   * *SSS*: Rates severity ($0-3$) of: fatigue, waking unrefreshed, cognitive symptoms, and severity of somatic symptoms.
2. Symptoms have been present at a similar level for **at least 3 months**.
3. **No longer a diagnosis of exclusion**: The presence of other clinically important diagnoses does not exclude a diagnosis of fibromyalgia.

---

## 🏥 Management
Treatment must be multidisciplinary and focus on function and symptoms rather than total pain elimination.

### 1. Patient Education (Most Critical Step)
* Reassure that fibromyalgia is a real biological disorder of central pain processing.
* Emphasize that it is **not** progressive, deforming, or life-threatening.
* Focus on active coping mechanisms.

### 2. Non-Pharmacological Therapy (Highly Effective)
* **Aerobic Exercise**: **Most effective long-term therapy.** Gradual, low-impact aerobic exercise (walking, swimming, water aerobics, cycling) tailored to tolerance. Start low and go slow.
* **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**: Helps modify pain perception, manage sleep disturbances, and reduce stress.
* **Sleep Hygiene**: Establish a regular sleep schedule, avoid caffeine/alcohol close to bedtime.

### 3. Pharmacological Therapy (Targeting Central Neurotransmitters)
* **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs)**:
  * **Amitriptyline (10 - 50 mg PO qhs)**: **First-line choice.** Given at bedtime. Promotes restorative sleep, relieves pain and fatigue.
* **Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs)**:
  * **Duloxetine (30 - 60 mg PO daily)** or **Milnacipran (50 mg PO bid)**: Approved for fibromyalgia pain. Highly effective if comorbid depression or anxiety is present.
* **Gabapentinoids (Calcium Channel $\alpha_2$-$\delta$ Ligands)**:
  * **Pregabalin (75 - 225 mg PO bid)** or **Gabapentin**: Binds to voltage-gated calcium channels in the CNS to reduce release of excitatory neurotransmitters. Improves pain and sleep quality.
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Inappropriate Medications in Fibromyalgia**:
  > * **Opioids**: Do **not** use. They are ineffective for central pain, carry risks of dependency, and can cause **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia** (making pain worse).
  > * **Corticosteroids**: Do **not** use. Fibromyalgia is not an inflammatory disease; steroids have no efficacy and cause severe long-term side effects.
  > * **Oral NSAIDs**: Have no role as monotherapy for fibromyalgia (ineffective for central sensitization).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Normal Labs Rule**: Normal CBC, ESR, CRP, CK, and TSH in a patient with years of severe, widespread body pain and fatigue is the classic clinical signature of **Fibromyalgia**. Do not keep ordering expensive imaging or labs once these baseline tests are confirmed normal.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Secondary Fibromyalgia Trap**: Up to 25% of patients with established Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE, or Sjögren's syndrome develop secondary Fibromyalgia. If a patient with well-controlled RA (normal joint exam, normal CRP) complains of new, severe, widespread pain and fatigue, suspect secondary fibromyalgia. Do not escalate immunosuppressants/biologics; treat with exercise and TCAs instead.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Aerobic Exercise Prescription**: Tell patients: "Exercise will make your pain slightly worse for the first 1-2 weeks. This is normal and does not mean your joints are being damaged. Keep going at a low intensity, and the pain and fatigue will improve."
