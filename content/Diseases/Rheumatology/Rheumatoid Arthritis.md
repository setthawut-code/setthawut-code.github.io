## type: disease

# Definition
Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) is a chronic, systemic, autoimmune inflammatory disease characterized by a symmetric, erosive, and destructive polyarthritis. It primarily targets the synovial lining of small joints, leading to progressive cartilage degradation, bone erosions, joint deformities, and systemic extra-articular manifestations.

# Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Affects approximately $0.5 - 1\%$ of the adult population globally.
* **Gender Ratio**: Significantly more common in females, with a $2-3:1$ female-to-male ratio.
* **Age of Onset**: Can occur at any age, but most commonly peaks between 30 and 50 years.
* **Risk Factors**: Genetic predisposition (strong link to **HLA-DR4** allele, specifically the "shared epitope"), female sex, family history, and **smoking** (the strongest environmental risk factor).

# Etiology
RA is triggered by environmental factors (such as smoking or periodontal infection with *Porphyromonas gingivalis*) acting on a genetically susceptible host:
* **Citrullination**: Environmental irritants trigger post-translational modification of proteins, converting the amino acid arginine to **citrulline** in mucosal tissues (lungs or gums).
* **Autoantibody Production**: In HLA-DR4 positive individuals, these citrullinated proteins are recognized as foreign, leading to the activation of immune cells and the production of autoantibodies: **Anti-Cyclic Citrullinated Peptide (anti-CCP)** and **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**.

# Pathophysiology
1. **Synovitis**: Autoantibodies and immune complexes localize in the joints, activating the complement system and recruiting macrophages, T-helper cells (Th1, Th17), and B-cells into the synovial membrane.
2. **Cytokine Cascade**: Infiltrating inflammatory cells release pro-inflammatory cytokines: **TNF-$\alpha$, IL-1, and IL-6**. These cytokines drive synovial cell proliferation and hypervascularity.
3. **Pannus Formation**: The synovium transforms into an invasive, tumor-like granulation tissue called a **pannus**.
4. **Bone and Cartilage Erosion**: The pannus releases matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) which digest the articular cartilage. It also expresses **RANKL**, which binds to osteoclasts and drives localized subchondral bone resorption (marginal erosions).
5. **Joint Instability**: Chronic inflammation stretches the surrounding joint capsule and tendons, causing subluxation, joint instability, and classic deformities.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Symmetric Polyarthritis**: Pain, swelling, and tenderness affecting $\ge 5$ joints symmetrically.
* **Morning Stiffness**: Joint stiffness lasting **$>1$ hour** in the morning, which **improves with physical activity** or warmth.
* **Constitutional Symptoms**: Low-grade fever, fatigue, weight loss, and generalized myalgias.

## Joint Involvement Pattern
* **Affected Joints**: Symmetrically involves the small joints of the hands and feet: **Metacarpophalangeal (MCP)**, **Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP)**, and Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joints, followed by wrists, ankles, knees, elbows, and shoulders.
* **Spared Joints**: Characteristically **spares the Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) joints** and the thoracic/lumbar spine. (cervical spine is the only spinal region commonly involved).

## Classic Hand Deformities (Late/Advanced Stage)
* **Swan-Neck Deformity**: Hyperextension of the PIP joint with flexion of the DIP joint.
* **Boutonniere Deformity**: Flexion of the PIP joint with hyperextension of the DIP joint.
* **Ulnar Deviation**: Lateral drift of the fingers at the MCP joints due to tendon subluxation.
* **Z-Deformity of the Thumb**: Flexion of the MCP joint and hyperextension of the IP joint.
* **Piano Key Sign**: Instability of the distal radioulnar joint leading to upward subluxation of the ulnar head (moves downward like a piano key when pressed).

## Extra-articular Manifestations (Occurs in up to 40%)
* **Cutaneous**: **Rheumatoid Nodules** (painless, firm subcutaneous nodules over extensor surfaces like the elbow, associated with high RF/anti-CCP titers).
* **Ocular**: Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (dry eyes, seen in secondary Sjögren's syndrome), **Episcleritis** (benign, self-limiting), and **Scleritis** (painful, can lead to scleral thinning/perforation).
* **Pulmonary**: **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)** (rheumatoid ILD, typically UIP pattern), **Pleural Effusion** (exudative, with characteristically **extremely low glucose ($<30\text{ mg/dL}$ or even $<10\text{ mg/dL}$)** due to local utilization).
* **Hematologic**: Mild anemia of chronic disease.
* **Felty's Syndrome**: Triad of **longstanding RA + Splenomegaly + Neutropenia** (increases risk of severe bacterial infections).
* **Vasculitis**: Rheumatoid vasculitis presenting as mononeuritis multiplex or digital ischemia/gangrene.

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Osteoarthritis]] (OA)**: Differentiated by mechanical joint pain (worse with activity, improves with rest), morning stiffness **$<30$ minutes**, involvement of the **DIP joints** (Heberden's nodes) and PIP joints (Bouchard's nodes), asymmetric pattern, and absence of systemic features.
* **[[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] (SLE)**: Arthritis in SLE is symmetric and polyarticular but characteristically **non-erosive** and **reversible** (**Jaccoud's arthropathy**). Differentiated by multi-organ involvement (rash, nephritis, cytopenias) and ANA/anti-dsDNA serology.
* **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA)**: Characterized by asymmetric involvement, prominent **DIP joint** involvement, dactylitis, and presence of psoriatic skin plaques and nail pitting.
* **Chronic Tophaceous [[Gout]]**: Can present as chronic polyarthritis. Tophi can mimic rheumatoid nodules, but synovial fluid will demonstrate monosodium urate crystals.

# Investigations

## Laboratory (Serology)
* **Anti-Cyclic Citrullinated Peptide (Anti-CCP)**: **Highly specific ($>95-98\%$)** for RA. Positive early in the disease and correlates with aggressive, erosive joint damage.
* **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**: Sensitive ($70-80\%$) but less specific ($~80\%$). Can be positive in other autoimmune conditions (Sjögren's, SLE), infections (IE, Hepatitis C), and healthy elderly.
* **Inflammatory Markers**: Elevated ESR and CRP (used to monitor disease activity and therapeutic response).
* **CBC**: Normocytic normochromic anemia (anemia of chronic disease), thrombocytosis (acute phase reactant).

## Synovial Fluid Analysis
* Done primarily to rule out septic or crystal arthritis during an acute monoarticular flare. Shows an inflammatory profile (WBC $2,000 - 50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with $>50\%$ PMNs) without crystals or organisms.

## Imaging
* **Plain Radiography of Hands and Feet**:
  * **Early**: Soft tissue swelling and **periarticular osteopenia** (demineralization of bone adjacent to inflamed joints).
  * **Late**: Symmetric **joint space narrowing** and **marginal bone erosions** (erosions at the margins of the joint where synovium meets bone, lacking cartilage protection).
* **Joint Ultrasound / MRI**: Much more sensitive than X-rays for detecting early synovitis, synovial hypertrophy, and subchondral bone erosions before they become visible on plain radiographs.

# Diagnostic Criteria
### 2010 ACR / EULAR Classification Criteria (Requires score $\ge 6 / 10$)
1. **Joint Involvement** (0–5 points): Based on number and size of involved joints (more points for many small joints).
2. **Serology** (0–3 points): Negative, low-positive, or high-positive RF and Anti-CCP.
3. **Acute-Phase Reactants** (0–1 point): Normal or abnormal ESR and CRP.
4. **Duration of Symptoms** (0–1 point): $<6$ weeks vs. $\ge 6$ weeks.

# Management

The goal is early, aggressive treatment to achieve **remission or low disease activity** ("Treat-to-Target").

## 1. Conventional Synthetic DMARDs (First-line)
* Must be started **immediately** upon diagnosis to prevent irreversible joint damage.
* **Methotrexate (MTX)**: The anchor drug for RA.
  * *Dose*: **7.5 - 25 mg PO or SC once weekly** (never daily!).
  * *Co-prescription*: **Folic Acid (1-5 mg daily, or 5 mg once weekly on a different day than MTX)** to reduce side effects (mucositis, alopecia, GI upset, cytopenias).
  * *Monitoring*: CBC, liver function tests (LFTs), and renal function (MTX is renally cleared). Contraindicated in pregnancy, chronic liver disease, and severe renal impairment.
* **Alternative Conventional DMARDs**:
  * **Sulfasalazine** (safe in pregnancy; causes leukopenia, rash).
  * **Leflunomide** (alternative to MTX; teratogenic, hepatotoxic).
  * **Hydroxychloroquine** (indicated for mild, non-erosive disease or as part of combination therapy; requires baseline and annual screening for retinal toxicity/maculopathy).

## 2. Bridging Therapy
* Conventional DMARDs take **6 to 12 weeks** to become fully effective.
* Use **low-dose oral corticosteroids** (e.g., **Prednisolone $\le 10\text{ mg/day}$**) or NSAIDs as bridging therapy to control pain and inflammation while DMARDs are taking effect. Corticosteroids should be tapered and discontinued once DMARD control is achieved.

## 3. Biologic and Targeted Synthetic DMARDs
Indicated if the patient has moderate-to-high disease activity despite optimized Methotrexate monotherapy or combination therapy for 3-6 months.
* **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors**: **Adalimumab**, **Etanercept**, **Infliximab**.
  * *Pre-treatment Screening*: **Must screen for latent Tuberculosis** (with PPD or IGRA) and **Hepatitis B/C** prior to initiation, as TNF blockers can reactivate these infections.
* **Non-TNF Biologics**:
  * **Rituximab** (anti-CD20 B-cell depleting antibody; highly effective in RF/anti-CCP positive patients).
  * **Abatacept** (T-cell co-stimulation inhibitor).
  * **Tocilizumab** (IL-6 receptor antagonist).
* **JAK Inhibitors** (targeted oral small molecules): **Tofacitinib**, **Baricitinib** (associated with increased risk of herpes zoster reactivation, thromboembolism, and cardiovascular events).

# Complications
* **Cervical Spine Subluxation (C1-C2 / Atlantoaxial Subluxation)**:
  * Destruction of the transverse ligament of the atlas by rheumatoid pannus allows the dens of C2 to slip posteriorly, compressing the cervical spinal cord.
  * Can be fatal during endotracheal intubation if the neck is flexed.
* **Cardiovascular Disease**: **The #1 cause of death in RA patients.** Systemic chronic inflammation accelerates atherosclerosis.
* **Osteoporosis**: Due to localized joint inflammation, systemic steroid therapy, and immobility.
* **Amyloidosis**: Chronic inflammatory state leads to secondary AA amyloidosis (rare).

# Prognosis
* Chronically progressive, variable course.
* Early initiation of DMARD therapy significantly improves long-term outcomes and reduces the risk of joint replacement surgery.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Flexion-Extension Cervical Spine X-Rays**: Every patient with longstanding, deforming Rheumatoid Arthritis must have cervical spine X-rays in flexion and extension **prior to undergoing elective surgery requiring general anesthesia**. If C1-C2 subluxation is present, normal neck extension during intubation can compress the spinal cord, leading to quadriplegia or death.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Methotrexate Dosing Warning**: Always counsel patients that Methotrexate is taken **ONCE WEEKLY**, not daily. Daily administration leads to severe bone marrow suppression, mucosal ulceration, and death.
