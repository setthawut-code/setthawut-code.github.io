# 🦴 ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (โรคหลอดเลือดอักเสบชนิดสัมพันธ์กับแอนกา)

> **Definition**: ANCA-Associated Vasculitis (AAV) is a group of systemic necrotizing vasculitides that predominantly affect small-sized blood vessels (capillaries, venules, and arterioles) and are characterized by the presence of antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA). It includes three distinct clinical entities:
> 1. **Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA)**
> 2. **Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA)**
> 3. **Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA)**

---

## 📊 Comparison of AAV Subtypes

| Feature | Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA) | Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA) | Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Former Name** | Wegener's Granulomatosis | - | Churg-Strauss Syndrome |
| **ANCA Pattern** | **c-ANCA / Anti-PR3** ($>90\%$ in active disease) | **p-ANCA / Anti-MPO** ($70-80\%$) | **p-ANCA / Anti-MPO** (only $40-50\%$; negative in others) |
| **Histopathology** | **Necrotizing granulomas**, necrotizing vasculitis, pauci-immune GN. | **Necrotizing vasculitis**, pauci-immune GN. **No granulomas**. | **Eosinophilic infiltrates**, necrotizing granulomas, vasculitis. |
| **Primary Organs** | Upper airway, Lungs, Kidneys. | Kidneys, Lungs. | Lungs (Asthma), Skin, Heart, Nerves. |
| **Key Symptoms** | Sinusitis, saddle nose, cavitary lung nodules, hematuria. | RPGN (kidney failure), Hemoptysis (alveolar hemorrhage). | Severe asthma, eosinophilia, mononeuritis multiplex, myocarditis. |

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
AAV is characterized by ANCA-mediated neutrophil activation leading to endothelial damage:
1. **Priming**: Cytokines (TNF-$\alpha$ or IL-1) released during minor infections prime neutrophils, causing the translocation of intracellular **Proteinase 3 (PR3)** and **Myeloperoxidase (MPO)** to the cell membrane.
2. **ANCA Binding**: Circulating PR3-ANCA or MPO-ANCA autoantibodies bind to these surface antigens, cross-linking the Fc receptors.
3. **Neutrophil Activation**: Activated neutrophils undergo degranulation, releasing toxic oxygen free radicals, lysosomal proteolytic enzymes, and **Neutrophil Extracellular Traps (NETs)**.
4. **Endothelial Injury**: Neutrophils adhere to and migrate through the vascular endothelium, causing necrotizing vascular injury and thrombosis.
5. **Pauci-immune Glomerulonephritis**: Renal injury is characterized by severe glomerular necrosis and crescents with **minimal or absent deposition of immunoglobulins or complement** (hence "pauci-immune").

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA)
* **Upper Respiratory Tract (Most common, $90\%$)**: Chronic refractory sinusitis, purulent/bloody nasal discharge, nasal mucosal ulcerations, and destruction of nasal cartilage leading to **Saddle Nose Deformity**. Otitis media and subglottic stenosis are also common.
* **Lower Respiratory Tract**: Cough, hemoptysis, and dyspnea. Chest CT reveals bilateral **cavitary nodules** or infiltrates.
* **Renal**: Pauci-immune crescentic glomerulonephritis, presenting as hematuria, proteinuria, and rapidly progressive renal failure.

### 2. Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA)
* **Renal ($>90\%$)**: Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN) is the hallmark. Patients present with hematuria, RBC casts in urine, and rapid doubling of serum creatinine within weeks.
* **Pulmonary ($30-50\%$)**: Pulmonary capillaritis leading to **Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH)**. Presents with hemoptysis, dyspnea, drop in hemoglobin, and diffuse bilateral ground-glass opacities on chest CT.
* **Absence of Granulomas**: Does not cause destructive upper airway lesions (no saddle nose) and lacks cavitary granulomatous nodules on chest imaging.

### 3. Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA)
* **Asthma**: Severe, corticosteroid-dependent asthma (often precedes vasculitis by years).
* **Eosinophilia**: Marked peripheral blood eosinophilia ($>1,500/\mu\text{L}$ or $>10\%$).
* **Transient Pulmonary Infiltrates**: Non-cavitary, migratory infiltrates on chest imaging.
* **Neuropathy**: Mononeuritis multiplex (e.g., asymmetric foot drop or wrist drop) due to vasculitis of the vasa nervorum.
* **Cardiac**: Eosinophilic myocarditis or coronary vasculitis (the #1 cause of death in EGPA).

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Goodpasture's Disease (Anti-GBM)**: Presents with pulmonary-renal syndrome (cavitary lesions, alveolar hemorrhage, GN), but is driven by **anti-GBM antibodies** targeting Type IV collagen. Biopsy shows linear IgG deposition on the glomerular basement membrane (contrasting with the pauci-immune pattern of AAV).
* **SLE or IgA Vasculitis**: Immune complex-mediated glomerulonephritis (biopsy shows abundant Ig/complement deposition).
* **Infectious Endocarditis**: Can mimic AAV with glomerulonephritis, purpura, and positive ANCA. Ruled out by blood cultures and echocardiogram.

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **ANCA Serology**:
  * **c-ANCA (PR3-ANCA)**: Highly associated with GPA.
  * **p-ANCA (MPO-ANCA)**: Associated with MPA and renal-limited EGPA.
* **Urinalysis**: Essential screening for glomerulonephritis. Look for **dysmorphic RBCs**, **RBC casts**, and proteinuria.
* **Renal Function**: Serial serum creatinine and BUN.
* **CBC**: Marked eosinophilia in EGPA; leukocytosis and thrombocytosis in active GPA/MPA.

### Imaging
* **Chest HRCT**:
  * *GPA*: Thick-walled cavitary nodules.
  * *MPA*: Ground-glass opacities (indicating alveolar hemorrhage).
  * *EGPA*: Non-segmental, transient migratory infiltrates.

### 🚨 Tissue Biopsy (Diagnostic Gold Standard)
* **Renal Biopsy**: Shows **Pauci-immune Necrotizing Crescentic Glomerulonephritis** (crescents in Bowman's space representing severe glomerular injury).
* **Nasal or Lung Biopsy**: Shows necrotizing vasculitis and granulomatous inflammation (for GPA).

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Induction of Remission (For active, organ-threatening disease)
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**:
  * IV Methylprednisolone (500 - 1,000 mg daily for 3 days) followed by oral Prednisolone (1 mg/kg/day, tapered slowly).
* **Immunosuppressive Agents (Combine with steroids)**:
  * **Cyclophosphamide** (IV pulses or PO daily) OR
  * **Rituximab** (Anti-CD20 B-cell depleting antibody; preferred in premenopausal women due to ovarian toxicity of cyclophosphamide).
* **Plasmapheresis (Plasma Exchange)**: Indicated for patients presenting with severe DAH (hypoxemic respiratory failure) or advanced renal failure (creatinine $>5.7\text{ mg/dL}$).

### 2. Maintenance of Remission (Once stable, usually after 3-6 months)
* Taper off steroids.
* Switch to safer maintenance immunosuppressants: **Rituximab** (q6 months) or **Azathioprine** (1.5-2.5 mg/kg/day) or **Methotrexate**.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Urinary Cast Verification**: Finding **Red Blood Cell Casts** in the urine of a vasculitis patient indicates active glomerulonephritis (glomerular bleed). This is an absolute indicator of organ-threatening disease and requires immediate high-dose steroid pulse therapy and cyclophosphamide/rituximab induction.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **DAH Presentation**: Diffuse Alveolar Hemorrhage (DAH) can present *without* hemoptysis in up to $30\%$ of cases (blood pooling in alveoli without triggering cough). Monitor chest CT and serial hematocrit; a sudden drop in Hb accompanied by new ground-glass opacities on chest imaging is DAH until proven otherwise.
