# 🦴 Dermatomyositis (โรคผิวหนังและกล้ามเนื้ออักเสบ)

> **Definition**: Dermatomyositis (DM) is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory myopathy characterized by progressive, symmetric proximal muscle weakness accompanied by pathognomonic cutaneous manifestations. It features a humoral-mediated microangiopathy and carries a strong paraneoplastic association in adult patients.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* **Gender Ratio**: Twice as common in females ($2:1$ female-to-male ratio).
* **Age Distribution**: Bimodal age of onset:
  * Juvenile Dermatomyositis: Ages 5 to 15 years (rarely associated with cancer).
  * Adult Dermatomyositis: Ages 40 to 60 years.
* **Malignancy Risk**: Adult-onset DM is a paraneoplastic condition in **$20 - 30\%$** of cases. The risk is highest in the first 2-3 years after diagnosis.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
DM is characterized by a humoral-mediated microangiopathy targeting capillaries in the perimysium and endomysium:
1. **Complement Activation**: In response to an autoimmune trigger, antibodies activate the classical complement pathway.
2. **Capillary Endothelial Damage**: Complement proteins deposit as **C5b-9 membrane attack complexes (MAC)** on the endothelial wall of endomysial capillaries.
3. **Microvascular Ischemia**: Capillary destruction and thrombosis cause capillary loss (dropout) and localized muscle ischemia.
4. **Perifascicular Atrophy**: Ischemia leads to degeneration and necrosis of muscle fibers at the periphery of the muscle fascicles (**perifascicular atrophy**), which is the pathological hallmark of DM.
5. **Inflammatory Infiltrate**: Infiltration of CD4+ T-helper cells and B-cells, located in the **perivascular** and **perimysial** spaces.

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Muscle Weakness
* **Symmetric, Proximal Weakness**: Subacute, progressive weakness affecting the shoulder girdle (difficulty lifting objects, brushing hair) and hip girdle (difficulty climbing stairs, rising from chairs).
* **Striated Upper Esophageal Muscle Involvement**: Presents as dysphagia, raising a high risk of aspiration.
* **Normal Neuro Exam**: Sensation and deep tendon reflexes are characteristically **normal** (distinguishes it from neuropathy/myasthenia).

### 2. Cutaneous Manifestations (Pathognomonic for DM)
* **Heliotrope Rash**: Erythematous-to-violaceous discoloration of the upper eyelids, frequently accompanied by periorbital edema.
* **Gottron's Papules**: Erythematous, scaly, flat-topped papules located over the dorsal aspect of the MCP, PIP, and DIP joints (extensor surfaces of the hands).
* **Gottron's Sign**: Macular erythema over extensor surfaces of knees, elbows, or ankles.
* **Shawl Sign**: Photosensitive, macular erythema over the upper back, shoulders, and posterior neck.
* **V-Sign**: Photosensitive, macular erythema over the anterior neck and upper chest.
* **Holster Sign**: Macular erythema on the lateral aspects of the thighs (below the greater trochanter).
* **Mechanic's Hands**: Hyperkeratotic, dry, painful cracking of the skin along the lateral and palmar aspects of the fingers.
* **Periungual Telangiectasias**: Dilated capillary loops at the nailfold with cuticular hypertrophy.

### 3. Systemic Manifestations
* **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**: Occurs in $30-40\%$ of patients. Presenting with dry cough and progressive dyspnea.
* **Polyarthritis**: Symmetric, non-erosive inflammatory arthritis of small joints.
* **Raynaud's Phenomenon**.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Polymyositis]]**: Presents with identical proximal muscle weakness and elevated CK, but lacks any cutaneous manifestations and has endomysial CD8+ cell pathology on biopsy.
* **Drug-Induced Myopathy (Statins)**: Myalgias, elevated CK, resolves after stopping statins.
* **Glucocorticoid-Induced Myopathy**: Proximal weakness but **muscle enzymes (CK) are completely normal** and there is no inflammation on biopsy.
* **Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR)**: Pain and stiffness in the shoulder/hip girdles in patients $>50$ years, but **no true muscle weakness** (weakness is due to pain) and CK is normal.

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **Muscle Enzymes**:
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: Elevated **$5 - 50$ times** the upper limit of normal during active disease.
  * **Aldolase**, **AST, ALT, and LDH**: Also elevated.
* **Myositis-Specific Antibodies (MSAs)**:
  * **Anti-Mi-2** (Anti-helicase): Highly specific for DM; associated with classic rashes, good response to steroids, and lower cancer risk.
  * **Anti-Jo-1**: Associated with **Antisynthetase Syndrome** (myositis, ILD, mechanic's hands, fever, Raynaud's, arthritis).
  * **Anti-MDA5**: Associated with amyopathic/hypomyopathic DM (minimal myositis but severe skin ulcers and rapidly progressive, often fatal ILD).
  * **Anti-TIF1-$\gamma$ & Anti-NXP2**: Associated with **cancer-associated myositis** in adults.

### Imaging & Diagnostics
* **Muscle MRI (T2/STIR)**: Shows hyperintensity (muscle edema) in active disease. Used to guide muscle biopsy.
* **Electromyography (EMG)**: Shows a myopathic triad (short-duration, small-amplitude MUAPs; increased insertional activity/fibrillations; high-frequency repetitive discharges).

### Muscle Biopsy (Diagnostic Gold Standard)
* Key finding: **Perivascular/perimysial inflammatory infiltrates** (CD4+ T-cells, B-cells) and **perifascicular atrophy**.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Immunosuppressive Therapy
* **First-line (Corticosteroids)**: High-dose oral **Prednisolone (1 mg/kg/day)**. If bulbar or respiratory muscles are involved, start with IV Methylprednisolone pulse (1 g IV daily for 3 days). Taper slowly over 9-12 months.
* **Steroid-Sparing Agents (Conventional DMARDs)**: Started early to reduce steroid side effects.
  * **Methotrexate** or **Azathioprine** (preferred if the patient has co-existing interstitial lung disease or liver disease).
* **Refractory Disease**:
  * **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: Highly effective for severe skin lesions, dysphagia, and refractory weakness.
  * **Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF)**: Preferred for active myositis-associated ILD.

### 2. Mandatory Malignancy Screening (Adult Form Only)
* Perform a thorough cancer screening at diagnosis and repeat annually for 3 years:
  * CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis.
  * Mammography and pelvic ultrasound / Pap smear (females).
  * PSA and digital rectal exam (males).
  * Upper endoscopy and colonoscopy.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Steroid CK Rule**: In a patient on high-dose steroids who develops worsening weakness:
  > * If **CK is elevated** $\rightarrow$ disease flare (increase steroids).
  > * If **CK is normal** $\rightarrow$ steroid myopathy (taper steroids and begin physical therapy).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Cancer Screening Alert**: Dermatomyositis in adults is a classic paraneoplastic syndrome. Diagnosing an adult with DM must trigger a comprehensive cancer workup. Ovarian, breast, lung, colorectal, and gastric cancers are the most commonly associated malignancies.
