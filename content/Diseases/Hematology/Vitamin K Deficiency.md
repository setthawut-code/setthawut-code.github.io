# 🩸 Vitamin K Deficiency (ภาวะขาดวิตามินเค)

> **Definition**: An acquired coagulopathy caused by inadequate tissue levels of Vitamin K, leading to impaired gamma-carboxylation of clotting Factors II, VII, IX, X and anticoagulant Proteins C and S, resulting in defective secondary hemostasis.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Risk Factors
Vitamin K is fat-soluble. It is sourced from diet (Vitamin K1, phylloquinone from green vegetables) and synthesized by gut microbiota (Vitamin K2, menaquinone).
* **Biliary Obstruction & Malabsorption**: Cholestasis, primary biliary cholangitis, chronic pancreatitis, Celiac disease, Crohn's disease, or cystic fibrosis (biliary salts are required for fat-soluble vitamin absorption).
* **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics**: Sterilizes intestinal flora, eliminating the primary source of endogenous Vitamin K2.
* **Newborns** (Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding / VKDB): 
  * Sterile neonatal gut (no bacterial synthesis).
  * Poor placental transfer of Vitamin K.
  * Very low Vitamin K content in breast milk.
  * *Prevention*: Routine administration of **1 mg IM Vitamin K at birth**.
* **Dietary Deficiency**: Malnutrition, prolonged total parenteral nutrition (TPN) without vitamin supplementation.
* **Warfarin Therapy**: Direct pharmacological inhibition of **Vitamin K Epoxide Reductase** (VKORC1).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **Carboxylation Failure**: Vitamin K acts as an essential cofactor for **$\gamma$-glutamyl carboxylase**. This enzyme adds carboxyl groups to glutamic acid residues on coagulation proteins:
   * **Clotting Factors**: **II (Prothrombin), VII, IX, and X**.
   * **Anticoagulants**: **Protein C and Protein S**.
2. **Calcium Binding Defect**: Gamma-carboxylation is mandatory for these factors to bind calcium ($Ca^{2+}$). Calcium binding allows these proteins to undergo conformational changes and anchor to negatively charged phospholipid membranes on activated platelets.
3. **Coagulation Arrest**: Without carboxylation, non-functional factor precursors (known as **PIVKAs** - *Proteins Induced by Vitamin K Absence*) circulate in plasma, unable to participate in the coagulation cascade.

```
                      VITAMIN K INTAKE / SYNTHESIS
                                   │
                                   ▼
                     Reduced Vitamin K (Active hydroquinone)
                                   │  ◄─── [Warfarin blocks recycling here (VKORC1)]
                                   ▼
        Glutamic Acid Residues on Factors II, VII, IX, X, C, S
                                   │  (gamma-glutamyl carboxylase)
                                   ▼
                     Gamma-Carboxylated Factors
                                   │
                                   ▼
                Calcium Binding & Membrane Anchoring
                                   │
                                   ▼
                           Normal Thrombin Burst
```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Easy Bruising & Ecchymoses**: Large, superficial skin hemorrhages.
* **Mucosal Bleeding**: Epistaxis, bleeding gums, hematuria, gastrointestinal bleeding.
* Spontaneous oozing from venipuncture sites, surgical wounds, or chest tube drainages.
* In neonates (VKDB): Umbilical stump oozing, generalized ecchymoses, and life-threatening intracranial hemorrhage.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Prothrombin Time (PT / INR)**: **Markedly prolonged.** 
  * *Mechanism*: **Factor VII** has the shortest plasma half-life of all clotting factors ($\sim 6$ hours); hence, PT is the earliest and most sensitive marker of Vitamin K deficiency.
* **aPTT**: **Prolonged** in moderate-to-severe deficiency (as factors IX, X, and II are depleted).
* **Platelet Count & Bleeding Time**: **Normal** (primary hemostasis is intact).
* **Mixing Study**: **Corrects to normal** (confirms a factor deficiency, ruling out inhibitors).
* **Specific Factor Assays**: Reveals decreased activity of factors II, VII, IX, and X.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Vitamin K1 (Phytonadione) Replacement
* **Oral Route (PO)**: Preferred for asymptomatic or mild bleeding. Corrects INR within 24 hours. Dosage: **2.5 to 10 mg PO**.
* **Intravenous Route (IV)**: Indicated for severe or life-threatening hemorrhage. Corrects INR within 6-12 hours. Dosage: **1 to 10 mg IV slowly**.
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **IV Vitamin K Anaphylaxis**: Rapid IV infusion of Vitamin K can precipitate severe, life-threatening **anaphylactoid reactions** (dyspnea, chest pain, hypotension). Always dilute the dose in 50 mL of D5W or NS and infuse slowly over **20 to 30 minutes**.
* **Subcutaneous Route (SC)**: **Do not use.** Absorption is highly erratic and unpredictable.

### 2. Immediate Correction (Life-Threatening Bleeding or Emergency Surgery)
* **Four-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC)** (Kcentra): Contains concentrated factors II, VII, IX, X.
  * **Preferred over FFP**: Achieves immediate INR correction, does not require blood-type cross-matching, and avoids fluid volume overload.
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)** ($15\text{ mL/kg}$ IV): Second-line if PCC is unavailable. Requires cross-matching and takes longer to infuse (volume overload risk).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Factor VII Short Half-Life**: In early Vitamin K deficiency (or starting Warfarin), the PT/INR will spike while the PTT remains normal. This is because Factor VII drops first. As deficiency worsens, factors IX, X, and II drop, prolonging the PTT as well.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The Fat-Soluble Malabsorption Trap**: A patient with chronic diarrhea (Celiac/Crohn's) or biliary obstruction presenting with easy bruising and a prolonged PT must be evaluated for Vitamin K deficiency. Co-check other fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E).
* > [!TIP]
  > **VKDB Prevention**: Breastfed infants are at higher risk for VKDB because breast milk contains negligible Vitamin K. Ensure the infant received the intramuscular Vitamin K injection at birth.
