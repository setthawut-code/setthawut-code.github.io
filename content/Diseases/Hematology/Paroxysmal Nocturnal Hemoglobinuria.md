# 🩸 Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria / PNH (โรคปัสสาวะสีโค้กตอนตื่นนอน)

> **Definition**: An acquired somatic clonal disorder of hematopoietic stem cells caused by a mutation in the **PIGA** gene. This leads to a deficiency of glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor proteins, rendering cells highly susceptible to complement-mediated intravascular lysis.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **PIGA Mutation**: Somatic mutation in the *phosphatidylinositol glycan anchor biosynthesis class A* (*PIGA*) gene on the X chromosome of a hematopoietic stem cell.
2. **GPI-Anchor Deficiency**: Cells derived from this clone lack the GPI anchor, preventing the surface expression of GPI-linked regulatory proteins:
   * **CD55** (Decay-Accelerating Factor / DAF): Inhibits C3 convertase formation.
   * **CD59** (Membrane Inhibitor of Reactive Lysis / MIRL): Prevents C9 polymerisation onto the C5b-8 complex, blocking the **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC)**.
3. **Complement Lysis**: Without CD55 and CD59, normal complement activation (triggered by subclinical respiratory acidosis during sleep or infection) proceeds unchecked, inserting MAC into RBC membranes and causing **intravascular hemolysis**.
4. **Thrombosis Mechanism**: Complement-mediated activation of platelets and endothelial cells, along with depletion of nitric oxide (NO) by free hemoglobin, triggers a massive pro-thrombotic state.

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
Classic clinical presentation is characterized by the triad of:
1. **Intravascular Hemolysis**: Episodic, classically presenting as **cola-colored urine in the morning** (urine concentrates overnight; clears during the day).
2. **Thrombosis** (Atypical Venous Sites): The #1 cause of death in PNH. Occurs in hepatic veins (**Budd-Chiari syndrome**), portal vein, sagittal sinus, mesenteric veins, or dermal veins.
3. **Pancytopenia / Bone Marrow Failure**: PNH clones often arise in the setting of, or progress to, [[Aplastic Anemia]] or Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS).
4. **Nitric Oxide Depletion Symptoms**: Free hemoglobin binds nitric oxide (NO), causing smooth muscle dystonia:
   * Esophageal spasm (dysphagia).
   * Male erectile dysfunction.
   * Severe, episodic abdominal pain.

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Flow Cytometry (Gold Standard)**: Demonstrates **absence of CD55 and CD59** on red blood cells, monocytes, and granulocytes. **FLAER** (fluorescently labeled aerolysin) assay is highly sensitive for detecting GPI-deficient white blood cell clones.
* **Hemolysis Markers**: Low haptoglobin, elevated LDH (classically $>10\times$ upper limit), elevated indirect bilirubin.
* **Urine Studies**: Positive for hemoglobin, **positive for hemosiderin** (indicates chronic intravascular hemolysis).
* **Ham Test & Sucrose Hemolysis Test**: Historical screening tests showing lysis in acidified or sucrose-rich serum. Now replaced by flow cytometry.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Complement-Targeted Therapy
* **Eculizumab** (Soliris): Humanized monoclonal antibody targeting **C5**. Blocks cleavage to C5a/C5b, preventing MAC formation. Prevents intravascular hemolysis, reduces transfusion dependence, and cuts thrombosis risk by 90%.
  * *Alternative*: **Ravulizumab** (longer half-life).
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Meningococcal Infection Risk**: C5-C9 blockade impairs defense against encapsulated bacteria. All patients **must receive Meningococcal vaccination** (quadrivalent and serogroup B) at least **2 weeks before starting Eculizumab**, and receive daily prophylactic penicillin throughout treatment.

### 2. Supportive Care
* **Folic Acid**: 1-5 mg PO daily.
* **Iron Replacement**: Required due to chronic loss via hemoglobinuria/hemosiderinuria. Monitor ferritin closely to avoid overload.
* **Anticoagulation**: Indicated for active thrombosis.

### 3. Definitive Cure
* **Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT)**: Curative; reserved for patients with severe coexisting bone marrow failure or refractory thrombosis.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Budd-Chiari Link**: A young patient presenting with signs of portal hypertension, ascites, and RUQ pain due to **hepatic vein thrombosis** (Budd-Chiari syndrome) combined with hemolytic anemia must be screened for **PNH** via flow cytometry.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Hemosiderinuria**: Unlike extravascular hemolysis where hemoglobin is recycled in spleen, intravascular hemolysis dumps free hemoglobin into urine. Renal tubular cells absorb it, turn it into hemosiderin, and are shed. Hemosiderinuria is a classic diagnostic marker for intravascular hemolysis.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Flow Cytometry Timing**: Flow cytometry can be performed at any time, even during or after blood transfusions (although transfused normal RBCs will express CD55/59, PNH clones will still be detected on granulocytes/monocytes, which are not transfused).
