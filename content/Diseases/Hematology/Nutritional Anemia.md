# 🩸 Nutritional Anemia (โรคโลหิตจางจากการขาดสารอาหาร)

> **Definition**: Anemia caused by deficiency of essential nutrients required for normal erythropoiesis, most commonly **iron**, **vitamin B12**, and **folate**.

---

## 📊 Classification by MCV

```
                       NUTRITIONAL ANEMIA
                               │
      ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
      ▼                        ▼                        ▼
  MICROCYTIC (MCV < 80)    NORMOCYTIC (MCV 80-100)  MACROCYTIC (MCV > 100)
  • Iron Deficiency        • Combined Deficiencies  • Megaloblastic:
                             (e.g., Iron + B12/Folate)  - Vitamin B12 Def.
                           • Early stage deficiency     - Folate Def.
```

---

## 🔍 Diagnostic Workup

### 1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Indices
* **Hemoglobin (Hb) & Hematocrit (Hct)**: Low.
* **Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)**: Directs to microcytic vs. macrocytic pathology.
* **Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW)**: **Elevated** in nutritional anemias (reflects anisocytosis; helps differentiate IDA [high RDW] from Thalassemia trait [normal RDW]).
* **Reticulocyte Count**: **Low** (inadequate bone marrow response due to lack of building blocks; reticulocyte production index [RPI] $<2$).

### 2. Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)
* **Microcytic/Hypochromic**: Small red cells with central pallor $>1/3$ diameter (suggests [[Iron Deficiency Anemia]]).
* **Macro-ovalocytes**: Large, oval-shaped red cells (suggests [[Vitamin B12 Deficiency Anemia]] or [[Folate Deficiency Anemia]]).
* **Hypersegmented Neutrophils**: PMNs with $\ge 6$ lobes or $\ge 5\%$ with $\ge 5$ lobes (early indicator of megaloblastic anemia).

### 3. Nutrient Status Panels

| Parameter | Iron Deficiency Anemia | Vitamin B12 Deficiency | Folate Deficiency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ferritin** | **Low ($<30\text{ ng/mL}$)** | Normal/High | Normal |
| **TIBC** | **High** | Normal | Normal |
| **Transferrin Sat.** | **Low ($<15\%$ )** | Normal | Normal |
| **Serum B12** | Normal | **Low ($<200\text{ pg/mL}$)** | Normal |
| **Serum/RBC Folate** | Normal | Normal | **Low** |
| **Homocysteine** | Normal | **High** | **High** |
| **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)** | Normal | **High** | **Normal** |

---

## 🏥 Management Principles
1. **Identify and Treat Underlying Cause**: Do not just replace nutrients. Rule out chronic blood loss (e.g., GI malignancy in elderly, menorrhagia in females) or malabsorption.
2. **Targeted Replacement**:
   * *Iron*: Oral ferrous sulfate/fumarate or IV iron (sucrose/carboxymaltose).
   * *Vitamin B12*: Oral or IM Cyanocobalamin.
   * *Folate*: Oral Folic acid.
3. > [!IMPORTANT]
   > **Dimorphic Blood Picture**: Combined iron and B12/folate deficiency can present with a **normal MCV** because the small cells (microcytic) and large cells (macrocytic) average out. RDW will be extremely high. Blood smear will show both populations (dimorphic picture).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!WARNING]
   > **Never Give Folate Alone in B12 Def.**: If a patient has combined B12 and folate deficiency (or unknown macrocytic cause), **never treat with folic acid alone**. Folic acid corrects the anemia but accelerates and worsens irreversible neurological damage (Subacute Combined Degeneration) from untreated B12 deficiency. Always replace B12 first or concurrently.
