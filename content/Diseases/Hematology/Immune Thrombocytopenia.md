# 🩸 Immune Thrombocytopenia / ITP (โรคเกล็ดเลือดต่ำจากภูมิคุ้มกันทำลาย)

> **Definition**: An acquired autoimmune disorder characterized by isolated thrombocytopenia (platelet count $<100,000/\mu\text{L}$) due to antibody-mediated platelet destruction and impaired platelet production, in the absence of other cytopenias or underlying causes.

---

## 📊 Subtypes & Etiology
* **Primary ITP**: Occurs in isolation without associated disease.
* **Secondary ITP**: Associated with an underlying condition:
  * Systemic Lupus Erythematosus ([[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]]), CLL, non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
  * Infections: **HIV**, **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV)**, ***H. pylori***.
* **Clinical Course**:
  * **Acute ITP**: Most common in **children**. Often follows a viral infection, presents abruptly, and is self-limiting (resolves within 6 months in $>80\%$ of cases).
  * **Chronic ITP**: Most common in **adults** (especially young females). Insidious onset, rarely self-limiting, and persists $>12$ months.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **Autoantibody Production**: B cells produce **IgG autoantibodies** directed against platelet membrane glycoproteins, most commonly **GP IIb/IIIa** or **GP Ib-IX**.
2. **Splenic Clearance**: Opsonized platelets are recognized by Fc receptors on splenic macrophages, leading to phagocytosis and extravascular destruction. Spleen is also the major site of autoantibody production.
3. **Megakaryocyte Suppression**: Autoantibodies also target megakaryocytes in the bone marrow, suppressing platelet production and compensating capacity.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Mucocutaneous Bleeding**: Petechiae, dry purpura (bruising on skin), wet purpura (bleeding from mucous membranes: epistaxis, gingival bleeding, menorrhagia, hematuria).
* **Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Rare ($<1\%$) but the most lethal complication; typically occurs when platelet count $<10,000/\mu\text{L}$.
* **Key Negative Finding**: **Absence of Splenomegaly**.
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **No Splenomegaly Rule**: Splenomegaly is not a feature of primary ITP (despite the spleen being the site of destruction). If the spleen is palpable, suspect portal hypertension, splenic sequestration, leukemia, or lymphoma.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
ITP is a diagnosis of exclusion.
* **CBC**: Isolated **thrombocytopenia (platelets $<100,000/\mu\text{L}$)**. Hemoglobin and WBC counts are normal (unless anemia is present due to heavy bleeding).
* **Peripheral Smear**: Decreased platelets with **large/giant platelets** (megathrombocytes, reflecting young platelets and active bone marrow turnover). **No schistocytes** (rules out TTP/HUS/DIC), no blasts.
* **Bone Marrow Aspirate** (Not routinely required): Shows **normal or increased megakaryocytes** (erythroid and myeloid lineages are normal). 
  * *Indications*: Age $>60$ years (rule out MDS), atypical features, systemic symptoms, or prior to splenectomy.
* **Secondary Screening**: Always test for **HIV**, **HCV**, and ***H. pylori***. Perform ANA screen if SLE suspected.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. When to Initiate Treatment
* **Platelet count $\ge 30,000/\mu\text{L}$ & No Bleeding**: Observation only (no treatment), regardless of adult/child status.
* **Platelet count $< 30,000/\mu\text{L}$ OR Active Bleeding**: Initiate treatment.

### 2. First-Line Pharmacotherapy
* **Corticosteroids** (Mainstay):
  * **Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day PO** for 2-3 weeks, followed by a gradual taper once platelets recover.
  * **Dexamethasone 40 mg PO daily for 4 days** (high-dose pulse; associated with faster response and fewer steroid side effects).
* **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**:
  * **1 g/kg/day IV for 1 to 2 days**.
  * *Mechanism*: Saturates Fc receptors on splenic macrophages, blocking opsonized platelet clearance.
  * *Indication*: **Life-threatening bleeding**, severe thrombocytopenia ($<10,000/\mu\text{L}$) refractory to steroids, or prior to urgent surgery. Platelets rise rapidly (within 24-48 hours) but effect is transient (lasts 1-2 weeks).

### 3. Second-Line Therapy (For Chronic/Refractory ITP)
* **Thrombopoietin (TPO) Receptor Agonists**: **Eltrombopag (50 mg PO daily)** or **Romiplostim** (weekly SC). Stimulates megakaryocyte proliferation and platelet production.
* **Rituximab**: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Eliminates B-cell clones producing the autoantibodies.
* **Splenectomy**: Surgical removal of the primary site of opsonized platelet destruction. Offers a $60-70\%$ long-term cure rate. Delay if possible for $>12$ months from diagnosis. Vaccinate against pneumococcus/meningococcus/H. influenzae 2 weeks prior.

### 4. Emergency Bleeding Protocol (Platelets < 10,000/mcL + Hemorrhage)
* Combine **Platelet Transfusion** + **IVIG (1 g/kg)** + **IV Methylprednisolone (1 g daily)**.
  * *Rationale*: Transfused platelets are rapidly destroyed by autoantibodies; IVIG must be given concurrently to blockade macrophage Fc receptors and prolong transfused platelet survival.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Platelet Transfusion Trap**: Never transfuse platelets to an asymptomatic ITP patient, even if the platelet count is $5,000/\mu\text{L}$. The autoantibodies will destroy the transfused platelets within minutes, offering no therapeutic benefit. Reserve transfusion strictly for severe/life-threatening active hemorrhage.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Pseudothrombocytopenia**: A falsely low platelet count caused by platelet clumping in EDTA-containing blood tubes. Suspect if the patient has a platelet count of $15,000/\mu\text{L}$ but has zero petechiae or bruising. Confirm by checking a peripheral blood smear and re-running the CBC in a sodium citrate (blue top) tube.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Evans Syndrome**: Co-occurrence of [[Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia]] (DAT positive) and ITP. Requires aggressive immunosuppression.
