# 🩸 Folate Deficiency Anemia (โรคโลหิตจางจากการขาดโฟเลต)

> **Definition**: Macrocytic, megaloblastic anemia caused by deficiency of folate (vitamin B9), leading to impaired DNA synthesis without neurological demyelination.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Risk Factors
* **Dietary Insufficiency**: Inadequate intake of fresh green leafy vegetables, fruits, and liver. Folate stores are small (lasting only **3 to 4 months**; B12 stores last 3 to 5 years). Folate is easily destroyed by prolonged cooking.
* **Chronic Alcoholism**: Decreases dietary intake, impairs intestinal folate absorption, and directly inhibits enterohepatic folate circulation.
* **Increased Demand**:
  * **Pregnancy and Lactation** (essential to prevent **neural tube defects**).
  * Chronic hemolytic anemias (e.g., [[Thalassemia]], sickle cell anemia).
  * Exfoliative skin disorders (e.g., severe psoriasis).
* **Malabsorption**: Celiac disease, tropical sprue.
* **Medications**:
  * **Methotrexate**, Trimethoprim, Pyrimethamine (inhibit **Dihydrofolate Reductase / DHFR**).
  * **Phenytoin** (impairs folate absorption in the gut).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
* Folate is converted to tetrahydrofolate (THF), a crucial coenzyme for single-carbon transfer reactions in the synthesis of purines and thymidylate (needed for DNA replication).
* **DNA-Cytoplasm Asynchrony**: Inadequate folate leads to halted DNA synthesis. Cells cannot divide, producing large RBC precursors with mature cytoplasm but immature nuclei (megaloblasts).
* Folate is **not** required for the conversion of methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. Therefore, **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) levels remain normal**, and **no neurological demyelination occurs**.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Anemia Signs**: Fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, mild scleral icterus (due to ineffective erythropoiesis).
* **Gastrointestinal**: Angular cheilitis, atrophic glossitis (smooth, painful tongue), and diarrhea/malabsorption.
* **Pregnancy**: High risk of fetal **neural tube defects** (spina bifida, anencephaly).
* **Key Negative Finding**: **No neurological symptoms** (no Subacute Combined Degeneration, normal reflexes, normal proprioception and vibration).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **MCV**: High ($>100\text{ fL}$).
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Macro-ovalocytes** and **hypersegmented neutrophils** ($\ge 5$ lobes).

### 2. Serum / RBC Folate & Metabolites
* **Serum Folate**: Low ($<3\text{ ng/mL}$). Highly sensitive to recent dietary intake (can normalize with a single hospital meal).
* **Red Blood Cell (RBC) Folate**: **Low ($<150\text{ ng/mL}$)**. Refers to tissue folate status over the lifetime of the RBC (120 days); a more stable and accurate measure of chronic deficiency.
* **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**: **Normal** (rules out B12 deficiency).
* **Homocysteine**: **Elevated**.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Oral Folate Replacement
* **Regimen**: **Folic Acid 1 to 5 mg PO daily** for 1 to 4 months or until hematologic recovery.
* *Note*: If drug-induced by DHFR inhibitors (e.g., Methotrexate toxicity), treat with **Folinic Acid (Leucovorin)** instead of folic acid, as it bypasses DHFR blockade.

### 2. Prophylaxis in Pregnancy
* **Low-Risk Females**: **0.4 mg PO daily** starting 1 month before conception through the first trimester.
* **High-Risk Females** (previous pregnancy with neural tube defect, or taking anticonvulsants): **4 mg PO daily**.

### 3. Monitoring
* **Reticulocyte Count**: Peaks in **5 to 7 days** after starting replacement.
* **Hematocrit/Hemoglobin**: Normalizes within **1 to 2 months**.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **B12 Rule in Megaloblastic Anemia**: Never start folic acid replacement in a patient with megaloblastic anemia until **Vitamin B12 deficiency has been explicitly ruled out** (by checking B12 level or MMA). Folic acid corrects the anemia of B12 deficiency but allows the subacute combined degeneration (SCD) to progress unchecked, leading to irreversible spinal cord damage.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Leucovorin Rescue**: In patients receiving high-dose Methotrexate for oncology, Folinic Acid (Leucovorin) is administered ("Leucovorin Rescue") to protect healthy tissues from folate depletion and bone marrow suppression.
