# 🩸 Hereditary Spherocytosis / HS (โรคเม็ดเลือดแดงรูปทรงกลมแต่กำเนิด)

> **Definition**: An inherited (mostly autosomal dominant) disorder of the red blood cell membrane characterized by defects in skeletal proteins, leading to vertical membrane instability, loss of surface area, formation of rigid spherocytes, and selective extravascular splenic clearance.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Pathophysiology
1. **Protein Defects**: Autosomal dominant mutations in genes coding for RBC membrane skeleton proteins:
   * **Ankyrin** (most common, $40-50\%$).
   * **$\beta$-Spectrin** or $\alpha$-Spectrin.
   * **Band 3** protein or Protein 4.2.
2. **Membrane Blebbing**: Defect disrupts the link between the lipid bilayer and cytoskeleton. Portions of the lipid bilayer uncouple and are shed as microvesicles.
3. **Spherocyte Formation**: Loss of membrane surface area relative to cytoplasm volume forces the cell into its lowest-energy physical shape—a sphere. Spherocytes are rigid and lack the deformability of normal biconcave discs.
4. **Splenic Destruction**: Spherocytes cannot deform to pass through the $2-3\ \mu\text{m}$ fenestrations of the splenic endothelial slit cords. They are trapped, damaged by acid/hypoxia (splenic conditioning), and phagocytosed by macrophages (**extravascular hemolysis**).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Classic Triad**: **Anemia** (mild to moderate), **Jaundice** (fluctuating), and **Splenomegaly** (due to congestion of splenic cords).
* **Gallstones**: Pigment (calcium bilirubinate) cholelithiasis, often presenting in childhood or young adulthood.
* **Aplastic Crisis**: Triggered by **Parvovirus B19** infection. Halts red cell production, causing a sudden, life-threatening drop in hemoglobin and reticulocytopenia.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **MCHC**: **Elevated ($>36\text{ g/dL}$)**. *Key diagnostic clue reflecting relative cell dehydration.*
* **MCV**: Normal or mildly decreased.
* **Reticulocyte Count**: High ($5-20\%$).
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Spherocytes** (small, round, hyperchromic RBCs lacking central pallor) and polychromasia.

### 2. Confirmatory Assays
* **Eosin-5-Maleimide (EMA) Binding Test (Gold Standard)**: Flow cytometry test measuring fluorescence of dye bound to Band 3 on RBCs. **Reduced fluorescence is diagnostic** (high sensitivity and specificity; preferred test).
* **Osmotic Fragility Test**: Incubated RBCs are exposed to hypotonic saline. Spherocytes lyse at higher saline concentrations than normal RBCs (decreased osmotic tolerance).
* **Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT / Coombs)**: **Must be Negative** (rules out Warm [[Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia]], which is morphologically identical on smear).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Medical Management
* **Folic Acid**: 1-5 mg PO daily to prevent folate depletion from high erythroid turnover.
* **Avoid Iron**: Do not prescribe iron supplements (hemolysis recycles iron; risk of secondary overload).

### 2. Surgical Management (Definitive)
* **Splenectomy**:
  * *Indication*: Moderate to severe anemia, poor growth, or severe transfusion dependence. Eliminates the primary site of RBC destruction, resolving anemia and jaundice (spherocytes remain on blood smear but survive normally).
  * *Timing*: Delay until **$>5 - 6$ years** of age to reduce risk of overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI).
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Splenectomy Prophylaxis**: Immunize against **S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, and H. influenzae** at least **2 weeks prior** to surgery. Prescribe daily oral penicillin prophylaxis post-splenectomy, and advise immediate medical attention for any fever.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **High MCHC Marker**: A patient with hemolytic anemia, splenomegaly, and a Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC) **$>36\text{ g/dL}$** has **Hereditary Spherocytosis** until proven otherwise.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Smear Mimicry**: Always perform a Coombs test (DAT) in a patient with spherocytes on blood smear.
  > * Coombs positive $\rightarrow$ **Warm AIHA**.
  > * Coombs negative $\rightarrow$ **Hereditary Spherocytosis** (or other non-immune causes like burns/microangiopathy).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Family History**: HS is autosomal dominant. Always ask if parents or siblings had early-onset gallstones or underwent splenectomy at a young age.
