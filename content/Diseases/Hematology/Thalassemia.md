# 🩸 Thalassemia (โรคธาลัสซีเมีย)

> **Definition**: A group of inherited autosomal recessive disorders characterized by reduced or absent synthesis of one or more globin chains ($\alpha$ or $\beta$) of hemoglobin, leading to microcytic anemia, ineffective erythropoiesis, and hemolytic anemia. Extremely prevalent in Southeast Asia and Thailand.

---

## 📊 Classification & Genetics

### 1. Alpha ($\alpha$)-Thalassemia (Chromosome 16 deletions)
Normally, there are 4 functional $\alpha$-globin genes ($\alpha\alpha/\alpha\alpha$):
* **Silent Carrier ($-\alpha/\alpha\alpha$)**: 1 gene deleted. Asymptomatic, normal CBC and Hb electrophoresis.
* **$\alpha$-Thalassemia Trait ($--/\alpha\alpha$ [cis] or $-\alpha/-\alpha$ [trans])**: 2 genes deleted. Asymptomatic, mild microcytosis, normal Hb electrophoresis in adults (requires DNA analysis to diagnose).
* **Hemoglobin H Disease ($--/-\alpha$)**: 3 genes deleted. Excess $\beta$-chains form unstable tetramers ($\beta_4 = \text{Hb H}$). Presents as chronic moderate hemolytic anemia, splenomegaly.
* **Hb Bart's Hydrops Fetalis ($--/--$)**: 4 genes deleted. No $\alpha$-chains. Excess $\gamma$-chains form tetramers ($\gamma_4 = \text{Hb Bart's}$), which have extremely high oxygen affinity and cannot release oxygen to tissues. Leads to severe intrauterine hypoxia, congestive heart failure, hydrops, and **death in utero** or shortly after birth.

### 2. Beta ($\beta$)-Thalassemia (Chromosome 11 point mutations)
Normally, there are 2 functional $\beta$-globin genes ($\beta/\beta$):
* **$\beta$-Thalassemia Minor / Trait ($\beta^+/\beta$ or $\beta^0/\beta$)**: 1 mutated gene. Mild microcytic anemia, asymptomatic. **Elevated Hb $A_2$ ($>3.5\%$)**.
* **$\beta$-Thalassemia Intermedia**: Moderately decreased $\beta$-globin synthesis. Moderate anemia, variable transfusion dependency, hepatosplenomegaly.
* **$\beta$-Thalassemia Major ($\beta^0/\beta^0$)**: Complete absence of $\beta$-globin. Severe transfusion-dependent hemolytic anemia presenting in late infancy (as Hb F switches to Hb A). Features skeletal deformities, hepatosplenomegaly.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **Globin Chain Imbalance**: Reduced synthesis of one chain causes relative excess of the opposite chain.
2. **Precipitation & Hemolysis**: The excess free chains precipitate within RBC precursors in the bone marrow, causing membrane damage and premature cell death (**ineffective erythropoiesis**), and in mature RBCs, leading to splenic clearance (**extravascular hemolysis**).
3. **Erythroid Hyperplasia**: Severe anemia stimulates erythropoietin (EPO) release, causing massive bone marrow expansion (cortical thinning, skeletal deformities, and extramedullary hematopoiesis).
4. **Iron Overload**: Caused by chronic blood transfusions + increased intestinal iron absorption (driven by suppressed hepcidin secondary to ineffective erythropoiesis).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Anemia & Hemolysis**: Pallor, scleral icterus, dark urine, splenomegaly, gallstones (pigment stones).
* **Bone Marrow Expansion Signs** (in severe $\beta$-thal major):
  * **Thalassemic Facies**: Frontal bossing, prominent maxilla with dental malocclusion, flat nasal bridge.
  * **X-ray skull**: **"Hair-on-end"** appearance (widening of diploic space).
* **Iron Overload Complications (Hemosiderosis)**: Heart failure, arrhythmias, liver cirrhosis, diabetes mellitus, hypogonadism, skin hyperpigmentation ("bronze diabetes").

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **Hb/Hct**: Low.
* **MCV**: Very low (often $<70\text{ fL}$, disproportionate to the mild anemia in traits).
* **RDW**: **Normal** in uncomplicated traits (helps differentiate from IDA).
* **Mentzer Index**: **$< 13$** ($\text{MCV}/\text{RBC}$; thalassemic traits have high RBC count/erythrocytosis).
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Target cells**, microcytes, hypochromia, teardrop cells, nucleated RBCs (severe cases).
* **Special Stain**: **Brilliant Cresyl Blue (BCB)** stain reveals **inclusion bodies ("golf-ball cells")** in Hb H disease.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. General Measures
* **Folic Acid**: 1-5 mg PO daily to support hyperactive erythropoiesis.
* **Avoid Empiric Iron**: **Do not give iron supplements** to patients with microcytic anemia unless concurrent iron deficiency is strictly proven. It worsens systemic iron overload.

### 2. Transfusion Therapy
* **Indication**: Severe anemia (Hb $<7\text{ g/dL}$) with growth failure, bone changes, or cardiac symptoms.
* **Target**: Maintain pre-transfusion Hb **$>9.5 - 10\text{ g/dL}$** to suppress endogenous ineffective erythropoiesis and prevent skeletal deformities.

### 3. Iron Chelation Therapy (Essential for Transfused Patients)
* **Indication**: Serum ferritin **$>1000\text{ ng/mL}$**, or after $\sim 20$ units of PRBC transfusions.
* **Agents**:
  * **Deferasirox** (Exjade): Oral daily (preferred). *Side effects: renal toxicity, GI upset.*
  * **Deferiprone** (L1): Oral. *Side effects: agranulocytosis (monitor CBC weekly).*
  * **Deferoxamine** (Desferal): SC or IV infusion over 8-12 hours. *Side effects: ototoxicity, visual changes.*

### 4. Surgical & Definitive Therapy
* **Splenectomy**: Indicated if transfusion requirement increases by $>50\%$ per year (hypersplenism) or symptomatic splenomegaly.
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Post-Splenectomy Sepsis Prevention**: Splenectomy increases risk of lethal sepsis by encapsulated organisms. Administer vaccinations against **S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, and H. influenzae** at least **2 weeks prior** to surgery, and provide prophylactic penicillin post-operatively.
* **Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT)**: Only curative option; best outcomes in young children with HLA-matched sibling donors.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **BCB Golf-Ball Cell Rule**: Finding "golf-ball" inclusion bodies on a Brilliant Cresyl Blue (BCB) incubated smear is diagnostic of **Hb H Disease**. These represent precipitated $\beta_4$ tetramers.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Pregnant Hb Bart's Screening**: In Thailand, screen pregnant couples for $\alpha$-thalassemia-1 trait ($--/$) to prevent Hb Bart's hydrops fetalis. If both parents carry the deletion, there is a 25% risk.
* > [!TIP]
  > **The RDW Clue**: In microcytic anemia, if MCV is 62 and RDW is normal (e.g., 12.5%), suspect Thalassemia trait. If MCV is 62 and RDW is high (e.g., 18.2%), suspect Iron Deficiency Anemia.
