# 🩸 Vitamin B12 Deficiency Anemia (โรคโลหิตจางจากการขาดวิตามินบี 12)

> **Definition**: Macrocytic, megaloblastic anemia caused by deficiency of vitamin B12 (cobalamin), leading to impaired DNA synthesis and demyelination of the nervous system.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Risk Factors
* **Dietary Deficiency**: Strict vegans or vegetarians (B12 is found exclusively in animal-sourced foods).
* **Pernicious Anemia**: Autoimmune destruction of **gastric parietal cells**, leading to loss of **Intrinsic Factor (IF)**, which is essential for B12 absorption.
* **Malabsorption**:
  * *Gastric*: Total or subtotal gastrectomy, bariatric surgery (loss of parietal cells).
  * *Ileal*: Crohn's disease, terminal ileal resection (**B12-IF complex is absorbed in the terminal ileum**).
  * *Competitors*: *Diphyllobothrium latum* (fish tapeworm) infection, Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO).
* **Medications**:
  * **Metformin** (interferes with calcium-dependent B12 absorption in the terminal ileum).
  * **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)** / H2 blockers (gastric acid is required to cleave B12 from food proteins).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
B12 is a coenzyme for two critical biochemical reactions:
1. **Methionine Synthase (DNA Synthesis)**: Converts homocysteine to methionine. In B12 deficiency, folate is trapped in the methyl-THF form ("folate trap"), which blocks purine/pyrimidine synthesis, halting RBC nuclear maturation while cytoplasm grows (megaloblastic changes).
2. **Methylmalonyl-CoA Mutase (Myelin Synthesis)**: Converts methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. B12 deficiency leads to accumulation of **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**, which disrupts myelin sheath synthesis, causing demyelination in the spinal cord and peripheral nerves.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Anemia Signs**: Fatigue, pallor, mild jaundice (due to ineffective erythropoiesis/intramedullary hemolysis).
* **Gastrointestinal**: **Hunter's Glossitis** (beefy, red, smooth, painful tongue).
* **Subacute Combined Degeneration (SCD) of the Spinal Cord**:
  * **Posterior Columns**: Loss of vibratory sensation and proprioception, sensory ataxia (positive Romberg sign).
  * **Lateral Corticospinal Tracts**: Spastic paresis, hyperreflexia, and positive Babinski sign.
  * **Symmetric Peripheral Neuropathy**: Numbness and paresthesias in a glove-and-stocking pattern.
  * **Neuropsychiatric**: Memory loss, irritability, dementia ("megaloblastic madness").

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **MCV**: High ($>100\text{ fL}$, often $>110\text{ fL}$).
* **Pancytopenia**: Low WBC and platelets (severe cases).
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Macro-ovalocytes** (large, oval RBCs) and **hypersegmented neutrophils** ($\ge 5\%$ of neutrophils with $\ge 5$ lobes, or any with $\ge 6$ lobes).

### 2. Serum B12 & Metabolites
* **Serum Vitamin B12**: Low ($<200\text{ pg/mL}$).
* **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**: **Elevated** (highly sensitive and specific; differentiates B12 deficiency from folate deficiency).
* **Homocysteine**: **Elevated**.

### 3. Etiology Testing (Pernicious Anemia)
* **Anti-Intrinsic Factor Antibodies**: Highly specific ($>95\%$) but low sensitivity.
* **Anti-Parietal Cell Antibodies**: Highly sensitive ($>90\%$) but low specificity.
* **Serum Gastrin**: Elevated.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Intramuscular (IM) Replacement (Preferred)
* **Indication**: Pernicious anemia, ileal resection, gastrectomy, or patients with neurological symptoms.
* **Regimen**: **Cyanocobalamin 1000 mcg IM daily for 1 week**, followed by weekly for 4 weeks, and then **monthly for life**.

### 2. Oral Replacement
* **Regimen**: Cyanocobalamin 1000 - 2000 mcg PO daily.
* *Mechanism*: Passive diffusion absorbs $\sim 1\%$ of the oral dose even without intrinsic factor or terminal ileum. Not recommended if neurological symptoms are severe.

### 3. Monitoring & Complications
* **Hypokalemia**: Rapid erythropoiesis consumes potassium. Monitor serum potassium in the first 48 hours of replacement, especially in severe anemia.
* **Reticulocyte Count**: Rises within **5 to 7 days** after starting therapy.
* **Neurological Recovery**: Reversible if treated within 6 months of onset; may become permanent if delayed.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!CAUTION]
  > **The Folate Trap Trap**: Treating a patient with macrocytic anemia using folic acid alone without checking B12 levels will correct the hematologic parameters (by-passing the folate trap) but **will worsen and accelerate the irreversible demyelinating neurological damage**. Always check both B12 and folate levels first.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **B12 vs. Folate Metabolite Rule**:
  > * High Homocysteine + High MMA $\rightarrow$ **Vitamin B12 Deficiency**.
  > * High Homocysteine + Normal MMA $\rightarrow$ **Folate Deficiency**.
* > [!TIP]
  > ** Hunter's Glossitis**: A smooth, red, burning tongue is a key physical sign of B12 deficiency. It results from atrophy of the filiform papillae due to rapid cell turnover failure.
