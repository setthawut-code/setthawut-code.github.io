# 🩸 Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation / DIC (ภาวะลิ่มเลือดกระจายในหลอดเลือด / Consumptive Coagulopathy)

> **Definition**: Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), also known as **Consumptive Coagulopathy**, is an acquired, life-threatening systemic clinicopathologic syndrome characterized by widespread microvascular thrombosis (causing tissue ischemia/organ failure) and secondary consumption of platelets and coagulation factors (causing severe, uncontrolled hemorrhage).

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
DIC is a pathological "double-edged sword" featuring:
1. **Systemic Microvascular Thrombosis**: Fibrin deposition in small vessels leads to organ ischemia, gangrene, and multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS).
2. **Severe Hemorrhage**: Exhaustion of platelets and coagulation factors (consumption phase) combined with hyperfibrinolysis leads to diffuse, life-threatening bleeding.
Always secondary to a severe systemic trigger; carries a high mortality rate ($20-50\%$).

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment (ABCDE)
* **Airway**: Assess patency. Severe DIC with pulmonary hemorrhage can cause asphyxiation; intracranial hemorrhage can cause coma and airway loss.
* **Breathing**: Monitor respiratory rate and $SpO_2$. Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage or pulmonary microvascular emboli can cause acute hypoxemic respiratory failure.
* **Circulation**:
  * **Bleeding Check**: Inspect **all venipuncture sites, surgical wounds, lines, and catheters** for persistent oozing. Check for hematuria, melena, or epistaxis.
  * **Thrombosis Check**: Inspect digits for cyanosis/ischemia, and skin for **purpura fulminans** (rapidly spreading necrotic purpuric lesions).
  * Measure blood pressure and heart rate (hypotension/tachycardia indicates shock).
* **Access**: Secure multiple large-bore IV lines; avoid central lines if possible due to high bleeding risk, or use ultrasound guidance.

## 🔍 Clinical Presentation & Diagnosis

### 1. Triggers
DIC is never a primary diagnosis; it is always triggered by another condition:
* **Sepsis / Severe Infection** (Most common trigger): Lipopolysaccharide (endotoxin) on Gram-negative bacteria activates tissue factor.
* **Obstetric Complications**: Abruptio placentae, amniotic fluid embolism, retained dead fetus (placental tissue factor enters maternal circulation).
* **Malignancy**: Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia (APL/AML-M3) (promyelocytes release tissue factor-like procoagulants), mucin-secreting adenocarcinomas (leads to chronic/Trousseau syndrome).
* **Tissue Trauma**: Severe burns, crush injuries, major brain trauma (shears release tissue factor).
* **Toxins**: Viper envenomation (venom contains factor X/prothrombin activators).

### 2. ISTH Diagnostic Scoring
Diagnostic score developed by the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis:
* **Platelet Count**:
  * $>100,000/\mu\text{L} \rightarrow$ 0 pts.
  * $50,000 - 100,000/\mu\text{L} \rightarrow$ 1 pt.
  * $<50,000/\mu\text{L} \rightarrow$ 2 pts.
* **Fibrin Degradation Markers (D-dimer / FDP)**:
  * No increase $\rightarrow$ 0 pts.
  * Moderate increase $\rightarrow$ 2 pts.
  * Strong increase $\rightarrow$ 3 pts.
* **Prothrombin Time (PT) Prolongation**:
  * $<3\text{ seconds} \rightarrow$ 0 pts.
  * $3 - 6\text{ seconds} \rightarrow$ 1 pt.
  * $>6\text{ seconds} \rightarrow$ 2 pts.
* **Fibrinogen Level**:
  * $>100\text{ mg/dL} \rightarrow$ 0 pts.
  * **$<100\text{ mg/dL} \rightarrow$ 1 pt.**
* *Interpretation*: **Score $\ge 5$ is diagnostic for overt DIC.**

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Key Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DIC** | Low platelets, prolonged PT/PTT, **low fibrinogen**, elevated D-dimer, schistocytes present. | Consumed Factor VIII (low). |
| **Severe Liver Failure** | Low platelets, prolonged PT/PTT, normal/high Factor VIII. | LFTs, normal/elevated Factor VIII (synthesized by endothelium). |
| **TTP / HUS** | Severe thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia. **Coagulation times (PT, PTT, Fibrinogen) are completely normal.** | Normal PT/PTT, ADAMTS13 activity $<10\%$ (TTP). |
| **HIT** | Thrombosis after heparin exposure. Normal PT/PTT, normal fibrinogen. | HIT antibody (PF4-ELISA) positive. |

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Coagulation Panel**: Prolonged PT and aPTT.
* **Platelet Count**: Thrombocytopenia (rapidly declining).
* **Serum Fibrinogen**: **Low** (hallmark of severe consumption).
* **D-Dimer / FDP**: Markedly elevated (reflects active fibrin formation and degradation).
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Schistocytes (fragmented RBCs)**, indicating microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) due to RBCs shearing against microvascular fibrin clots.

## 🏥 Management Principles

### 1. Treat the Underlying Cause (Primary & Essential)
* The coagulopathy will not resolve until the trigger is controlled:
  * *Sepsis*: Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics + source control (drain abscesses, debride tissue).
  * *Obstetrics*: Immediate delivery of fetus/placenta or evacuation of uterus.
  * *APL*: Immediate initiation of **ATRA** (All-Trans Retinoic Acid).

### 2. Targeted Blood Product Replacement
*Only indicated if patient is actively bleeding, or requires invasive procedures, or has extremely severe parameters:*
* **Packed RBCs**: Transfuse to maintain Hemoglobin **$>7-8\text{ g/dL}$**.
* **Platelet Transfusion**: Keep platelets **$>50,000/\mu\text{L}$** (if actively bleeding) or $>20,000/\mu\text{L}$ (if high risk).
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)**: Give **$15\text{ mL/kg}$** if PT/aPTT $>1.5\times$ control.
* **Cryoprecipitate**: Give **10 units** if Fibrinogen is **$<150\text{ mg/dL}$** (efficiently replenishes fibrinogen, Factor VIII, and vWF in a small volume).

### 3. Heparin Therapy (Controversial)
* Indicated **only** when thrombosis dominates (e.g., digital ischemia, venous thromboembolism, or purpura fulminans) and there is **no active bleeding**. Use low-dose continuous infusion unfractionated heparin (UFH).

* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Contraindication of Antifibrinolytics (TXA)**: The use of Tranexamic Acid (TXA) is **strictly contraindicated** in DIC (except in rare cases of documented primary hyperfibrinolysis like APL). Inhibiting fibrinolysis stops the body from clearing microvascular clots, leading to catastrophic organ infarction, tissue necrosis, and death.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Factor VIII Differentiator**: To distinguish DIC from severe liver failure (which both cause thrombocytopenia and prolonged PT/PTT): check **Factor VIII levels**. Factor VIII is synthesized in endothelial cells (not hepatocytes) and is normal or elevated in liver failure, but **markedly decreased in DIC** due to consumption.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Line Oozing Sign**: In a critically ill patient, spontaneous, persistent oozing from a previous venipuncture site or around a central line catheter is a highly sensitive sign of early DIC.
