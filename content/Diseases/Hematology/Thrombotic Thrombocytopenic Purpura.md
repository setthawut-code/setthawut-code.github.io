# 🩸 Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura / TTP (โรคทีทีพี)

> **Definition**: A life-threatening microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) characterized by microvascular platelet-rich thrombosis and severe thrombocytopenia, caused by a severe deficiency of the **ADAMTS13** metalloprotease. It constitutes a medical emergency.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
1. **ADAMTS13 Role**: Under normal conditions, the ADAMTS13 enzyme cleaves large, highly adhesive ultra-large Von Willebrand Factor (vWF) multimers into smaller, less active fragments.
2. **Enzyme Deficiency**: In TTP, there is a severe deficiency of ADAMTS13 activity (**$<10\%$**). This is typically acquired due to autoantibodies (IgG) neutralizing the enzyme, or rarely inherited (Upshaw-Schulman syndrome).
3. **Platelet-vWF Aggregation**: Uncleaved ultra-large vWF multimers remain anchored to the endothelium, binding platelets via their GP Ib receptors. This triggers spontaneous platelet aggregation and microvascular platelet-rich thrombosis.
4. **Schistocyte Formation**: Red blood cells passing through the partially occluded microvessels are physically sheared by the high-shear force against the thrombi, producing fragmented RBCs (**schistocytes**).
5. **Normal Coagulation Panel**: The coagulation cascade is not activated. Therefore, clotting factors are not consumed, and **PT, aPTT, and fibrinogen levels remain completely normal** (key difference from DIC).

                Accumulation of Ultra-Large vWF Multimers

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
Classic clinical presentation is defined by the **FAT RN Pentad**:
* **F — Fever**: Present in only a minority of patients, usually low-grade.
* **A — Anemia**: Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (pallor, severe fatigue, mild jaundice).
* **T — Thrombocytopenia**: Severe, often presenting with petechiae, purpura, and mucosal epistaxis.
* **R — Renal Insufficiency**: Usually mild-to-moderate (proteinuria, hematuria, mild creatinine elevation).
* **N — Neurological Symptoms**: Characteristically **fluctuating** and transient: headache, confusion, aphasia, visual disturbances, seizures, hemiparesis, or coma.

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Classic Dyad Rule**: The complete pentad is present in $<40\%$ of patients. The presence of **isolated thrombocytopenia and MAHA (schistocytes on blood smear)**, with normal PT/PTT and no other obvious cause, is sufficient to diagnose TTP and mandate immediate treatment.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **CBC**: Severe thrombocytopenia (often $<20,000/\mu\text{L}$), anemia.
* **Peripheral Smear**: **Schistocytes** ($>1\%$ of RBCs; helmet cells, triangular cells) and polychromasia.
* **Hemolysis Panel**: Elevated LDH (classically extremely high, reflecting tissue ischemia + hemolysis), low/absent haptoglobin, elevated indirect bilirubin.
* **Coagulation Profile**: **PT/INR, aPTT, and Fibrinogen are NORMAL**.
* **Direct Coombs Test (DAT)**: **Negative** (excludes immune hemolytic anemia).
* **ADAMTS13 Activity Assay**: **$<10\%$ activity is diagnostic.** (Order immediately, but **do not wait** for the results to start treatment).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Therapeutic Plasma Exchange (TPE / Plasmapheresis)
* **First-Line & Emergent**: Must be initiated immediately upon clinical suspicion. Delaying treatment carries a $>90\%$ mortality rate. With TPE, survival is $>85\%$.
* *Mechanism*: Removes the anti-ADAMTS13 autoantibodies and ultra-large vWF multimers, while replacing functional ADAMTS13 enzyme via donor fresh frozen plasma.
* *Duration*: Perform daily TPE until platelet count is $>150,000/\mu\text{L}$ and LDH normalizes for at least 2 consecutive days.

### 2. Immunosuppressive Therapy
* **Systemic Corticosteroids**: Methylprednisolone 1 g/day IV for 3 days or Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day PO. Inhibits autoantibody production.
* **Rituximab**: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Administered early to prevent relapse and clear autoantibody-producing B-cell clones.

### 3. Adjunctive Therapy
* **Caplacizumab**: An anti-vWF nanobody that blocks vWF-platelet GP Ib interaction, preventing further microvascular thrombosis.

### 4. Transfusion Restriction
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Avoid Platelet Transfusions**: Platelet transfusions are **strictly contraindicated** in TTP (unless life-threatening hemorrhage is present). Transfusing platelets adds "fuel to the fire" by promoting further microvascular thrombosis, worsening organ ischemia, and precipitating sudden stroke, myocardial infarction, or death.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **TTP vs. DIC Differentiation**:
  > * TTP $\rightarrow$ **Normal PT/PTT**, Normal Fibrinogen. Thrombosis is platelet-rich.
  > * DIC $\rightarrow$ **Prolonged PT/PTT**, **Low Fibrinogen**. Thrombosis is fibrin-rich (clotting factors are consumed).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **LDH Marker**: The serum LDH in TTP is a marker of both red blood cell lysis and widespread tissue ischemia from microvascular clotting. A rising LDH indicates active disease; a falling LDH is the earliest indicator of treatment response.
* > [!TIP]
  > **PLASMIC Score**: A clinical prediction tool for ADAMTS13 deficiency. High scores (6-7) based on platelet count $<30k$, hemolysis markers, normal PT/INR, and absence of active cancer/transplant are $99\%$ predictive of TTP.
