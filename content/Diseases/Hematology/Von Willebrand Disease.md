# 🩸 Von Willebrand Disease / vWD (โรคฟอนวิลเลอบรานด์)

> **Definition**: The most common inherited bleeding disorder, caused by a quantitative deficiency or qualitative defect of Von Willebrand Factor (vWF). It impairs both primary hemostasis (platelet adhesion) and secondary hemostasis (by reducing Factor VIII stabilization).

---

## 📊 Classification & Subtypes
* **Type 1 (Most common, $70-80\%$ )**: Autosomal dominant. **Mild-to-moderate quantitative deficiency** of structurally normal vWF. Mild bleeding symptoms.
* **Type 2 ($15-20\%$ )**: Autosomal dominant. **Qualitative defect** producing dysfunctional vWF.
  * *Type 2B Warning*: Mutation causes increased affinity for platelet GP Ib receptors, leading to platelet coat aggregation and clearance $\rightarrow$ **presents with mild thrombocytopenia**.
* **Type 3 (Rare)**: Autosomal recessive. **Complete absence** of vWF. Severe mucosal and deep tissue bleeding (resembles hemophilia due to extremely low Factor VIII).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
vWF is a large multimeric glycoprotein synthesized and stored in **Weibel-Palade bodies** of endothelial cells and $\alpha$-granules of megakaryocytes. It has two dual functions:
1. **Platelet Adhesion (Primary Hemostasis)**: Acts as a physical bridge binding subendothelial collagen to the platelet **GP Ib-IX-V receptor** under high shear stress (arterioles).
2. **Factor VIII Carrier (Secondary Hemostasis)**: Binds to and stabilizes circulating **Factor VIII**, protecting it from rapid proteolytic degradation by Activated Protein C. Without vWF, Factor VIII half-life drops from 12 hours to $<2$ hours.

```
                         VASCULAR ENDOTHELIAL INJURY
                                      │
                                      ▼
                        Exposed Subendothelial Collagen
                                      │
           vWF Binds Collagen & Platelet GP Ib-IX-V Receptor
                            (Platelet Adhesion)
                                      │
                                      ▼
              Coagulation Cascade: vWF Carries & Stabilizes FVIII
                                      │
                                      ▼
                             Stable Fibrin Clot
```

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Mucocutaneous Bleeding** (Platelet-type bleeding pattern):
  * Easy bruising, recurrent epistaxis, bleeding gums.
  * Prolonged oozing from minor cuts.
  * **Menorrhagia** (heavy, prolonged menstrual bleeding; often the primary presenting symptom in adolescent females).
  * Prolonged bleeding after dental extractions or tonsillectomy.
* **Hemarthrosis / Hematomas**: Rare (occurs only in severe Type 3 vWD).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. Coagulation Profile & Platelets
* **Bleeding Time (BT)**: **Prolonged** (reflects adhesion defect; PFA-100 is now preferred).
* **aPTT**: **Normal or Prolonged** (prolonged if Factor VIII levels are significantly low).
* **PT / INR**: **Normal**.
* **Platelet Count**: **Normal** (Except in Type 2B, which shows mild thrombocytopenia).

### 2. Specific vWF Assays (Diagnostic)
* **vWF Antigen (vWF:Ag)**: Measures total amount of vWF protein (low in Type 1 and 3).
* **Ristocetin Cofactor Activity (vWF:RCo)**: Measures vWF functional activity by assessing its ability to agglutinate platelets in the presence of ristocetin (low in all types).
* **Factor VIII Activity**: Often decreased ($30-50\%$ of normal).
* **vWF Multimer Analysis**: Evaluates structural size distribution (distinguishes Type 1 from Type 2).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. General Principles
* **Strictly avoid Aspirin and NSAIDs** (they inhibit platelet function, exacerbating the primary hemostasis defect). Use Acetaminophen for pain control.

### 2. Desmopressin (DDAVP)
* **First-line therapy for Type 1 vWD.** Administered IV or intranasally.
* *Mechanism*: Vasopressin analog that stimulates the release of stored vWF and Factor VIII from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies into plasma.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **DDAVP Hyponatremia Risk**: DDAVP is an ADH agonist. To prevent severe dilutional hyponatremia and seizures, **restrict fluid intake to $<1\text{ L}$ for 24 hours** after administration. Do not repeat doses more than once every 24-48 hours to avoid tachyphylaxis (depletion of endothelial stores).
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Type 2B DDAVP Contraindication**: DDAVP is **contraindicated in Type 2B vWD**. It causes release of abnormal vWF with hyper-affinity for platelets, triggering intravascular platelet clumping, worsening thrombocytopenia, and risking thrombosis.

### 3. Factor Replacement Therapy
* **vWF-containing Factor VIII concentrates** (e.g., Humate-P, Alphanate) or recombinant vWF (vonvendi).
* *Indication*: Major surgery, life-threatening bleeding, or patients with Type 2 or Type 3 vWD who are unresponsive to DDAVP.

### 4. Adjunctive Therapy
* **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: Useful oral rinse or systemic medication for dental extractions and epistaxis.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Adolescent Menorrhagia Screen**: Any young female presenting with severe menorrhagia starting at menarche, with a normal pelvic ultrasound, should be screened for **vWD** by ordering a vWF panel.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **FVIII Mimicry**: Because vWF stabilizes Factor VIII, a severe Type 3 vWD patient can present with a very prolonged aPTT and factor VIII $<1\%$, mimicking Hemophilia. Differentiate by checking Bleeding Time / PFA-100 (abnormal in vWD, normal in Hemophilia) and vWF antigen (absent in vWD).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Type O Blood Variant**: Patients with Type O blood naturally have $20-30\%$ lower circulating vWF levels than non-O individuals. Use type-adjusted reference ranges to avoid overdiagnosing Type 1 vWD.
