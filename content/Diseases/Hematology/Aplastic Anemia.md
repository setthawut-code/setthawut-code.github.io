# 🩸 Aplastic Anemia (โรคโลหิตจางจากไขกระดูกฝ่อ)

> **Definition**: A life-threatening syndrome of bone marrow failure characterized by peripheral blood **pancytopenia** and a markedly hypocellular bone marrow, where normal hematopoietic tissue is replaced by fat.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Triggers
* **Acquired (Most common, $>80\%$ )**: Immune-mediated destruction of hematopoietic stem cells (CD34+) by autoreactive CD8+ T cytotoxic lymphocytes secreting IFN-gamma and TNF-alpha.
  * **Idiopathic**: No clear trigger found.
  * **Drugs & Toxins**: **Chloramphenicol**, benzene, insecticides, carbamazepine, propylthiouracil (PTU), gold.
  * **Viral Infections**: **Non-A-E-G hepatitis** (hepatitis-associated aplastic anemia), **EBV**, HIV, **Parvovirus B19** (typically causes transient red-cell aplasia).
  * **Radiation**.
* **Inherited**: **Fanconi Anemia** (autosomal recessive DNA repair defect; associated with short stature, absent thumbs/radial anomalies, cafe-au-lait spots).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
Signs and symptoms are direct consequences of pancytopenia:
* **Anemia**: Fatigue, progressive pallor, exertional dyspnea, palpitation.
* **Thrombocytopenia**: Easy bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gingival bleeding, menorrhagia).
* **Leukopenia/Neutropenia**: Recurrent, severe bacterial or fungal infections, fever.
* **Negative Physical Finding**: **Absence of Splenomegaly or Lymphadenopathy**. 
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **The Organomegaly Rule**: The presence of splenomegaly or lymphadenopathy argues strongly *against* aplastic anemia and suggests alternative diagnoses such as leukemia, lymphoma, myelofibrosis, or portal hypertension.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **Pancytopenia**: Simultaneous anemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia.
* **Reticulocyte Count**: Markedly decreased (Absolute Reticulocyte Count **$<20,000/\mu\text{L}$** or Reticulocyte Index $<1\%$; reflects failure of marrow production).
* **Peripheral Smear**: Normocytic-normochromic (or mildly macrocytic) RBCs. No abnormal/dysplastic cells, no blasts.

### 2. Bone Marrow Aspirate & Biopsy (Gold Standard)
* **Hypocellularity**: Markedly decreased cellularity (typically **$<25\%$** of normal for age).
* **Fatty Replacement**: Marrow space is replaced by fat cells (adipocytes) and fibrous stroma.
* **No Infiltration**: Absence of abnormal cells (blasts, lymphoma cells, granulomas, or fibrosis).

```
                      APLASTIC ANEMIA CAMITTA CRITERIA
              (For Diagnosis of Severe Aplastic Anemia - SAA)
 
  Requires BM cellularity < 25% PLUS at least two of the following:
  1. Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) < 500 / microL
  2. Platelet Count < 20,000 / microL
  3. Absolute Reticulocyte Count < 20,000 / microL (or corrected < 1%)
 
  * Very Severe Aplastic Anemia (vSAA): Same as SAA, but ANC < 200 / microL.
```

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Supportive Care (Critical)
* **Infection Prevention**: Neutropenic precautions, prophylactic antibiotics/antifungals, immediate evaluation and empiric IV antibiotics (e.g., Cefepime/Piperacillin-Tazobactam) for neutropenic fever.
* **Transfusions**: Transfuse leukoreduced PRBCs and platelets to maintain stability.
  * *Constraint*: Avoid excess transfusions in candidates for stem cell transplant to minimize alloimmunization and graft rejection.

### 2. Definitive Therapy

#### A. Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT)
* **Preferred First-Line**: In **younger patients ($<40-50$ years)** who are fit and have an **HLA-matched sibling donor**.

#### B. Immunosuppressive Therapy (IST)
* **Preferred First-Line**: In **older patients ($>40-50$ years)** or those without an HLA-matched sibling donor.
* **Regimen**: Triple therapy:
  1. **Antithymocyte Globulin (ATG)**: Equine ATG (derived from horses) is superior to rabbit ATG. Infused IV over several days.
     * > [!WARNING]
       > **ATG Serum Sickness**: ATG can precipitate **Serum Sickness** (Type III hypersensitivity) 7-14 days after infusion, presenting as fever, pruritic urticarial rash, and arthralgias. Prevent or treat with co-administered systemic corticosteroids (methylprednisolone).
  2. **Cyclosporine A (CSA)**: Oral immunosuppressant. Continued for at least 6 months, then tapered slowly. Requires monitoring of blood pressure, renal function (Creatinine), and trough drug levels.
  3. **Eltrombopag**: Oral thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor agonist. Added to ATG/CSA to stimulate stem cell proliferation, increasing response rates to $>80\%$.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **No Blasts Rule**: If a bone marrow biopsy of a pancytopenic patient shows cellularity $<10\%$ but with any increase in myeloblasts ($>5\%$), the diagnosis is **Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS)** or Hypoplastic AML, not Aplastic Anemia.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Parvovirus B19 vs. Aplastic Anemia**:
  > * In patients with pre-existing hemolytic states (Thalassemia, G6PD, HS), Parvovirus B19 causes a **transient aplastic crisis** (affecting only the erythroid lineage; pure red cell aplasia; self-limiting).
  > * In normal individuals, Parvovirus B19 rarely causes full aplastic anemia unless they are immunocompromised.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Fanconi Thumb Clue**: In a child presenting with cytopenias, inspect their hands. Presence of a **hypoplastic or absent thumb or radius** suggests **Fanconi Anemia**. Confirm with a chromosome breakage test (using diepoxybutane or mitomycin C).
