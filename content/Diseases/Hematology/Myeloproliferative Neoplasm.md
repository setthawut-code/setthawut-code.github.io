# 🩸 Myeloproliferative Neoplasms / MPN (กลุ่มโรคไขกระดูกผลิตเม็ดเลือดมากผิดปกติ)

> **Definition**: A group of clonal stem cell disorders characterized by the autonomous, hyperproductive proliferation of one or more myeloid lineages (erythroid, megakaryocytic, or granulocytic) in the bone marrow, presenting with elevated peripheral blood counts and a high risk of thrombosis or transformation to acute myeloid leukemia.

---

## 🦠 Subtype Pathophysiology & Features

### 1. Polycythemia Vera (PV)
* **Pathophysiology**: JAK2 V617F mutation causes constitutive activation of the JAK2 tyrosine kinase, rendering erythroid progenitors hypersensitive and **completely independent of Erythropoietin (EPO)** for growth.
* **Key Clinical Signs**:
  * **Aquagenic Pruritus**: Intense, severe itching triggered by contact with warm water (e.g., bath or shower). Caused by histamine release from hyperproliferative basophils/mast cells.
  * **Hyperviscosity Syndrome**: Sluggish blood flow causing headache, dizziness, tinnitus, and visual disturbances.
  * **Thrombosis**: High risk of arterial (stroke, MI) or venous (portal vein, Budd-Chiari syndrome) thrombosis.
  * **Erythromelalgia**: Burning pain, redness, and warmth in the hands and feet caused by microvascular platelet occlusion (responds rapidly to aspirin).

### 2. Essential Thrombocythemia (ET)
* **Pathophysiology**: Clonal proliferation of megakaryocytes leading to isolated thrombocytosis.
* **Key Clinical Signs**:
  * Vasomotor symptoms (headaches, visual changes, erythromelalgia).
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **Paradoxical Bleeding**: At extremely high platelet counts (**$>1.5 \text{ million}/\mu\text{L}$**), patients actually have a high risk of **bleeding** rather than thrombosis. This occurs because the massive platelet mass adsorbs and degrades large vWF multimers, causing an **Acquired von Willebrand Syndrome**. Avoid aspirin in these patients until platelet count is reduced.

### 3. Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF)
* **Pathophysiology**: Clonal megakaryocytes release high levels of fibrogenic cytokines (e.g., **TGF-$\beta$**, PDGF) into the marrow. This stimulates non-clonal fibroblasts to deposit collagen, leading to progressive osteosclerosis and marrow replacement.
* **Key Clinical Signs**:
  * **Extramedullary Hematopoiesis**: Because the marrow is fibrotic, blood production shifts to the spleen and liver, causing **massive hepatosplenomegaly** and portal hypertension.
  * **Leukoerythroblastosis**: Smear shows nucleated red cells and immature granulocytes (myelocytes/metamyelocytes) that are prematurely pushed out of the fibrotic bone marrow.
  * **Teardrop RBCs (Dacrocytes)**: Formed as RBCs are physically squeezed and damaged trying to exit the fibrotic marrow.
  * **"Dry Tap"**: Bone marrow aspiration fails to yield fluid due to dense fibrosis (requires bone marrow trephine biopsy for diagnosis).

## 🔬 Diagnostic Criteria Summary

### 1. Polycythemia Vera (Requires all 3 Major, or 2 Major + 1 Minor)
* **Major**:
  1. Hb $>16.5\text{ g/dL}$ (men) / $>16.0\text{ g/dL}$ (women), or Hct $>49\%$ (men) / $>48\%$ (women).
  2. Bone marrow biopsy showing hypercellularity with panmyelosis (erythroid, granulocytic, and megakaryocytic proliferation).
  3. Presence of JAK2 V617F or JAK2 Exon 12 mutation.
* **Minor**:
  * **Subnormal (low) serum EPO level**.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Polycythemia Vera
* **Phlebotomy**: The cornerstone of therapy. Draw blood regularly to maintain Hematocrit **$<45\%$** (reduces thrombosis risk by 4-fold).
* **Low-dose Aspirin**: 75-100 mg PO daily (prevents thrombotic events and relieves erythromelalgia).
* **Cytoreduction**: **Hydroxyurea** is first-line for high-risk patients (age $>60$ years or history of thrombosis). Alternately, Interferon-alpha.

### 2. Essential Thrombocythemia
* **Low-risk** (age $<60$, no history of thrombosis, platelets $<1.5\text{ million}$): Observation + low-dose aspirin.
* **High-risk** (age $>60$, prior thrombosis, or platelets $>1.5\text{ million}$): **Hydroxyurea** (target platelets $<450,000/\mu\text{L}$) + low-dose aspirin.

### 3. Primary Myelofibrosis
* **Supportive**: PRBC transfusions, EPO stimulating agents for anemia.
* **Targeted therapy**: **Ruxolitinib** (JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor) effectively reduces splenomegaly and constitutional symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss) by blocking cytokine signaling.
* **Definitive**: Allogeneic HSCT (only curative option, reserved for high-risk fit patients).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Aquagenic Pruritus + Low EPO**: A patient with an elevated hematocrit who complains of intense body itching after taking a hot shower, and has a suppressed serum erythropoietin level, has **Polycythemia Vera** until proven otherwise.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Secondary Polycythemia**: Do not confuse PV with secondary polycythemia (chronic hypoxia from COPD, OSA, high altitude, or EPO-secreting tumors like renal cell carcinoma). Secondary polycythemia will show **elevated/high EPO**, whereas PV will show **low/suppressed EPO**.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Teardrop Cell Signature**: Finding dacrocytes (teardrop cells) and nucleated RBCs on a peripheral smear, combined with a splenomegaly that reaches the pelvis, is the classic clinical signature of **Primary Myelofibrosis**.
