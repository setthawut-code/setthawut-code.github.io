# 🩸 Lymphoma (โรคต่อมน้ำเหลืองโต / มะเร็งต่อมน้ำเหลือง)

> **Definition**: Malignant clonal neoplasms of lymphocytes originating in lymph nodes or other lymphoid tissues. Broadly classified into Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL).

---

## 📊 Hodgkin vs. Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

| Feature | Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) | Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathology Hallmark** | **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells** (CD15+, CD30+; CD20-) | Clonal proliferation of B cells ($85\%$) or T cells ($15\%$) |
| **Pattern of Spread** | **Contiguous** (predictable spread to adjacent lymph node chains); rarely extranodal | **Non-contiguous** (unpredictable, hematogenous hematogenous spread); frequent **extranodal** (GI tract, BM, CNS) |
| **B Symptoms** | Highly common (correlates with prognosis) | Less common, typically in advanced or high-grade forms |
| **Epidemiology** | Bimodal age distribution (peaks 20-30 yrs and $>55$ yrs) | Increases incidence with age (majority $>50$ years) |
| **Associations** | Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) | EBV, HIV, H. pylori (MALT), autoimmune diseases |
| **Chemotherapy** | **ABVD** | **R-CHOP** (for DLBCL) |

---

## 🦠 Specific Lymphoma Subtypes

### 1. Hodgkin Lymphoma
* **Reed-Sternberg Cells**: Large, binucleated B cells with prominent nucleoli resembling **"owl's eyes"**.
* **Subtypes**:
  * **Nodular Sclerosis** (most common, $70\%$): Prevalent in young females; features collagen bands surrounding neoplastic nodules. Good prognosis.
  * **Mixed Cellularity**: Prevalent in older males; strongly associated with **EBV**.
  * **Lymphocyte-Rich**: Best prognosis.
  * **Lymphocyte-Depleted**: Worst prognosis; seen in elderly/HIV.

### 2. Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

#### A. Indolent (Low-Grade) Lymphomas
Slowly progressive, responsive to therapy but generally incurable.
* **Follicular Lymphoma**: 
  * *Genetics*: Translocation **t(14;18)**, which places the **BCL-2** gene under the heavy-chain immunoglobulin promoter, overexpressing BCL-2. This blocks apoptosis, allowing cell survival.
* **MALT Lymphoma** (Marginal Zone):
  * Highly associated with **Helicobacter pylori** infection in gastric mucosa. **Treating H. pylori with triple therapy (antibiotics + PPI) can cure early-stage gastric MALT lymphoma.**

#### B. Aggressive (High-Grade) Lymphomas
Rapidly progressive, fatal if untreated, but potentially curable with aggressive regimens.
* **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)**: The most common NHL overall. Presents with rapidly enlarging lymph nodes and B symptoms.
* **Burkitt Lymphoma**:
  * *Genetics*: Translocation **t(8;14)**, placing the **c-MYC** proto-oncogene under the immunoglobulin promoter, driving extreme cell proliferation.
  * *Biopsy Hallmark*: **"Starry Sky" appearance** (sheets of highly proliferative dark basophilic tumor cells with scattered pale, lipid-laden macrophages engulfing apoptotic debris).
  * *Association*: Strongly associated with **EBV**. Endemic form presents as a jaw mass in African children; sporadic form presents as an abdominal mass. Highly vulnerable to Tumor Lysis Syndrome.

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Painless Lymphadenopathy**: Rubber, firm, non-tender enlargement of lymph nodes (typically cervical, supraclavicular, or axillary).
* **B Symptoms**:
  * **Fever** $>38^\circ\text{C}$ (unexplained, persistent).
  * **Drenching Night Sweats** (requires changing clothes/sheets).
  * **Unexplained Weight Loss** ($>10\%$ of baseline body weight within 6 months).
* **HL-Specific Symptoms**:
  * **Alcohol-induced pain** in involved lymph nodes (rare but pathognomonic).
  * Pel-Ebstein fever: Recurrent cycles of high fever lasting 1-2 weeks, followed by afebrile intervals.
* **Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome**: Caused by compression of the SVC by a large mediastinal lymph node mass, presenting with facial swelling, dyspnea, and dilated neck/chest collateral veins.

## 🔬 Staging: Ann Arbor System
Staging is evaluated via CT/PET scans and bone marrow biopsy:
* **Stage I**: Single lymph node region or single extranodal site.
* **Stage II**: $\ge 2$ lymph node regions on the **same side** of the diaphragm.
* **Stage III**: Lymph node regions on **both sides** of the diaphragm.
* **Stage IV**: Diffuse or disseminated involvement of one or more extranodal organs (e.g., bone marrow, liver, lung).
* **Suffixes**:
  * **A**: Absent B symptoms.
  * **B**: **Present B symptoms** (e.g., Stage IIIB).

---

## 🔬 Diagnostic Workup
* **Excisional Lymph Node Biopsy (Gold Standard)**:
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Excisional Biopsy Mandatory**: Always perform an excisional lymph node biopsy. Fine needle aspiration (FNA) or core needle biopsy is **inadequate** for primary diagnosis because preserving the lymph node architecture (follicular vs. diffuse) is essential to classify the lymphoma.
* **PET-CT Scan**: Essential for initial staging and post-treatment response evaluation.
* **Bone Marrow Biopsy**: Performed to rule out Stage IV marrow involvement.
* **Serum LDH**: Correlates with tumor burden and serves as a strong prognostic marker.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Hodgkin Lymphoma
* **ABVD Regimen**:
  * **A** — **Adriamycin (Doxorubicin)**. *Side effect: dose-dependent dilated cardiomyopathy.*
  * **B** — **Bleomycin**. *Side effect: pulmonary fibrosis (monitor DLCO).*
  * **V** — **Vinblastine**. *Side effect: peripheral neuropathy.*
  * **D** — **Dacarbazine**.

### 2. Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (DLBCL)
* **R-CHOP Regimen**:
  * **R** — **Rituximab** (anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody).
  * **C** — **Cyclophosphamide**. *Side effect: hemorrhagic cystitis.*
  * **H** — **Hydroxydoxorubicin (Doxorubicin)**. *Side effect: cardiotoxicity.*
  * **O** — **Oncovin (Vincristine)**. *Side effect: peripheral neuropathy/ileus.*
  * **P** — **Prednisolone**.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **MALT H. pylori Cure**: A patient with early gastric MALT lymphoma does not immediately need chemotherapy. Test for *H. pylori* (urea breath test/biopsy) and treat with triple eradication therapy (clarithromycin + amoxicillin + PPI). This leads to complete lymphoma regression in $>75\%$ of patients.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Doxorubicin Cardiotoxicity**: Before initiating ABVD or R-CHOP, **always obtain a baseline Echocardiogram** to assess Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF). Doxorubicin is cardiotoxic and contraindicated if LVEF is $<30-40\%$.
* > [!TIP]
  > **B Symptoms Prognosis**: The presence of "B" suffix in staging (e.g., Stage IIB vs. IIA) indicates a significantly worse prognosis and often dictates a more aggressive chemotherapy or radiation schedule.
