# 🩸 Leukemia (โรคมะเร็งเม็ดเลือดขาว)

> **Definition**: A group of malignant clonal disorders of hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow characterized by autonomous, uncontrolled proliferation of white blood cells. This leads to replacement of normal marrow elements, resulting in cytopenias (anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia) and organ infiltration.

---

## 📊 Classification of Leukemias

Leukemias are classified by clinical course (Acute vs. Chronic) and lineage (Myeloid vs. Lymphoid):

```
                                  LEUKEMIA
                                     │
            ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
            ▼                                                 ▼
      ACUTE LEUKEMIAS                                 CHRONIC LEUKEMIAS
 (Blasts >= 20% in blood/BM)                       (Mature-appearing cells)
  • Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)                    • Chronic Myelogenous (CML)
  • Acute Lymphoblastic (ALL)                       • Chronic Lymphocytic (CLL)
```

---

## 🦠 Acute Leukemias (Rapid onset, bone marrow failure)

### 1. Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)
* **Epidemiology**: Primarily older adults (median age $\sim 65-70$ years).
* **Smear / Bone Marrow**: **Myeloblasts** containing **Auer rods** (pink/red needle-like cytoplasmic inclusions of fused lysosomal granules containing myeloperoxidase).
* **Cytochemistry**: **Myeloperoxidase (MPO)** and Sudan Black B positive.
* **Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia (APL / AML-M3)**:
  * *Genetics*: Translocation **t(15;17)**, fusing the PML and RARA (Retinoic Acid Receptor Alpha) genes.
  * > [!CAUTION]
    > **APL DIC Emergency**: APL is a **hematologic emergency**. Promyelocyte granules contain tissue factor-like procoagulants; their release triggers **catastrophic, life-threatening DIC and hyperfibrinolysis**. 
    > **Immediately start All-Trans Retinoic Acid (ATRA)** upon suspicion. Do not wait for cytogenetic confirmation. ATRA forces the promyelocytes to mature, arresting the coagulopathy.
  * *Smear*: Promyelocytes packed with multiple Auer rods ("faggot cells").
* **Treatment**: "7+3" induction chemotherapy (Cytarabine for 7 days + Daunorubicin for 3 days) followed by consolidation (high-dose cytarabine or HSCT).

### 2. Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL)
* **Epidemiology**: Most common pediatric malignancy (peak incidence ages 2-5).
* **Smear / Bone Marrow**: **Lymphoblasts** with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, clumped chromatin. **No Auer rods**.
* **Markers**: **TdT (Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl Transferase)** positive (diagnostic marker for immature lymphocytes). B-cell markers (CD19, CD22, CD10) or T-cell markers (CD3, CD7).
* **Clinical**: Bone pain, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy. Propensity to invade the central nervous system and testes.
* **Treatment**: Multi-agent chemotherapy (Vincristine, L-asparaginase, Anthracycline, Prednisone). **Requires CNS prophylaxis (intrathecal methotrexate)**.

## 🦠 Chronic Leukemias (Insidious onset, high cell counts)

### 1. Chronic Myelogenous Leukemia (CML)
* **Pathophysiology**: Clonal stem cell disorder driven by the **Philadelphia Chromosome** translocation **t(9;22)**. This fuses the *BCR* and *ABL1* genes, encoding a chimeric **BCR-ABL1 tyrosine kinase** that drives autonomous myeloid proliferation.
* **Smear**: Massive leukocytosis with the **full spectrum of granulocytes** (myeloblasts, promyelocytes, myelocytes [peaks], metamyelocytes, bands, neutrophils) with prominent **basophilia** and eosinophilia.
* **Leukemoid Reaction Differentiation**:
  * **CML**: **Low/zero Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) score**, presence of t(9;22), splenomegaly, basophilia.
  * **Leukemoid Reaction** (reactive leukocytosis to severe infection): **High LAP score**, no translocation, toxic granulation, dohle bodies.
* **Phases**: Chronic phase ($<10\%$ blasts) $\rightarrow$ Accelerated phase ($10-19\%$ blasts) $\rightarrow$ **Blast Crisis ($\ge 20\%$ blasts; transforms to acute leukemia [AML or ALL])**.
* **Treatment**: Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs: **Imatinib**, Dasatinib, Nilotinib).

### 2. Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)
* **Epidemiology**: Most common leukemia in Western older adults (median age $>70$ years).
* **Pathophysiology**: Monoclonal accumulation of mature-appearing, dysfunctional CD5+ B-lymphocytes.
* **Smear**: Absolute lymphocytosis ($>5,000/\mu\text{L}$) with characteristic **smudge cells** (fragile neoplastic cells ruptured during slide preparation).
* **Complications**:
  * Autoimmune cytopenias ([[Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia]], [[Immune Thrombocytopenia]]).
  * **Richter Transformation**: Progression of CLL to an aggressive High-Grade **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)**. Heralded by rapid lymph node growth, fever, weight loss, and skyrocketing LDH.
* **Treatment**: Indicated only if symptomatic (cytopenias, painful adenopathy). CD20 inhibitors (Rituximab/Obinutuzumab) + BTK inhibitors (Ibrutinib/Acalabrutinib).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Auer Rod Rule**: Finding even a single Auer rod in a blast cell on a blood smear is diagnostic of **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)** and rules out ALL.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)**: Initiating chemotherapy in high-burden acute leukemia or high-grade lymphoma can trigger massive cell lysis, releasing potassium, phosphorus, and uric acid. This causes hyperkalemia (arrhythmias), hyperphosphatemia (calcium phosphate precipitation), and hyperuricemia (acute kidney injury). Prevent with **aggressive hydration** + **Rasburicase** or Allopurinol. (Check G6PD before Rasburicase).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Smudge Cells**: A CBC of an 80-year-old showing an asymptomatic WBC of $80,000/\mu\text{L}$ with $90\%$ lymphocytes, and the smear report noting "many smudge cells," is diagnostic of **CLL**. Observation is the standard care.
