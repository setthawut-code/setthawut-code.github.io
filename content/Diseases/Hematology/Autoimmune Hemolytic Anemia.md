# 🩸 Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia / AIHA (โรคโลหิตจางจากภูมิคุ้มกันทำลายเม็ดเลือดแดง)

> **Definition**: An autoimmune disorder where pathogenic autoantibodies bind to red blood cell surface antigens, triggering premature RBC destruction via the complement system or splenic macrophage phagocytosis. Divided into Warm and Cold subtypes based on antibody thermal characteristics.

---

## 📊 Classification & Subtypes

| Feature | Warm AIHA ($~80\%$ of cases) | Cold Agglutinin Disease (Cold AIHA) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Antibody Class** | **IgG** (Warm reactive; active at $37^\circ\text{C}$) | **IgM** (Cold reactive; active at $<30^\circ\text{C}$) |
| **Hemolysis Site** | **Extravascular** (Splenic macrophages engulf IgG-coated membranes) | **Extravascular & Intravascular** (Liver Kupffer cells clear C3b-coated RBCs; MAC causes intravascular lysis) |
| **Blood Smear** | **Spherocytes** | **RBC Agglutination (Clumping)** |
| **DAT (Coombs) Profile** | Positive for **IgG** or IgG + C3 | Positive for **C3d** only (IgM dissociates at $37^\circ\text{C}$ wash) |
| **Etiology** | • Idiopathic ($50\%$ )<br>• Secondary to **SLE**, CLL, lymphomas<br>• Drugs: Methyldopa, penicillins, cephalosporins | • Idiopathic<br>• Secondary to **Mycoplasma pneumoniae** (anti-I)<br>• **Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV)** (anti-i) |
| **Clinical Signs** | Pallor, jaundice, splenomegaly | **Acrocyanosis** (numbness/cyanosis of fingers, toes, nose, ears on cold exposure) |
| **First-Line Therapy** | **Systemic Corticosteroids** | **Avoid Cold Exposure** (Rituximab if severe) |
| **Splenectomy Role** | Effective for refractory cases | **Ineffective** (destruction is primarily hepatic) |

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology

### 1. Warm AIHA (IgG)
1. **Opsonization**: IgG antibodies bind to Rh antigens on RBC membranes at body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$).
2. **Splenic Partial Phagocytosis**: In the spleen, macrophages expressing Fc receptors bind the IgG-coated RBCs. Instead of complete phagocytosis, they bite off portions of the lipid bilayer membrane.
3. **Spherocytosis**: The RBC seals its membrane, but with a reduced surface-area-to-volume ratio, turning the biconcave disc into a rigid **spherocyte**, which is subsequently trapped and destroyed in splenic sinusoids.

### 2. Cold AIHA (IgM)
1. **Cold Binding**: In cool extremities (temperature $<30^\circ\text{C}$), IgM binds to RBC surface antigens (I/i) and activates the classical complement pathway.
2. **Agglutination**: Pentameric IgM cross-links RBCs, causing microvascular clumping (causing acrocyanosis).
3. **Complement Clearance**: As RBCs return to core body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$), IgM dissociates, leaving **C3b** deposited on the cell. Kupffer cells in the liver recognize C3b and phagocytose the cells (extravascular), or the complement cascade completes to MAC, causing intravascular lysis.

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. Hemolysis Workup
* **CBC**: Normocytic or macrocytic (due to reticulocytosis or cell clumping).
* **Reticulocyte Count**: Elevated.
* **Hemolysis Markers**: Low haptoglobin, high LDH, high indirect bilirubin.

### 2. Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT / Direct Coombs)
**Gold Standard for diagnosis.** Demonstrates antibodies or complement components bound to the patient's RBC surface.
* **DAT IgG Positive**: Indicates Warm AIHA.
* **DAT C3 Positive**: Indicates Cold AIHA (or drug-induced).

### 3. Peripheral Blood Smear
* **Warm AIHA**: Prominent **spherocytes** (microcytic-looking hyperchromic round cells lacking central pallor) and polychromasia (reticulocytes).
* **Cold AIHA**: **RBC agglutination** (clumping of RBCs into clusters). *Note: Warming the slide to $37^\circ\text{C}$ reverses the clumping.*

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Warm AIHA
* **First-line**: **Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day PO**. Rapidly reduces autoantibody production and downregulates splenic macrophage Fc receptors.
* **Second-line** (if refractory or steroid-dependent): **Rituximab** (anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody).
* **Third-line**: **Splenectomy** (removes the primary site of extravascular hemolysis).
* **Folic Acid**: 1-5 mg PO daily.

### 2. Cold AIHA
* **Cornerstone**: **Strict cold avoidance** (warm clothing, avoiding cold foods/drinks).
* **Immunosuppression**: **Rituximab** is the drug of choice if symptomatic.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Steroid and Splenectomy Failure**: Corticosteroids and splenectomy are **ineffective** in Cold AIHA. Do not use them. The spleen is not the primary site of clearance for C3b-coated RBCs (which occurs in the liver).
* **Transfusion rule**: If transfusion is mandatory, use an **in-line blood warmer** to prevent IgM activation.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Direct vs. Indirect Coombs**:
  > * **Direct Coombs** $\rightarrow$ Detects antibodies/complement **attached to patient's RBCs** (diagnostic for AIHA).
  > * **Indirect Coombs** $\rightarrow$ Detects antibodies **circulating free in patient's serum** (used for blood cross-matching).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The Transfusion Dilemma**: In severe AIHA, cross-matching will show pan-reactivity (all donor blood will appear "incompatible"). If the patient is unstable, transfuse the **"least incompatible"** packed RBCs slowly, monitor closely, and never delay transfusion in life-threatening anemia.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Spherocytes Differentiation**: Spherocytes are found in both [[Hereditary Spherocytosis]] (HS) and Warm AIHA. A positive DAT (Coombs) confirms Warm AIHA, while a negative DAT points to HS.
