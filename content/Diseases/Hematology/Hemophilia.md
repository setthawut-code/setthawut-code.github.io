# 🩸 Hemophilia (โรคฮีโมฟีเลีย / โรคเลือดออกง่ายฮีโมฟีเลีย)

> **Definition**: Inherited X-linked recessive bleeding disorders characterized by deficiency of clotting **Factor VIII** (Hemophilia A) or **Factor IX** (Hemophilia B), resulting in defective secondary hemostasis and deep tissue bleeding.

---

## 📊 Classification & Severity
* **Hemophilia A** ("Classic Hemophilia"): Factor VIII deficiency ($80-85\%$ of cases).
* **Hemophilia B** ("Christmas Disease"): Factor IX deficiency ($15-20\%$ of cases).
* **Severity Classification** (based on baseline factor activity levels):
  * **Severe ($<1\%$ activity)**: Frequent spontaneous joint or muscle bleeding.
  * **Moderate ($1-5\%$ activity)**: Bleeding triggered by minor trauma; rare spontaneous bleeding.
  * **Mild ($5-40\%$ activity)**: Bleeding occurs only after major trauma, surgery, or dental extraction.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology
* **Intrinsic Pathway Defect**: Factors VIII and IX form the intrinsic tenase complex (with calcium and phospholipid) that activates Factor X. 
* **Stable Clot Failure**: Deficiency of either factor compromises the amplification phase of coagulation. The initial platelet plug (primary hemostasis) forms normally, but secondary hemostasis fails to generate enough thrombin to form a stable fibrin mesh.
* **Inheritance**: **X-linked recessive**. Affects males almost exclusively; females are carriers (though can bleed if they have extreme lyonization or Turner syndrome).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Hemarthrosis** (Hallmark of severe disease): Spontaneous bleeding into joints (knees, elbows, ankles). Presents with swelling, warmth, pain, and restricted movement. Recurrent flares cause chronic hemophilic arthropathy (destructive cartilage loss and bony changes).
* **Deep Muscle Hematomas**: Hemorrhage into large muscle groups. **Iliopsoas hemorrhage** is classic, presenting with groin pain, hip flexion contracture, and paresthesias due to femoral nerve compression.
* **Delayed Bleeding**: Bleeding starts hours or days after minor trauma/surgery (reflects failure of stable fibrin clot consolidation, despite intact initial platelet plug).
* **Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Leading cause of bleeding-related death.
* **Negative physical findings**: **No petechiae** or superficial purpura (these signify primary hemostasis/platelet defects, not coagulation factor defects).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. Coagulation Profile
* **aPTT**: **Prolonged** (reflects defect in the intrinsic pathway).
* **PT / INR**: **Normal** (extrinsic/common pathways intact).
* **Platelet Count & Bleeding Time**: **Normal** (primary hemostasis/platelet function is intact).

### 2. aPTT Mixing Study (Correction Study)
Differentiates factor deficiency from the presence of coagulation inhibitors (antibodies). Patient plasma is mixed 1:1 with normal control plasma and aPTT is retested:
* **aPTT Corrects to normal**: Indicates a **Factor Deficiency** (the normal plasma supplied the missing Factor VIII or IX).
* **aPTT Fails to correct**: Indicates the presence of a **Factor Inhibitor** (antibodies in the patient's plasma neutralized the factors in the normal plasma).

### 3. Factor Assays (Diagnostic)
* Specific quantitative assays of Factor VIII or IX activity confirm the subtype and severity.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. General Principles
* **Avoid Aspirin and NSAIDs** (they inhibit platelet function, worsening bleeding). Use Acetaminophen or COX-2 inhibitors for joint pain.
* **Avoid Intramuscular (IM) Injections** (can cause severe muscle hematomas; give vaccines subcutaneously instead).

### 2. Factor Replacement Therapy (First-Line)
* **Hemophilia A**: **Factor VIII Concentrate** (Recombinant or plasma-derived).
  * *Dosing rule*: **1 unit/kg** of Factor VIII increases plasma level by **$2\%$**.
  * *Target levels*: 
    * Minor bleeding (hemarthrosis): Target $30-50\%$ activity.
    * Life-threatening bleeding (CNS, airway) or major surgery: Target **$80-100\%$ activity**.
* **Hemophilia B**: **Factor IX Concentrate**.
  * *Dosing rule*: **1 unit/kg** of Factor IX increases plasma level by **$1\%$** (due to larger volume of distribution).
  * *Target levels*: Similar targets, but requires higher doses.

### 3. Alternative/Adjunctive Agents
* **Desmopressin (DDAVP)**: Releases stored Factor VIII and vWF from endothelial cells (Weibel-Palade bodies). **Effective only for mild Hemophilia A.** Ineffective in severe Hemophilia A or any Hemophilia B.
* **Antifibrinolytic Therapy**: **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)** or EACA. Extremely useful adjunctive therapy for mucosal bleeding (dental extractions, epistaxis). *Contraindicated in patients with hematuria (risk of renal pelvis clot obstruction).*

### 4. Management of Factor Inhibitors (Complication)
If a patient develops IgG antibodies (inhibitors) against replaced factors:
* Use **Bypassing Agents**: **Recombinant Factor VIIa** (NovoSeven) or **Activated Prothrombin Complex Concentrate** (APCC/Feiba) to bypass factors VIII/IX in the cascade.
* **Emicizumab**: A bispecific monoclonal antibody that mimics Factor VIIIa by binding Factor IXa and Factor X together. Used for prophylaxis in Hemophilia A with inhibitors.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Mixing Study Rule**: A child with a prolonged aPTT but normal PT and platelets has a suspected factor deficiency. If the aPTT corrects on a 1:1 mixing study, proceed to test Factor VIII, IX, and XI levels. If it does not correct, suspect a Factor VIII inhibitor or Lupus Anticoagulant.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Urgent Factor First, Scan Later**: If a severe hemophiliac presents with head trauma or severe headache, **administer Factor VIII/IX replacement immediately to target 100% activity before sending the patient for a CT scan.** Never delay factor infusion to wait for imaging.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Iliopsoas Groin Pain**: If a hemophilic patient presents with groin pain and cannot extend their hip, suspect an iliopsoas hematoma. Confirm with an ultrasound or CT. Keep the patient on bed rest and replace factor levels immediately.
