# 🩸 Deep Vein Thrombosis / DVT (ภาวะหลอดเลือดดำส่วนลึกอุดตัน)

> **Definition**: The formation of a blood clot (thrombus) within a deep vein, most commonly in the lower extremities (calf, popliteal, femoral, or iliac veins) or pelvis. The primary acute complication is life-threatening **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)**.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology: Virchow's Triad
Three factors predispose to thrombus formation:
1. **Endothelial Injury**: Trauma, surgery, venipuncture, indwelling catheters, or local shear stress.
2. **Venous Stasis**: Prolonged immobility (bed rest, long-distance flights), paralysis, obesity, pregnancy, or venous obstruction (e.g., tumor).
3. **Hypercoagulability**:
   * *Inherited*: **Factor V Leiden** (activated protein C resistance; most common), Prothrombin G20210A mutation, Protein C or S deficiency, Antithrombin III deficiency.
   * *Acquired*: Malignancy (secretes procoagulants; **Trousseau syndrome**), pregnancy, oral contraceptive pills (estrogen increases clotting factors), **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)**, Nephrotic syndrome.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Classic Presentation**: Unilateral calf or thigh pain, swelling (edema), warmth, and erythema.
* **Calf Circumference Discrepancy**: A difference of **$>3\text{ cm}$** measured $10\text{ cm}$ below the tibial tuberosity is highly clinically significant.
* Prominent, dilated superficial collateral veins.
* **Homan's Sign**: Calf pain elicited by passive dorsiflexion of the foot. *Unreliable and not recommended (risk of dislodging the clot and causing PE).*
* **Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens**: Massive iliofemoral DVT causing total venous occlusion, presenting with a painful, swollen, cyanotic blue leg. Can progress to gangrene (medical emergency).

## 🔬 Diagnostic Approach & Algorithm

```
                             SUSPECTED DVT
                                   │
                                   ▼
                        Calculate Wells Score
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                   ▼
 Wells Score < 2 (Unlikely)                          Wells Score >= 2 (Likely)
         │                                                   │
         ▼                                                   ▼
   Check D-Dimer                                     Perform Compression
         │                                            Duplex Ultrasound
   ┌─────┴──────────────┐                                    │
   ▼                    ▼                              ┌─────┴──────────────┐
Negative             Positive                          ▼                    ▼
(Rule Out)           (Get Ultrasound)               Positive             Negative
                                                   (Anticoagulate)       (Repeat in 1 wk)
```

### 1. Wells Score for DVT
* Active cancer (1 pt).
* Bedridden $>3$ days or major surgery within 12 weeks (1 pt).
* Unilateral calf swelling $>3$ cm (1 pt).
* Collateral superficial non-varicose veins (1 pt).
* Entire leg swollen (1 pt).
* Localized tenderness along deep venous system (1 pt).
* Pitting edema confined to symptomatic leg (1 pt).
* Paralysis, paresis, or recent cast immobilization (1 pt).
* Alternative diagnosis as likely or more likely than DVT (-2 pts).
* *Score $\ge 2$*: DVT likely. *Score $<2$*: DVT unlikely.

### 2. Diagnostics
* **D-Dimer**: High sensitivity, low specificity. **Use only to rule out DVT in low-probability patients** (Wells $<2$). A negative D-dimer ($<500\text{ ng/mL}$) rules out DVT. Do not order in high-probability patients.
* **Compression Duplex Ultrasound (Gold Standard)**: Diagnostic finding is **non-compressibility** of the vein segment (the vein lumen fails to collapse under direct probe pressure).

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Acute Anticoagulation (Mainstay)
* **Standard Regimen (Bridge)**:
  * **Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (LMWH)** (e.g., **Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h** or 1.5 mg/kg SC daily) OR **Unfractionated Heparin (UFH)** (IV infusion targeted to aPTT $1.5-2.5\times$ control).
  * Co-start **Warfarin** on Day 1.
  * **Bridge rules**: Continue LMWH/UFH for **at least 5 days** AND until the INR is therapeutic ($2.0 - 3.0$) for **at least 24 hours** before discontinuing heparin.
* **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) (Preferred)**:
  * **Rivaroxaban** (15 mg PO bid for 21 days, then 20 mg daily) or **Apixaban** (10 mg PO bid for 7 days, then 5 mg bid). No heparin bridging required. (Avoid in severe renal impairment, $CrCl < 15\text{ mL/min}$, or Antiphospholipid Syndrome).

### 2. Duration of Therapy
* **Provoked DVT** (due to transient risk factor like surgery, trauma): **3 months** of anticoagulation.
* **Unprovoked DVT or Ongoing Risk Factor** (cancer, APS, recurrent DVT): **Indefinite (long-term)** anticoagulation.

### 3. Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Filter
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **IVC Filter Indications**: An IVC filter is **indicated only** if the patient has a documented acute proximal DVT AND:
  > 1. Absolute contraindication to anticoagulation (e.g., active severe bleeding, recent intracranial hemorrhage).
  > 2. Recurrent pulmonary embolism despite therapeutic anticoagulation.
  > Do not use routinely; filters increase the long-term risk of recurrent DVT.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The D-Dimer Trap**: Never order a D-dimer in a patient with a high clinical suspicion of DVT (Wells $\ge 2$). A positive result doesn't help (still need ultrasound), and a negative result cannot safely rule out DVT in high-probability patients. Proceed directly to ultrasound.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **LMWH vs. UFH in Cancer**: In patients with active malignancy who develop DVT, LMWH (Enoxaparin) or specific DOACs are preferred over Warfarin. Warfarin is notoriously difficult to control in cancer patients due to drug interactions, cachexia, and frequent procedures.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens**: If a patient's leg is massively swollen, painful, and turning blue/purple, it is a vascular emergency. Free thrombus has blocked arterial inflow due to extreme venous pressure. Contact vascular surgery immediately for catheter-directed thrombolysis.
