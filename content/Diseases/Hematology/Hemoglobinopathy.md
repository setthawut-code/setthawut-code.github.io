# 🩸 Hemoglobinopathy (โรคโครงสร้างฮีโมโกลบินผิดปกติ)

> **Definition**: A group of inherited disorders characterized by structural abnormalities (qualitative defects) in the globin polypeptide chains, resulting in unstable hemoglobin, altered oxygen affinity, or abnormal erythrocyte morphology. Contrasts with thalassemia (quantitative defects).

---

## 📊 Major Structural Variants

### 1. Hemoglobin E (Hb E) — $\beta^{26\text{ Glu}\rightarrow\text{Lys}}$
Most common variant in Southeast Asia and Thailand. Codon 26 mutation creates an alternate splicing site, resulting in mild quantitative deficiency ($\beta$-thalassemia-like behavior) + structural abnormality.
* **Hb E Trait (Heterozygous, AE)**: Asymptomatic. MCV normal or slightly low.
  * *HPLC*: **Hb A $\sim 70-75\%$**, **Hb E $\sim 25-30\%$**, Hb $A_2$ normal.
* **Homozygous E (EE)**: Asymptomatic. Mild microcytic hypochromic indices.
  * *HPLC*: **Hb E $>85-90\%$**, Hb F $<10\%$, **no Hb A**.
* **Hb E / $\beta$-Thalassemia**: Co-inheritance of Hb E and $\beta$-thalassemia. Highly prevalent in Thailand. Phenotypically mimics $\beta$-thalassemia major or intermedia (severe anemia, thalassemic facies, splenomegaly, transfusion dependency).
  * *HPLC*: **Hb E + Hb F only** (no Hb A in $\beta^0/\beta^{\text{E}}$).

### 2. Hemoglobin S (Hb S — Sickle Cell) — $\beta^{6\text{ Glu}\rightarrow\text{Val}}$
Prevalent in African, Middle Eastern, and Mediterranean populations. Valine substitution exposes a hydrophobic patch, causing hemoglobin polymerisation under hypoxic conditions.
* **Sickle Cell Trait (AS)**: Asymptomatic. Protected against severe *P. falciparum* malaria.
  * *HPLC*: **Hb A $>50\%$**, **Hb S $\sim 35-45\%$**.
* **Sickle Cell Anemia (SS)**: Severe hemolytic anemia and recurrent vaso-occlusive events.
  * *HPLC*: **Hb S $>85-90\%$**, Hb F variable, **no Hb A**.

### 3. Hemoglobin C (Hb C) — $\beta^{6\text{ Glu}\rightarrow\text{Lys}}$
Prevalent in West Africa. Causes mild hemolytic anemia.
* *Peripheral Smear*: Characteristic rod-shaped **Hb C crystals** inside target cells.

---

## 🦠 Sickle Cell Disease Complications

1. **Vaso-occlusive Crisis (Pain Crisis)**: Microvascular occlusion by rigid, sickled RBCs. Precipitated by hypoxia, dehydration, cold, or infection. Causes severe bone, joint, or abdominal pain.
2. **Acute Chest Syndrome (ACS)**: Vaso-occlusion in pulmonary vasculature. Presents with fever, chest pain, tachypnea, hypoxia, and new pulmonary infiltrates. *Medical Emergency; #1 cause of death in SCD.*
3. **Splenic Sequestration Crisis**: Acute, massive pooling of blood in the spleen. Causes sudden severe anemia, splenomegaly, and hypovolemic shock.
4. **Autosplenectomy**: Recurrent splenic infarctions in childhood lead to a shrunken, fibrotic, non-functional spleen by age 5. Increases risk of sepsis by **encapsulated organisms** (*S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, H. influenzae*).
5. **Aplastic Crisis**: Acute drop in Hb and **near-zero reticulocytes** due to **Parvovirus B19** infection infecting erythroid precursors in the bone marrow.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **CBC & Smear**:
  * In EE: Microcytosis ($MCV < 75\text{ fL}$), numerous target cells.
  * In SS: Normocytic or macrocytic anemia. Smear shows **sickle cells (drepanocytes)**, target cells, Howell-Jolly bodies (sign of autosplenectomy).
* **Solubility / Sickling Test**: Sodium metabisulfite induces sickling in Hb S containing blood (screening tool).
* **HPLC / Hb Electrophoresis** (Gold Standard): Confirms variant presence and percentages.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Hb E Syndromes
* **Hb E Trait / EE**: No treatment required. Reassurance.
* **Hb E / $\beta$-Thalassemia**: Manage similarly to Thalassemia (folic acid, blood transfusions, iron chelation).

### 2. Sickle Cell Disease
* **Crisis Prevention**:
  * **Hydroxyurea**: Stimulates production of **Hb F (Fetal Hemoglobin)**, which interferes with Hb S polymerisation, reducing pain crises and ACS.
  * Prophylactic Penicillin (in children $<5$) and pneumococcal/meningococcal vaccinations.
* **Acute Crisis Management**:
  * High-flow oxygen, aggressive IV hydration, and rapid pain control (opioids).
  * Empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics (Ceftriaxone) if fever is present.
  * **Simple or Exchange Transfusion**: Indicated for stroke, acute chest syndrome, or severe priapism.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Howell-Jolly Bodies**: Finding round, blue nuclear remnants (Howell-Jolly bodies) in RBCs on a peripheral smear of an adult sickle cell patient indicates **autosplenectomy**. This means the patient is functionally asplenic and highly vulnerable to lethal pneumococcal bacteremia.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Aplastic vs. Splenic Crisis**:
  > * **Aplastic Crisis** (Parvovirus B19) $\rightarrow$ Low Hb, **low/zero reticulocytes**, normal spleen.
  > * **Splenic Sequestration** $\rightarrow$ Low Hb, **high reticulocytes**, rapidly enlarging painful spleen.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Hb E-Beta Thalassemia Severity**: Hb E/$\beta$-thalassemia is clinically heterogeneous. A patient can present with mild anemia requiring no transfusions, or severe transfusion-dependency. The phenotype depends on whether the $\beta$-mutation is $\beta^0$ (severe) or $\beta^+$ (milder), and concurrent inheritance of $\alpha$-thalassemia (which reduces chain imbalance, improving anemia).
