# 🩸 Iron Deficiency Anemia / IDA (โรคโลหิตจางจากการขาดธาตุเหล็ก)

> **Definition**: Microcytic, hypochromic anemia caused by inadequate iron stores to support normal erythropoiesis and hemoglobin synthesis.

---

## 📊 Etiology & Risk Factors
* **Chronic Blood Loss** (Most common cause in adults):
  * *Premenopausal females*: Menorrhagia (heavy menstrual bleeding).
  * *Males & Postmenopausal females*: Gastrointestinal bleeding (must investigate for **colorectal cancer**, peptic ulcer disease, hookworm infection).
* **Increased Demand**: Pregnancy, lactation, infancy, and adolescence.
* **Malabsorption**: Celiac disease, prior gastrectomy or gastric bypass, *H. pylori* infection, achlorhydria (iron absorption requires acidic gastric environment).
* **Dietary Deficiency**: Poor intake, strict vegan diet.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Stages
Iron deficiency progresses through three sequential stages:
1. **Iron Depletion (Stage 1)**: Loss of storage iron. **Ferritin decreases**, but serum iron and Hb remain normal.
2. **Iron-Deficient Erythropoiesis (Stage 2)**: Depleted stores limit RBC production. Serum iron and transferrin saturation decrease; TIBC increases; Hb remains normal.
3. **Iron Deficiency Anemia (Stage 3)**: Articulate anemia develops. **Hb drops**, and cells become microcytic and hypochromic.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **General Anemia Symptoms**: Fatigue, weakness, exertional dyspnea, pallor.
* **Tissue Iron Depletion Signs**:
  * **Pica**: Craving for non-nutritive substances (ice [**pagophagia**], clay, dirt, starch).
  * **Koilonychia**: Spoon-shaped nails.
  * **Angular Cheilitis**: Fissures at the corners of the mouth.
  * Atrophic glossitis (smooth, red, painful tongue).
  * **Plummer-Vinson Syndrome** triad: Iron deficiency anemia, **esophageal webs** (causing dysphagia), and glossitis.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. CBC & Blood Smear
* **Hb/Hct**: Low.
* **MCV**: Low ($<80\text{ fL}$).
* **RDW**: **Elevated** (reflects anisocytosis; helps differentiate from Thalassemia trait which has normal RDW).
* **Peripheral Smear**: Microcytic, hypochromic RBCs, **pencil cells (elliptocytes)**, and rare target cells.

### 2. Serum Iron Panel (Diagnostic Key)
* **Serum Ferritin**: **$<30\text{ ng/mL}$** (most sensitive and specific test). 
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **The Ferritin Inflammation Trap**: Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant. In patients with concurrent inflammation, infection, chronic kidney disease, or malignancy, ferritin levels will be falsely elevated. In inflammatory states, a ferritin level **$<100\text{ ng/mL}$** can still indicate iron deficiency.
* **Serum Iron**: Low.
* **Total Iron Binding Capacity (TIBC)**: **High** (reflects increased circulating transferrin).
* **Transferrin Saturation**: **Low ($<15\%$ )** (calculated as: $\text{Serum Iron} / \text{TIBC} \times 100$).

### 3. Diagnostic Differentiation

| Lab Marker | Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA) | Anemia of Chronic Disease (ACD) | Thalassemia Trait |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MCV** | Low ($<80$) | Normal or Mildly Low | Very Low ($<70$) |
| **Ferritin** | **Low ($<30$)** | Normal or **High** | Normal or High |
| **TIBC** | **High** | Low or Normal | Normal |
| **Transferrin Sat.** | **Low ($<15\%$)** | Normal or Low | Normal or High |
| **RDW** | **High** | Normal | **Normal** |
| **Mentzer Index** ($\text{MCV}/\text{RBC}$) | $> 13$ | Variable | **$< 13$** |

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Identify and Treat Underlying Cause (Crucial)
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Elderly Colon Rule**: Any male or postmenopausal female presenting with unexplained iron deficiency anemia **must undergo upper GI endoscopy and colonoscopy** to rule out occult gastrointestinal malignancy (specifically colon cancer) regardless of negative fecal occult blood tests. Do not just prescribe iron.

### 2. Oral Iron Therapy (First-Line)
* **Regimen**: **Ferrous Sulfate 200 mg (contains 60 mg elemental iron) PO daily** or every other day.
  * *Dosing interval*: Alternate-day dosing is preferred; it reduces GI side effects and matches or exceeds daily absorption due to lower hepcidin induction.
* **Administration Rules**: Take on an empty stomach (1 hour before or 2 hours after meals) with **Vitamin C** (ascorbic acid increases absorption). Avoid taking with antacids, calcium, tea, or coffee.
* **Side Effects**: Nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhea, and **dark/black stools** (harmless, warn patient).

### 3. Intravenous (IV) Iron Therapy
* **Indications**: Intolerance to oral iron, malabsorption states (Celiac, IBD, gastric bypass), severe ongoing blood loss, or patient non-compliance.
* **Preparations**: **Iron Sucrose** (Venofer) or **Ferric Carboxymaltose** (Injectafer).

### 4. Response Monitoring
* **Reticulocyte Count**: Peaks in **7 to 10 days** after starting therapy (earliest sign of response).
* **Hemoglobin**: Rises by **$1-2\text{ g/dL}$ every 3-4 weeks**.
* **Duration**: Continue iron supplementation for **3 to 6 months** *after* hemoglobin normalizes to completely replenish tissue storage pools.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Mentzer Index Rule**: To quickly screen a patient with microcytic anemia:
  > * $\text{MCV} / \text{RBC count} > 13 \rightarrow$ Suggests **Iron Deficiency Anemia**.
  > * $\text{MCV} / \text{RBC count} < 13 \rightarrow$ Suggests **Thalassemia Trait** (bone marrow hyperproduces small RBCs, keeping RBC count high).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Hepcidin Regulation**: Daily dosing of iron triggers a transient rise in hepcidin (an iron absorption blocker) lasting 24 hours. Dosing iron every other day avoids this hepcidin spike, increasing fractional iron absorption and improving gastrointestinal tolerance.
