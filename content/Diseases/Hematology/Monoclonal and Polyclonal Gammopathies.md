# 🩸 Monoclonal and Polyclonal Gammopathies (โรคความผิดปกติของอิมมูโนโกลบูลินชนิดเดี่ยวและหลายชนิด)

> **Definition**: **Monoclonal Gammopathies** (plasma cell dyscrasias) represent clonal proliferation of a single plasma cell clone producing a homogenous immunoglobulin or light chain (M-protein). **Polyclonal Gammopathies** represent a diffuse, heterogeneous increase in multiple immunoglobulin classes due to immune system activation by an underlying inflammatory, infectious, or liver disorder.

---

## 🔬 Electrophoresis Pattern Differentiation

* **Monoclonal Pattern**: Sharp, narrow, tall spike (**M-Spike**) in the gamma or beta globulin region on Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP). Indicates clonal expansion of a single B-cell/plasma cell clone.
* **Polyclonal Pattern**: Broad-based, diffuse, dome-shaped increase in the gamma region on SPEP. Indicates heterogeneous activation of multiple immunoglobulin-producing B-cell clones.

## 🩸 1. Monoclonal Gammopathies (Plasma Cell Dyscrasias)

### A. Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS)
* **Definition**: Precursor, asymptomatic plasma cell clone.
* **Diagnostic Criteria**:
  1. Serum monoclonal protein (M-protein) **$< 3\text{ g/dL}$** AND
  2. Bone marrow clonal plasma cells **$< 10\%$** AND
  3. **No CRAB or SLiM criteria** (no end-organ damage, normal calcium, renal function, hemoglobin, and bone imaging).
* **Clinical Course**: Risk of progression to Multiple Myeloma, AL Amyloidosis, or Waldenström Macroglobulinemia is **$1\%$ per year**.
* **Management**: Watchful waiting. Check CBC, creatinine, calcium, and SPEP annually. No chemotherapy indicated.

### B. Smoldering Multiple Myeloma (SMM)
* **Definition**: Asymptomatic intermediate stage between MGUS and active MM.
* **Diagnostic Criteria**:
  1. Serum M-protein **$\ge 3\text{ g/dL}$** (or urine M-protein $\ge 500$ mg/24h) AND/OR
  2. Bone marrow clonal plasma cells **$10–59\%$** AND
  3. **No CRAB or SLiM criteria** (asymptomatic).
* **Clinical Course**: High risk of progression to active MM ($10\%$ per year for the first 5 years).
* **Management**: Close observation (every 3-6 months). High-risk SMM may be considered for early treatment trials (e.g., Lenalidomide).

### C. AL Amyloidosis (Primary Systemic Amyloidosis)
* **Pathophysiology**: Clonal plasma cell disorder producing unstable **monoclonal light chains** (usually **$\lambda$** type). Light chains misfold, aggregate, and deposit extracellularly as insoluble fibrils (containing $\beta$-pleated sheets) in organs, causing tissue compression and organ dysfunction.
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * **Kidneys**: Nephrotic syndrome (severe proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, edema).
  * **Heart**: Restrictive cardiomyopathy (diastolic heart failure, thickened ventricles, low voltage on ECG, conduction blocks).
  * **Gastrointestinal**: **Macroglossia** (pathognomonic enlarged tongue; present in $10-15\%$), hepatosplenomegaly, and malabsorption.
  * **Neurological**: Autonomic neuropathy (severe orthostatic hypotension), peripheral sensory neuropathy, and carpal tunnel syndrome.
  * **Vascular/Skin**: Capillary fragility leading to **periorbital purpura ("raccoon eyes")** after minor pressure/coughing, and waxy skin papules.
* **Diagnosis**:
  * **Tissue Biopsy** (abdominal fat pad aspirate, rectal biopsy, or affected organ): **Congo Red stain** showing **Apple-green birefringence** under polarized light (diagnostic hallmark).
  * Serum/Urine Immunofixation or Free Light Chain assay (SFLC) showing clonal light chain excess.
* **Management**: Target the underlying plasma cell clone. Chemotherapy (**Bortezomib** + Cyclophosphamide + Dexamethasone; CyBorD regimen) followed by autologous stem cell transplant in eligible patients.

### D. Waldenström Macroglobulinemia (WM) / Lymphoplasmacytic Lymphoma
* **Definition**: Malignant proliferation of **lymphoplasmacytic cells** (cells showing features of both B-lymphocytes and plasma cells) in the bone marrow, secreting a monoclonal **IgM** paraprotein.
* **Genetics**: Somatic mutation in **MYD88 L265P gene** present in $>90\%$ of cases.
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * **Hyperviscosity Syndrome**: IgM is a large pentamer, greatly increasing blood viscosity when elevated. Presents with:
    * Neurological: Headache, dizziness, tinnitus, ataxia, and confusion.
    * Ophthalmic: Blurry vision, retinal hemorrhages, and **"sausage-link" (link-of-sausage) dilation of retinal veins**.
    * Hemorrhagic: Mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gingival bleeding) due to IgM coating platelets and impairing function.
  * Systemic: B symptoms (fever, drenching night sweats, weight loss), fatigue (due to anemia).
  * Physical: Lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly.
  * *Note: **No lytic bone lesions or hypercalcemia** (unlike Multiple Myeloma).*
* **Diagnosis**:
  * SPEP/IFE confirming IgM M-protein.
  * Bone marrow biopsy showing lymphoplasmacytic infiltration.
  * Positive MYD88 L265P mutation.
* **Management**:
  * **Emergency (Hyperviscosity)**: **Plasmapheresis (Plasma Exchange)** to rapidly remove circulating IgM and decrease viscosity.
  * **Definitive**: Immunotherapy (**Rituximab** combined with Bendamustine) or BTK inhibitors (**Ibrutinib**; targets downstream MYD88 signaling).
  * *Precaution*: Avoid initiating Rituximab in patients with high IgM levels without prior plasmapheresis, as Rituximab can trigger a transient, dangerous **IgM flare**.

## 🩸 2. Polyclonal Gammopathies

* **Definition**: Diffuse, non-clonal increase in all immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM). Reversible when the primary stimulus is removed.
* **Pathophysiology**: Chronic antigenic stimulation triggers cytokine release (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha) which drives the activation of multiple B-cell clones.
* **Key Etiologies**:
  1. **Chronic Liver Disease (Classic)**: Cirrhosis (especially alcohol-related, viral hepatitis, or autoimmune hepatitis).
     * **Beta-Gamma Bridging**: A pathognomonic SPEP pattern in cirrhosis. IgA and IgG levels are so elevated that they fill the trough between the beta and gamma globulin bands, creating a continuous bridge.
  2. **Chronic Infections**: Tuberculosis, infective endocarditis, osteomyelitis, HIV, visceral leishmaniasis.
  3. **Autoimmune Diseases**: Rheumatoid Arthritis, SLE, Sjögren Syndrome, IgG4-Related Disease.
  4. **Malignancies**: Hodgkin lymphoma, solid tumors.
* **Management**: Treat the underlying cause (e.g., antivirals for HBV/HCV, immunosuppressants for SLE/RA, antibiotics for infections). No indication for plasma exchange or myeloma-specific chemotherapy.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **No Bones in Waldenström**: Do not mistake Waldenström Macroglobulinemia for Multiple Myeloma. WM features an **IgM M-spike, adenopathy, and hyperviscosity**, but **does NOT cause lytic bone lesions, hypercalcemia, or severe renal failure**.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Raccoon Eyes**: A patient presenting with unexplained proteinuria or heart failure who develops bruising around both eyes (**raccoon eyes**) after a simple valsalva maneuver (coughing, vomiting) has **AL Amyloidosis** due to amyloid deposition in capillary walls.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Beta-Gamma Bridging**: If you see a broad elevation of the beta and gamma globulins bridging together on an SPEP report, look for clinical signs of **liver cirrhosis** (ascites, spider angiomas, palmar erythema) rather than a hematologic malignancy.
