## type: disease

# Definition
Hereditary cerebellar ataxias are a genetically heterogeneous group of progressive neurodegenerative disorders characterized by slowly progressive cerebellar dysfunction (gait instability, incoordination, dysarthria, and visual disturbances), inherited in autosomal recessive, autosomal dominant, or X-linked patterns.

# Primary Clinical Entities

## 1. Friedreich's Ataxia (FRDA) (Autosomal Recessive)
The most common hereditary ataxia, representing $50\%$ of all cases.
* **Genetics**: Caused by a **GAA trinucleotide repeat expansion** in the *FXN* gene on chromosome 9. This leads to transcriptional silencing and severe deficiency of **frataxin**, a mitochondrial protein involved in iron-sulfur cluster assembly.
* **Pathology**: Frataxin deficiency leads to mitochondrial iron accumulation, severe oxidative stress, and progressive degeneration of the **posterior columns, spinocerebellar tracts, corticospinal tracts**, and peripheral sensory nerves.
* **Clinical Presentation (Onset $<20$ years)**:
  * Progressive gait and limb ataxia.
  * **Neurological**: Loss of vibration and proprioception (posterior columns), sensory neuropathy (areflexia in legs), but accompanied by UMN signs (spasticity, **bilateral positive Babinski signs**).
  * **Skeletal**: High-arched feet (**pes cavus**), hammertoes, and progressive scoliosis.
  * **Systemic**: **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy** (present in $>90\%$, the primary cause of death due to arrhythmias or heart failure), and Diabetes Mellitus ($10\%$).

## 2. Spinocerebellar Ataxias (SCAs) (Autosomal Dominant)
A large, diverse group of adult-onset cerebellar ataxias (SCA1 to SCA48).
* **Genetics**: The most common subtypes (SCA1, SCA2, SCA3, SCA6, SCA7) are caused by **CAG trinucleotide repeat expansions** (polyglutamine diseases), resulting in a toxic gain-of-function in the mutated proteins.
* **Clinical Presentation (Onset $30-50$ years)**:
  * Slowly progressive cerebellar ataxia, dysarthria, and nystagmus.
  * *SCA3 (Machado-Joseph Disease)*: The most common SCA. Presents with ataxia, parkinsonism, prominent dystonia, facial fasciculations, and peripheral neuropathy.
  * *SCA7*: Characterized by progressive maculopathy leading to blindness.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Motor**: Insidious, slowly progressive gait instability, difficulty running, hand clumsiness, and drop of handwriting quality.
* **Speech**: Scanning dysarthria (slow, slurred, irregular emphasis on syllables).
* **Cardiorespiratory (In Friedreich's)**: Dyspnea, palpitations, and chest pain (due to cardiomyopathy).

## Physical Examination
* **Ataxia & Coordination**: Wide-based, unsteady gait. Intention tremor, dysmetria (finger-to-nose overshoot), and dysdiadochokinesia.
* **Neurological Paradox (Friedreich's)**:
  * **Absent lower limb reflexes** (LMN sign due to sensory neuropathy).
  * **Bilateral extensor plantar responses / Babinski signs** (UMN sign due to lateral corticospinal tract degeneration).
  * Loss of vibration and proprioception (sensory ataxia, positive Romberg).
* **Skeletal Deformities (Friedreich's)**: High-arched feet (pes cavus), claw toes, and kyphoscoliosis.

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Cerebellar Ataxia Acquired]]**: Differentiated by: acute or subacute onset (weeks/months), absence of skeletal deformities, and MRI/laboratory identification of toxic, metabolic, or paraneoplastic causes.
* **Ataxia with Vitamin E Deficiency (AVED)**:
  > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **AVED Diagnostic Mimic**: An autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the $\alpha$-tocopherol transfer protein gene. It is **clinically indistinguishable from Friedreich's Ataxia** (progressive ataxia, loss of reflexes, proprioceptive loss, cardiomyopathy). However, it is **highly treatable and reversible with high-dose Vitamin E supplementation**. Always check Vitamin E levels in suspected Friedreich's patients.
* **Multiple Sclerosis**: Can present with progressive ataxia, but has a relapsing course, white matter plaques on MRI, and CSF oligoclonal bands.

---

# Investigations

## Laboratory
* **Genetic Testing (Gold Standard)**:
  * Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) and triplet repeat-primed PCR to detect GAA expansions in *FXN* (for Friedreich's) or CAG expansions in the SCA panel.
* **Serum Vitamin E**: Mandatory to rule out treatable AVED.
* **Oral Glucose Tolerance Test**: Screen for diabetes mellitus (in Friedreich's).

## Cardiovascular Panel (Mandatory in Friedreich's)
* **[[ECG]]**: Shows widespread T-wave inversions and left ventricular hypertrophy.
* **[[Echocardiography]]**: Demonstrates progressive symmetric or asymmetric **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy**.

## Imaging & Diagnostics
* **MRI Brain and Spine**:
  * Shows progressive cerebellar and brainstem atrophy (predominantly vermian) in SCAs.
  * In Friedreich's, shows severe **atrophy/thinning of the cervical spinal cord**, while the brain/cerebellum is initially normal.
* **[[EMG/NCS]]**:
  * Shows a severe **sensory axonal neuropathy** (absent or severely reduced Sensory Nerve Action Potentials [SNAPs]) in Friedreich's ataxia.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by genetic testing showing pathological repeat expansions in the presence of compatible progressive cerebellar syndromes.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* **Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation**: Physical therapy (gait training, adaptive mobility devices), occupational therapy (hand function, home modification), and speech therapy.
* **Cardiac Optimization (Friedreich's)**: Close monitoring by cardiology. Treat heart failure and arrhythmias with standard therapies (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers). Avoid high-intensity sports due to cardiomyopathy.

## Definitive / Disease-Modifying Therapy
* **Omaveloxolone**:
  * The first approved disease-modifying drug for Friedreich's Ataxia (for patients $\ge 16$ years).
  * *Mechanism*: Activates the **Nrf2 pathway**, which restores mitochondrial function, reduces oxidative stress, and slows clinical disease progression.
  * *Dose*: $150$ mg PO once daily. Monitor LFTs and BNP periodically.
* **No cure exists** for Spinocerebellar Ataxias (SCAs); treatment remains strictly symptomatic:
  * Treat spasticity with **Baclofen**.
  * Treat tremors with Propranolol or Clonazepam.
* **AVED Treatment**: High-dose **Vitamin E (800 - 1500 mg/day PO)** is curative if initiated early.

---

# Complications
* **Cardiac Failure & Arrhythmias**: The leading cause of death in Friedreich's ataxia ($60-80\%$ of cases).
* **Falls**: Fractures and head injuries.
* **Loss of Ambulation**: Most patients with Friedreich's require a wheelchair within 10-15 years of onset.

# Prognosis
* Friedreich's Ataxia is progressive; patients typically lose the ability to walk by their late twenties, and have a shortened life expectancy (death in the 3rd to 5th decade due to cardiomyopathy).
* SCAs are progressive, with variable onset and severity; lifespan is moderately reduced depending on the specific subtype.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Vitamin E Screening Rule**: Every child or young adult suspected of having Friedreich's ataxia **must** have their serum **Vitamin E level** checked. Missing AVED is a tragic, irreversible error, as early Vitamin E replacement completely arrests the disease.
* > [!TIP]
  > **The Triad of Friedreich's Ataxia**: Remember the classic combination: **Progressive ataxia + Absent reflexes (LMN) + Babinski signs (UMN)**. If scoliosis, pes cavus, or a cardiac murmur is also present, the diagnosis of FRDA is virtually assured.
