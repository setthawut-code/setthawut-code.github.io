## type: disease

# Definition
Entrapment neuropathy is a localized peripheral nerve injury caused by mechanical compression, stretching, or constriction as the nerve passes through a narrow anatomical space, such as an osteofibrous canal, fascial sheath, or fibromuscular tunnel. It represents a form of **mononeuropathy**.

# Common Entrapment Syndromes
While **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome]]** is the most common, peripheral nerve entrapments can occur at several classic anatomical sites:

| Syndrome | Affected Nerve | Site of Entrapment | Clinical Presentation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome** | **Median Nerve** | Wrist (flexor retinaculum) | Numbness in digits 1-3, night pain, thenar atrophy, palm spared (detailed in **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome]]**). |
| **Cubital Tunnel Syndrome** | **Ulnar Nerve** | Elbow (medial epicondyle) | Numbness in digit 5 and ulnar half of 4. Weakness of grip, **claw hand** deformity, wasting of hypothenar and interossei. |
| **Guyon's Canal Syndrome** | **Ulnar Nerve** | Wrist (pisohamate ligament) | Similar to Cubital Tunnel, but **spares sensory loss on dorsum of hand**; motor weakness in hand muscles. |
| **Saturday Night Palsy** | **Radial Nerve** | Humerus (spiral groove) | **Wrist drop** (loss of wrist/finger extension), sensory loss on the dorsum of the hand/thumb web space. |
| **Meralgia Paresthetica** | **Lateral Femoral Cutaneous Nerve** | Inguinal Ligament | Pure sensory syndrome: burning pain, numbness, and tingling on the **lateral aspect of the thigh**. *No motor weakness*. |
| **Peroneal Nerve Palsy** | **Common Peroneal Nerve** | Fibular Neck | **Foot drop** (loss of ankle dorsiflexion/eversion), sensory loss on the dorsum of the foot and lateral calf. |
| **Tarsal Tunnel Syndrome** | **Posterior Tibial Nerve** | Medial Malleolus | Burning pain and numbness on the **sole of the foot**, worsened by walking/standing. |

# Pathophysiology
1. **Mechanical Compression**: Repetitive movement, trauma, or local tissue changes (synovitis, edema, osteophytes) raise tissue pressure inside a narrow anatomical channel.
2. **Microvascular Ischemia**: Elevated tissue pressure exceeds venous capillary pressure, causing venous congestion, localized capillary leak, and nerve edema.
3. **Demyelination & Conduction Block**: Mild or acute compression causes focal demyelination of the myelin sheath at the site of compression. This slows or blocks electrical conduction (**Neuropraxia**), presenting as reversible numbness/weakness.
4. **Axonal Degeneration**: Prolonged or severe compression leads to axonal ischemia and necrosis (**Axonotmesis**), causing muscle denervation/atrophy and permanent sensory loss.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* Numbness, tingling, burning, or paresthesias strictly limited to the sensory distribution of the affected nerve.
* Weakness, loss of motor coordination, or muscle wasting in muscles distal to the site of entrapment.
* Pain that is often exacerbated by specific movements, postures, or direct pressure over the entrapment site.

## Physical Examination
* **Sensory Exam**: Map the sensory loss. Verify that it matches a single peripheral nerve distribution and does not cross dermatomal lines (rules out radiculopathy).
* **Motor Exam**: Test muscles innervated by the compressed nerve. Look for muscle wasting (LMN signs, e.g., hypothenar wasting in ulnar neuropathy, thenar wasting in median neuropathy).
* **Provocative Maneuvers**:
  * **Tinel's Sign**: Percussion over the site of entrapment reproduces shooting paresthesias in the nerve's distribution (e.g., tapping behind the medial epicondyle in cubital tunnel syndrome).
  * **Motor Deformities**:
    * **Ulnar Claw Hand (Main en Griffe)**: Hyperextension at the MCP joints and flexion at the IP joints of digits 4 and 5, due to loss of the lumbrical muscles innervated by the ulnar nerve.
    * **Wrist Drop**: Inability to dorsiflex the wrist (radial nerve palsy).

# Differential Diagnosis
* **Cervical or Lumbar Radiculopathy**: Compression of a spinal nerve root. Presents with dermatomal sensory loss, radiating neck/back pain, and reflex loss (e.g., L5 radiculopathy mimics peroneal nerve foot drop, but is accompanied by loss of ankle inversion [tibialis posterior] and back pain).
* **Plexopathy (Brachial or Lumbosacral)**: Multifocal deficits involving multiple nerves in a single limb (e.g., Erb's palsy, diabetic amyotrophy).
* **[[Polyneuropathy]]**: Symmetric, distal "stocking-glove" sensorimotor deficits, rather than focal, isolated nerve dysfunction.

---

# Investigations

## Special Tests
* **[[EMG/NCS]] (Gold Standard Confirmation)**:
  * **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)**: Demonstrates focal conduction slowing, prolonged distal latency, or **conduction block** across the segment of entrapment, while other segments of the same nerve are normal.
  * **Electromyography (EMG)**: Assesses for active denervation (fibrillation potentials) and chronic reinnervation in target muscles, helping to grade the severity and chronicity of the injury.
* **Neuromuscular Ultrasound**:
  * Visualizes the nerve directly. Shows swelling/increased cross-sectional area immediately proximal to the compression site (the "hourglass" sign).

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Diagnosis is established clinically by mapping sensory and motor deficits to a single peripheral nerve distribution, finding a positive Tinel's sign at the anatomical tunnel, and confirming with focal conduction slowing on NCS.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* **Conservative Interventions (First-line)**:
  * **Splinting / Orthotics**: Immobilize the joint in a neutral position to reduce pressure (e.g., night elbow splint at 45 degrees of extension for cubital tunnel syndrome).
  * **Activity Modification**: Avoid resting elbows on hard tables (for cubital tunnel), avoid crossing legs (for peroneal palsy), or lose weight and avoid tight belts (for meralgia paresthetica).
  * **Pharmacotherapy**: NSAIDs for pain; local corticosteroid injections under ultrasound guidance for rapid symptomatic relief.

## Definitive Management
* **Surgical Decompression**:
  * Indicated for: progressive motor weakness, muscle atrophy, severe axonal loss on EMG, or failure of conservative therapy.
  * *Procedures*:
    * Decompression of cubital tunnel (with or without ulnar nerve anterior transposition).
    * Carpal tunnel release (detailed in **[[Carpal Tunnel Syndrome]]**).
    * Surgical release of peroneal nerve at the fibular neck.

---

# Complications
* **Permanent Muscle Atrophy & Weakness**: Fixed claw hand, wrist drop, or foot drop.
* **Neuropathic Pain & Dysesthesias**: Persistent burning pain and paresthesias.
* **Sensory Loss**: Predisposes to painless injuries and ulcers (especially in tarsal tunnel syndrome).

# Prognosis
* Excellent for mild demyelinating conduction block (neuropraxia) treated early.
* Poorer recovery if chronic axonotmesis (axonal loss) with severe muscle atrophy is present prior to surgery.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Foot Drop Differentiator (L5 vs. Peroneal)**: To distinguish peroneal nerve entrapment from an L5 radiculopathy, test **foot inversion** (tibialis posterior, L5-innervated but tibial-nerve supplied). In peroneal nerve palsy, foot inversion is **normal**. In L5 radiculopathy, foot inversion is **weak**.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Meralgia Paresthetica Reassurance**: The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is a **pure sensory nerve**. Meralgia paresthetica (tight-pants syndrome) never causes muscle weakness or reflex changes. Reassure the patient and advise weight loss and avoiding tight clothing.
