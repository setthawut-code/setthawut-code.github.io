## type: disease

# Definition
A brain abscess is a focal, suppurative (pus-filled) infection within the brain parenchyma, typically surrounded by a vascularized collagen capsule. It represents a critical infectious space-occupying lesion.

# Epidemiology
* Relatively rare but highly lethal if untreated, with an incidence of $0.3 - 0.9$ cases per 100,000 annually.
* More common in males (2:1 ratio) and in patients with predisposing conditions (immunosuppression, congenital heart disease).

# Etiology & Infection Routes
Brain abscesses arise from three primary mechanisms:

```
Brain Abscess Routes of Entry
└── 1. Contiguous Direct Extension (40-50%)
    ├── Otitis media / Mastoiditis -> Spreads to Temporal Lobe or Cerebellum
    ├── Frontal / Ethmoid Sinusitis -> Spreads to Frontal Lobe
    └── Dental infections -> Spreads to Frontal Lobe
└── 2. Hematogenous Dissemination (25-35%)
    ├── Cyanotic Congenital Heart Disease (R-to-L shunt bypasses lung filtration)
    ├── Infective Endocarditis, Pulmonary AV Fistulas
    └── Lung abscess / Bronchiectasis
└── 3. Direct Traumatic Penetration (10%)
    └── Penetrating head trauma, post-neurosurgery
```

### Key Causative Pathogens:
* **Streptococci species** (especially *Streptococcus anginosus* / *milleri* group) - present in $30-40\%$ of cases.
* **Anaerobes** (*Bacteroides*, *Prevotella*, *Fusobacterium*) - common in otogenic and dental sources.
* **Enterobacteriaceae** (*Proteus*, *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*) - common in otogenic sources.
* **Staphylococcus aureus** - primary pathogen post-trauma or neurosurgery.
* *Toxoplasma gondii*, *Nocardia*, and fungi (*Aspergillus*, Mucorales) - seen in patients with HIV/AIDS or transplant recipients.

# Pathophysiology
The pathology evolves through four distinct stages:
1. **Early Cerebritis (Days 1-3)**: Localized polymorphonuclear leukocyte (PMN) infiltrate, microvascular congestion, and marked cytotoxic edema.
2. **Late Cerebritis (Days 4-9)**: Maximum local tissue destruction and liquefactive necrosis. Early reticulin network (early capsule) is formed by fibroblasts.
3. **Early Capsule Formation (Days 10-13)**: Neovascularization forms a collagenous capsule, walling off the necrotic core.
   * *Clinical Pearl*: The capsule develops faster and thicker on the highly vascularized **cortical side** than the less vascularized **ventricular side**. This creates an anatomical vulnerability: expanding abscesses tend to rupture medially into the lateral ventricles, causing highly fatal ventriculitis.
4. **Late Capsule Formation (Days 14+)**: A dense, mature collagenous wall surrounds a completely liquefied necrotic center.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Generalized Progressive Headache**: The most common symptom ($>70\%$). It is localized to the side of the abscess and is constant, dull, and refractory to analgesics.
* **[[Fever]]**: Present in only $50\%$ of patients (absence of fever does not rule out brain abscess).
* **[[Seizure]]**: New-onset focal or generalized seizures occur in $30-40\%$ of patients.
* **Nausea & Projective Vomiting** (due to elevated ICP).

## Physical Examination
* **The Clinical Triad**: **Headache, fever, and focal neurologic deficits** (present in only $20\%$ of patients).
* **Focal Neurologic Deficits**: Hemiparesis, dysphasia, or visual field defects (e.g., contralateral homonymous superior quadrantanopia in temporal lobe abscess).
* **Signs of Raised ICP**: Papilledema, CN VI (Abducens) palsy.
* **Cerebellar Signs**: Nystagmus, ataxia, dysmetria (classic in cerebellar abscesses secondary to mastoiditis).

---

# Differential Diagnosis
* **High-grade Glioma or Brain Metastasis**: Ring-enhancing mass on MRI. Differentiated by **DWI sequence** (metastases show central facilitation, whereas abscesses show restricted diffusion).
* **[[Encephalitis]]**: Diffuse cerebral inflammation without a focal capsule or necrotic core.
* **[[Meningitis]]**: Diffuse meningeal enhancement; prominent neck stiffness; CSF is purulent throughout without focal mass effect.

---

# Investigations

## Imaging
* **[[Brain MRI]] (with gadolinium contrast) - Gold Standard**:
  * **Classical Finding**: A thin, smooth, symmetrically **ring-enhancing lesion** with surrounding hypodense vasogenic edema.
  * **Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI)**: Demonstrates **restricted diffusion in the necrotic core** (high signal on DWI, low signal on ADC map). This is highly specific for a bacterial abscess (reflecting the high viscosity of pus/dead cells) and differentiates it from a necrotic brain tumor.
* **[[CT Brain]] (with contrast)**: Shows ring-enhancing mass with midline shift; used if MRI is contraindicated.

## Special Tests
* **CT-guided Stereotactic Aspiration**: Diagnostic and therapeutic. The aspirated pus is sent for Gram stain, aerobic/anaerobic cultures, acid-fast stain, and cytology.
> [!CAUTION]
  > **LP is strictly contraindicated** in suspected brain abscess. The mass effect and midline shift create a high risk of precipitating fatal transtentorial herniation during lumbar puncture.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by clinical focal signs combined with brain MRI demonstrating a ring-enhancing lesion with central restricted diffusion, and positive culture/PCR from stereotactic aspirate or blood cultures.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
> [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Antibiotics and Aspiration**: Obtain blood cultures and perform stereotactic aspiration *first* if the patient is stable, to ensure microbiological yield. If the patient is unstable or showing signs of herniation, start empiric IV antibiotics immediately.
* **Empiric Intravenous Antibiotics (adjusted for source)**:
  * **Direct Extension (Otogenic, Sinus, Dental)**:
    * **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + Metronidazole 500 mg IV q6h**
    * *Metronidazole is crucial*: It penetrates the necrotic abscess cavity and capsule exceptionally well, and has excellent bactericidal activity against anaerobes.
  * **Hematogenous (Endocarditis, Congenital Heart Shunt)**:
    * **Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole + Vancomycin 15 - 20 mg/kg IV q8-12h** (Vancomycin is added to cover *S. aureus*).
  * **Post-Traumatic or Post-Neurosurgery**:
    * **Vancomycin + Ceftazidime 2 g IV q8h** (Ceftazidime is added to cover *Pseudomonas*).
* **Glucocorticoids (Dexamethasone)**:
  * Indicated **only if** there is significant vasogenic edema causing midline shift, high ICP, or impending brain herniation.
  * *Dosing*: Dexamethasone 4-10 mg IV q6h. **Taper rapidly** once symptoms improve, as steroids reduce antibiotic penetration through the abscess wall and delay capsule formation.

## Definitive Management
* **Surgical Drainage**:
  * **Stereotactic Aspiration**: The primary surgical choice. Safe for deep or eloquent area abscesses.
  * **Craniotomy and Excision**: Indicated for large, multiloculated abscesses, gas-forming infections, posterior fossa abscesses (high herniation risk), or those failing needle aspiration.
* **Duration**: Antibiotic therapy must be continued for at least **4 to 6 weeks** intravenously, followed by oral therapy guided by follow-up MRI scans showing collapse of the cavity.

---

# Complications
* **Intraventricular Rupture**: The abscess ruptures into the lateral ventricles. Presents as sudden catastrophic worsening, high fever, meningeal rigidity, and ventriculitis. Associated with $>80\%$ mortality.
* **Brain Herniation**: Transtentorial or tonsillar herniation due to mass effect.
* **Post-abscess Epilepsy**: Occurs in $30-50\%$ of survivors.

# Prognosis
Mortality has dropped to $<10\%$ due to stereotactic drainage and contrast neuroimaging. However, permanent neurological deficits (weakness, visual defects, epilepsy) remain common.

# Clinical Pearls
* **Cerebellar Abscess Path**: Chronic otitis media spreads directly to the **anterior cerebellum** or the **temporal lobe** via the tegmen tympani. If a patient with chronic ear discharge develops ataxia or dysmetria, scan the posterior fossa immediately.
* **Metronidazole Advantage**: Unlike most beta-lactams, Metronidazole is not active against aerobes, but its unique pharmacokinetics allow it to pass through the lipid-rich abscess capsule and remain highly active in the acidic, necrotic environment of pus.
