## type: disease

# Definition
Encephalitis is an acute inflammatory infection of the brain parenchyma (cerebral cortex and subcortical structures), characterized by clinical evidence of neurologic dysfunction (altered mental status, focal seizures, behavior changes, or focal deficits) accompanying signs of brain inflammation.

# Epidemiology
* **Herpes Simplex Virus type 1 (HSV-1)** is the most common cause of sporadic, fatal encephalitis worldwide, occurring in all age groups and throughout the year.
* **Arboviruses** (e.g., **Japanese Encephalitis Virus [JE]** in Southeast Asia, West Nile Virus, Dengue) are seasonal, mosquito-borne, and endemic in specific regions.
* **Autoimmune Encephalitis** (e.g., Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis) has been increasingly recognized, often affecting young women and children.

# Etiology
* **Viral Encephalitis (Direct invasion)**:
  * **Herpes simplex virus 1 (HSV-1)**: Responsible for $>90\%$ of adult viral cases (due to reactivation of latent virus in the trigeminal ganglion).
  * **Japanese Encephalitis (JE)**: Flavivirus transmitted by *Culex* mosquitoes; endemic in agricultural regions of Asia (e.g., rice paddies, pig farming).
  * **Other viruses**: Varicella-zoster virus (VZV), Rabies, Enteroviruses, Cytomegalovirus (CMV in immunocompromised).
* **Autoimmune Encephalitis**:
  * **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis**: Mediated by autoantibodies against the GluN1 subunit of the NMDA receptor. Strongly associated with **ovarian teratomas** ($50\%$ of young female cases).
  * LGI1 or CASPR2 autoimmune encephalitis.

# Pathophysiology
* **Viral Entry**: HSV-1 enters the brain via retrograde axonal transport along the olfactory or trigeminal nerves, directly accessing the frontal and **temporal lobes**. Arboviruses enter hematogenously crossing the blood-brain barrier.
* **Cellular Injury**: Intracellular replication of the virus triggers cell lysis, neuronal death, and secondary microglial activation. This leads to necrotizing focal inflammation, perivascular cuffing, and severe cerebral edema.
* **Localization**:
  * *HSV-1*: Predilection for the temporal lobes, insular cortex, and orbital frontal cortex (limbic system).
  * *JE*: Predilection for the **thalamus, basal ganglia**, and brainstem (presenting with parkinsonian-like extrapyramidal features).

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Encephalopathic Dysfunction**: Altered mental status, personality or behavioral changes (e.g., bizarre behavior, psychosis, agitation), confusion, and memory deficits.
* **[[Fever]] & [[Headache]]** (often severe, generalized).
* **[[Seizure]]**: Common and often focal, reflecting localized cortical irritation (specifically temporal lobe seizures).

## Physical Examination
* **Focal Neurologic Deficits**: Aphasia (often Wernicke's or motor, due to temporal/frontal localization), hemiparesis, or cranial nerve palsies.
* **Cognitive deficits**: Anterograde amnesia, disorientation, or delirium.
* **Extrapyramidal signs**: Tremor, rigidity, or dystonia (classic in Japanese Encephalitis due to thalamic/basal ganglia involvement).
* **Dyskinesias**: Prominent oro-facial-lingual dyskinesias (chewing, lip-smacking, tongue protrusion) are pathognomonic for **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis**.

---

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Meningitis]]**: Features prominent nuchal rigidity, Kernig's/Brudzinski's signs, and **no early signs of brain parenchymal dysfunction** (normal cognition and absence of early seizures).
* **[[Brain Abscess]]**: Progressive focal deficits, localized mass effect, ring-enhancing lesion.
* **Psychiatric Psychosis**: New-onset schizophrenia or bipolar mania. Delirium/Encephalitis must be ruled out (fever, CSF pleocytosis, and abnormal EEG are absent in psychiatric disease).
* **Toxic-Metabolic Encephalopathy**: Hypoglycemia, hepatic or uremic encephalopathy. Normal CSF, normal MRI, diffuse EEG slowing.

---

# Investigations

## Laboratory
* **Routine Labs**: CBC, electrolytes (SIADH is common in encephalitis), renal and hepatic panels.
* **Serum Autoantibodies**: Check for anti-NMDA, anti-LGI1, or anti-CASPR2 antibodies if autoimmune etiology is suspected.

## Imaging
* **[[Brain MRI]] (FLAIR, T2, and DWI)**: The diagnostic imaging modality of choice.
  * **HSV Encephalitis**: Demonstrates high signal intensity (hyperintensity) in the **medial temporal lobes, insular cortex, and inferior frontal lobes** (FLAIR/T2), often asymmetric.
  * **Japanese Encephalitis**: Demonstrates bilateral hyperintensity in the **thalamus** and basal ganglia.
* **[[CT Brain]]**: Insensitive in the early stages; may show hypodensity in the temporal lobes later in the course.

## Special Tests
* **[[Lumbar Puncture]] (LP) and [[CSF Analysis]]**:
  * *Profile*: Typically shows lymphocytic pleocytosis ($10 - 500$ cells/$\mu$L), elevated protein ($50-150$ mg/dL), and **normal glucose**.
  * **CSF HSV-1 PCR**: The gold standard diagnostic. Highly sensitive ($>96\%$) and specific. *Note: Can be falsely negative in the first 24-48 hours of symptoms; repeat LP if suspicion remains high.*
* **[[EEG]] (Electroencephalogram)**:
  * Shows **Periodic Lateralizing Epileptiform Discharges (PLEDs)** over the temporal leads, indicating localized focal temporal lobe inflammation (highly suggestive of HSV).

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Probable viral encephalitis requires encephalopathy (altered mental status $>24$ hours) plus at least three of: fever, new-onset seizures, focal neurologic deficits, CSF pleocytosis, MRI parenchymal abnormalities, or EEG abnormalities. Definitive diagnosis requires CSF PCR or serology confirming the pathogen.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Empiric Acyclovir Rule**: Start intravenous acyclovir immediately in any patient presenting with suspected encephalitis. Delayed therapy is associated with poor prognosis and severe cognitive deficits.
* **Antiviral Regimen**:
  * **IV Acyclovir 10 mg/kg every 8 hours** for 14-21 days (infuse over 1 hour to prevent crystallization).
  * *Acyclovir Crystalluria Prevention*: Hydrate the patient aggressively with IV crystalloids to maintain high urine flow. Adjust dose in renal impairment.
* **Seizure Control**: IV Levetiracetam or IV Valproic Acid for seizures.

## Definitive Management
* *HSV-1 Encephalitis*: Complete the 14-21 day course of IV Acyclovir. Confirm CSF PCR is negative via repeat LP before stopping therapy.
* *Autoimmune Encephalitis*:
  * First-line: High-dose Methylprednisolone (1 g IV daily for 3-5 days) + IVIG ($2$ g/kg over 5 days) or Plasmapheresis.
  * Screen for ovarian teratoma using transvaginal ultrasound or pelvic MRI; **surgical excision of the teratoma** is mandatory for resolution of anti-NMDA encephalitis.

---

# Complications
* **Neurological**: Intractable epilepsy (status epilepticus), permanent retrograde/anterograde amnesia (due to bilateral hippocampal damage), Kluver-Bucy syndrome (hyperorality, hypersexuality, docility).
* **Systemic**: SIADH (hyponatremia), aspiration pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis.

# Prognosis
* *Untreated HSV Encephalitis*: Mortality exceeds $70\%$.
* *Treated HSV*: Mortality is $10-20\%$. Up to $50\%$ of survivors suffer permanent cognitive deficits, memory loss, or behavioral changes.

# Clinical Pearls
* **MRI insular ribbon sparing clue**: Medial temporal lobe and insular cortex hyperintensity with sparing of the putamen on MRI FLAIR is classic for HSV-1, differentiating it from middle cerebral artery infarction.
* **The "False Negative" PCR**: If the LP is performed very early ($<24-48$ hours of onset), the HSV PCR may be negative. If clinical suspicion is high, continue Acyclovir and perform a repeat LP in 3-7 days.
