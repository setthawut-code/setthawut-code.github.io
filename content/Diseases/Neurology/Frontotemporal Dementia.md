## type: disease

# Definition
Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD), historically referred to as *Pick's Disease* in some pathological subtypes, is a group of progressive neurodegenerative disorders characterized by the selective degeneration of the frontal and temporal lobes. It clinically manifests as prominent changes in personality, social behavior, executive function, and/or language, with relative sparing of memory and visuospatial skills early in the course.

# Epidemiology
* **Age of Onset**: Characteristically occurs at a younger age than Alzheimer's disease. It is a leading cause of **early-onset dementia** (onset typically between **$45 - 65$ years**).
* **Prevalence**: Equal distribution among men and women.

# Etiology
* **Genetics**: FTD has a strong genetic component; up to $30 - 40\%$ of cases are familial.
* **Key Mutations**:
  * **C9orf72** (hexanucleotide repeat expansion on chromosome 9): The most common genetic cause, linking FTD with **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)**.
  * **MAPT** (Microtubule-associated protein tau).
  * **GRN** (Progranulin).

# Pathophysiology
FTD is pathologically classified under **Frontotemporal Lobar Degeneration (FTLD)**, defined by the specific intracellular protein aggregate:
1. **FTLD-Tau ($40\%$)**: Accumulation of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. Pathologically may show **Pick bodies** (round, silver-staining cytoplasmic inclusions of tau in neurons).
2. **FTLD-TDP ($50\%$)**: Accumulation of TAR DNA-binding protein 43 (TDP-43), which is also the primary pathology in ALS.
3. **FTLD-FUS ($10\%$)**: Accumulation of Fused in Sarcoma (FUS) protein.

These protein accumulations lead to progressive synaptic dysfunction, gliosis, and severe neuronal loss in the frontal and temporal lobes.

# Clinical Subtypes & Manifestations

## 1. Behavioral Variant FTD (bvFTD) (Most Common, 60%)
Characterized by progressive changes in personality and conduct. Requires at least three of the following features:
* **Early Behavioral Disinhibition**: Socially inappropriate behavior (e.g., making offensive remarks, touching strangers), loss of manners, impulsivity, or reckless financial/social decisions.
* **Early Apathy or Inertia**: Loss of drive, motivation, and interest in hobbies or social relationships (frequently misdiagnosed as depression).
* **Early Loss of Sympathy or Empathy**: Coldness, indifference to the feelings of others.
* **Early Preserved / Compulsive Behaviors**: Repetitive stereotyped movements (e.g., tapping, pacing, counting) or hoarding.
* **Hyperorality & Dietary Changes**: Marked craving for carbohydrates/sweets, binge eating, altered food preferences, or placing non-food objects in the mouth.
* **Executive Dysfunction**: Impaired planning, organizing, and mental flexibility, with relative sparing of episodic memory and visuospatial skills.

## 2. Language Variants (Primary Progressive Aphasias - PPA)
* **Semantic Variant PPA (svPPA)**:
  * Progressive loss of word meaning (semantic memory).
  * Speech is fluent and grammatically correct, but "empty" of meaning. Patient has severe anomia (cannot name objects) and single-word comprehension deficits.
  * Associated with bilateral (predominantly **left**) anterior temporal lobe atrophy.
* **Non-Fluent / Agrammatic Variant PPA (nfvPPA)**:
  * Progressive difficulty in producing speech (apraxia of speech).
  * Speech is halting, effortful, and agrammatic (omitting prepositions/conjunctions), but word comprehension is preserved.
  * Associated with left inferior frontal lobe (Broca's area) atrophy.

## Physical Examination
* **Cognitive Screening**:
  * Done using **MoCA** or **MMSE**. bvFTD patients may score well on memory sections early on, but show severe deficits on **verbal fluency** (naming words starting with a letter) and **executive function** tests (Luria 3-step motor test, clock drawing).
* **Neurological Exam**:
  * Check for frontal release signs (snout, grasp, palmomental reflexes).
  * **ALS-FTD Overlap**: Check for lower motor neuron signs (atrophy, fasciculations) or upper motor neuron signs in limbs, indicating co-existing motor neuron disease.
  * Check for parkinsonism (bradykinesia, rigidity).

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Alzheimer Disease]]**: Characterized by early **episodic memory loss** (forgetting events), visuospatial disorientation, and relatively preserved social behavior early on.
* **Primary Psychiatric Disorders** (Late-onset schizophrenia, major depression, bipolar disorder): bvFTD is frequently misdiagnosed as a psychiatric illness due to apathy and disinhibition. Differentiated by progressive cognitive decline on formal testing and frontal atrophy on MRI.
* **Vascular Dementia**: Stepwise decline with vascular risk factors and diffuse white matter disease on MRI.

---

# Investigations

## Imaging
* **[[MRI Brain]] (Preferred)**:
  * Shows asymmetric, focal **atrophy of the frontal and anterior temporal lobes**, classically presenting as "knife-edge" gyral thinning, with relative sparing of the posterior parietal and occipital lobes.
* **FDG-PET Scan**:
  * Shows hypometabolism localized to the frontal and temporal lobes.

## Laboratory
* **Dementia Screening**: B12, TSH, RPR (VDRL) to exclude reversible causes.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Diagnosis of probable bvFTD is established by the FTDC criteria (requiring progressive behavioral decline, at least 3 clinical features of disinhibition/apathy/loss of empathy/hyperorality/compulsive behaviors, executive deficits on testing, and frontal/temporal lobe atrophy on MRI).

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* **Behavioral Interventions**: Set up structured daily schedules, limit access to money/credit cards (to prevent impulsive spending), lock cabinets containing food/sweets (for hyperorality), and arrange caregiver support.
* **Speech Therapy**: Highly beneficial for patients presenting with semantic or non-fluent PPA.

## Definitive Management
* **No Disease-Modifying Therapy Exists**.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Cholinesterase Inhibitor Warning**: Unlike in Alzheimer's disease, cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., **Donepezil**, Galantamine) and Memantine **do not work** in FTD and can actually **worsen** behavioral symptoms, leading to increased agitation and irritability. Avoid their use.
* **Symptomatic Pharmacotherapy**:
  * **SSRIs** (e.g., **Sertraline** $50 - 200$ mg PO daily, **Fluvoxamine**, or Citalopram): First-line agents to treat disinhibition, compulsive behaviors, irritability, and carbohydrate cravings.
  * **Atypical Antipsychotics** (e.g., Quetiapine, Risperidone): Used at low doses only if agitation or aggression is severe and poses a safety risk to the patient or caregiver. (Use with caution due to the black box warning of increased mortality in elderly dementia patients).

---

# Complications
* **Financial and Legal Issues**: Due to disinhibition and poor financial judgment (e.g., gambling, buying sprees).
* **Malnutrition / Choking**: Due to rapid, compulsive eating (hyperorality).
* **Aspiration Pneumonia**: Due to bulbar dysfunction in late stages.

# Prognosis
* FTD is progressive and terminal.
* It progresses more rapidly than Alzheimer's disease, with an average survival of $6 - 8$ years from the onset of symptoms (or $3 - 5$ years from diagnosis).

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Age 50 Dementia Clue**: If a patient under the age of 60 presents with progressive behavior changes, loss of empathy for their family, or has started stealing or making inappropriate comments, do not assume it is a psychiatric breakdown. Obtain an **MRI Brain** to look for **frontal lobe atrophy** (suspect **bvFTD**).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Donepezil Dangers in FTD**: Always check the dementia diagnosis before prescribing Donepezil. Prescribing donepezil to a patient with frontotemporal dementia can precipitate severe behavioral outbursts and agitation.
