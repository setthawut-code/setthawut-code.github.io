## type: disease

# Definition
A brain tumor is an abnormal growth of cells within the cranial vault. Brain tumors are classified into primary (originating from the brain tissue, meninges, or cranial nerves) and secondary/metastatic (originating from systemic malignancies outside the CNS and spreading hematogenously to the brain).

# Common Brain Tumor Types

| Tumor Type | Primary / Secondary | Key MRI Features | Key Clinical Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meningioma** | Primary (Most common benign, $35\%$) | Extra-axial, well-circumscribed, homogeneous enhancement, **dural tail sign**. | Arises from arachnoid cap cells. Slowly progressive, benign. Curative with surgical resection. |
| **Glioblastoma (GBM)** | Primary (Most common malignant primary, $15\%$) | Intra-axial, irregular ring-enhancement, central necrosis, crosses corpus callosum (**"butterfly glioma"**). | WHO Grade IV astrocytoma. Highly aggressive, invasive, poor prognosis. |
| **Primary CNS Lymphoma** | Primary | Periventricular, homogeneous contrast enhancement. | Strongly associated with **HIV/AIDS** and **EBV infection**. Highly responsive to corticosteroids and methotrexate. |
| **Vestibular Schwannoma** | Primary | Located at the **cerebellopontine angle (CPA)**; enhances intensely. | Arises from CN VIII. Causes progressive unilateral **[[Hearing Loss]]**, tinnitus, and vertigo. Associated with NF-2. |
| **Brain Metastases** | Secondary (Most common brain tumor overall, $>50\%$) | **Multiple** lesions, located at the **gray-white matter junction**, ring-enhancing with **massive surrounding vasogenic edema**. | Originate from: **Lung** ($50\%$), **Breast** ($15-20\%$), **Melanoma** ($10\%$), Renal Cell, or Colon cancer. |

# Pathophysiology
1. **Local Parenchymal Invasion & Destruction**: Tumor cells infiltrate surrounding brain tissue, disrupting normal synaptic networks and causing focal neurological deficits.
2. **Vasogenic Edema**:
   * Brain tumors induce local angiogenesis, releasing **Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF)**.
   * The newly formed tumor capillaries lack the tight junctions of a normal blood-brain barrier (BBB).
   * This "leaky" barrier allows high-protein fluid to extravasate into the extracellular space, producing **vasogenic edema** (primarily affecting white matter tracts).
3. **Mass Effect & Monroe-Kellie Doctrine**:
   * The skull is a rigid, non-compliant container. Tumor growth and surrounding vasogenic edema increase intracranial volume.
   * As compensatory mechanisms fail, pressure rises rapidly (**[[Acute Increased Intracranial Pressure]] / ICP**).
   * This compromises cerebral blood flow, leading to tissue ischemia, and can cause fatal **brain herniation** (uncal, central, or tonsillar).

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Classic Tumor Headache**:
  * Progressive, dull, generalized headache that is **worse in the morning** (due to nocturnal hypoventilation raising $CO_2$, which dilates cerebral vessels and increases ICP) or worse when lying flat, coughing, or bending forward.
* **Focal Neurologic Deficits**: Gradual, subacute onset of hemiparesis, sensory loss, aphasia, or visual field deficits, depending on tumor location.
* **New-Onset Seizures**: A classic presentation. **Any adult presenting with a first-ever seizure** must be evaluated for a brain tumor.
* **Raised ICP Signs**: Projectile vomiting (often without nausea) and progressive cognitive decline/lethargy.

## Physical Examination
* **Fundoscopy**: **Papilledema** (blurring of optic disc margins, loss of venous pulsations) indicating elevated ICP.
* **Neurological Deficits**: Hemiparesis, asymmetric reflexes, positive Babinski signs, or CN VI (Abducens) palsy (false localizing sign of high ICP).

---

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Brain Abscess]]**: Also presents with headache, focal signs, and a ring-enhancing lesion. Distinguished by: acute/subacute onset, **fever**, a source of infection (otitis, sinusitis), and **restricted diffusion in the center of the cavity on DWI MRI** (abscess pus restricts diffusion; necrotic tumor fluid does not).
* **Acute Ischemic Stroke / Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Presents with sudden-onset focal deficits (seconds to minutes) compared to the subacute progression of tumors (weeks to months).
* **Demyelinating Plaque (Tumefactive MS)**: Can mimic a tumor on MRI. Differentiated by CSF analysis (oligoclonal bands) and clinical relapses.

# Investigations

## Imaging
* **[[MRI Brain]] (with and without Gadolinium Contrast) (Gold Standard)**:
  * Highly sensitive for identifying tumor anatomy, vascularity, and surrounding edema.
  * *Vasogenic Edema*: Appears as high T2/FLAIR signal tracking along white matter pathways, sparing the gray cortex.

## Laboratory
* **Systemic Cancer Staging**: If metastases are suspected, order: CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis, Mammography, or PET scan to identify the primary tumor source.
* **CSF Cytology**: Ordered if leptomeningeal carcinomatosis is suspected. (Must rule out mass effect on MRI first before LP).

## Histopathology
* **Stereotactic Brain Biopsy or Surgical Resection**: Mandatory to obtain tissue for definitive histopathological and molecular diagnosis (e.g., IDH mutation, MGMT methylation status in gliomas).

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by characteristic contrast-enhanced MRI brain findings, with definitive diagnosis established by tissue biopsy showing histopathological and molecular tumor markers.

---

# Management

## Initial Management (Medical Optimization)
* **Reduction of Vasogenic Edema**:
  * **Dexamethasone**: The undisputed treatment of choice. Administer **$4 - 10$ mg IV or PO every 6 hours** (along with PPI prophylaxis to prevent steroid-induced ulcers). 
  * *Mechanism*: Stabilizes the leaky endothelial tight junctions of tumor capillaries, rapidly resolving vasogenic edema and decreasing mass effect.
* **Seizure Management**:
  * **Prophylactic Anticonvulsants are not recommended** for tumor patients who have never had a seizure.
  * If the patient has had a seizure, initiate non-enzyme-inducing AEDs (e.g., **Levetiracetam**) to avoid interfering with chemotherapy metabolism.

## Definitive Management
* **Neurosurgical Resection**: Maximal safe surgical resection to debulk the tumor, relieve mass effect, and obtain tissue.
* **Radiation Therapy**:
  * *Whole-brain radiation therapy (WBRT)*: Primarily for multiple brain metastases.
  * *Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS / Gamma Knife)*: Focused radiation for single or small numbers of metastases or benign meningiomas.
* **Chemotherapy**:
  * **Temozolomide** (an alkylating agent that crosses the BBB): Combined with radiation for Glioblastoma.
  * High-dose Methotrexate for Primary CNS Lymphoma.

---

# Complications
* **Brain Herniation**: Caused by uncontrolled mass effect and raised ICP.
* **Venous Thromboembolism (DVT / PE)**: Highly common in patients with glioblastoma due to tumor secretion of procoagulant factors (warrants LMWH prophylaxis).
* **Corticosteroid Side Effects**: Cushingoid features, hyperglycemia, opportunistic infections.

# Prognosis
* **Glioblastoma**: Extremely poor. Even with aggressive surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy, the median survival is only **$12 - 15$ months**.
* **Meningioma**: Excellent. Surgical resection is often curative.
* **Brain Metastases**: Depends on the control of the systemic primary cancer, ranging from months to years.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Dexamethasone in Lymphoma Trap**: If Primary CNS Lymphoma is suspected, **avoid administering Dexamethasone** prior to obtaining a brain biopsy. Corticosteroids are highly toxic to lymphoma cells and can cause the tumor to rapidly shrink and disappear on imaging ("ghost tumor"), leading to non-diagnostic biopsies.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Vasogenic vs. Cytotoxic Edema**: Vasogenic edema (caused by tumors/abscesses) affects only the white matter and responds dramatically to steroids. Cytotoxic edema (caused by ischemic stroke) affects both gray and white matter and does **not** respond to steroids.
