## type: disease

# Definition
Vitamin deficiency neuropathies are a group of neurological disorders caused by a lack of essential vitamins required for peripheral nerve myelin maintenance, axonal transport, and energy metabolism. The primary deficiencies involved are **Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)**, **Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)**, **Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)**, and **Vitamin E**.

# Primary Clinical Syndromes

| Vitamin | Primary Cause of Deficiency | Neurological Syndrome | Key Clinical Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B12** *(Cobalamin)* | Pernicious anemia (intrinsic factor deficiency), vegan diet, gastric bypass, metformin use, Crohn's disease | **Subacute Combined Degeneration (SCD)** & Polyneuropathy | Loss of vibration/proprioception, sensory ataxia (positive Romberg), spastic paraparesis, hyperreflexia + Babinski, megaloblastic anemia, glossitis. |
| **B1** *(Thiamine)* | Chronic alcohol abuse, severe malnutrition, hyperemesis gravidarum | **Dry Beriberi** & **Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome** | *Dry Beriberi*: Painful, burning sensory-motor axonal neuropathy. <br>*Wernicke's Triad*: Encephalopathy, oculomotor palsy/nystagmus, gait ataxia. <br>*Korsakoff*: Confabulation, anterograde amnesia. |
| **B6** *(Pyridoxine)* | **Isoniazid (INH)** therapy (without B6 replacement), malnutrition, penicillamine | Distal Sensory Polyneuropathy | Symmetrical paresthesias and numbness. *Note: Pyridoxine megadose toxicity ($>100-200$ mg/day) also causes a severe sensory ganglionopathy.* |
| **E** *(Tocopherol)* | Fat malabsorption (cystic fibrosis, cholestasis, abetalipoproteinemia) | Spinocerebellar Ataxia Mimic | Progressive limb ataxia, loss of proprioception/vibration, areflexia, and hemolytic anemia. (Mimics Friedreich's ataxia). |

# Pathophysiology
1. **Vitamin B12 (Subacute Combined Degeneration)**:
   * B12 is a cofactor for methionine synthase (converts homocysteine to methionine, essential for myelin basic protein synthesis) and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (converts MMA to succinyl-CoA).
   * Deficiency leads to accumulation of methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine, resulting in abnormal myelin synthesis.
   * Pathologically causes **demyelination and axonal loss in the posterior columns** (causing loss of vibration/proprioception) and the **lateral corticospinal tracts** (causing spasticity/weakness) of the spinal cord.
2. **Vitamin B1 (Thiamine / Beriberi)**:
   * Thiamine pyrophosphate is a rate-limiting cofactor for key enzymes in carbohydrate metabolism (pyruvate dehydrogenase, $\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase).
   * Deficiency compromises cellular ATP production, selectively damaging cells with high metabolic demand: peripheral axons (axonal transport failure) and brainstem/diencephalic nuclei (Wernicke's encephalopathy).
3. **Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine / INH-induced)**:
   * Isoniazid (INH) structurally binds to pyridoxine, forming a complex that is rapidly excreted in urine, depleting cofactors required for GABA and myelin synthesis.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Symmetric Paresthesias**: Tingling, numbness, and burning pain starting in the toes and spreading proximally.
* **Sensory Ataxia**: Clumsiness, difficulty walking in the dark, and frequent falls.
* **Cognitive / Mood Changes**: Delirium, irritability, memory loss, or confabulation (making up stories to fill memory gaps, classic in Korsakoff).

## Physical Examination
* **Subacute Combined Degeneration (B12 Deficiency - The Classic Dual Exam)**:
  * Presents with a confusing mixture of upper and lower motor neuron signs:
    * **UMN signs**: Spastic tone in legs, **bilateral positive Babinski signs**, and clonus.
    * **LMN signs**: **Absent ankle (Achilles) reflexes** (due to concurrent peripheral neuropathy).
    * **Sensory**: Marked loss of vibration and joint position sense (posterior columns). **Positive Romberg Sign** (unsteadiness when standing with feet together and eyes closed).
* **Wernicke's Encephalopathy (Emergency)**:
  * Global confusion, disorientation, nystagmus (horizontal or vertical), bilateral abducens (CN VI) palsies, and a wide-based gait ataxia.
* **Glossitis**: Smooth, shiny, "beefy red" tongue (atrophy of filiform papillae in B12 deficiency).

---

# Differential Diagnosis
* **Neurosyphilis (Tabes Dorsalis)**: Also presents with posterior column degeneration (sensory ataxia, positive Romberg) and shooting pains. Differentiated by: **Argyll Robertson pupils** (constrict to accommodation but not light), positive VDRL/FTA-ABS, and absence of corticospinal (spastic) signs.
* **Friedreich's Ataxia**: Early-onset hereditary spinocerebellar ataxia with cardiomyopathy and scoliosis.
* **[[Polyneuropathy]] (Diabetic / Toxic)**: Length-dependent sensory-motor neuropathy, but lacks spinal cord signs (no spasticity, no Babinski).

## Laboratory
* **Vitamin B12 Assay**:
  * *Low*: $<200$ pg/mL is diagnostic.
  * *Borderline*: $200 - 400$ pg/mL. **Order Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) and Homocysteine**. In B12 deficiency, **both MMA and Homocysteine are elevated**. (In folate deficiency, only Homocysteine is elevated; MMA is normal).
* **Complete Blood Count ([[CBC]])**:
  * **Macrocytic Anemia (MCV $>100$ fL)** with a peripheral blood smear showing **hypersegmented neutrophils** (PMNs with $\ge 6$ lobes, diagnostic of megaloblastic anemia).
* **Serum Thiamine (B1) and Pyridoxine (B6) levels**.
* **Anti-Intrinsic Factor (IF) and Anti-Parietal Cell Antibodies**: Diagnostic for **Pernicious Anemia** (autoimmune destruction of gastric parietal cells/IF).

## Imaging & Diagnostics
* **MRI Spine (Thoracic/Cervical)**:
  * In SCD, T2-weighted sequences show **symmetric hyperintensity in the posterior and lateral columns** of the spinal cord (classically presenting as an inverted "V" shape on axial view).
* **[[EMG/NCS]]**:
  * Confirms a symmetric, distal, sensory-motor axonal polyneuropathy.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by compatible clinical neurological findings (sensory ataxia, UMN signs, encephalopathy) accompanied by documented low vitamin levels or elevated metabolic intermediates (MMA, homocysteine) and pathognomonic MRI spinal changes.

---

# Management

## Vitamin B12 Deficiency / SCD
* **Intramuscular (IM) Cyanocobalamin / Hydroxocobalamin**:
  * **$1,000$ mcg IM daily for 1 week**, followed by $1,000$ mcg IM weekly for 1 month, and then **$1,000$ mcg IM monthly for life** (if Pernicious Anemia or irreversible malabsorption is present).
  * *Oral Therapy*: High-dose oral therapy ($1,000 - 2,000$ mcg daily) is effective once initial deficits are corrected, relying on passive diffusion.

## Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) / Wernicke's Encephalopathy
* **Emergency Protocol**:
  * **Thiamine 500 mg IV three times daily for 2 days**, followed by $250$ mg IV/IM daily for 3-5 days.
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Thiamine Before Glucose Rule**: In Wernicke's encephalopathy, **always administer Thiamine IV BEFORE or concurrently with intravenous glucose/Dextrose**. Giving glucose to a thiamine-deficient patient consumes the remaining thiamine pyrophosphate cofactors, precipitating acute, irreversible brainstem necrosis and death.

## Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine) / INH Prophylaxis
* **Isoniazid Therapy**: Always co-administer **Pyridoxine 25 - 50 mg PO daily** in patients taking Isoniazid for Tuberculosis to prevent neuropathy, especially in patients with diabetes, alcohol abuse, or pregnancy.

---

# Complications
* **Permanent Disability**: Spastic paraparesis, ataxia, and neuropathic pain if B12 treatment is delayed ($>6$ months).
* **Korsakoff Psychosis**: Permanent anterograde amnesia and confabulation.
* **High-Output Heart Failure**: Wet Beriberi complication.

# Prognosis
* Neurological recovery is inversely related to the duration and severity of the deficiency. Early B12 therapy leads to complete recovery; delayed treatment results in permanent spinal tract injury.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Babinski-Areflexia Paradox**: Subacute Combined Degeneration is one of the classic conditions that causes a **positive Babinski sign (UMN) in the presence of absent ankle reflexes (LMN)**. (Other causes include Friedreich's ataxia, tabes dorsalis, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Folate Trap**: Treating B12 deficiency with **Folic Acid alone** will correct the megaloblastic anemia (hematological cure) but will **accelerate the catastrophic, irreversible neurological damage** (Subacute Combined Degeneration). Always check B12 levels before prescribing folate.
