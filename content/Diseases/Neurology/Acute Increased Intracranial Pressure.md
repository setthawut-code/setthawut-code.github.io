# 🧠 Acute Increased Intracranial Pressure (ภาวะความดันในกะโหลกศีรษะสูงเฉียบพลัน)

> **Definition**: Acute Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP) is a life-threatening neurological emergency characterized by a sustained rise in pressure within the cranial vault to $> 20 - 22$ mmHg. This leads to compromised cerebral blood flow, brain tissue ischemia, and potentially fatal brain herniation.

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
The skull is a rigid, non-compliant container containing brain tissue ($~80\%$), blood ($~10\%$), and cerebrospinal fluid ($~10\%$) (Monro-Kellie doctrine). Any volume increase (tumor, hemorrhage, edema) raises ICP. High ICP decreases cerebral blood flow, causing diffuse brain ischemia. Left untreated, it leads to brain tissue shift through the tentorial notch or foramen magnum (**herniation**), compressing the brainstem and causing cardiorespiratory arrest and death.

---

## 📊 Epidemiology
* Frequently complicates severe traumatic brain injury (TBI), large hemispheric ischemic strokes (malignant MCA syndrome), and acute intracranial hemorrhages.
* A leading cause of preventable in-hospital mortality in patients with acute neurological insults.

---

## 🦠 Etiology & Pathophysiology

### Increased ICP Pathophysiology Tree
```
Increased ICP Pathophysiology
└── 1. Mass Lesions (Traumatic or Neoplastic)
    ├── Epidural Hematoma, Subdural Hematoma, Intracerebral Hemorrhage
    └── Primary or metastatic brain tumors, brain abscess
└── 2. Cerebral Edema (Cytotoxic or Vasogenic)
    ├── Large ischemic stroke (e.g., malignant MCA syndrome)
    ├── Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy post-cardiac arrest
    └── Meningitis / Encephalitis
└── 3. Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Flow Obstruction
    └── Acute obstructive hydrocephalus or meningeal malignancy
└── 4. Venous Outflow Obstruction
    └── Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST)
```

### Pathological Mechanisms
* **Monro-Kellie Doctrine**: The cranial vault is a rigid bony structure with a fixed total volume. An increase in the volume of any component (brain, blood, CSF, or mass) must be compensated by displacement of the others (primarily CSF and venous blood into the spinal canal). Once compensatory reserves are exhausted, ICP rises exponentially.
* **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP)**: The pressure gradient driving cerebral blood flow. As ICP rises, CPP falls:
  $$\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$$
  To maintain perfusion, the brain autoregulates vascular resistance. When autoregulation fails or ICP approaches MAP, severe global ischemia occurs.
* **Herniation Syndromes**: High focal pressure shifts brain tissue across rigid intracranial dural structures:
  * **Uncal (Transtentorial) Herniation**: Medial temporal lobe (uncus) herniates over the tentorial notch, compressing the ipsilateral oculomotor nerve (causing a dilated, fixed pupil) and the brainstem (causing contralateral hemiparesis).
  * **Tonsillar Herniation**: Cerebellar tonsils shift downward through the foramen magnum, compressing the medulla oblongata, leading to respiratory arrest and death.

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Altered consciousness occurs rapidly as ICP rises.
* **Intubate if GCS $\le 8$**. Use drugs that minimize spikes in blood pressure and ICP during intubation (e.g., Fentanyl and Propofol; avoid Ketamine if hemodynamics are stable).
* Prevent coughing or gagging during intubation (which spikes ICP) by ensuring adequate neuromuscular blockade.

### Breathing
* Look for breathing abnormalities. Cushing's reflex causes irregular breathing (Cheyne-Stokes or ataxic breathing).
* Maintain normal oxygenation ($SpO_2 > 94\%$).
* **Optimized Ventilation**: Hypercapnia ($PaCO_2 > 45$ mmHg) causes cerebral vasodilation, increasing intracranial blood volume and worsening ICP. Keep $PaCO_2$ strictly between **$35$ and $40$ mmHg**.

### Circulation
* **Assess for Cushing's Triad** (A late, physiological response to brainstem compression):
  1. **Hypertension** (with widened pulse pressure - the body attempts to maintain cerebral perfusion).
  2. **Bradycardia** (reflex response to hypertension).
  3. **Irregular respirations** (Cheyne-Stokes or ataxic breathing).
* **Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP) Target**:
  Maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) to target a CPP of **$60 - 70$ mmHg**. Do not aggressively lower blood pressure in a patient with high ICP, as this will cause cerebral infarction.

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations & Recognition

### Symptoms & Triad of High ICP
* **Generalized Headache**: Typically severe, worse in the morning (due to recumbent position rising venous pressure) or with coughing/straining (Valsalva).
* **Projectile Vomiting**: Often sudden, unrelated to food intake, and sometimes without preceding nausea.
* **Progressive Cognitive Decline**: Somnolence, confusion, irritability, progressing to stupor and [[Coma]].

### Physical Examination & Herniation Signs
* **Uncal Herniation Signs**:
  * **Ipsilateral Dilated, Fixed Pupil** ("blown pupil" - due to compression of the oculomotor nerve).
  * **Contralateral Hemiparesis** (due to compression of the ipsilateral cerebral peduncle).
* **Papilledema**: Swelling of the optic disc, loss of venous pulsations, and hemorrhages on fundoscopic exam. (May be absent in acute onset/trauma).
* **Cranial Nerve VI (Abducens) Palsy**: Causes horizontal diplopia and failure of lateral gaze (a false-localizing sign due to the long intracranial course of the nerve).
* **Decerebrate or Decorticate Posturing**: Reflects brainstem motor pathway compression.

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis
* **Hypertensive Encephalopathy**: Severe systemic hypertension with headache/visual changes; Head CT shows diffuse cerebral edema but no midline shift or localized mass.
* **Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (Pseudotumor Cerebri)**: Chronic elevated ICP, papilledema, normal CSF chemistry, and normal CT scan; commonly seen in young, obese women. Does not cause acute herniation.
* **Severe [[Meningitis]]**: Fever, neck stiffness, headache. Brain CT shows no mass effect; CSF confirms inflammation.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

### Laboratory
* **Routine Panels**: Electrolytes, glucose, coagulation profile.
* **Serum Osmolality**: Monitor baseline before starting osmotic therapy.

### Imaging
* **[[CT Brain]] (without contrast)**: The primary emergency imaging choice. Look for:
  1. **Midline Shift**: Lateral displacement of septum pellucidum.
  2. **Effacement of Sulci**: Loss of cortical gyri definitions.
  3. **Ventricle Compression**: Slit-like lateral ventricles.
  4. **Basal Cistern Compression**: Loss of CSF spaces around the midbrain (predicts herniation).

### Special Tests
* **ICP Monitoring**: Indicated in patients with severe TBI ($GCS \le 8$) and an abnormal CT scan.
  * *Intraventricular Catheter (EVD)*: Gold standard; allows both pressure measurement and drainage of CSF.
  * *Parenchymal bolt*: Measures pressure but does not allow drainage.
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **LP Contraindication**: Never perform a lumbar puncture if increased ICP with a mass lesion is suspected. The sudden lower spinal pressure can precipitate immediate tonsillar herniation.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by directly measured ICP $> 22$ mmHg via intracranial monitor, or suggested by clinical features of Cushing's triad/blown pupil alongside diagnostic CT scan findings of mass effect.

---

## 🏥 Management

### Immediate Management: ICP Reduction Protocol
Stabilize and implement emergency osmotherapy:

```
Acute ICP Reduction Protocol
├── 1. Optimize Positioning (Elevate head of bed to 30 degrees; keep head midline)
│   └── Goal: Enhances jugular venous drainage. Avoid tight cervical collars.
├── 2. Osmotherapy (Pillar of medical ICP reduction)
│   ├── Option A: MANNITOL (20% solution): 0.5 - 1.0 g/kg IV bolus over 15 - 20 minutes
│   │   └── Note: Acts as osmotic diuretic. Avoid if patient is hypotensive or renal failure
│   └── Option B: HYPERTONIC SALINE (3% NaCl): 250 mL IV bolus over 15 - 20 minutes
│       └── Note: Preferred if patient is hypotensive (increases intravascular volume)
├── 3. Temporary Hyperventilation (Rescue therapy only)
│   ├── Action: Hyperventilate to target PaCO2 of 30 - 35 mmHg
│   └── Mechanism: Hypocapnia causes vasoconstriction, reducing intracranial blood volume
│   └── Warning: Use for < 2 hours; prolonged hyperventilation causes cerebral ischemia
├── 4. Sedation & Analgesia (Propofol or Fentanyl; reduces cerebral metabolic demand)
└── 5. Glucocorticoids (For Vasogenic Edema around tumors/abscesses)
    └── DEXAMETHASONE: 10 mg IV bolus, then 4 mg IV every 6 hours (do not use in trauma/stroke)
```

### Definitive Management
* **External Ventricular Drain (EVD)**: Direct drainage of CSF to lower ICP.
* **Surgical Evacuation**: Craniotomy for evacuation of epidural, subdural, or intraparenchymal hematomas.
* **Decompressive Craniectomy**: Removal of a large bone flap to allow the swollen brain to expand.

---

## 📊 Monitoring
* **GCS & Pupillary Checks**: Assess every 15-30 minutes during the acute phase.
* **Arterial Line Blood Pressure Monitoring**.
* **Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Monitoring**: (In ICU) Target ICP **$< 20 - 22$ mmHg** and CPP **$60 - 70$ mmHg**.
* **Serum Osmolality & Electrolytes**: Monitor sodium closely during osmotherapy (keep serum osmolality $< 320$ mOsm/kg to prevent acute kidney injury).

---

## 🫁 Complications & Prognosis
* **Brain Herniation**: Irreversible brainstem compression leading to death.
* **Cerebral Infarction**: Widespread ischemic damage due to CPP collapse.
* **Diabetes Insipidus (DI) or SIADH**: Due to hypothalamic-pituitary damage.
* **Prognosis**: Dependent on the etiology. Patients presenting with signs of brainstem herniation (Cushing's triad, fixed dilated pupils) have a poor prognosis unless decompressed immediately.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Avoid Hypotension**: Systolic BP must be maintained $> 100 - 110$ mmHg. A single episode of hypotension in a patient with elevated ICP dramatically doubles mortality by dropping CPP and causing secondary brain ischemia.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Mannitol vs. Hypertonic Saline in Trauma**: If a patient with a traumatic brain injury is hypotensive (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg) due to bleeding, **do not use Mannitol**. Mannitol is a potent osmotic diuretic that will worsen hypotension and cerebral ischemia. Use **3% Hypertonic Saline**, which reduces ICP while expanding intravascular volume.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The ICP Hyperventilation Trap**: Hyperventilation is a powerful tool to lower ICP, but it is a double-edged sword. Widespread cerebral vasoconstriction from severe hypocapnia ($PaCO_2 < 30$ mmHg) can cause severe cerebral ischemia, converting penumbra into completed stroke. Use it only as a brief rescue bridge while preparing for surgery ($< 2$ hours).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **The Mannitol Osmolar Gap Trap**: When using repeated doses of Mannitol, check the **osmolar gap** (measured osmolality - calculated osmolality). If the gap is $> 20$ mOsm, Mannitol is accumulating in the brain parenchyma, which can cause rebound cerebral edema and acute kidney injury. Hold Mannitol.
