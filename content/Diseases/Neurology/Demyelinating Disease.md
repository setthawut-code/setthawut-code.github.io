## type: disease

# Definition
Demyelinating diseases of the central nervous system (CNS) are immune-mediated disorders characterized by the selective destruction of myelin sheaths surrounding axons, with relative preservation of the axons themselves. The primary clinical entities include **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)**, **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)**, **Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein Antibody-Associated Disease (MOGAD)**, and **Acute Disseminated Encephalomyelitis (ADEM)**.

# Epidemiology
* **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)**: The most common demyelinating disease. Affects primarily young adults ($20-40$ years) with a strong female predominance ($3:1$). Incidence increases with geographic latitude (higher in Northern European populations).
* **NMOSD**: More common in non-Caucasian populations (Asians, Africans) and exhibits an extreme female predominance ($9:1$). 
* **ADEM**: Primarily pediatric, monophasic, and often post-infectious or post-vaccinal.

# Etiology & Pathophysiology
The underlying mechanism is autoimmune, targeting different components of CNS myelin:

```
                            │  DEMYELINATING DISEASE │

             │                                                     │
   T-Cell Mediated:                                      Autoantibody (AQP4-IgG)
   CD4+/CD8+ T-cells cross                               targets Aquaporin-4 water
   BBB -> myelin basic protein                           channels on astrocytic
   attack -> focal plaques                               foot processes -> Complement
             │                                           astrocyte destruction
             ▼                                                     │
   CSF Oligoclonal Bands (+)                                       ▼
                                                         Longitudinal Spinal Lesions
```

1. **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)**:
   * *Pathogenesis*: Cell-mediated autoimmune response. Autoreactive CD4+ and CD8+ T-lymphocytes cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and recognize myelin antigens (e.g., myelin basic protein). This initiates an inflammatory cascade involving microglial activation, macrophage-mediated myelin stripping, and focal axonal transection.
   * *Course*: Characterized by "dissemination in space" (lesions in different CNS locations) and "dissemination in time" (lesions occurring at different times). **Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS)** is the most common phenotype ($85\%$).
2. **Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder (NMOSD)**:
   * *Pathogenesis*: Humoral-mediated autoimmune channelopathy. Pathogenic IgG autoantibodies target **Aquaporin-4 (AQP4)** water channels, which are highly concentrated on astrocyte foot processes at the BBB.
   * *Consequence*: Antibody binding activates the classical complement pathway, leading to astrocyte destruction, secondary demyelination, and severe axonal necrosis in the optic nerves and spinal cord.
3. **MOGAD**:
   * *Pathogenesis*: Autoantibodies target **Myelin Oligodendrocyte Glycoprotein (MOG)** on the outermost lamellae of myelin sheaths.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
Demyelinating lesions can occur anywhere in the CNS, presenting with characteristic syndromes:
* **Optic Neuritis (ON)**:
  * Acute, unilateral, painful vision loss. Pain is classically worsened by eye movements.
  * Associated with a central scotoma (blind spot in center of vision) and color vision loss (dyschromatopsia).
* **Acute [[Myelitis]]**:
  * Weakness, numbness, and sensory loss below the level of the lesion.
  * In MS, myelitis is typically mild and partial. 
  * In NMOSD, myelitis is characteristically severe, presenting as **Longitudinally Extensive Transverse Myelitis (LETM)** causing paraplegia/tetraplegia and neurogenic bladder.
* **Brainstem and Cerebellar Syndromes**:
  * Diplopia, vertigo, ataxia, dysarthria, and tremor.
  * **Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO)**: Caused by a lesion in the **medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF)**. On lateral gaze, the adducting eye fails to cross the midline (ipsilateral to the lesion) while the abducting eye exhibits horizontal nystagmus.
* **Sensory Phenomena**:
  * **Lhermitte's Sign**: An electric shock-like sensation radiating down the spine or limbs upon flexion of the neck.
  * **Uhthoff's Phenomenon**: Transient worsening of neurological symptoms (e.g., visual blurriness) when body temperature is elevated (due to exercise, hot baths, or fever), caused by conduction block in demyelinated axons.

## Physical Examination
* **Visual Exam**: Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (**RAPD / Marcus Gunn pupil**) in the affected eye. Optic disc may be swollen (papillitis) or normal (retrobulbar optic neuritis).
* **Motor & Reflexes**: Upper motor neuron signs below the level of spinal/brain lesions (spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski sign).
* **Sensory Level**: Identifiable sensory boundary on the trunk.

# Differential Diagnosis
* **CNS Vasculitis**: Presents with multifocal strokes, headaches, and systemic symptoms; MRI shows ischemic changes rather than demyelinating plaques.
* **Neurosarcoidosis**: Presents with cranial neuropathies, meningeal enhancement, and elevated serum/CSF ACE levels.
* **Subacute Combined Degeneration ([[Vitamin Deficiency Neuropathy]] / B12)**: Symmetric paresthesias and proprioceptive loss without optic neuritis; diagnosed via low B12 and elevated MMA.

---

# Investigations

## Imaging
* **[[MRI Brain]] and Spine (with and without Gadolinium) (Gold Standard)**:
  * **MS Features**: Multiple well-demarcated, ovoid T2-hyperintense lesions located in: **periventricular** white matter, **juxtacortical** regions, **infratentorial** regions (brainstem/cerebellum), or the **spinal cord**.
    * **Dawson's Fingers**: Periventricular lesions oriented perpendicular to the lateral ventricles, reflecting inflammation along the medullary veins.
    * **Active Lesions**: Show ring or nodular enhancement with gadolinium contrast (indicating active BBB breakdown).
  * **NMOSD Features**: Spinal MRI shows **Longitudinally Extensive Transverse Myelitis (LETM)**—a continuous T2-hyperintense spinal cord lesion spanning **$\ge 3$ vertebral segments**. Brain MRI is often normal or shows lesions in AQP4-rich areas (hypothalamus, area postrema).

## Laboratory
* **AQP4-IgG (NMO-IgG) Antibody**: Highly specific ($>99\%$) for NMOSD (tested via cell-based assay).
* **MOG-IgG Antibody**: Diagnoses MOGAD.
* **Cerebrospinal Fluid Analysis ([[CSF Analysis]] / [[Lumbar Puncture]])**:
  * **MS**: CSF shows **Oligoclonal Bands (OCBs)** (present in $>95\%$ of MS patients; represents intrathecal IgG synthesis) and an elevated IgG Index. Pleocytosis is mild ($WBC < 50/\mu$L, mononuclear).
  * **NMOSD**: OCBs are typically **absent**. CSF may show a marked pleocytosis ($WBC > 50/\mu$L), sometimes with neutrophils or eosinophils.

## Special Tests
* **Visual Evoked Potentials (VEP)**: Demonstrates delayed latency of the P100 wave, indicating subclinical demyelination along the optic nerve pathway.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
* **Multiple Sclerosis**: Diagnosed using the **McDonald Criteria (2017)**, requiring evidence of dissemination in space (DIS) and dissemination in time (DIT) on MRI or clinical attacks, or OCBs in CSF replacing DIT.
* **NMOSD**: Requires AQP4-IgG positivity plus at least 1 core clinical characteristic (optic neuritis, LETM, or area postrema syndrome [intractable hiccups/nausea]).

---

# Management

## Initial Management (Acute Relapses)
* **High-Dose Corticosteroids**:
  * **Methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily for 3 to 5 consecutive days**. (Speeds up recovery from the relapse, though does not alter long-term disability).
* **Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis)**:
  * Indicated for severe, disabling relapses (e.g., severe optic neuritis causing blindness, transverse myelitis) that are **refractory to high-dose corticosteroids**. (Highly effective if started early, particularly in NMOSD).

## Definitive Management (Disease-Modifying Therapies - DMTs)

* **Multiple Sclerosis**:
  * Target: Reduce relapses and prevent accumulation of brain lesions.
  * *Moderate-Efficacy (Injectable/Oral)*: Interferon-beta, Glatiramer acetate, Teriflunomide, Dimethyl fumarate.
  * *High-Efficacy (Monoclonal Antibodies/Pills)*:
    * **Ocrelizumab**: Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (depletes B-cells). Approved for both relapsing MS and primary progressive MS.
    * **Natalizumab**: Anti-$\alpha_4$-integrin antibody (prevents lymphocytes from crossing the BBB).
      * > [!IMPORTANT]
        > **JCV Screening Rule**: Patients on Natalizumab must be regularly screened for **John Cunningham Virus (JCV) antibodies**. If JCV-positive, they are at high risk for developing **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)**, a fatal demyelinating brain infection.
* **NMOSD**:
  * Standard MS therapies (like interferon-beta and fingolimod) can **worsen** NMOSD relapses.
  * First-line maintenance therapies include: **Rituximab** (B-cell depletion), **Eculizumab** (C5 complement inhibitor), or **Satralizumab** (IL-6 receptor blocker).

# Complications
* **Mobility Impairment**: Severe spastic paraparesis requiring wheelchair dependency.
* **Neurogenic Bladder / Bowel Dysfunction**: Recurrent UTIs and constipation.
* **Depression**: Highly prevalent in MS patients; requires screening and treatment.
* **PML (Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy)**: Fatal opportunistic brain infection associated with natalizumab therapy.

# Prognosis
* MS has a highly variable course. Many patients remain minimally disabled for decades, while others progress to severe disability.
* NMOSD has a worse prognosis than MS; relapses are typically severe and do not recover well, leading to a stepwise accumulation of permanent blindness and paraplegia.

# Clinical Pearls
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **JCV testing on Natalizumab**: Before starting Natalizumab, check the patient's **JCV index**. If they are JCV antibody-positive, do not keep them on Natalizumab for more than 2 years due to the risk of PML.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Lhermitte's Sign Mechanism**: Lhermitte's sign is not specific to MS but is a general marker of **posterior column pathology** in the cervical spinal cord. Neck flexion stretches the demyelinated posterior columns, causing hyper-irritability and firing, which is perceived as an electric shock.
