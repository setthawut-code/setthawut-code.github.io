## type: disease

# Definition
Myopathy is a primary disease of skeletal muscle tissue characterized by structural or functional impairment of muscle fibers, resulting in muscle weakness, wasting, or pain. It is distinguished from neuropathic disorders by the classic pattern of proximal, symmetric weakness with the **sparing of sensory function** and preservation of deep tendon reflexes.

# Epidemiology
* **Prevalence**: Varies widely by etiology. Statin-induced myalgia/myopathy is highly common, affecting up to $5 - 10\%$ of patients on statin therapy. Inflammatory myopathies (e.g., polymyositis, dermatomyositis) are rare, with an annual incidence of 2 to 10 per million.
* **Demographics**: Inflammatory myopathies show a female predominance (2:1) and bimodal age distribution (childhood and ages 40-60).

# Etiology & Classification
Myopathies are classified etiologically into hereditary and acquired forms:

```
                                  ┌────────────────────────┐
                                  │      MYOPATHY TYPES    │
                                  └───────────┬────────────┘
                                              │
                    ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
                    ▼                                                   ▼
      ┌──────────────────────────┐                        ┌──────────────────────────┐
      │         ACQUIRED         │                        │        HEREDITARY        │
      └────────────┬─────────────┘                        └────────────┬─────────────┘
                   │                                                   │
                   ├─ Inflammatory (Polymyositis, DM, IBM)             ├─ Muscular Dystrophies (Duchenne, Becker)
                   ├─ Toxic/Drug (Statins, Steroids, Alcohol)          ├─ Metabolic (McArdle, Pompe)
                   ├─ Endocrine (Hypothyroidism, Cushing's)            └─ Mitochondrial Myopathies
                   └─ Critical Illness Myopathy (CIM)
```

1. **Inflammatory Myopathies (Autoimmune)**:
   * **Polymyositis (PM)** and **Dermatomyositis (DM)**.
   * **Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM)**: A slowly progressive myopathy affecting older adults ($>50$ years).
2. **Toxic & Drug-Induced**:
   * **Statin-Induced Myopathy**: Ranges from benign myalgias to life-threatening rhabdomyolysis.
   * **Glucocorticoid-Induced Myopathy**: Characterized by type II muscle fiber atrophy, often presenting as chronic weakness without muscle pain.
   * **Alcoholic Myopathy** (acute or chronic), Colchicine, Zidovudine.
3. **Endocrine & Metabolic**:
   * **Hypothyroid Myopathy**: Presents with proximal weakness, fatigue, muscle cramps, and pseudohypertrophy.
   * **Thyrotoxic Myopathy** and **Cushing's Myopathy** (due to excess cortisol).
4. **Hereditary Myopathies**:
   * Muscular Dystrophies (e.g., Duchenne, Becker, Facioscapulohumeral [FSHD], Myotonic Dystrophy).
   * Metabolic/Mitochondrial myopathies (e.g., McArdle disease, Pompe disease).

# Pathophysiology
* **Muscle Fiber Injury**: The core pathology involves direct dysfunction of the sarcolemma, myofibrils, or mitochondria, impairing muscle contraction.
* **Polymyositis**: CD8+ T-cell-mediated autoimmune process. Cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells invade non-necrotic muscle fibers expressing MHC class I antigens, causing fiber necrosis.
* **Dermatomyositis**: Humoral-mediated microangiopathy. Complement activation ($C_{5b-9}$ membrane attack complex) targets endothelial cells of endomysial capillaries, causing ischemia, capillary loss, and secondary **perifascicular atrophy**.
* **Glucocorticoid Myopathy**: Glucocorticoids inhibit protein synthesis (via downregulating mTOR pathway) and promote protein degradation (via ubiquitin-proteasome pathway), selectively targeting fast-twitch glycolytic type IIb fibers.
* **Statin-Induced**: Statins block HMG-CoA reductase, decreasing cholesterol and coenzyme Q10 (ubiquinone) synthesis, leading to mitochondrial membrane instability and dysfunction.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Proximal, Symmetric Weakness**: The hallmark symptom. Patients report difficulty rising from a low chair, climbing stairs, combing their hair, or reaching for overhead shelves.
* **Sensation is Intact**: **No numbness, tingling, or paresthesias** (crucial differentiator from neuropathy).
* **Myalgia**: Muscle pain and tenderness (present in acute statin myopathy, polymyositis, and hypothyroid myopathy; classically **absent** in steroid-induced myopathy).
* **Bulbar/Respiratory Symptoms**: Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) and dyspnea (diaphragmatic weakness).
* **IBM Exception**: Presents as **asymmetric, distal weakness** (specifically targeting finger flexors [weak grip] and quadriceps).

## Physical Examination
* **Motor Exam**: Symmetric weakness of proximal girdle muscles (shoulder and pelvic girdle). Distal strength is preserved (except in IBM or late-stage dystrophies).
* **Reflexes**: **Normal (preserved)** deep tendon reflexes (e.g., 2+ knee and ankle jerks). Reflexes are only lost in extremely advanced, end-stage muscle atrophy.
  * *Hypothyroid Myopathy*: Shows **delayed relaxation phase of deep tendon reflexes** (hung-up reflexes).
* **Sensory Exam**: Completely normal sensory modalities (light touch, pinprick, vibration, proprioception).
* **Cutaneous Findings (Pathognomonic for Dermatomyositis)**:
  * **Gottron's Papules**: Violaceous, flat-toop papules over the dorsal aspect of the interphalangeal and metacarpophalangeal joints.
  * **Heliotrope Rash**: Violaceous discoloration of the upper eyelids, often accompanied by periorbital edema.
  * **Shawl Sign** & **V-Sign**: Erythema over the upper back/shoulders (Shawl) or anterior neck/chest (V-sign).

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Polyneuropathy]]**: Distal, symmetric stocking-glove sensory loss, early loss of reflexes (especially Achilles), and distal weakness (foot drop).
* **[[Myasthenia Gravis]]**: Fluctuating, fatiguable weakness. Characterized by ocular symptoms (ptosis, diplopia) and bulbar symptoms, which are rare or late in myopathies (except ocular myopathies).
* **Motor Neuron Disease (ALS)**: Asymmetric weakness with **both UMN signs** (spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski) and **LMN signs** (severe atrophy, fasciculations) without sensory loss.

# Investigations

## Laboratory
* **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: The most sensitive indicator of muscle damage.
  * *Markedly Elevated ($10 - 100\times$ Normal)*: Polymyositis, Dermatomyositis, rhabdomyolysis, hypothyroid myopathy.
  * *Normal / Mildly Elevated ($<2\times$ Normal)*: Glucocorticoid myopathy, thyrotoxic myopathy.
* **Transaminases (AST & ALT)**:
  > [!WARNING]
    > **Transaminase Pitfall**: AST and ALT are highly concentrated in skeletal muscle. Active myopathy or rhabdomyolysis will cause elevated AST/ALT, which is frequently misdiagnosed as acute liver injury. Check CK to differentiate.
* **Autoantibodies** (For PM/DM):
  * **ANA** (positive in $80\%$).
  * **Myositis-Specific Antibodies (MSAs)**: **Anti-Jo-1** (associated with Antisynthetase Syndrome: interstitial lung disease, Raynaud's, mechanic's hands), **Anti-Mi-2** (classic DM, good prognosis), **Anti-SRP** (severe necrotizing myopathy).
* **TSH / Free T4**: Rule out hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism.

## Neurophysiology & Histopathology
* **[[EMG/NCS]]**:
  * Nerve conduction studies (NCS) are typically **normal** (normal velocities and amplitudes).
  * Needle EMG shows the **Myopathic Triad**:
    1. Short-duration, low-amplitude, polyphasic Motor Unit Action Potentials (MUAPs) on voluntary contraction.
    2. Increased insertional activity with fibrillations and positive sharp waves (denervation-like signals).
    3. Early recruitment of motor units.
* **Muscle Biopsy (Gold Standard)**:
  * *Polymyositis*: Endomysial inflammatory infiltrates (predominantly CD8+ T-cells) invading and surrounding healthy, non-necrotic muscle fibers.
  * *Dermatomyositis*: Perivascular and perimysial inflammatory infiltrates (predominantly B-cells and CD4+ T-cells) accompanied by **perifascicular atrophy** (atrophy of muscle fibers at the periphery of the fascicle).
  * *Steroid-Induced*: Type II muscle fiber atrophy without inflammation.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Diagnosis of inflammatory myopathies is based on the Bohan and Peter criteria (requiring proximal symmetric weakness, elevated muscle enzymes, myopathic EMG, and diagnostic biopsy findings).

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* **Discontinue Offending Toxins**: Stop statins immediately (if statin-induced myopathy); pain and CK usually normalize within weeks. Taper corticosteroids (if steroid-induced myopathy is suspected).
* **Treat Systemic Endocrine Causes**: Correct thyroid dysfunction (levothyroxine replacement for hypothyroid myopathy).

## Definitive Management (Polymyositis & Dermatomyositis)
* **Immunosuppression**:
  * **First-line**: High-dose **oral Prednisone** ($1$ mg/kg/day, typically $60 - 80$ mg/day), maintained for 4-8 weeks until strength improves and CK levels decrease, followed by a slow, gradual taper over 6-12 months.
  * **Steroid-Sparing Agents** (Started early to minimize steroid toxicities): **Methotrexate** or **Azathioprine**. **Mycophenolate Mofetil** is preferred if interstitial lung disease is present.
  * **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: Reserved for severe, dysphagia-threatening, or corticosteroid-refractory cases.
* **Malignancy Screening (Crucial for Dermatomyositis)**:
  * Up to $20 - 30\%$ of adult patients with Dermatomyositis have an **associated underlying malignancy** (ovarian, breast, lung, GI cancers).
  * **Mandatory**: Age-appropriate cancer screening (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, mammogram, colonoscopy, pelvic ultrasound) at diagnosis and annually for 3 years.
* **Physical Therapy**:
  * Encourage low-intensity, non-fatiguing aerobic and resistive exercise. Avoid eccentric exercises during periods of acute inflammation (elevated CK).

# Complications
* **Respiratory Failure**: Weakness of respiratory muscles requires ventilatory support.
* **Aspiration Pneumonia**: Due to pharyngeal/cricopharyngeal weakness (dysphagia).
* **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**: Associated with anti-Jo-1 antibodies.
* **Cardiac Arrhythmias & Myocarditis**.

# Prognosis
* Acquired toxic/endocrine myopathies have an excellent prognosis with complete recovery upon addressing the cause.
* PM and DM have good response rates to immunosuppressants, but relapses are common. IBM is slowly progressive and refractory to standard immunosuppressive therapies.

# Clinical Pearls
> [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Statin Myalgia vs. Myopathy**: Myalgia refers to muscle ache/weakness *without* CK elevation. Myopathy refers to muscle symptoms *with* CK elevation ($>10\times$ upper limit of normal). Rhabdomyolysis is severe myopathy with CK $>10,000$ U/L and myoglobinuria (brown urine) predisposing to acute renal failure.

> [!TIP]
  > **The Reflex Rule**: In a patient presenting with severe proximal leg weakness (e.g., unable to stand from a chair), the presence of **normal knee and ankle reflexes** strongly points to a **myopathy**. The loss of these reflexes suggests a neuropathic cause (like GBS or chronic radiculopathy).
