# 🧫 Sexually Transmitted Infection (โรคติดต่อทางเพศสัมพันธ์)

> **Definition**: A group of infections transmitted primarily through sexual contact, categorized by clinical syndromes: urethritis/cervicitis, genital ulcer disease, and vaginitis/vaginosis.

---

## 🦠 1. Urethritis and Cervicitis

Inflammation of the urethra or cervix, presenting as discharge and dysuria.

### Pathogens & Presentations
* **Gonococcal Urethritis/Cervicitis** (*Neisseria gonorrhoeae*):
  * **Microbiology**: Gram-negative intracellular diplococci.
  * **Presentation**: Acute onset, copious **purulent (thick, yellow-green) discharge**, dysuria.
* **Non-gonococcal Urethritis/Cervicitis** (primarily ***Chlamydia trachomatis*** serovars D-K):
  * **Microbiology**: Obligate intracellular bacterium (cannot be seen on Gram stain; lacks peptidoglycan wall).
  * **Presentation**: Subacute onset, scanty **mucoid/watery (clear) discharge**, dysuria, or asymptomatic ($70\%$ of females).

### Investigations
* **Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT)**: Gold standard. High sensitivity on first-catch urine (males) or vaginal/cervical swabs (females).
* **Gram Stain**: Useful in males. Shows Gram-negative diplococci inside polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs).

### Management
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Dual Empiric Therapy**: Always co-treat for both Gonorrhea and Chlamydia, as co-infection is common.
* **Regimen**:
  * **For Gonorrhea**: **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM** single dose.
  * **For Chlamydia**: **Doxycycline 100 mg PO bid for 7 days** (or Azithromycin 1 g PO single dose if pregnant/non-compliant).

## 🦠 2. Genital Ulcer Diseases (GUD)

Differential diagnosis based on ulcer characteristics and lymphadenopathy:

| Disease | Pathogen | Ulcer Characteristics | Inguinal Lymph Nodes | Diagnosis | First-line Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Syphilis** (Primary) | *Treponema pallidum* | • **Painless**, single<br>• Clean base, indurated | • Painless, bilateral, rubbery | • Darkfield microscopy<br>• Serology (VDRL/RPR $\rightarrow$ TPPA/FTA-ABS) | • **Benzathine Penicillin G** 2.4 million units IM single dose |
| **Genital Herpes** | HSV-1, HSV-2 | • **Painful**, multiple<br>• Vesicles on erythematous base $\rightarrow$ shallow ulcers | • Painful, bilateral | • PCR (most sensitive)<br>• Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells) | • **Acyclovir** 400 mg PO tid for 7–10 days |
| **Chancroid** | *Haemophilus ducreyi* | • **Painful**, single/multiple<br>• Purulent base, ragged edges | • Painful, unilateral **buboes** (suppurative) | • Gram stain (school of fish)<br>• Culture | • **Azithromycin** 1 g PO single dose OR **Ceftriaxone** 250 mg IM single dose |
| **Lymphogranuloma Venereum (LGV)** | *C. trachomatis* L1–L3 | • Painless, transient, small papule/ulcer (often healed) | • Painful, unilateral, matted (**Groove Sign** over inguinal ligament) | • NAAT | • **Doxycycline** 100 mg PO bid for 21 days |
| **Granuloma Inguinale** (Donovanosis) | *Klebsiella granulomatis* | • Painless, progressive<br>• Beefy-red, highly vascular | • Pseudobuboes (granulation tissue, no real nodes) | • Tissue smear (shows safety-pin **Donovan Bodies** inside macrophages) | • **Azithromycin** 1 g PO weekly for $\ge 3$ weeks |

### Syphilis Stages & Management Notes
* **Primary**: Painless chancre.
* **Secondary**: Diffuse maculopapular rash (classically involving **palms and soles**), **condyloma lata** (highly infectious gray-white plaques in intertriginous areas), generalized lymphadenopathy, fever.
* **Tertiary**: **Gummas** (necrotic granulomas), cardiovascular syphilis (aortitis/aneurysm), neurosyphilis (**Tabes dorsalis**: degeneration of posterior columns $\rightarrow$ sensory ataxia; **Argyll Robertson pupil**: accommodates but does not react to light).
* **Treatment**:
  * Early (Primary/Secondary/Early Latent $<1$ year): **Benzathine Penicillin G** 2.4 MU IM $\times 1$ dose.
  * Late Latent ($>1$ year or unknown): **Benzathine Penicillin G** 2.4 MU IM weekly $\times 3$ doses.
  * Neurosyphilis: **Aqueous Crystalline Penicillin G** 18–24 MU/day IV (administered as 3-4 MU q4h) for **10–14 days**.

## 🦠 3. Vaginitis and Vaginosis

Common causes of vaginal discharge:

| Parameter | Bacterial Vaginosis (BV) | Trichomoniasis | Vulvovaginal Candidiasis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathogen** | Overgrowth of anaerobes (*Gardnerella vaginalis*) | *Trichomonas vaginalis* (flagellated protozoan) | *Candida albicans* (fungus) |
| **Discharge** | Thin, off-white, homogenous | **Frothy, yellow-green**, foul-smelling | **Thick, white, clumpy** ("cottage cheese") |
| **Vaginal pH** | $> 4.5$ | $> 4.5$ | Normal ($< 4.5$) |
| **Whiff Test** (KOH) | Positive (fishy odor) | Positive / Negative | Negative |
| **Microscopy** | **Clue cells** (epithelial cells covered in bacteria; fuzzy borders) | **Motile trichomonads** with flagella; increased PMNs | **Pseudohyphae** and budding yeast |
| **Cervix / Wall** | Normal | **Strawberry Cervix** (petechiae / colpitis macularis) | Erythematous, pruritic walls |
| **Treatment** | **Metronidazole** 500 mg PO bid for 7 days | **Metronidazole** 2 g PO single dose OR 500 mg bid for 7 days. **Treat partner!** | **Fluconazole** 150 mg PO single dose |

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!WARNING]
  > **Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction**: Acute febrile reaction (fever, chills, headache, myalgia, worsening rash) occurring within 24 hours of initiating penicillin therapy for syphilis. Caused by rapid lysis of spirochetes releasing endotoxin-like lipoproteins. **Self-limiting**. Treat supportively with NSAIDs/Acetaminophen. *Do not confuse with penicillin allergy.*
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Partner Treatment**: In Gonorrhea, Chlamydia, and Trichomoniasis, **partners must be treated concurrently** to prevent re-infection. Abstain from intercourse until both partners complete therapy (7 days).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Disulfiram-like Reaction**: Warn patients taking **Metronidazole** to strictly avoid alcohol during therapy and for 7 days after, due to inhibition of acetaldehyde dehydrogenase (causes severe nausea, vomiting, flushing, tachycardia).
