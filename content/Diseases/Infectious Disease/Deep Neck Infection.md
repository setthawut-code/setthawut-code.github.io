# 🦠 Deep Neck Space Infections (การติดเชื้อในช่องลึกบริเวณคอ)

> **Definition**: A group of acute, rapidly progressive bacterial infections involving the deep fascial spaces of the head and neck. They carry an extremely high risk of sudden, fatal airway compromise and descent into the mediastinum.

---

## 📊 Key Deep Space Infections

### 1. Ludwig's Angina
* **Definition**: A rapidly spreading, non-gangrenous **bilateral cellulitis** involving the submandibular, sublingual, and submental spaces.
* **Etiology**: Most commonly **odontogenic** ($>80\%$ of cases), typically arising from infections of the **2nd or 3rd mandibular molars** (whose roots extend below the mylohyoid muscle).
* **Clinical Presentation**:
  * **"Brawny" (woody/hard), non-fluctuant swelling** of the submandibular region.
  * **Tongue elevation and displacement** superiorly and posteriorly, causing oral airway occlusion.
  * Drooling, trismus (difficulty opening mouth), dysphagia, muffled ("hot potato") voice, and inspiratory stridor.

### 2. Retropharyngeal Space Infection
* **Definition**: Infection located in the space between the posterior pharyngeal wall and the prevertebral fascia.
* **Etiology**: More common in children (due to suppuration of retropharyngeal lymph nodes). In adults, it is usually triggered by **foreign body trauma** (e.g., swallowed fish bone or chicken bone).
* **Clinical Presentation**: Severe neck pain, odynophagia, drooling, muffled voice, and **hyperextension of the neck** (to maximize airway diameter).
* **Diagnosis**: Lateral neck X-ray shows **widening of the prevertebral soft tissue** ($>7\text{ mm}$ at C2, or $>14\text{ mm}$ at C6 in children/ $>22\text{ mm}$ in adults).

### 3. Parapharyngeal Space Infection
* **Definition**: Infection located in the lateral pharyngeal space.
* **Etiology**: Complication of tonsillitis, pharyngitis, or odontogenic infection.
* **Clinical Presentation**: **Severe trismus** (due to irritation of the medial pterygoid muscle), medial deviation of the lateral pharyngeal wall and tonsil, and swelling at the angle of the jaw.
* *High-risk*: The parapharyngeal space contains the carotid sheath (internal jugular vein and carotid artery). Risk of jugular vein thrombosis (Lemierre's syndrome) and carotid rupture.

---

## 🦠 Pathogens
Typically **polymicrobial**, reflecting oral flora:
* *Streptococcus* species (Viridans group, *S. pyogenes*).
* Oral anaerobes (*Peptostreptococcus*, *Fusobacterium nucleatum*, *Bacteroides* species, *Prevotella*).
* *Staphylococcus aureus* (in IV drug users or post-trauma).

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Airway Status (Absolute Priority)**: Prioritize airway safety over any imaging.
* **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) of the Neck (Gold Standard)**: Differentiates cellulitis from drainable abscess, maps the exact fascial spaces involved, and evaluates for vascular complications (thrombosis, hemorrhage) or mediastinal extension.
* **Lateral Neck Radiography**: Useful quick screen for retropharyngeal swelling.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Airway Stabilization (First-Line & Paramount)
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Airway Emergency**: Ludwig's angina and deep neck abscesses are volatile. Airway closure can happen rapidly. Keep the patient upright. **Prepare for emergent intubation or awake fiberoptic intubation/tracheostomy in the operating room** with ENT and anesthesiology teams present. Avoid routine blind oral intubation (can rupture the abscess or cause laryngospasm).

### 2. Empiric Intravenous Antibiotics
* **Regimen**: Cover Gram-positives, Gram-negatives, and oral anaerobes:
  * **Ampicillin-Sulbactam (Unasyn) 3 g IV q6h** OR
  * **Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h** OR
  * **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily PLUS Metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h** (to cover anaerobes).
* Continue IV therapy until the patient is afebrile and clinically stable, then step down to oral Amoxicillin-Clavulanate or Clindamycin to complete a 14-day course.

### 3. Surgical Drainage (Source Control)
* **Indications**: Any fluctuant abscess cavity on CT, Ludwig's angina (requires decompression of submandibular space), or failure to clinically improve within 24-48 hours of IV antibiotics.
* **Procedure**: External incision and drainage (I&D) and placement of drains.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Descending Mediastinitis**: The "danger space" (located behind the retropharyngeal space) extends from the skull base directly down into the posterior mediastinum. A retropharyngeal abscess can rapidly descend, causing highly lethal **descending necrotizing mediastinitis** (severe chest pain, dyspnea, widening of mediastinum on CXR).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Odontogenic Origin**: In a patient presenting with submandibular swelling and difficulty swallowing, always perform a thorough dental exam. A decayed lower molar is the most common cause of Ludwig's Angina.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Trismus Significance**: The presence of severe trismus in deep neck infection signifies involvement of either the masticator space or the parapharyngeal space, as the pterygoid muscles are directly irritated by the inflammatory process.
