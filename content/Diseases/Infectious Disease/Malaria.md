# 🦠 Malaria (โรคไข้จับสั่น)

> **Definition**: A protozoan parasitic disease caused by species of the genus *Plasmodium*, transmitted by the bite of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes, presenting with cyclical fevers, hemolytic anemia, and potential multiorgan dysfunction.

---

## 🦠 Life Cycle & Pathophysiology

### 1. Life Cycle
1. **Transmission**: Bite of female ***Anopheles* mosquito** (night-biting, breeds in slow-moving forest water) injecting **sporozoites**.
2. **Exo-erythrocytic (Hepatic) Phase**: Sporozoites invade hepatocytes, multiply, and form schizonts.
   * *Note: **P. vivax** and **P. ovale** can form **hypnozoites** (dormant stage in liver) which cause relapses months or years later.*
3. **Erythrocytic Phase**: Hepatocytes rupture, releasing merozoites that infect red blood cells (RBCs). Merozoites develop: ring stage $\rightarrow$ trophozoite $\rightarrow$ schizont. Rupture of RBC schizonts releases merozoites and pyrogens into blood, triggering clinical paroxysms.

### 2. Virulence of *Plasmodium falciparum*
* **Microvascular Sequestration**: *P. falciparum* produces **PfEMP1** (Plasmodium falciparum erythrocyte membrane protein 1) on knobs of infected RBCs. PfEMP1 mediates cytoadherence to endothelial cell receptors (CD36, ICAM-1, VCAM-1) in capillaries.
* **Rosetting**: Adherence of infected RBCs to uninfected RBCs.
* Sequestration prevents passage through spleen (evades splenic clearance). Obstruction of microvasculature leads to ischemia, tissue hypoxia, and organ failure (e.g., cerebral malaria, renal failure).

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Classic Malaria Paroxysm**:
  * **Cold Stage** ($15–60$ mins): Shivering, rigors, rapid rise in temperature.
  * **Hot Stage** ($2–6$ hours): High fever ($>40^\circ\text{C}$), severe headache, vomiting.
  * **Sweating Stage** ($2–4$ hours): Profuse diaphoresis, rapid drop in temperature.
* **Fever Patterns** (often irregular initially):
  * **Tertian (q48h)**: *P. falciparum*, *P. vivax*, *P. ovale*.
  * **Quartan (q72h)**: *P. malariae*.
  * **Quotidian (q24h)**: *P. knowlesi* (zoonotic parasite from macaques, replicates rapidly).
* **Severe Malaria** (Almost exclusively caused by *P. falciparum* or *P. knowlesi*):
  * **Cerebral Malaria**: Altered consciousness, coma, or seizures.
  * **Severe Anemia**: Hemoglobin $<7$ g/dL or Hct $<20\%$.
  * **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**: Pigment nephropathy (Blackwater Fever: dark red/black urine due to massive intravascular hemolysis and hemoglobinuria).
  * **Hypoglycemia**: Blood glucose $<40$ mg/dL (exacerbated by parasite glucose consumption and quinine therapy).
  * Pulmonary edema / ARDS.
  * Circulatory collapse (algid malaria) or shock (MAP $<60$ mmHg).
  * Metabolic acidosis (plasma bicarbonate $<15$ mmol/L or lactate $>5$ mmol/L).
  * Hyperparasitemia (parasitemia $>10\%$).

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Blood Smears (Gold Standard)**:
  * **Thick Blood Smear**: Evaluates a large volume of blood. Used for **screening** and **quantifying parasitemia**.
  * **Thin Blood Smear**: Spreads cells in a monolayer. Used for **species identification**.
* **Key Microscopic Features**:

| Species | Thin Smear Diagnostic Clues | RBC Size/Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ***P. falciparum*** | • **Multiple rings** per RBC<br>• **Double chromatin dots** (headphone shape)<br>• **Applique/Accole forms** (rings on RBC edge)<br>• **Banana/crescent-shaped gametocytes** | Normal |
| ***P. vivax*** | • Large trophozoites with ameboid cytoplasm<br>• Presence of **Schüffner's dots** (pink granules) | **Enlarged** |
| ***P. ovale*** | • Similar to *P. vivax* but RBCs have **fimbriated/oval edges** | **Enlarged** |
| ***P. malariae*** | • **Band forms** of trophozoites stretching across RBC<br>• Basket forms | Normal |

* **Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs)**: Detects PfHRP2 (specific to *P. falciparum*) or pan-malarial antigen (pLDH). Useful when microscopy is unavailable.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Uncomplicated Malaria

* ***P. falciparum***:
  * **First-line**: **Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)** for 3 days. E.g., **Artesunate-Mefloquine** (or Artemether-Lumefantrine).
  * Add a single dose of **Primaquine** 0.25 mg/kg on Day 1 (gametocytocidal to prevent transmission).
* ***P. vivax* or *P. ovale***:
  * **Step 1**: **Chloroquine** 25 mg/kg total over 3 days (kills blood-stage parasites).
  * **Step 2**: **Primaquine** 0.25–0.5 mg/kg daily for **14 days** (radical cure: kills dormant hypnozoites in the liver).
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **G6PD Deficiency Screening**: Must perform **G6PD screening before giving Primaquine**. In G6PD-deficient individuals, Primaquine triggers severe **acute intravascular hemolysis**. If G6PD-deficient, give Primaquine 0.75 mg/kg once weekly for 8 weeks under close supervision.

### 2. Severe Malaria (Medical Emergency)
* **First-line**: **IV Artesunate** 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12, and 24 hours, then daily.
* Once patient can tolerate oral medications, switch to a complete 3-day course of oral ACT.
* *Alternative*: IV Quinine dihydrochloride. Monitor for **cinchonism** (tinnitus, headache, nausea) and hypoglycemia. Requires ECG monitoring (QTc prolongation).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **G6PD and Primaquine**: Never skip G6PD testing before starting Primaquine for *P. vivax/ovale*. Intravascular hemolysis can lead to severe anemia, AKI, and death.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Hypoglycemia Mimics**: A patient with severe malaria receiving IV quinine/quinidine who becomes suddenly confused or unconscious has **hypoglycemia**, NOT worsening cerebral malaria. Always check bedside capillary blood glucose.
* > [!TIP]
  > ***P. knowlesi* Danger**: Has a 24-hour replication cycle (fastest of all malaria). Can progress from mild symptoms to fatal hyperparasitemia in hours. Treat as severe malaria.
