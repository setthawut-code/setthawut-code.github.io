# 🦠 Peritonsillar Abscess / Quinsy (ฝีรอบต่อมทอนซิล)

> **Definition**: A localized accumulation of pus in the peritonsillar space between the palatine tonsil capsule and the superior pharyngeal constrictor muscle. It is a complication of acute tonsillitis and is the most common deep neck space infection in young adults.

---

## 🦠 Pathogens
Typically **polymicrobial**, reflecting pharyngeal flora:
* **Group A Streptococcus (GAS)** (*Streptococcus pyogenes*).
* *Staphylococcus aureus* (including MRSA).
* Oral anaerobes: ***Fusobacterium necrophorum*** (associated with Lemierre's syndrome), *Peptostreptococcus*, and *Prevotella*.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Systemic**: High fever, chills, malaise, and dehydration.
* **Localized**:
  * **Severe, Unilateral Sore Throat** and odynophagia.
  * **Trismus**: Inability to open the mouth fully (due to inflammation and spasm of the adjacent medial pterygoid muscle).
  * **Muffled "Hot Potato" Voice**: Slurred speech due to pharyngeal swelling.
  * Drooling (due to severe pain on attempting to swallow saliva).
  * Ipsilateral ear pain (referred pain via cranial nerve IX).
* **Physical Examination**:
  * Marked **unilateral tonsillar swelling** pushing the tonsil medially and anteriorly.
  * **Deviation of the uvula to the contralateral (opposite) side**.
  * Erythema and fluctuance of the soft palate superior to the tonsil.
  * Tender, unilateral anterior cervical lymphadenopathy.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Clinical Diagnosis**: In typical cases, history and visual exam are sufficient.
* **Needle Aspiration**: Confirms diagnosis (pus withdrawal) and provides specimen for culture.
* **Intraoral Ultrasound**: Highly sensitive to differentiate peritonsillar cellulitis (no fluid collection) from abscess (hypoechoic fluid collection), and guides needle aspiration.
* **CECT of the Neck**: Indicated if trismus prevents adequate oral examination, or if spread to the parapharyngeal/retropharyngeal space is suspected.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Drainage (Source Control)
* **First-line**: Must be performed to relieve pain and protect the airway. Three options:
  1. **Needle Aspiration**: Most common, least painful, and highly effective. Aspirate the point of maximal fluctuance (typically the superior tonsillar pole).
  2. **Incision and Drainage (I&D)**: Scalpel incision followed by hemostatic spread to break loculations.
  3. **Quinsy Tonsillectomy**: Bilateral tonsillectomy performed during acute abscess phase. Reserved for patients with recurrent tonsillitis or failed needle/I&D.

### 2. Antimicrobial Therapy
* **Intravenous Regimen**:
  * **Ampicillin-Sulbactam (Unasyn) 3 g IV q6h** OR
  * **Penicillin G 2-4 million units IV q4h PLUS Metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h** OR
  * **Clindamycin 600-900 mg IV q8h** (preferred for severe penicillin allergy).
* **Oral Step-Down**: Once afebrile and tolerating oral intake, transition to **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 875/125 mg PO bid** or **Clindamycin 300-450 mg PO tid** to complete a **10 to 14-day course**.

### 3. Adjunctive Corticosteroids
* A single dose of **IV Dexamethasone (4 - 10 mg)** or oral Prednisolone significantly reduces swelling, relieves pain, decreases trismus, and speeds up discharge.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
     > **Uvular Deviation Rule**: In a patient with sore throat, finding **unilateral tonsillar swelling with uvular deviation to the opposite side** is the clinical signature of a **Peritonsillar Abscess**. Tonsillitis alone causes bilateral swelling without uvular deviation.
* > [!WARNING]
     > **Carotid Artery Anatomy**: When performing needle aspiration or I&D of a peritonsillar abscess, **always orient the needle/blade sagittal (straight back) or medial**. Avoid directing it laterally. The internal carotid artery lies approximately **$1-2\text{ cm}$ posterolaterally** to the tonsillar capsule; accidental puncture can cause catastrophic hemorrhage.
* > [!TIP]
     > **Lemierre's Syndrome**: A rare, lethal complication of peritonsillar abscess or pharyngitis caused by *Fusobacterium necrophorum*. The infection spreads contiguously to the parapharyngeal space, causing **internal jugular vein septic thrombophaly**, which sheds septic emboli to the lungs (cavitary pulmonary nodules) and joints.
