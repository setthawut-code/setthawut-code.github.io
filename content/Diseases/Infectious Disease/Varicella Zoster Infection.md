# 🦠 Varicella Zoster Infection (การติดเชื้อไวรัสอีสุกอีใสและงูสวัด)

> **Definition**: Infections caused by Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV; Human Herpesvirus 3 - HHV-3). Primary infection causes **Varicella (Chickenpox)**. Reactivation of latent VZV in sensory ganglia causes **Herpes Zoster (Shingles)**.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Latency

* **Organism**: Varicella-Zoster Virus (double-stranded DNA, enveloped).
* **Primary Infection (Varicella)**: Inhalation of respiratory droplets or direct contact with vesicle fluid $\rightarrow$ replication in nasopharynx $\rightarrow$ primary viremia $\rightarrow$ replication in liver/spleen $\rightarrow$ secondary viremia $\rightarrow$ hematogenous seeding to skin (rash).
* **Latency**: During primary infection, VZV ascends sensory nerve fibers from skin lesions to establish lifelong latency in **dorsal root ganglia** or **cranial nerve ganglia**.
* **Reactivation (Herpes Zoster)**: Decline in cell-mediated immunity (aging, stress, immunosuppression, HIV) $\rightarrow$ viral reactivation $\rightarrow$ virus travels down sensory nerve to corresponding dermatome $\rightarrow$ severe localized pain and vesicular eruption.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Varicella (Chickenpox)
* **Prodrome**: Low-grade fever, malaise, headache.
* **Rash**: Highly pruritic. Centripetal distribution (starts on face/trunk, spreads to extremities).
* **Evolution**: Progresses rapidly (hours) from macules $\rightarrow$ papules $\rightarrow$ clear vesicles on an erythematous base (**"dewdrops on a rose petal"**) $\rightarrow$ pustules $\rightarrow$ crusts.
* **Key Feature**: **Polymorphous rash** (lesions in all different stages of development coexisting in the same anatomical area).

### 2. Herpes Zoster (Shingles)
* **Prodrome**: Severe burning, paresthesia, or throbbing pain in a specific dermatome, preceding the rash by $3–5$ days (often misdiagnosed as myocardial infarction, pleurisy, or biliary colic).
* **Rash**: Painful, clustered vesicles on an erythematous base in a **unilateral, dermatomal distribution** (does not cross the midline). Most common in thoracic and lumbar dermatomes.
* **Key Complications**:
  * **Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN)**: Pain persisting $>90$ days after the onset of the shingles rash. Risk factors: age $\ge 50$ years, severe prodromal pain.
  * **Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus (HZO)**: Reactivation in the ophthalmic branch of trigeminal nerve ($CN\text{ }V_1$). 
    * **Hutchinson's Sign**: Vesicles on the tip, side, or root of the nose. Indicates nasociliary nerve involvement, carrying a high risk of corneal ulceration and permanent blindness.
  * **Ramsay Hunt Syndrome (Herpes Zoster Oticus)**: Reactivation in the geniculate ganglion ($CN\text{ }VII$). Triad: ipsilateral facial nerve palsy, severe otalgia, and vesicles in the external auditory canal or auricle.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Clinical Diagnosis**: Standard approach.
* **PCR (Gold Standard)**: Highest sensitivity and specificity. Detects VZV DNA from scraping of vesicle base.
* **Direct Fluorescent Antibody (DFA)**: Rapid, detects VZV antigens.
* **Tzanck Smear**: Scraping of the base of an active vesicle stained with Giemsa. Shows **multinucleated giant cells** with intranuclear inclusions. *Note: Cannot differentiate VZV from HSV-1 or HSV-2.*

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Varicella (Chickenpox)
* **Children ($<12$ years)**: Supportive care (antipyretics - **avoid Aspirin** due to Reye Syndrome; cool compresses, antihistamines). Routine antivirals not recommended.
* **Adults, Pregnant Women, or Immunocompromised**: 
  * **Oral Acyclovir** 800 mg PO 5 times daily for 5–7 days. Start **within 24 hours** of rash onset. (Use IV Acyclovir if severe/immunocompromised).

### 2. Herpes Zoster (Shingles)
* **Antiviral Therapy**:
  * **Indications**: Age $\ge 50$, severe pain, involvement of face/eye, or immunocompromised.
  * **Regimen**: **Acyclovir 800 mg PO 5 times daily for 7 days** OR **Valacyclovir 1000 mg PO tid for 7 days**.
  * **Timing**: Ideally initiate **within 72 hours of rash onset** to accelerate healing and reduce acute pain. Start after 72 hours if new vesicles are actively forming or in HZO/immunocompromised.
* **Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN) Treatment**:
  * **First-line**: **Gabapentin** (300–1800 mg/day) OR **Pregabalin** (75–300 mg/day) OR Tricyclic Antidepressants (**Amitriptyline** 10-50 mg qhs).
  * Topical Lidocaine patch or Capsaicin cream.
* **HZO Management**: Emergency ophthalmology referral. Topical corticosteroids (for keratitis/iritis) plus oral/IV antivirals.

### 3. Prevention
* **Varicella Vaccine**: Live attenuated vaccine. 2-dose series for children $\ge 12$ months.
* **Herpes Zoster Vaccine**: Recombinant subunit vaccine (**Shingrix**). Recommended for all adults $\ge 50$ years (2 doses, 2-6 months apart).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Hutchinson's Sign**: If a shingles patient presents with a vesicular lesion on the tip of the nose, **immediately refer to ophthalmology** and start oral Valacyclovir. The nasociliary nerve supplies both the nose tip and the cornea; ocular involvement can lead to keratitis, uveitis, and blindness.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Reye Syndrome Risk**: Never use Aspirin for fever control in chickenpox or influenza in children/adolescents. It can precipitate Reye syndrome (rapidly progressive encephalopathy, hepatic dysfunction, and microvesicular steatosis).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Infectiousness**: Patients with shingles are infectious (via contact with vesicle fluid) to individuals who have never had chickenpox or the varicella vaccine. Cover lesions to prevent transmission.
