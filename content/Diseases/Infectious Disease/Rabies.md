## type: disease

# Definition
Rabies is an acute, progressive, fatal viral encephalomyelitis caused by the Rabies virus, transmitted through the saliva of an infected mammal. Once clinical symptoms manifest, the disease is virtually $100\%$ fatal.

# Epidemiology
* An estimated 59,000 human deaths occur globally each year, primarily in Asia and Africa.
* **Vector**: Dogs are the reservoir host and transmit $>99\%$ of human cases globally. In Western countries, wild animals (bats, raccoons, skunks, foxes) are the primary reservoirs.

# Etiology
* **Rabies Lyssavirus**: An enveloped, single-stranded RNA virus belonging to the *Rhabdoviridae* family (distinguished by its characteristic **bullet-shaped** morphology).
* **Transmission**: Animal bite or scratch inoculating virus-laden saliva into skeletal muscle, or inhalation of bat guano aerosol (rare).

# Pathophysiology
1. **Local Replication**: Following inoculation, the virus replicates locally in skeletal muscle cells at the bite site (can remain localized during the incubation period).
2. **Neuromuscular Junction Binding**: The virus binds to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction.
3. **Retrograde Axonal Transport**: Travels retrogradely via fast axonal transport ($50-100$ mm/day) along sensory or motor axons into the spinal cord, and ascends to the brain.
4. **Encephalomyelitis**: Rapid viral replication occurs in the brainstem, limbic system, and Purkinje cells of the cerebellum.
   * *Histopathological Hallmark*: **Negri Bodies** (eosinophilic, round cytoplasmic inclusion bodies containing viral nucleocapsids, classic in Purkinje cells of the cerebellum or pyramidal cells of the hippocampus).
5. **Anterograde Spread**: Travels via anterograde axonal transport along peripheral nerves to highly innervated tissues, including the **salivary glands** (enabling transmission), skin of the neck, and heart.

# Clinical Manifestations

## Symptoms
* **Incubation Period**: Typically **1 to 3 months** (can range from $<1$ week to $>1$ year, depending on the severity of the bite and its proximity to the central nervous system).
* **Prodrome** ($2-10$ days): Non-specific fever, headache, sore throat, and anxiety.
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
  * **Paresthesia at Bite Site**: **Paresthesia, pain, or intense pruritus at the site of the original (often healed) bite** is present in $50-80\%$ of cases and is highly specific for early rabies.
* **Clinical Forms**:
  * **Furious (Encephalitic) Rabies** ($80\%$ of cases):
    * **Hydrophobia**: Painful, violent diaphragmatic and pharyngeal muscle spasms triggered by attempts to swallow water, or even the sight, sound, or mention of water.
    * **Aerophobia**: Spasms triggered by a light draft of air blowing on the skin.
    * Extreme agitation, combative behavior, hallucinations, hypersalivation ("foaming at the mouth" due to pharyngeal paralysis and excess saliva production), and autonomic instability.
  * **Dumb (Paralytic) Rabies** ($20\%$ of cases):
    * Ascending, flaccid paralysis (resembling Guillain-Barré syndrome).

## Physical Examination
* **Agitation and Fluctuating Consciousness**: Alternating between periods of intense hyperactivity/hallucinations and lucid intervals.
* **Hyper-reflexia & Meningismus**.
* **Hypersalivation and autonomic storm** (mydriasis, sweating, piloerection).

---

# Differential Diagnosis
* **[[Tetanus]]**: Presents with trismus (lockjaw), risus sardonicus, and generalized muscle spasms, but **consciousness is fully preserved**, hydrophobia is absent, and the primary muscle involved is the masseter rather than pharyngeal muscles.
* **[[Encephalitis]] (Other Viral)**: HSV encephalitis presents with focal seizures and aphasia, but lacks hydrophobia, aerophobia, or history of animal bite.
* **[[Guillain-Barre Syndrome]] (GBS)**: Resembles dumb (paralytic) rabies; GBS features early sensory paresthesias, symmetric ascending weakness, and albuminocytologic dissociation, but lacks prodromal bite-site pain and is not fatal within days.

# Investigations

## Laboratory & Special Tests
* **Nape-of-Neck Skin Biopsy (Direct Fluorescent Antibody [DFA])**:
  * Skin biopsy taken from the posterior hairline of the neck. Hair follicles are highly innervated; DFA staining shows green fluorescent rabies viral antigen clusters around cutaneous nerves.
* **RT-PCR of Saliva or CSF**:
  * High specificity to detect rabies RNA. Saliva should be collected 3 times, 3-4 hours apart.
* **CSF Analysis**: Shows mild lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein (meningoencephalitis pattern).
* **Post-Mortem Brain Biopsy**: Definitive diagnosis. Staining of the cerebellum or hippocampus shows **Negri bodies** inside Purkinje/pyramidal neurons.

---

# Diagnostic Criteria
Confirmed by clinical features of hydrophobia/aerophobia combined with positive RT-PCR of saliva or positive DFA stain on a neck skin biopsy.

---

# Management

## Initial Management
* > [!WARNING]
  > **No Effective Treatment Post-Symptom Onset**: Once clinical signs of rabies appear, treatment is supportive and palliative. The disease is universally fatal within 1-2 weeks. The "Milwaukee Protocol" has failed and is not recommended.
* **Palliative Care**: Admit to a quiet, dark isolation room. Administer heavy sedation (IV Midazolam, IV Haloperidol) and opioids to minimize painful pharyngeal spasms and agitation.

## Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) - 100% Effective if Started Early
Mandatory for any high-risk mammal bite/scratch if the animal cannot be observed for 10 days, is suspected to be rabid, or was captured and tested positive.

```
Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) Protocol (Unvaccinated)
├── 1. Wound Care (IMMEDIATE & CRITICAL)
│   ├── Wash all wounds thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes
│   └── Apply povidone-iodine or 70% alcohol (mechanically clears and inactivates virus)
├── 2. Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) (Passive Immunization)
│   ├── Dose: 20 IU/kg (human RIG) or 40 IU/kg (equine RIG)
│   └── Action: INFILTRATE as much of the dose as possible directly into and around all wounds
│   └── Note: Inject remaining RIG IM at a site distant from the vaccine (e.g., contralateral arm)
└── 3. Rabies Vaccine (Active Immunization)
    ├── Dose: 1.0 mL (or 0.5 mL) IM in the DELTOID muscle (never gluteal - poor absorption)
    └── Schedule: Days 0, 3, 7, and 14 (add Day 28 if immunocompromised)
```

# Complications
* Autonomic collapse and cardiac arrhythmias.
* Aspiration pneumonia and respiratory arrest.
* Inevitable death.

# Prognosis
* *Pre-symptomatic PEP*: $100\%$ survival.
* *Post-symptomatic*: $0\%$ survival ($>99.9\%$ fatality rate; only a handful of survivors have ever been documented worldwide).

# Clinical Pearls
* **RIG Infiltration is Paramount**: Do not inject all the Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) intramuscularly in the buttock. RIG must be **infiltrated directly into the tissue surrounding the bite wound**, as it acts locally to neutralize the virus before it binds to the motor endplates.
* **Lucid Intervals**: Between wild spasms of furious rabies, the patient may become completely calm, lucid, and able to communicate normally. Do not mistake this temporary recovery for clinical improvement.
