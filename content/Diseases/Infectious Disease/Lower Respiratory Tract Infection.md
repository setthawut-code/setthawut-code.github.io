# 🦠 Lower Respiratory Tract Infection / LRTI (การติดเชื้อทางเดินหายใจส่วนล่าง)

> **Definition**: A group of acute infections affecting the respiratory tract structures below the vocal cords (trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, and alveoli). Primarily classified into Acute Bronchitis, Acute Bronchiolitis, and Pneumonia.

---

## 📊 LRTI Clinical Syndromes

### 1. Acute Bronchitis
* **Definition**: Self-limiting inflammation of the large airways (bronchi).
* **Pathogens**: $>90\%$ **viral** (Rhinovirus, Coronavirus, Influenza, RSV). Non-viral causes like *Bordetella pertussis* (whooping cough), *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, or *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* are rare ($<10\%$).
* **Clinical Presentation**: 
  * **Cough** (dry or productive) is the cardinal symptom, typically lasting **1 to 3 weeks**.
  * May feature mild chest wall tenderness, wheezing, or rhonchi.
  * *Negative Finding*: **Absence of systemic signs like high fever, tachypnea, or consolidation** (rules out pneumonia).
* **Management**: Symptomatic care only:
  * Bronchodilators (e.g., Albuterol) if wheezing or prior airway hyperreactivity is present.
  * Cough suppressants or expectorants.
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **The Sputum Color Trap**: Purulent (green/yellow) sputum is caused by cell debris and myeloperoxidase from inflammatory cells during normal viral desquamation. It is **not** an indicator of bacterial infection and does not justify antibiotic therapy. Avoid prescribing antibiotics.

### 2. Acute Bronchiolitis (Pediatric Focus)
* **Definition**: Acute viral inflammation of the small bronchioles, occurring in **infants $<2$ years** old (peak age 2-6 months).
* **Pathogen**: **Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV)** is the most common cause ($>60-80\%$).
* **Clinical Presentation**:
  * Starts with a mild viral prodrome (rhinorrhea, low-grade fever).
  * Progresses to lower tract signs: **Tachypnea, wheezing**, fine crackles, nasal flaring, and subcostal/intercostal chest wall retractions.
  * Poor feeding, dehydration. **Apnea** is a major risk in infants $<1$ month of age or born prematurely.
* **Management**: Primarily supportive:
  * **Humidified Oxygen**: Maintain $SpO_2 \ge 90-92\%$.
  * **Nasal Suctioning**: Frequent clearance of upper airway secretions (essential before feeding).
  * Hydration (IV fluids or nasogastric tube if feeding is compromised).
  * *Note*: Bronchodilators (Albuterol), epinephrine, and systemic corticosteroids are **not** routinely recommended due to lack of proven efficacy.

### 3. Pneumonia
* **Definition**: Infection of the lung parenchyma (alveoli and terminal bronchioles), leading to exudative consolidation.
* **Classification**:
  * **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)**: Acquired outside of a healthcare setting. *See detailed guidelines in [[Community Acquired Pneumonia]].*
  * **Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP)**: Pneumonia occurring $\ge 48$ hours after hospital admission, which was not incubating at the time of admission.
  * **Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP)**: Pneumonia occurring $\ge 48$ hours after endotracheal intubation.
* **HAP/VAP Pathogens**: High prevalence of multidrug-resistant (MDR) pathogens: ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa***, ***Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA)**, and *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.
* **HAP/VAP Management**: Empiric broad-spectrum anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam (Piperacillin-Tazobactam or Cefepime/Meropenem) + MRSA coverage (Vancomycin or Linezolid) based on risk factors.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **[[Chest X-Ray]]**: **Mandatory to differentiate Bronchitis from Pneumonia.** 
  * *Acute Bronchitis*: Normal X-ray, or mild non-specific bronchial wall thickening.
  * *Pneumonia*: Demonstrates focal lobar consolidation, patchy bronchopneumonic infiltrates, or interstitial markings.
* **RSV Antigen/PCR**: Throat or nasal swab to confirm RSV in hospitalized infants.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Bronchitis vs. Pneumonia Rule**: A patient presenting with cough, purulent sputum, and normal vital signs (no fever, heart rate $<100$, respiratory rate $<20$) who has a **completely normal chest X-ray** has **Acute Bronchitis**. Do not prescribe antibiotics; treat symptomatically.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Apnea in RSV**: Apnea can be the presenting symptom of RSV bronchiolitis in young infants (especially those born prematurely or $<1$ month old) before any wheezing or respiratory distress develops. Monitor high-risk infants closely in the hospital.
