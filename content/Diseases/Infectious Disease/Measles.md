# 🦠 Measles / Rubeola (โรคหัด)

> **Definition**: Extremely contagious viral infection caused by the Measles virus (genus *Morbillivirus*, family *Paramyxoviridae*), characterized by high fever, respiratory symptoms, pathognomonic **Koplik's spots**, and a maculopapular rash.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Transmission

* **Transmission**: **Airborne transmission** via droplet nuclei (highly infectious; virus can remain suspended in air and infectious in closed spaces for up to **2 hours**).
* **Pathogenesis**: Enters respiratory tract $\rightarrow$ infects alveolar macrophages/dendritic cells $\rightarrow$ lymphatic spread $\rightarrow$ primary viremia $\rightarrow$ reticuloendothelial replication $\rightarrow$ secondary viremia $\rightarrow$ widespread dissemination to skin, respiratory mucosa, and conjunctiva.
* **Histopathology**: Characterized by **Warthin-Finkeldey giant cells** (multinucleated giant cells with eosinophilic intranuclear and intracytoplasmic inclusions in lymphoid tissue).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

Clinical course follows prodrome, enanthem, exanthem, and recovery:

### 1. Prodromal Phase (Days 1–4)
* High fever (often $>40^\circ\text{C}$).
* **The 3 Cs (Classic Triad)**:
  1. **Cough** (harsh, brassy).
  2. **Coryza** (severe runny nose, congestion).
  3. **Conjunctivitis** (watery, red eyes, often with photophobia).

### 2. Enanthem (Day 3–4)
* **Koplik's Spots**: Pathognomonic sign. Small, irregular, red spots with a **bluish-white center** on the buccal mucosa, classically opposite the lower second molars ("grains of salt on a red background"). Appears $1-2$ days before the rash and fades as the rash spreads.

### 3. Exanthem (Day 4–5)
* Erythematous, maculopapular, blanching rash.
* **Cephalocaudal Progression**: Starts on the face (behind the ears, at the hairline), spreads **downward** to neck, trunk, extremities, and palms/soles over 3 days.
* Fades in the same order of appearance (top to bottom), leaving a brownish copper-like discoloration and fine desquamation.

### 4. Key Complications
* **Otitis Media**: Most common complication.
* **Pneumonia**: Most common cause of **measles-related death in children** (can be primary viral interstitial giant-cell pneumonia or secondary bacterial pneumonia).
* **Encephalitis**: Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM) or subacute measles encephalitis.
* **Subacute Sclerosing Panencephalitis (SSPE)**: Rare, fatal neurodegenerative disease occurring **7–10 years after primary infection** due to persistent mutated measles virus in the brain. Presents as progressive cognitive decline, myoclonus, and dementia.
* **Vitamin A Deficiency**: Measles depletes vitamin A stores, risking corneal ulceration and blindness (**keratomalacia**).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Clinical Diagnosis**: Classic 3 Cs + Koplik's spots + cephalocaudal rash.
* **Serology (Best Initial)**: Detection of **Measles-specific IgM antibody** in serum (positive from rash onset up to 1 month).
* **RT-PCR**: Detects measles RNA in throat/nasopharyngeal swabs, urine, or blood.

---

## 🏥 Management

No specific antiviral therapy. Mainly supportive:

### 1. Supportive Care
* Hydration, antipyretics (avoid Aspirin), humidification for cough.

### 2. Vitamin A Supplementation (Mandatory)
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Vitamin A Therapy**: Administer **Vitamin A immediately to all children diagnosed with acute measles** (regardless of prior nutritional status). Give oral doses daily for **2 days**:
  > * Age $\ge 12$ months: $200,000$ IU.
  > * Age $6-11$ months: $100,000$ IU.
  > * Age $<6$ months: $50,000$ IU.
  > * *Rationale: Restores depleted stores, significantly reducing risk of blindness (keratomalacia), severe pneumonia, and overall mortality.*

### 3. Isolation & Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)
* **Airborne Isolation**: Place hospitalized patients in negative-pressure rooms; maintain airborne precautions for **4 days after rash onset** (or duration of illness in immunocompromised).
* **PEP for Susceptible Contacts**:
  * **Measles Vaccine**: Administer within **72 hours** of exposure.
  * **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: Administer within **6 days** of exposure to high-risk individuals (pregnant women, infants $<6$ months, immunocompromised).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!WARNING]
  > **SSPE Prevention**: The only way to prevent Subacute Sclerosing Panencephalitis (SSPE) is MMR vaccination to prevent primary measles infection. There is no cure for SSPE once it develops.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Koplik's Spots Timing**: Koplik's spots are transient, usually disappearing within 48 hours of rash onset. If you examine a measles patient on day 3 of the rash, Koplik's spots will likely be absent.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Immunosuppression**: Measles induces transient but profound cell-mediated immunosuppression lasting weeks to months, making patients highly susceptible to secondary bacterial infections (otitis, pneumonia).
