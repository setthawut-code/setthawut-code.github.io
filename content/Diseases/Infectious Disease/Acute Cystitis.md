# 🦠 Acute Cystitis (กระเพาะปัสสาวะอักเสบเฉียบพลัน)

> **Definition**: Acute superficial bacterial infection of the bladder mucosa, presenting with localized lower urinary tract symptoms without systemic manifestations.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Microbiology

* **Pathogenesis**: Retrograde bacterial ascent from perineum/urethra into bladder. Facilitated by short female urethra, sexual intercourse, spermicide use, or bladder catheterization.
* **Common Pathogens**:
  * ***Escherichia coli*** ($75-90\%$): Expresses **Type 1 fimbriae** (mannose-sensitive adhesins) which bind to uroplakin receptors on bladder superficial umbrella cells.
  * ***Staphylococcus saprophyticus*** ($5-15\%$): Gram-positive, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative, **novobiocin-resistant** coccus. Common in young, sexually active females ("honeymoon cystitis").
  * *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Proteus mirabilis*.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Local Symptoms**:
  * **Dysuria** (burning pain during urination).
  * **Frequency** and **Urgency**.
  * **Suprapubic pain** or tenderness.
  * Hematuria (gross or microscopic; "hemorrhagic cystitis").
  * Nocturia and cloudy, foul-smelling urine.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **No Systemic Signs**: Absence of fever, chills, flank pain, nausea, vomiting, or costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness. *Presence of these symptoms indicates progression to [[Acute Pyelonephritis]].*

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Uncomplicated Cystitis**: Diagnosis is purely **clinical**. In healthy, non-pregnant, premenopausal women with classic symptoms, no laboratory workup is required before starting treatment.
* **Urinalysis (UA) / Dipstick**:
  * **Pyuria** (WBC $>5-10$/hpf) or positive **Leukocyte Esterase** (most sensitive marker).
  * **Nitrite positive** (highly specific for Gram-negative enterics; negative in Gram-positive or *Pseudomonas* infections).
  * Hematuria.
  * *Note: No casts are present.*
* **Urine Culture**:
  * **Indications**: Complicated cystitis, pregnancy, males, elderly, atypical symptoms, failure to respond to initial therapy, or recurrent UTIs ($\ge 3$/year).
  * **Diagnostic Cutoff**: $\ge 10^5$ CFU/mL in clean-catch midstream urine. (Lower cutoff of $\ge 10^3$ CFU/mL is significant in symptomatic patients).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Uncomplicated Cystitis (Healthy, Non-pregnant Females)
* **First-line Regimens**:
  * **Nitrofurantoin monohydrate/macrocrystals (Macrobid)**: **100 mg PO bid for 5 days**. (Contraindicated if CrCl $<30$ mL/min. Does not achieve therapeutic levels in renal tissue, hence useless in pyelonephritis).
  * **Fosfomycin tromethamine**: **3 g PO single dose** mixed in water.
  * **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole DS**: **1 tablet PO bid for 3 days** (avoid if local resistance rates $>20\%$).
* **Second-line**:
  * Fluoroquinolones (**Ciprofloxacin** 250 mg bid or **Levofloxacin** 250-500 mg qd for 3 days). *Avoid as first-line due to collateral damage (resistance) and disabling side effects (tendinitis/tendon rupture, QTc prolongation, aortic aneurysm).*
  * Beta-lactams (**Amoxicillin-Clavulanate** 500/125 mg PO bid for 3–5 days).

### 2. Complicated Cystitis (Pregnancy, Males, Diabetics, Indwelling Catheters)
* **Antibiotics**: **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg PO bid or Levofloxacin for **7 days**.
* **Pregnancy Regimen**:
  * **First-line**: **Cephalexin** 500 mg PO bid OR **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate** 500/125 mg PO bid for **5–7 days**.
  * **Nitrofurantoin**: Safe in 2nd/3rd trimester. Avoid during 1st trimester (potential birth defects) and at term/labor (weeks $\ge 38$; risk of **neonatal hemolytic anemia** due to immature G6PD).
  * **Avoid**: Fluoroquinolones (bone/cartilage toxicity) and TMP-SMX (folate antagonist in 1st trimester; risk of neonatal kernicterus in 3rd trimester).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB)**: Positive urine culture ($\ge 10^5$ CFU/mL) without any UTI symptoms. **Do NOT screen or treat**, except in two specific populations:
  > 1. **Pregnant women** (untreated ASB leads to pyelonephritis in $30\%$ and increases risk of preterm birth/low birth weight).
  > 2. Patients undergoing **urologic procedures** with anticipated mucosal bleeding.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Sterile Pyuria**: Presence of WBCs in urine ($>10$/hpf) but negative routine bacterial cultures. Suspect:
  > * Sexually transmitted pathogens (*Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, *Herpes simplex*).
  > * Urinary tract tuberculosis (Renal TB).
  > * Non-infectious causes (interstitial nephritis, stones, Kawasaki disease).
