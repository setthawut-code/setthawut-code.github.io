# 🦠 Mumps (โรคคางทูม)

> **Definition**: Acute, contagious viral illness caused by the Mumps virus (genus *Orthuravirus*, family *Paramyxoviridae*), characterized by painful swelling of the salivary glands (most commonly the parotid glands).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Transmission

* **Transmission**: Respiratory droplets, direct contact with saliva, or contaminated fomites.
* **Pathogenesis**: Inhalation of virus $\rightarrow$ replication in upper respiratory tract epithelial cells $\rightarrow$ regional lymph nodes $\rightarrow$ systemic viremia $\rightarrow$ target organs (glandular tissue: parotids, testes, ovaries, pancreas; and central nervous system).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Prodrome**: Low-grade fever, headache, malaise, anorexia, ear pain on chewing.
* **Parotitis** ($95\%$ of symptomatic cases):
  * Painful swelling of the parotid gland. Typically starts unilaterally, becomes **bilateral in $70–80\%$** within a few days.
  * Swelling **elevates the earlobe** and obscures the angle of the mandible.
  * Pain is exacerbated by chewing or drinking acidic liquids (citrus fruits).
  * Erythema and edema at the orifice of **Stensen's duct** (opposite the upper second molar).
* **Key Complications**:
  * **Epididymo-orchitis**: Most common complication in post-pubertal males ($20-30\%$). Classically unilateral. Presents with high fever, severe testicular pain, swelling, and scrotal erythema $1-2$ weeks after parotitis. Can lead to **testicular atrophy** ($30-50\%$ of cases); bilateral orchitis can cause **subfertility/infertility** (rare).
  * **Oophoritis**: Post-pubertal females ($5\%$). Presents as lower abdominal pain; does not impair fertility.
  * **Acute Pancreatitis**: Epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting, elevated serum lipase.
  * **Aseptic Meningitis**: Benign course, resolves without sequelae.
  * **Sensorineural Hearing Loss**: Usually unilateral, sudden, and transient; rarely permanent.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Clinical Diagnosis**: Primary method during outbreaks.
* **RT-PCR (Best Diagnostic)**: Detects mumps viral RNA from **buccal swab** (massage parotid gland for 30 seconds beforehand), throat swab, CSF, or urine.
* **Serology**: Detection of mumps-specific **IgM antibody** (ELISA).
* **Serum Amylase**: **Elevated** in both parotitis and pancreatitis. (Order **Serum Lipase** to confirm suspected pancreatitis, as salivary amylase is elevated from parotitis alone).

---

## 🏥 Management

Management is entirely supportive. No specific antiviral therapy exists:

* **Supportive Care**:
  * Analgesics/Antipyretics (Acetaminophen or Ibuprofen).
  * Warm or cold compresses to the parotid area.
  * Soft diet, avoid acidic foods.
* **Orchitis Management**:
  * Bed rest.
  * Scrotal support (athletic supporter) or elevation.
  * Ice packs to scrotum.
  * *Note: Corticosteroids do not prevent testicular atrophy.*
* **Infection Control**:
  * **Droplet isolation** for **5 days** after the onset of parotid swelling.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Mandible Angle Obscuration**: Swelling from parotitis extends anteriorly and inferiorly, obscuring the angle of the mandible. This distinguishes it from cervical lymphadenopathy, which is located below the mandible and does not obscure the mandible angle.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Amylase Pitfall**: Do not assume a mumps patient has acute pancreatitis just because their serum amylase is elevated. The inflamed parotid glands release high amounts of salivary amylase. Use serum lipase for diagnosing pancreatitis.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Prevention**: Prevented via the **MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella) vaccine** (live attenuated). 2-dose schedule: first at $12-15$ months, second at $4-6$ years.
