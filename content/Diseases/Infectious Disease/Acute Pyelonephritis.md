# 🦠 Acute Pyelonephritis (กรวยไตอักเสบเฉียบพลัน)

> **Definition**: Acute bacterial infection of the renal parenchyma and renal pelvis, typically ascending from the lower urinary tract, presenting with systemic symptoms and flank pain.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Microbiology

* **Pathogenesis**: Bacteria ascend from urethra/bladder via ureters to renal pelvis/parenchyma. Vesicouretal reflux (VUR) or urinary tract obstruction facilitates ascent. Hematogenous spread is rare (usually *S. aureus* bacteremia).
* **Common Pathogens**:
  * ***Escherichia coli*** ($>80\%$ of cases): Expresses **P fimbriae** (adhesins) which bind to galactose disaccharide receptors on uroepithelial cells, enabling tissue adherence and ascent against urine flow.
  * ***Proteus mirabilis***: Produces **urease** (splits urea into ammonia, raising urine pH $\rightarrow$ promotes **struvite/triple phosphate stones**).
  * *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Enterobacter* spp.
  * *Enterococcus faecalis* (associated with urological procedures).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Classic Presentation**:
  * **Systemic**: High **[[Fever]]** ($>38^\circ\text{C}$), shaking chills, rigors, nausea, vomiting, tachycardia.
  * **Local**: Unilateral or bilateral **flank pain** and **Costovertebral Angle (CVA) tenderness**.
  * **Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (LUTS)**: Dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain (may be absent in up to $30\%$).
* **Complications**:
  * Perinephric or renal abscess.
  * Papillary necrosis (especially in diabetes, sickle cell disease).
  * **Septic shock** / Urosepsis.
  * **Emphysematous Pyelonephritis**: Severe necrotizing gas-producing infection (classically in diabetics).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Urinalysis (UA)**:
  * **Pyuria** (WBC $>10$/hpf), positive leukocyte esterase.
  * **Nitrite positive** (indicates Gram-negative enterics converting nitrate to nitrite).
  * **WBC Casts**: **Pathognomonic for upper UTI** (pyelonephritis); differentiates it from lower UTI (cystitis).
  * Hematuria.
* **Urine Culture (Gold Standard)**:
  * Significant bacteriuria defined as $\ge 10^5$ CFU/mL. Essential for identifying organism and drug sensitivity.
* **Blood Cultures**:
  * Positive in $15-20\%$ of cases. Indicated in all hospitalized/toxic-appearing patients.
* **Renal Function Tests**: BUN, Creatinine (check for acute kidney injury).
* **Imaging (Renal Ultrasound or Abdomen/Pelvis CT with contrast)**:
  * Not routinely needed for simple, uncomplicated pyelonephritis.
  * **Indications**: Failure to improve after 48-72 hours of appropriate antibiotics, suspected obstruction (renal colic/stones), history of nephrolithiasis, recurrent pyelonephritis, or immunocompromised state.
  * CT findings: Striated nephrogram, perinephric stranding, gas in renal parenchyma (emphysematous).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Outpatient Management (Uncomplicated, tolerates PO, stable)
* **Empiric Oral Regimen**:
  * **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg PO bid for 7 days OR **Levofloxacin** 750 mg PO qd for 5 days.
  * *Note*: If local fluoroquinolone resistance $>10\%$, give initial IV dose of **Ceftriaxone 1 g** or Aminoglycoside, then start oral fluoroquinolone.
  * *Alternative*: **TMP-SMX DS** 1 tablet PO bid for 14 days (only if susceptibility known).

### 2. Inpatient Management (Complicated, pregnant, vomiting, septic, hemodynamically unstable)
* **Empiric IV Regimen**:
  * **First-line**: **Ceftriaxone** 1–2 g IV daily.
  * **Pseudomonas risk** (healthcare contact, indwelling catheter, instrumentation): **Piperacillin-Tazobactam** 4.5 g IV q6h OR **Ceftazidime** 2 g IV q8h.
  * **ESBL risk** (prior history, recent broad-spectrum antibiotics): **Meropenem** 1 g IV q8h OR Ertapenem 1 g IV daily.
* **Transition to Oral Therapy**:
  * Once patient is afebrile for 24-48 hours and tolerating oral intake.
  * Complete a total course of **10–14 days**.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **WBC Casts**: Casts form in renal tubules. Their presence in urine rules out simple lower cystitis and points directly to renal parenchymal involvement (pyelonephritis or acute interstitial nephritis).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Pregnancy Warning**: **Pyelonephritis in pregnancy requires immediate hospitalization and IV antibiotics** (Ceftriaxone is safe). Increased risk of preterm labor and ARDS. Fluoroquinolones and TMP-SMX (in 1st/3rd trimesters) are contraindicated.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Proteus and Stones**: *Proteus mirabilis* produces urease $\rightarrow$ alkaline urine pH ($>7.5$) $\rightarrow$ precipitation of magnesium ammonium phosphate $\rightarrow$ **struvite staghorn calculi**. Antibiotics cannot cure infection unless stone is surgically removed.
