# 🍄 Chronic Pulmonary Fungal Infection (การติดเชื้อราเรื้อรังของปอด)

> **Definition**: Long-standing pulmonary parenchymal infection caused by fungal pathogens (most commonly *Aspergillus* species) occurring in patients with pre-existing structural lung disease (e.g., prior TB, COPD, bronchiectasis) or mild immunocompromise.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Classification

* **Causative Agent**: *Aspergillus fumigatus* (most common).
* **Transmission**: Inhalation of ubiquitous environmental conidia (spores).
* **Clinical Spectrum of Pulmonary Aspergillosis**:

### 1. Simple Aspergilloma (Fungus Ball)
* **Pathophysiology**: Conidia colonize **pre-existing lung cavity** (e.g., healed TB cavity, bronchiectasis, sarcoidosis). Spores germinate, forming mass of hyphae, fibrin, mucus, and cellular debris inside cavity. **Non-invasive**.
* **Complication**: Erosion into cavity wall blood vessels causes **severe, life-threatening hemoptysis** (bronchial artery origin).

### 2. Chronic Pulmonary Aspergillosis (CPA)
* **Pathophysiology**: Semi-invasive, slowly progressive destruction of lung parenchyma in non-immunocompromised patients with underlying lung disease.
* **Subtypes**: Chronic cavitary pulmonary aspergillosis (CCPA), chronic fibrosing pulmonary aspergillosis (CFPA).

### 3. Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA)
* **Pathophysiology**: **Hypersensitivity reaction** (Type I and Type IV) to *Aspergillus* colonization in bronchial tree. Occurs in asthma or cystic fibrosis patients. Not an invasive tissue infection.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Simple Aspergilloma**: Often asymptomatic. Hemoptysis is most common symptom (ranges from mild streaking to massive, life-threatening hemoptysis).
* **CPA**: Weight loss, chronic productive cough, hemoptysis, fatigue, low-grade fever, progressive dyspnea. Mimics pulmonary TB.
* **ABPA**: Uncontrolled asthma, recurrent wheezing, productive cough with **brown mucoid plugs**, fever, and episodic pulmonary infiltrates.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. Simple Aspergilloma & CPA
* **Chest CT (Gold Standard for Imaging)**:
  * **Aspergilloma**: Well-defined, mobile, gravity-dependent mass inside a cavity, outlined by a crescent of air (**Monod sign** or **air crescent sign**).
  * **CPA**: One or more cavities showing thick walls, pleural thickening, pericavitary infiltrates, or fungal balls.
* **Aspergillus-specific IgG Serology**: Diagnostic hallmark. Positive in $>90\%$ of CPA/aspergilloma cases. Differentiates Aspergillus infection from TB.
* **Microbiology**:
  * **Sputum Fungal Culture**: Grows *Aspergillus* (septate hyphae branching at **acute $45^\circ$ angles**). Low sensitivity, but positive culture confirms species.
  * **Galactomannan Antigen**: Detected in serum or Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) fluid. High specificity for invasive disease.

### 2. ABPA Diagnostic Criteria
* History of asthma or cystic fibrosis.
* Elevated **total serum IgE** (typically $>1,000$ IU/mL).
* Elevated **Aspergillus-specific IgE and IgG**.
* Immediate skin reactivity to Aspergillus antigen.
* Peripheral blood **eosinophilia** ($>500/\mu\text{L}$).
* Chest CT: **Central bronchiectasis** (finger-in-glove sign representing mucus impaction).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Aspergilloma
* **Asymptomatic**: Observation.
* **Symptomatic (Massive Hemoptysis)**:
  * **First-line (Definitive)**: **Surgical resection** (lobectomy or segmentectomy).
  * **Alternative (Unfit for Surgery)**: **Bronchial Artery Embolization (BAE)** to control bleeding. Instillation of intracavitary amphotericin B is rarely used.

### 2. Chronic Pulmonary Aspergillosis (CPA)
* **First-line Antifungal Therapy**: **Oral Triazoles** (Itraconazole 200 mg PO bid OR Voriconazole 200 mg PO bid) for **minimum 6 months**.
* **Alternative (Intolerant/Resistant)**: IV Amphotericin B or IV Micafungin.

### 3. ABPA
* **Goal**: Decrease immunological inflammatory response.
* **Treatment**: **Oral Corticosteroids** (Prednisolone 0.5 mg/kg/day tapered over 3-6 months) combined with **Itraconazole** 200 mg PO daily/bid (to reduce fungal antigen load).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Fungal Morphology**:
  > * ***Aspergillus***: **Septate hyphae** with **acute-angle ($45^\circ$) branching**.
  > * ***Mucor / Rhizopus*** (Mucormycosis): **Non-septate (coenocytic) hyphae** with **right-angle ($90^\circ$) branching**. (Requires emergency surgical debridement + IV Amphotericin B).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Voriconazole Side Effects**:
  > * Transient **visual disturbances** (blurred vision, altered color perception, photophobia) in $30\%$ of patients.
  > * **Photosensitivity skin rash** (warn patient to use sunscreen; long-term use associated with squamous cell carcinoma).
  > * Hepatotoxicity and prolonged QTc.
