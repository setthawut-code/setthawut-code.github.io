# 🦠 Tuberculosis (วัณโรค)

> **Definition**: Chronic infectious disease caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* complex, presenting as necrotizing granulomatous inflammation primarily in lungs but can disseminate to any organ.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Transmission

* **Transmission**: Inhalation of airborne droplet nuclei containing *M. tuberculosis* expelled by active pulmonary TB patients.
* **Primary Infection**:
  * Bacteria engulfed by alveolar macrophages. Evade phagolysosomal fusion.
  * Local multiplication $\rightarrow$ **Ghon focus** (subpleural inflammatory lesion, usually mid-to-lower lung zones).
  * Spread to hilar lymph nodes $\rightarrow$ **Ghon complex** (Ghon focus + regional lymphadenopathy).
  * Calcification of Ghon complex $\rightarrow$ **Ranke complex**.
* **Latent TB Infection (LTBI)**:
  * Cell-mediated immunity (CD4+ T cells, IFN-gamma, TNF-alpha) walls off bacteria in solid, non-necrotizing granulomas.
  * Patient asymptomatic, non-infectious, positive TST/IGRA.
* **Reactivation (Post-Primary) TB**:
  * Occurs when immune surveillance declines (HIV, aging, TNF-alpha inhibitors, steroids, malnutrition).
  * Reactivation typically in **apical and posterior segments of upper lobes** (high oxygen tension).
  * Delayed-type hypersensitivity reaction causes caseous necrosis and **cavitary lesions**. Erosion into airways leads to hematogenous spread and infectious aerosol production.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Pulmonary TB (Classic Presentation)
* **Symptoms**: Chronic productive cough $>2-3$ weeks, hemoptysis, low-grade fever (classically diurnal, rising in afternoon/evening), drenching night sweats, progressive weight loss, cachexia, anorexia.
* **Signs**: Dullness to percussion, decreased breath sounds, or post-tussive apical crackles.

### 2. Extrapulmonary TB (EPTB)
* **TB Lymphadenitis (Scrofula)**: Most common EPTB. Painless, matted cervical lymphadenopathy.
* **Pleural TB**: Exudative, lymphocyte-predominant pleural effusion with high adenosine deaminase (ADA) levels.
* **TB Meningitis**: Subacute presentation, basilar meningeal enhancement, cranial neuropathies. CSF: lymphocytic pleocytosis, high protein, very low glucose.
* **Pott's Disease (TB Spondylitis)**: Hematogenous spread to spine. Destroys intervertebral discs and adjacent vertebral bodies, causing kyphosis (gibbus deformity) and psoas abscess.
* **Renal TB**: Presentation of **sterile pyuria** (WBCs in urine with negative routine bacterial culture).
* **Miliary TB**: Hematogenous dissemination. Millet seed-like micronodular lesions throughout lungs, liver, spleen, bone marrow.

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Sputum Examination (for suspected Pulmonary TB)**:
  * **Sputum AFB Smear**: Acid-fast bacilli identified by Ziehl-Neelsen or auramine-rhodamine staining. Requires 3 consecutive morning specimens. Fast, but low sensitivity ($50-60\%$).
  * **GeneXpert MTB/RIF (PCR)**: Rapid molecular test. Detects *M. tuberculosis* DNA and Rifampicin resistance (mutations in *rpoB* gene) within 2 hours. Preferred initial test for suspected TB/drug resistance.
  * **Mycobacterial Culture**: Gold standard. Liquid media (MGIT: 1-2 weeks) or solid media (Lowenstein-Jensen: 3-8 weeks). Required for definitive drug susceptibility testing (DST).
* **Chest X-Ray**:
  * **Primary TB**: Hilar/mediastinal lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion, or patchy consolidation (middle/lower lobes).
  * **Reactivation TB**: Apical/posterior upper lobe infiltrates, fibronodular lesions, and **cavitation**.
  * **Miliary TB**: Diffuse, symmetric 1-3 mm micronodular (millet-seed) pattern.
* **Screening for Latent TB**:
  * **Tuberculin Skin Test (TST / Mantoux)**: Intradermal injection of PPD. Measure induration at 48-72 hours. Cutoffs: $\ge 5$ mm (HIV, close contacts, immunosuppressed), $\ge 10$ mm (healthcare workers, high-prevalence countries), $\ge 15$ mm (no risk factors).
  * **Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (IGRA)**: Blood test measuring IFN-gamma release by T cells exposed to TB antigens. No cross-reactivity with BCG vaccine.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Drug-Susceptible Active TB Regimen
* **Standard 6-Month Regimen**:
  * **Intensive Phase (2 Months)**: **HRZE** (Isoniazid + Rifampicin + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol) daily.
  * **Continuation Phase (4 Months)**: **HR** (Isoniazid + Rifampicin) daily.
  * *Note*: Extend continuation phase to 7 months (total 9 months) if cavitary pulmonary TB and culture remains positive at month 2, or if Pyrazinamide was not included in intensive phase.
  * *Note*: Extend total treatment to 9-12 months for TB Meningitis or Bone/Joint TB.

### 2. First-Line Antituberculosis Drug Profiles

| Drug | Abbr. | Key Adverse Effects | Clinical Action / Monitoring |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Isoniazid** | **H** | • **Peripheral neuropathy** (due to Vitamin B6 excretion)<br>• Hepatotoxicity<br>• Drug-induced Lupus | • **Co-administer Pyridoxine (Vit B6)** 10-50 mg/day in high-risk patients (diabetes, pregnancy, HIV, renal failure, malnutrition)<br>• Monitor LFTs |
| **Rifampicin** | **R** | • **Orange-red discoloration** of urine, sweat, tears<br>• Hepatotoxicity<br>• Potent **CYP450 inducer** | • Warn patients about fluid color changes (stains contact lenses)<br>• Check drug interactions (decreases efficacy of OCPs, Warfarin, HIV ARVs) |
| **Pyrazinamide** | **Z** | • **Hepatotoxicity** (most severe)<br>• **Hyperuricemia** (gout)<br>• Arthralgias | • Avoid in acute gout or severe liver disease<br>• Monitor uric acid and LFTs |
| **Ethambutol** | **E** | • **Optic neuritis** (decreased visual acuity, red-green color blindness) | • **Not hepatotoxic**<br>• Baseline and periodic visual acuity and color vision testing<br>• Contraindicated in young children unable to test |

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **TNF-alpha Blockers**: TNF-alpha is required to maintain granuloma structure. Use of TNF-alpha inhibitors (e.g., Infliximab, Adalimumab) causes granuloma breakdown and catastrophic reactivation of latent TB (often extrapulmonary or miliary). Always screen for LTBI with TST/IGRA before starting biologics.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Hepatotoxicity Management**: If AST/ALT $>3\times$ upper limit of normal (ULN) with symptoms (nausea, vomiting, jaundice), or $>5\times$ ULN without symptoms: **stop HRZ immediately**. Switch to non-hepatotoxic regimen (e.g., Ethambutol + Levofloxacin + Aminoglycoside) until LFTs normalize, then reintroduce drugs sequentially (Rifampicin, then Isoniazid, then Pyrazinamide).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Steroid Use in TB**: Add adjunctive corticosteroids (Prednisone or Dexamethasone) in **TB Meningitis** and **TB Pericarditis** to reduce mortality and constrictive complications.
