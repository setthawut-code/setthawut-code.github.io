# 🦠 Pyothorax / Empyema Thoracis (ภาวะมีหนองในโพรงเยื่อหุ้มปอด)

> **Definition**: Pyothorax, also referred to as **Empyema Thoracis**, is defined as the accumulation of purulent fluid (pus) within the pleural cavity, typically arising as a complication of untreated or severe pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Stages
Parapneumonic effusions and empyema progress through three distinct pathophysiological stages:
1. **Exudative Stage (Simple Parapneumonic Effusion)**: 
   * Local parenchymal inflammation increases capillary permeability, leaking sterile fluid into the pleural space. Fluid is free-flowing.
   * *Labs*: **pH $>7.2$**, **glucose $>60\text{ mg/dL}$**, **LDH $<1000\text{ U/L}$**, and negative cultures.
2. **Fibropurulent Stage (Complicated Parapneumonic Effusion)**:
   * Bacterial invasion across the damaged pleura occurs. Fibrin deposition creates meshworks, causing loculations and pockets.
   * *Labs*: **pH $<7.2$**, **glucose $<60\text{ mg/dL}$**, **LDH $>1000\text{ U/L}$**, or positive Gram stain/culture.
3. **Organizing Stage (Empyema)**:
   * Fibroblasts migrate into the pleural space, transforming the fibrin mesh into a thick, inelastic fibrous membrane (**"pleural peel"**). This traps the lung, preventing re-expansion and causing restrictive lung dysfunction. Frank pus is present.

```
                          PNEUMONIA / PARENCHYMAL INFECTION
                                          │
                                          ▼
                      EXUDATIVE STAGE (Simple Effusion)
                      • Sterile, free-flowing fluid
                      • pH > 7.2, Glucose > 60, LDH < 1000
                                          │
                                          ▼
                    FIBROPURULENT STAGE (Complicated Effusion)
                      • Bacterial invasion, fibrin loculations
                      • pH < 7.2, Glucose < 60, LDH > 1000
                                          │
                                          ▼
                        ORGANIZING STAGE (Overt Empyema)
                      • Fibrous "Pleural Peel" (Trapped Lung)
                      • Frank pus, acidic, low glucose
```

## 🦠 Pathogens
Usually **polymicrobial**, mirroring the source (pneumonia or aspiration):
* **Aerobic**: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (including MRSA), and Gram-negative bacilli (*Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*).
* **Anaerobic** (very common in aspiration pneumonia/poor dentition): *Bacteroides* species, *Peptostreptococcus*, and *Fusobacterium nucleatum*.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations
* **Presentation**: Fever, chills, pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain on deep inspiration), dyspnea, productive cough, and systemic symptoms (night sweats, weight loss).
* **Physical Examination**:
  * **Dullness to percussion** over the affected hemithorax.
  * **Decreased tactile fremitus** (fluid insulates sound transmission; contrasts with consolidation where fremitus increases).
  * **Decreased or absent breath sounds** and egophony at the fluid level.
  * Tracheal deviation to the contralateral (opposite) side (in massive effusions).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

### 1. Thoracentesis & Pleural Fluid Analysis (Diagnostic Key)
* **Essential Step**: Perform diagnostic thoracentesis immediately for any pleural effusion associated with pneumonia.
* **Empyema Fluid Criteria**:
  * **Visual**: Frank **pus**, turbid, or putrid-smelling fluid (highly specific for anaerobes).
  * **Biochemical**: **pH $<7.2$**, **Glucose $<60\text{ mg/dL}$**, **LDH $>1000\text{ U/L}$**.
  * **Microbiology**: Positive Gram stain or culture.

### 2. Imaging
* **Chest X-Ray**: Shows pleural effusion. Loculated fluid appears as a fixed, D-shaped or lenticular density along the chest wall that does not shift on lateral decubitus films.
* **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) Chest (Gold Standard)**: 
  * Demonstrates the **"Split Pleura Sign"**: Thickening and enhancement of both the visceral and parietal pleura separated by the pleural fluid.
  * Identifies loculations, guides chest tube placement, and evaluates for pulmonary abscesses.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Pleural Drainage (Mandatory)
* **Indications for Chest Tube (Tube Thoracostomy)**:
  * Presence of frank **pus** or turbid fluid on thoracentesis.
  * Positive Gram stain or culture of pleural fluid.
  * Complicated chemistry: **pH $<7.2$** or **Glucose $<60\text{ mg/dL}$**.
  * Large, free-flowing, or loculated effusions.
* **Intrapleural Fibrinolytics**: For loculated empyema failing to drain. Instill **tPA (Alteplase) + DNase** via the chest tube twice daily for 3 days to dissolve fibrin bands and liquefy purulent debris, avoiding surgery in $>80\%$ of cases.

### 2. Intravenous Antibiotics
* **Regimen**: Cover Gram-positives, Gram-negatives, and anaerobes:
  * **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q24h PLUS Metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h** OR
  * **Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h** OR
  * **Ampicillin-Sulbactam 3 g IV q6h**.
* **Duration**: **4 to 6 weeks** (requires prolonged therapy due to poor antibiotic penetration into the infected pleural space).

### 3. Surgical Debridement
* **Indications**: Failed drainage with chest tube and fibrinolytics, persistent sepsis, or trapped lung (chronic organizing stage).
* **Methods**: **Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery (VATS)** for early loculated stages; **Open Decortication** (peeling off the fibrous layer from the visceral pleura) for chronic organizing empyema.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
     > **The Fremitus Clue**: To differentiate lung consolidation (pneumonia) from pleural effusion (pyothorax) on physical exam:
     > * Both show dullness to percussion and decreased breath sounds.
     > * **Tactile Fremitus** is **increased** over consolidation (solid conducts sound better).
     > * **Tactile Fremitus** is **decreased/absent** over pleural effusion (fluid decouples the chest wall from the lung).
* > [!WARNING]
     > **Low Glucose Pathophysiology**: The extremely low glucose level in empyema fluid ($<60\text{ mg/dL}$, often close to 0) is caused by the active glycolytic metabolism of bacteria and invading neutrophils.
* > [!TIP]
     > **pH Measurement**: Pleural fluid pH must be measured using a blood gas analyzer. Litmus paper or urine dipstick tests are highly inaccurate.
