# 🧫 Pulmonary Nocardiosis (โรคโนคาร์เดียของปอด)

> **Definition**: Opportunistic bacterial infection caused by *Nocardia* species (typically *N. asteroides* complex), presenting as subacute or chronic necrotizing pneumonia with high propensity for hematogenous dissemination.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Microbiology

* **Organism**: *Nocardia* spp. Environmental saprophyte (found in soil/decaying vegetation).
* **Microbiology**:
  * **Gram Stain**: Gram-positive, beaded, branching filamentous bacilli.
  * **Acid-Fastness**: **Weakly (partially) acid-fast** (retains carbolfuchsin dye when decolorized with weak $1\%$ sulfuric acid; modified Kinyoun stain). *Contrast with Mycobacterium (strongly acid-fast, decolorized with $3\%$ HCl) and Actinomyces (non-acid-fast).*
* **Risk Factors**: Predominantly immunocompromised hosts (cell-mediated immunity defects):
  * Chronic corticosteroid use.
  * Solid organ or hematopoietic stem cell transplant.
  * HIV/AIDS (CD4+ $<200$ cells/$\mu\text{L}$).
  * Lymphoma.
  * *Note: Up to 1/3 of cases occur in immunocompetent hosts.*
* **Pathogenesis**: Inhalation of dust/soil $\rightarrow$ alveolar macrophage evasion (inhibits phagolysosome fusion) $\rightarrow$ necrotizing abscesses. Disseminates via blood to **brain** and **skin**.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

### 1. Pulmonary Nocardiosis (Most Common)
* Subacute to chronic pneumonia.
* **Symptoms**: Productive cough (purulent sputum), hemoptysis, pleuritic chest pain, fever, weight loss, night sweats. Mimics lung cancer or tuberculosis.

### 2. Disseminated Nocardiosis
* Defined as infection in $\ge 2$ non-contiguous organs.
* **Central Nervous System (CNS)**: Most common site of dissemination ($44\%$ of cases). Presents as **multiloculated brain abscesses**. Symptoms: headache, focal neurological deficits, seizures, fever.
* **Cutaneous**: Subcutaneous nodules, pustules, non-healing ulcers, or cold abscesses.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Direct Microscopy (Best Initial)**:
  * Sputum or bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid.
  * Gram stain showing **Gram-positive branching filaments**.
  * Modified acid-fast stain (modified Kinyoun) showing **partially acid-fast filaments**.
* **Culture (Gold Standard)**:
  * Aerobic culture. *Nocardia* grows slowly (3 to 14 days, up to 4 weeks).
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Notify Lab**: Must explicitly instruct microbiology lab to hold cultures for 2-4 weeks if Nocardia is suspected, to prevent discarding plates early.
* **Imaging**:
  * **Chest CT**: Non-specific. Shows cavitary nodules, lobar consolidation, pleural effusion, or empyema.
  * **Brain MRI (with Gadolinium)**: **Mandatory** for ALL patients diagnosed with pulmonary or systemic nocardiosis (even if asymptomatic) to screen for silent brain abscesses. Shows ring-enhancing lesions.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Antibiotic Therapy
* **First-line Agent**: **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)**.
  * High-dose therapy: $15$ mg/kg/day of TMP component (usually 2 double-strength tablets PO/IV q8h).

### 2. Treatment Regimens by Severity

| Severity | Regimen | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mild / Isolated Pulmonary** (Immunocompetent) | • **TMP-SMX** monotherapy | • **6 months** |
| **Severe Pulmonary / Disseminated** (CNS excluded / Immunocompromised) | • **TMP-SMX** + **Amikacin** OR<br>• **TMP-SMX** + **Imipenem** (or Meropenem) | • **6–12 months** |
| **CNS Nocardiosis / Brain Abscess** | • Triple therapy: **TMP-SMX** + **Imipenem** + **Linezolid** | • **12 months** |

### 3. Surgical Intervention
* Indicated for large, accessible brain abscesses, empyema, or large subcutaneous abscesses.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!WARNING]
  > **Nocardia vs. Actinomyces**:
  > * **Nocardia**: Aerobe, **weakly acid-fast**, environmental source, infects immunocompromised, no sulfur granules. Treatment = **TMP-SMX**.
  > * **Actinomyces**: Anaerobe, **non-acid-fast**, normal oral flora, forms **sulfur granules** (yellow clumps in pus). Treatment = **Penicillin G**.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Sulfonamide Allergy**: If patient has severe sulfa allergy or develops severe myelosuppression/renal failure from TMP-SMX, use **Linezolid** or **Ceftriaxone + Amikacin** as alternative.
