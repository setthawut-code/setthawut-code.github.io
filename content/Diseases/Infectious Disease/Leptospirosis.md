# 🦠 Leptospirosis (โรคไข้ฉี่หนู)

> **Definition**: A worldwide zoonotic bacterial infection caused by pathogenic spirochetes of the genus *Leptospira*, presenting as a biphasic illness ranging from mild febrile syndrome to life-threatening multiorgan failure (**Weil's Disease**).

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Transmission

* **Organism**: *Leptospira interrogans* (coiled, hook-ended, highly motile spirochete).
* **Reservoirs**: Rodents (rats, most common), dogs, livestock. Organisms colonize **renal tubules** of hosts and are shed in urine.
* **Transmission**: Direct contact of broken skin or mucous membranes with water, wet soil, or mud contaminated with urine of infected animal carriers (classic during floods, farming, or water sports).
* **Pathogenesis**: 
  1. Spirochetes penetrate tissues $\rightarrow$ enter blood (**leptospiremic phase**).
  2. Disseminate to all organs, causing **systemic vasculitis** (endothelial damage).
  3. Deposition of immune complexes leads to tissue injury (**leptospiruric/immune phase**).
  4. Tubular injury in kidneys causes impairment of sodium/potassium absorption (non-oliguric hypokalemic AKI).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

Classically biphasic (Anicteric vs. Icteric form):

### 1. Anicteric Leptospirosis ($90\%$ of cases)
* **First Phase (Septicemic Phase, 4-9 days)**:
  * Abrupt high fever, chills, severe frontal headache, retro-orbital pain.
  * **Severe Myalgia**: Classically involving **calves** and **lower back**. Extremely tender to palpation.
  * **Conjunctival Suffusion**: Bilateral conjunctival injection/redness **without purulent discharge** (highly specific clue).
  * Vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain.
* **Second Phase (Immune Phase, 6-30 days)**:
  * Fever recurs after a brief afebrile period.
  * Aseptic meningitis (lymphocytic pleocytosis in CSF).
  * Uveitis, rash.

### 2. Weil's Disease (Severe Icteric Leptospirosis, $5-10\%$)
Severe form with rapid onset of multiorgan failure, presenting as a triad:
* **Jaundice**: Deep, orange-colored jaundice due to hepatocellular dysfunction.
* **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**: Classically **non-oliguric** and **hypokalemic** initially (due to proximal tubule damage leading to wasting of $K^+$), progressing to oliguric AKI.
* **Hemorrhagic Diathesis**: Pulmonary hemorrhage (presents as hemoptysis, severe dyspnea, bilateral infiltrates; high mortality), epistaxis, purpura, GI bleeding.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Serology (Gold Standard)**:
  * **Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT)**: Confirms diagnosis by showing a $\ge 4$-fold rise in antibody titer between acute and convalescent sera (or a single titer $\ge 1:400$ in endemic areas).
  * **IgM ELISA**: Rapid screening test, positive after day 5.
* **PCR**: Detects Leptospira DNA. Positive in blood/CSF during first 7 days (septicemic phase) and in urine after day 7 (immune phase).
* **Routine Labs**:
  * **Complete Blood Count**: Neutrophilic leukocytosis, **thrombocytopenia** (indicates severe disease).
  * **Creatine Kinase (CK)**: **Elevated** (due to severe muscle necrosis/myositis; differentiates from dengue/malaria).
  * **LFTs**: **Bilirubin-Transaminase Dissociation**: Markedly elevated bilirubin (conjugated) with only mild-to-moderate elevations in AST/ALT ($<200-500$ U/L) and elevated Alkaline Phosphatase.
  * **Renal Panel**: Elevated BUN/Creatinine, **hypokalemia** (early AKI).

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Mild Leptospirosis (Outpatient)
* **Antibiotics** (for 7 days):
  * **Doxycycline** 100 mg PO bid.
  * **Amoxicillin** 500 mg PO tid (preferred in pregnant women/children).

### 2. Severe Leptospirosis (Inpatient)
* **Antibiotics** (for 7 days):
  * **Penicillin G** 1.5 million units IV q6h.
  * **Ceftriaxone** 1–2 g IV qd (equally effective, easier dosing).
* **Supportive Care**:
  * Hemodialysis for acute kidney injury.
  * Careful fluid management (avoid volume overload if pulmonary hemorrhage is suspected).
  * High-frequency ventilation for severe pulmonary hemorrhage syndrome.

### 3. Prophylaxis
* **Doxycycline** 200 mg PO once weekly (for high-risk individuals with short-term exposure, e.g., disaster relief workers during floods).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Calf Tenderness & Red Eyes**: A patient presenting with high fever, severe calf pain preventing walking, and bilateral red eyes without discharge has **Leptospirosis** until proven otherwise.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Bilirubin-ALT Dissociation**: If a patient has deep jaundice (bilirubin $>15$ mg/dL) but transaminases are only minimally elevated ($100-300$ U/L) in the setting of acute fever, think **Weil's Disease**, NOT viral hepatitis.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction**: Can occur within hours of starting IV penicillin or ceftriaxone. Monitor patient closely for sudden hypotension and fever spike; treat supportively.
