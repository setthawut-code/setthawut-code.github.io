# 🦠 Bacterial Meningitis (เยื่อหุ้มสมองอักเสบจากเชื้อแบคทีเรีย)

> **Definition**: Acute, life-threatening bacterial infection of the leptomeninges (pia mater, arachnoid mater) and the subarachnoid space CSF, presenting as rapid-onset meningeal irritation and systemic toxicity.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Key Pathogens

* **Pathogenesis**: Nasopharyngeal colonization $\rightarrow$ mucosal invasion $\rightarrow$ bacteremic dissemination $\rightarrow$ crossing blood-brain barrier (BBB) $\rightarrow$ replication in CSF (low complement/antibody levels). Inflammatory response causes vasculitis, cerebral edema, increased intracranial pressure (ICP), and decreased cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP).
* **Common Pathogens by Age Group**:

| Age / Population | Key Bacterial Pathogens | Empiric Antibiotic Regimen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neonates** ($<1$ month) | • *Streptococcus agalactiae* (GBS)<br>• *Escherichia coli*<br>• *Listeria monocytogenes* | • Ampicillin + Gentamicin (or Cefotaxime) |
| **Infants & Adults** ($1$ mo – $50$ yr) | • ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** (most common)<br>• ***Neisseria meningitidis*** (epidemics, rash) | • **Ceftriaxone** 2 g IV q12h + **Vancomycin** 15-20 mg/kg IV q8-12h |
| **Elderly** ($>50$ yr) / **Immunocompromised** | • *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*<br>• ***Listeria monocytogenes***<br>• Gram-negative bacilli | • **Ceftriaxone** 2 g IV q12h + **Vancomycin** + **Ampicillin** 2 g IV q4h |
| **Penetrating Trauma / Neurosurgery** | • *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA)<br>• *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* | • Vancomycin + **Ceftazidime** 2 g IV q8h (or Meropenem) |

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Classic Triad**: **Fever**, **altered mental status**, and **neck stiffness (nuchal rigidity)**. (Present in $\sim 44\%$ of cases; however, $>95\%$ have at least two of: headache, fever, neck stiffness, and altered mental status).
* **Meningeal Signs**:
  * **Kernig's Sign**: Flex hip and knee to $90^\circ$, then attempt to extend knee. Positive if extension elicits pain/resistance in hamstrings.
  * **Brudzinski's Sign**: Involuntary flexion of hips and knees when the neck is passively flexed.
* **Signs of Raised ICP**: Severe headache, projectile vomiting, papilledema, cranial nerve palsies (especially CN VI palsy), seizures, Cushing's triad (bradycardia, hypertension, irregular respirations; suggests impending herniation).
* **Skin Findings**: Petechial or purpuric rash (maculopathic) points to ***Neisseria meningitidis*** (see [[Meningococcemia]]).

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Lumbar Puncture (LP)**: **First-line diagnostic test**. Perform immediately unless contraindicated.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **CT Scan Prior to LP**: Must obtain a Head CT scan before performing LP **only** if the patient has any of the following (risk of brain herniation):
  > 1. Focal neurological deficits (e.g., hemiparesis, CN palsy).
  > 2. Papilledema.
  > 3. New-onset seizures (within 1 week).
  > 4. Immunocompromised state (HIV/AIDS, transplant, immunosuppressive therapy).
  > 5. History of CNS disease (stroke, mass lesion, focal infection).
  > 6. Severe alteration in consciousness.
  > *If CT is required, **obtain blood cultures and administer empiric antibiotics + dexamethasone immediately**, then send for CT. Do not delay therapy for imaging.*

### CSF Analysis Differential

| Parameter | Normal | Bacterial Meningitis | Viral Meningitis | Fungal / TB Meningitis |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Opening Press.** | $50–200$ | **Elevated** ($>200-500$ mm $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) | Normal or mild elevation | Elevated |
| **WBC Count** | $<5$ | **Very High** ($1,000–10,000/\mu\text{L}$) | Mild ($10–500/\mu\text{L}$) | Moderate ($50–500/\mu\text{L}$) |
| **WBC Diff** | Lymphs | **PMNs ($\ge 80\%$)** | Lymphocytes | **Lymphocytes** |
| **Protein** | $<40$ | **Very High** ($>100–500$ mg/dL) | Normal or mild ($<100$) | High ($>100–500$ mg/dL) |
| **Glucose** | $>40$ | **Very Low** ($<40$ mg/dL)<br>(CSF:blood ratio $<0.4$) | Normal (CSF:blood ratio $\ge 0.6$) | **Low** (CSF:blood ratio $<0.3-0.4$) |
| **Gram Stain** | Negative | **Positive in $60-90\%$** | Negative | Negative |

* **Additional Tests**: CSF PCR (multiplex panel for bacterial DNA), CSF Latex Agglutination, Blood Cultures (positive in $50-80\%$).

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Dexamethasone Therapy
* **Rationale**: Reduces subarachnoid space inflammatory response triggered by bacterial lysis after antibiotic administration, decreasing rates of **sensorineural hearing loss**, focal neurological deficits, and mortality in *S. pneumoniae* meningitis.
* **Dosing**: **10 mg IV** administered **prior to or concurrently with the first dose of antibiotics** (do not give if antibiotics have already been administered).
* **Duration**: 4 days (continue only if culture grows *S. pneumoniae* or *H. influenzae*; stop if other pathogens).

### 2. Antimicrobial Therapy (Empiric)
* Start immediately. Use high doses of bactericidal drugs that penetrate blood-brain barrier:
  * **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h** (or Cefotaxime 2 g IV q4-6h) + **Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg IV q8-12h** (targeting drug-resistant *S. pneumoniae*).
  * Add **Ampicillin 2 g IV q4h** if age $>50$, immunocompromised, or pregnant (to cover *Listeria*).
* Adjust therapy once CSF culture/sensitivities are available.

### 3. Chemoprophylaxis for Contacts
* Indicated for close contacts of patients with ***Neisseria meningitidis*** or ***Haemophilus influenzae***.
* **Regimens (Meningococcal)**:
  * **Rifampin** 600 mg PO bid for 2 days OR
  * **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg PO single dose OR
  * **Ceftriaxone** 250 mg IM single dose (preferred in pregnancy).

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!WARNING]
  > ***Listeria* Cephalosporin Resistance**: *Listeria monocytogenes* (Gram-positive rod, exhibits tumbling motility) is **intrinsically resistant to all cephalosporins** (including Ceftriaxone). Failure to add **Ampicillin** in patients $>50$ years or immunocompromised can be fatal.
* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Antibiotic Timing**: Never delay antibiotics for a lumbar puncture or a Head CT scan. If LP is delayed, take blood cultures and administer Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin + Dexamethasone immediately.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Droplet Isolation**: Place patients with suspected meningococcal or *H. influenzae* meningitis under respiratory droplet precautions for the first 24 hours of effective antibiotic therapy.
