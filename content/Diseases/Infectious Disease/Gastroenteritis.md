# 🦠 Gastroenteritis (กระเพาะอาหารและลำไส้อักเสบ)

> **Definition**: Acute inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract mucosa, presenting as diarrhea ($\ge 3$ loose/watery stools in 24 hours) with or without vomiting, abdominal pain, or fever.

---

## 🦠 Classification & Pathophysiology

Divided into non-inflammatory (watery/secretory) and inflammatory (bloody/invasive) diarrhea:

### 1. Non-Inflammatory (Watery) Diarrhea
* **Mechanism**: Fecal-oral. Non-invasive pathogens secrete enterotoxins that stimulate fluid secretion (increases cAMP/cGMP/intracellular calcium) in small intestine, impairing absorption. No mucosal destruction.
* **Key Pathogens**:
  * ***Vibrio cholerae***: Cholera toxin permanently activates Gs protein $\rightarrow$ adenylate cyclase $\rightarrow$ cAMP $\rightarrow$ massive Cl⁻ and water secretion. Presents with painless, voluminous **"rice-water" stools** leading to rapid hypovolemic shock.
  * **Enterotoxigenic *E. coli* (ETEC)**: Most common cause of **Traveler's diarrhea**. Produces heat-labile (LT, increases cAMP) and heat-stable (ST, increases cGMP) toxins.
  * ***Staphylococcus aureus***: Rapid onset ($1–6$ hours) of severe vomiting/cramps after ingestion of preformed enterotoxin (found in mayonnaise, meats). Heat-stable toxin.
  * ***Bacillus cereus***: Emetic form (onset $1-5$ hours; preformed toxin in reheated fried rice) or diarrheal form (onset $8-16$ hours).
  * **Viruses (Norovirus / Rotavirus)**: Norovirus is leading cause in all ages (outbreaks in closed spaces, e.g., cruise ships, schools). Rotavirus common in young children.

### 2. Inflammatory (Bloody/Dysentery) Diarrhea
* **Mechanism**: Pathogens invade large bowel mucosa or produce cytotoxins, causing mucosal damage, ulceration, and inflammatory response (fecal PMNs).
* **Key Pathogens**:
  * ***Shigella*** spp. (*S. dysenteriae*): Very low infectious dose. Produces **Shiga toxin** (cleaves 28S rRNA, inhibiting protein synthesis). Causes high fever, crampy pain, **tenesmus**, and bloody/mucoid diarrhea. Associated with **Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)** and seizures in children.
  * ***Campylobacter jejuni***: Associated with undercooked poultry/unpasteurized milk. Curved, Gram-negative rod ("gull-wing" shape). Causes bloody diarrhea. Associated with **Guillain-Barré Syndrome** (molecular mimicry) and Reactive Arthritis.
  * **Enterohemorrhagic *E. coli* (EHEC / STEC)**: Serotype **O157:H7**. Transmitted via undercooked ground beef. Produces Shiga-like toxin. Presents with severe bloody diarrhea **WITHOUT fever**. High risk of **HUS**.
  * ***Salmonella enteritidis***: Inflammatory diarrhea linked to poultry, eggs, or reptile exposure.
  * ***Clostridioides difficile***: Antibiotic-associated pseudomembranous colitis (triggered by Clindamycin, Cephalosporins, Fluoroquinolones). Produces Toxin A (enterotoxin) and Toxin B (cytotoxin).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

* **Watery Diarrhea**: Large volume, watery stools, periumbilical cramping, bloating, nausea, vomiting. Mild or absent fever. Dehydration is main risk.
* **Bloody Diarrhea (Dysentery)**: Small volume stools containing blood/mucus, severe lower abdominal cramps, tenesmus (constant urge to defecate), high fever, systemic toxicity.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Uncomplicated Case**: Self-limiting; no diagnostics needed.
* **Indications for Workup**: Severe dehydration, fever $>38.5^\circ\text{C}$, bloody diarrhea, symptoms $>7$ days, immunocompromised state, or suspected outbreak.
* **Stool Tests**:
  * **Fecal Leukocytes/Lactoferrin**: Positive in inflammatory/invasive diarrhea.
  * **Stool Culture**: Identifies bacterial pathogens.
  * **Stool PCR (Multiplex Panel)**: Rapid detection of viral, bacterial, and parasitic DNA.
  * ***C. difficile* Toxin Assay**: Perform glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH) antigen and Toxin A/B PCR or EIA in patients with recent antibiotic exposure.

---

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Fluid Rehydration (First-line / Essential)
* **Mild-Moderate Dehydration**: **Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)**.
* **Severe Dehydration** (lethargy, hypotension, delayed capillary refill): **IV fluids** (Lactated Ringer's or Normal Saline) with rapid infusion.

### 2. Symptomatic Therapy
* **Antimotility Agents (Loperamide)**: 
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **Loperamide Contraindication**: Do NOT use antimotility agents in patients with **bloody/inflammatory diarrhea, high fever, or suspected *C. difficile***. Inhibiting motility delays clearance of toxins/pathogens, risking **toxic megacolon** and worsening systemic toxicity.

### 3. Antimicrobial Therapy
* **Empiric Therapy**: Indicated only for severe febrile illness, bloody diarrhea, or immunocompromised hosts.
  * **First-line**: **Azithromycin** 500 mg PO qd for 3 days (preferred in Southeast Asia due to high *Campylobacter* fluoroquinolone resistance) OR **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg PO bid for 3 days.
* ***C. difficile* Treatment**:
  * Discontinue causative antibiotic.
  * **First-line**: **Oral Vancomycin 125 mg qid for 10 days** OR **Fidaxomicin** 200 mg PO bid for 10 days.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **EHEC Treatment Warning**: **Do NOT give antibiotics to patients with suspected EHEC (O157:H7) infection**. Antibiotics stress bacteria, increasing Shiga-like toxin release, which significantly increases the risk of developing **HUS** (triad: microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, acute kidney injury).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Cholera Fluid Management**: Fluid replacement volume must match ongoing stool losses. "Cholera cots" are used to measure diarrhea volume in real-time.
