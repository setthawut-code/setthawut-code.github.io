# 🦠 Typhoid and Paratyphoid Fever (ไข้ไทฟอยด์และไข้พาราไทฟอยด์)

> **Definition**: Systemic infection (Enteric Fever) caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi or Paratyphi (A, B, or C), characterized by prolonged fever, abdominal symptoms, and potential for severe gastrointestinal complications.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Transmission

* **Transmission**: Fecal-oral route (ingestion of food or water contaminated by feces/urine of active cases or chronic carriers).
* **Pathogenesis**:
  1. Ingestion $\rightarrow$ invasion of intestinal mucosa via **M cells** in **Peyer's patches** (distal ileum).
  2. Phagocytosed by mononuclear cells (macrophages). Relies on intracellular survival mechanisms to evade killing.
  3. Dissemination via lymphatics to mesenteric lymph nodes, then thoracic duct, into bloodstream (**primary bacteremia**).
  4. Taken up by reticuloendothelial system (liver, spleen, bone marrow, and gallbladder).
  5. Multiplies in gallbladder (excreted in bile back into bowel lumen).
  6. Re-entry into bloodstream (**secondary bacteremia**). Hyper-inflammatory immune response in Peyer's patches leads to necrosis, ulceration, and potential hemorrhage/perforation.
* **Chronic Carrier State**: Defined as excretion of bacteria in stool/urine for $>1$ year. Resides in **gallbladder** (associated with biofilms on gallstones).

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

Classically unfolds over 3-4 weeks if untreated:

* **Week 1**:
  * Abrupt or insidious onset of **step-ladder fever** (escalates daily).
  * Frontal headache, dry cough, myalgia.
  * **Relative Bradycardia** (**Faget's sign**: slow pulse relative to high body temperature).
  * Constipation (more common than diarrhea initially) or mild abdominal discomfort.
* **Week 2**:
  * Persistent high fever.
  * **Rose Spots**: Transient, blanching, erythematous macules (2-4 mm) on chest and abdomen (lasts 2-3 days).
  * Apathetic, confused state ("typhoid state").
  * Hepatosplenomegaly, abdominal distension.
  * **"Pea-soup" diarrhea**.
* **Week 3 (Complications)**:
  * **Intestinal Hemorrhage**: Erosion of Peyer's patches into ileal vessels. Presents as sudden drop in temperature, tachycardia, and melena/hematochezia.
  * **Intestinal Perforation**: Typicallly in distal ileum. Presents with acute abdomen (peritonitis, guard, rigidity, free air under diaphragm).
  * **Typhoid Encephalopathy**: Delirium, stupor, coma (associated with high mortality).
* **Week 4**: Defervescence and recovery, or chronic carriage.

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Cultures (Gold Standard)**:
  * **Bone Marrow Culture**: Most sensitive ($>90\%$), remains positive even after antibiotic initiation.
  * **Blood Culture**: Positive in $60-80\%$ of cases during Week 1-2.
  * **Stool Culture**: Positive during Week 2-3 (useful for carrier screening).
  * **Urine Culture**: Positive during Week 3.
* **Widal Test (Serology)**:
  * Measures agglutinating antibodies against Salmonella O (somatic) and H (flagellar) antigens.
  * **Poor sensitivity and specificity** (high false-positive rates due to cross-reactivity, vaccination, or endemic exposure). Not recommended for definitive diagnosis.
* **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**:
  * **Leukopenia** with relative lymphocytosis (leukocytosis suggests intestinal perforation).
  * Thrombocytopenia.
  * Mild transaminitis (raised AST/ALT) and elevated ESR.

## 🏥 Management

### 1. Empirical Treatment (Based on Local Resistance)
* **Uncomplicated Enteric Fever**:
  * **First-line**: **Ceftriaxone** 2 g IV qd for 7–14 days OR **Azithromycin** 500–1000 mg PO qd for 5–7 days (effective against fluoroquinolone-resistant strains).
  * **Fluoroquinolones**: **Ciprofloxacin** 500 mg PO bid for 7–10 days (use only if susceptibility is confirmed; high rates of resistance in Asia).
* **Severe Enteric Fever (Encephalopathy, Shock, Peritonitis)**:
  * **First-line**: **Dexamethasone** (initial dose 3 mg/kg IV over 30 mins, followed by 1 mg/kg q6h for 48 hours) administered *before* antibiotics to reduce mortality. Combine with IV Ceftriaxone or Carbapenem.

### 2. Chronic Carrier Treatment
* **Antibiotics**: **Ciprofloxacin** 750 mg PO bid OR **Amoxicillin** 100 mg/kg/day PO (plus probenecid) for **4–6 weeks**.
* **Surgery**: **Cholecystectomy** required if gallstones are present, as antibiotics alone cannot eradicate bacteria embedded in gallstone biofilms.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Faget's Sign (Relative Bradycardia)**: Heart rate disproportionately slow for the degree of fever. Other causes: Yellow fever, Leptospirosis, Legionnaires' disease, Mycoplasma, and Brucellosis.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Avoid NSAIDs**: Do not use NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Aspirin) for fever control in typhoid patients due to increased risk of gastrointestinal bleeding. Use Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) instead.
