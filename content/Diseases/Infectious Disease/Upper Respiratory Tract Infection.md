# 🦠 Upper Respiratory Tract Infection / URTI (การติดเชื้อทางเดินหายใจส่วนบน)

> **Definition**: A broad group of acute infections affecting the upper mucosal structures of the airway, including the nose, paranasal sinuses, pharynx, larynx, middle ear, and epiglottitis. Mostly viral, but specific presentations mandate antibiotic therapy to prevent suppurative and non-suppurative complications.

---

## 📊 URTI Clinical Syndromes

### 1. Common Cold (Acute Viral Rhinosinusitis)
* **Pathogens**: **Rhinovirus** (most common, $30-50\%$), Coronavirus, Adenovirus, Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV).
* **Clinical**: Rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, sneezing, mild sore throat, low-grade fever.
* **Management**: Symptomatic care (decongestants, nasal saline spray, hydration). **Antibiotics are strictly contraindicated.**

### 2. Acute Pharyngotonsillitis
* **Etiology**:
  * *Viral* ($>70\%$ of cases): Adenovirus, Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV), Coxsackievirus (herpangina).
  * *Bacterial*: **Group A Streptococcus (GAS)** (*Streptococcus pyogenes*).
* **GAS Diagnostic Evaluation (Modified Centor Criteria)**:
  * Tonsillar exudates or swelling (1 pt)
  * Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy (1 pt)
  * History of fever $>38^\circ\text{C}$ (1 pt)
  * **Absence of cough** (1 pt)
  * Age $<15$ years (+1 pt); Age $>44$ years (-1 pt)
  * **Action**:
    * *Score $\le 1$*: No throat culture or antibiotic (likely viral).
    * *Score $2-3$*: Perform Rapid Antigen Detection Test (RADT) or throat culture. Treat only if positive.
    * *Score $\ge 4$*: Empiric treatment or testing.
* **Management of GAS Pharyngitis**:
  * **Oral Penicillin V for 10 days** (or Amoxicillin). If penicillin-allergic, Macrolides (Azithromycin) or Clindamycin.
  * *Single-dose alternative*: **Benzathine Penicillin G 1.2 million units IM** (for adults; resolves compliance issues).
  * *Indication*: Treatment **prevents Acute Rheumatic Fever** (must start within 9 days of symptom onset). *Note: Antibiotics do not prevent Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN).*
  * > [!WARNING]
    > **Amoxicillin Rash in Mononucleosis**: If a patient presenting with sore throat, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, and splenomegaly is treated with Amoxicillin or Ampicillin, they are at high risk of developing a diffuse, pruritic **maculopapular rash**. This is not a true drug allergy but is characteristic of **EBV Infectious Mononucleosis**.

### 3. Acute Bacterial Rhinosinusitis (ABRS)
* **Pathogens**: *S. pneumoniae*, non-typeable *H. influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*.
* **ABRS vs. Viral Rhinosinusitis Indicators**:
  1. Symptoms lasting **$\ge 10$ days** without clinical improvement.
  2. **Double Sickening**: Initial improvement followed by sudden onset of high fever, worsening headache, and increased nasal discharge.
  3. Severe onset: High fever ($>39^\circ\text{C}$) and purulent nasal discharge for at least 3-4 consecutive days.
* **Management**: First-line is **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate** (Augmentin) PO for 5-7 days.

### 4. Acute Otitis Media (AOM)
* **Pathogens**: *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*.
* **Otoscopy**: **Bulging tympanic membrane** (most specific sign), loss of landmarks/light reflex, decreased mobility on pneumatic otoscopy, and erythema.
* **Management**: Amoxicillin (high-dose: $80-90\text{ mg/kg/day}$ in pediatric patients).

### 5. Acute Epiglottitis (Medical Emergency)
* **Pathogen**: Historically ***Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib)** (now rare in vaccinated populations; adults present with Group A Strep/S. pneumoniae).
* **Clinical Presentation**: The **4 Ds**: **D**rooling, **D**ysphagia, **D**yspnea, and **D**istress. Patients classically assume the **"tripod position"** (leaning forward on hands, chin extended, mouth open). Inspiratory stridor.
* > [!CAUTION]
  > **Airway Laryngospasm Trap**: In a patient with suspected epiglottitis, **do not attempt to examine the throat with a tongue depressor or perform direct laryngoscopy** in the clinic or ER. Any stimulation can trigger sudden, fatal laryngospasm. Keep the patient calm and secure the airway in the operating room with an anesthesiologist/ENT surgeon present.
* **Diagnosis**: Lateral neck X-ray showing the **"thumbprint sign"** (swollen epiglottis).
* **Management**: Airway stabilization (nasotracheal intubation or tracheostomy) + **IV Ceftriaxone**.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **Throat Culture / RADT**: For suspected GAS pharyngitis.
* **Lateral Neck X-ray**: For suspected epiglottitis (shows thumbprint sign, rules out croup [steeple sign]).
* **CT Sinuses**: Not indicated for uncomplicated ABRS. Reserve for orbital/intracranial complications (potts puffy tumor, cavernous sinus thrombosis).

---

## 🏥 Medication Summary for URTI
* **Penicillin V**: 250-500 mg PO tid/qid (for GAS pharyngitis; must complete 10 days).
* **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate**: 875/125 mg PO bid (first-line for ABRS).
* **Ceftriaxone**: 2 g IV q24h (for Epiglottitis).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Centor Criteria PSGN Limit**: Antibiotic treatment of GAS pharyngitis prevents Acute Rheumatic Fever (and suppurative peritonsillar abscess), but **does not prevent Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN)**. PSGN can still occur 1-3 weeks after treated or untreated streptococcal infection.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Epiglottitis vs. Croup**:
  > * **Epiglottitis** $\rightarrow$ High fever, toxic appearance, **no barky cough**, **drooling**, **thumbprint sign** on lateral X-ray.
  > * **Croup** (Laryngotracheobronchitis) $\rightarrow$ Low-grade fever, **barking cough**, no drooling, **steeple sign** (subglottic narrowing) on AP neck X-ray.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Rhinovirus Sialic Acid**: Rhinoviruses bind to ICAM-1 receptors on respiratory cells. They grow best at $33^\circ\text{C}$ (nasal cavity), which is why they cause localized upper respiratory symptoms rather than systemic lower tract disease.
