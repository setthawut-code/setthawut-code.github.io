# 🦠 Community-Acquired Pneumonia / CAP (โรคปอดอักเสบชุมชน)

> **Definition**: An acute infection of the lung parenchyma (alveoli and interstitial space) acquired outside of a hospital or long-term care facility, presenting with clinical symptoms of lower tract infection and new infiltrates on chest X-ray.

---

## 🦠 Pathogens & Clinical Differentiation

### 1. Typical Pneumonia
Characterized by abrupt onset, high fever, productive cough, and lobar consolidation:
* ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** (Most common overall): Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci. Classically presents with **rust-colored sputum**.
* ***Haemophilus influenzae***: Gram-negative coccobacillus. Highly common in elderly and patients with COPD/smoking history.
* ***Staphylococcus aureus***: Gram-positive cocci in clusters. Severe, necrotizing pneumonia; classically follows a **recent influenza infection**. High risk of cavitary lesions, pneumatoceles, and empyema.
* ***Klebsiella pneumoniae***: Gram-negative encapsulated bacillus. Classically seen in **chronic alcoholics** and malnourished patients. Characterized by **"currant-jelly" (thick, bloody) sputum** and a **bulging fissure sign** on chest X-ray due to heavy lobar exudates.

### 2. Atypical Pneumonia
Characterized by gradual onset, dry cough, interstitial infiltrates, and systemic symptoms out of proportion to chest findings ("walking pneumonia"):
* ***Mycoplasma pneumoniae***: Most common atypical pathogen; prevalent in young adults in close quarters (dormitories, barracks). Associated with **cold agglutinins** (causing autoimmune hemolytic anemia) and erythema multiforme.
* ***Legionella pneumophila***: Acquired by inhaling aerosols from contaminated water systems (air conditioners, cooling towers).
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
    > **Legionella Triad**: Suspect *Legionella* in a patient with severe pneumonia presenting with **high fever**, **gastrointestinal symptoms** (watery diarrhea), **neurological symptoms** (confusion/headache), and laboratory findings of **hyponatremia** and elevated liver transaminases.
* ***Chlamydia pneumoniae***: Mild atypical pneumonia in young adults.

## 🔍 Severity Assessment: CURB-65 Score
Used to decide the site of care (Outpatient vs. Ward vs. ICU):

| Criteria | Clinical Definition | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C** — Confusion | New-onset disorientation to person, place, or time | 1 |
| **U** — Urea | Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) $>19\text{ mg/dL}$ ($>7\text{ mmol/L}$) | 1 |
| **R** — Respiration | Respiratory rate **$\ge 30\text{ breaths/minute}$** | 1 |
| **B** — Blood Pressure | Systolic BP $<90\text{ mmHg}$ or Diastolic BP $\le 60\text{ mmHg}$ | 1 |
| **65** — Age | Age **$\ge 65$ years** | 1 |

### 📊 Site of Care Recommendation
* **Score 0 to 1**: Low risk ($<1.5\%$ mortality). **Outpatient** treatment is safe.
* **Score 2**: Moderate risk ($9\%$ mortality). **Inpatient** admission or close outpatient monitoring.
* **Score 3 to 5**: High risk ($15-22\%$ mortality). **Inpatient admission mandatory**. Score 4-5 warrants intensive care unit (ICU) evaluation.

---

## 🔬 Investigations
* **[[Chest X-Ray]] (Gold Standard for Diagnosis)**:
  * *Typical*: Lobar consolidation (air bronchograms).
  * *Atypical*: Diffuse, patchy, or bilateral interstitial infiltrates.
* **Sputum Gram Stain and Culture**: Obtain prior to starting antibiotics (low sensitivity but guides de-escalation).
* **Blood Cultures (Two Sets)**: Indicated for all hospitalized patients with severe CAP (positive in $10-15\%$).
* **Urinary Antigen Tests**:
  * *S. pneumoniae* urinary antigen.
  * *L. pneumophila* urinary antigen (detects serogroup 1).
  * *Benefit*: Highly specific, fast, and remains positive even after antibiotic therapy has started.

## 🏥 Management: Empiric Antibiotics

### 1. Outpatient Treatment (Previously healthy, no comorbidities)
* **First-line**:
  * **Amoxicillin 1 g PO tid** (targets S. pneumoniae) OR
  * **Doxycycline 100 mg PO bid** (covers typical + atypical) OR
  * **Azithromycin 500 mg PO on Day 1, then 250 mg daily** (only if local macrolide resistance is $<25\%$).

### 2. Outpatient Treatment (With comorbidities: Diabetes, Heart/Lung/Kidney Disease, Alcoholism)
* **First-line**: Combination therapy:
  * **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 875/125 mg PO bid** PLUS **Azithromycin 500 mg PO daily** (or Doxycycline).
* **Alternative**:
  * **Respiratory Fluoroquinolone** monotherapy: **Levofloxacin 750 mg PO daily** OR **Moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily**.

### 3. Inpatient Treatment (Non-ICU Ward)
* **First-line**:
  * **Ceftriaxone 1 - 2 g IV daily** PLUS **Azithromycin 500 mg IV/PO daily** (covers atypical).
* **Alternative**:
  * **Levofloxacin 750 mg IV daily** (monotherapy).

### 4. Inpatient Treatment (ICU)
* **First-line**: Double coverage (never use fluoroquinolone monotherapy in ICU):
  * **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily** PLUS **Azithromycin 500 mg IV daily** OR **Levofloxacin 750 mg IV daily**.
* **Special Coverage additions**:
  * *If Pseudomonas risk* (prior history, bronchiectasis): Switch Ceftriaxone to **Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h** or **Cefepime 2 g IV q8h**.
  * *If MRSA risk*: Add **Vancomycin** or **Linezolid**.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **No Infiltrate, No Pneumonia**: A patient presenting with cough, fever, and crackles cannot be diagnosed with pneumonia without a **new infiltrate on chest X-ray**. If the X-ray is normal, treat as acute bronchitis or consider CT scan if early/dehydrated (dehydration can occasionally mask an infiltrate).
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Legionella Hyponatremia**: A severe pneumonia patient presenting with high fever, watery diarrhea, and a serum sodium of **$128\text{ mEq/L}$** has *Legionella* infection. Ceftriaxone does not cover it; you must add a Macrolide (Azithromycin) or a Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin).
* > [!TIP]
  > **Mycoplasma cold agglutinins**: *M. pneumoniae* shares antigens with RBC membranes. Neoplastic/inflammatory response produces IgM autoantibodies (cold agglutinins) that bind RBCs at low temperatures, causing transient Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia.
