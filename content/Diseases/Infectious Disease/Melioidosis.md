# 🦠 Melioidosis (โรคเมลิออยโดซิส)

> **Definition**: Infectious disease caused by the Gram-negative bacillus *Burkholderia pseudomallei*, endemic in tropical regions (especially Northeast Thailand). Known as **"The Great Mimicker"** due to diverse presentations.

---

## 🦠 Pathophysiology & Risk Factors

* **Organism**: *Burkholderia pseudomallei*. Saprophytic, Gram-negative, motile rod found in soil and surface water.
* **Microbiology**: Gram stain shows **Gram-negative bacilli with bipolar staining (safety-pin appearance)**.
* **Transmission**: 
  * Direct contact of inoculating skin wounds/scratches with contaminated wet soil or water (classic in rice farmers during rainy season).
  * Inhalation of dust/aerosols (associated with severe pneumonia during storms).
  * Ingestion of contaminated water.
* **Key Risk Factors**:
  * **Diabetes Mellitus** (most common and critical risk factor; $>50\%$ of cases).
  * Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD).
  * Thalassemia.
  * Heavy alcohol use / Liver disease.

---

## 🔍 Clinical Manifestations

Can present as acute localized, acute septicemic, or chronic infection:

### 1. Acute Localized Infection
* Cutaneous ulcers, nodules, or abscesses at site of inoculation. Often associated with cellulitis and regional lymphadenitis.

### 2. Pulmonary Infection (Most Common)
* Ranges from mild bronchitis to severe necrotizing pneumonia.
* **Presentation**: High fever, productive cough, hemoptysis, pleuritic chest pain.
* **CXR**: Patchy infiltrates, lobar consolidation, and **cavitary lesions** (closely mimics pulmonary [[Tuberculosis]]).

### 3. Septicemic Melioidosis (High Mortality)
* Rapid onset of severe sepsis and septic shock.
* Characterized by hematogenous dissemination and formation of **multiple visceral abscesses**:
  * **Splenic and Hepatic Abscesses**: Classically multiple, small, and closely grouped, yielding a **"swiss-cheese"** or **"honeycomb"** pattern on CT.
  * **Prostatic Abscess**: Common in adult males. Causes pelvic pain, urinary retention.
  * **Parotid Abscess**: Classic presentation in children in endemic regions (e.g., Thailand).
  * Septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, or brain abscesses (neuromelioidosis).

### 4. Chronic Melioidosis
* Symptoms lasting $>2$ months. Mimics TB with prolonged fever, weight loss, night sweats, and localized cavitary lung lesions or chronic osteomyelitis.

---

## 🔬 Investigations

* **Culture (Gold Standard)**:
  * Isolation of *B. pseudomallei* from blood, sputum, urine, skin lesions, or abscess aspirate.
  * Selective media (e.g., **Ashdown's agar**) used to isolate from non-sterile sites.
* **Microscopy**: Gram stain showing safety-pin-like Gram-negative rods.
* **Serology (IHA)**: Indirect Hemagglutination Assay. Has **low specificity** in endemic areas because a large portion of the healthy population has baseline positive antibodies due to environmental exposure. A single titer is rarely diagnostic.
* **Abdominal Ultrasound / CT**:
  * **Mandatory in all septicemic cases** to screen for occult splenic, hepatic, or prostatic abscesses that require prolonged therapy or drainage.

---

## 🏥 Management

Requires a two-phase treatment strategy to prevent high mortality and relapse:

### Phase 1: Intensive Phase (Intravenous)
* **Goal**: Reduce mortality from acute sepsis.
* **Duration**: **Minimum 10–14 days** (extend to 4–8 weeks for deep organ abscesses, osteomyelitis, or CNS involvement).
* **Antibiotics**:
  * **First-line**: **Ceftazidime** 2 g IV q8h (child: 50 mg/kg q8h) OR **Meropenem** 1 g IV q8h (preferred if in septic shock).

### Phase 2: Eradication Phase (Oral)
* **Goal**: Prevent relapse (relapse rate is high if oral phase is skipped or shortened).
* **Duration**: **Minimum 12–20 weeks (3–5 months)**.
* **Antibiotics**:
  * **First-line**: **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)**.
    * Dose is weight-based (typically 2 double-strength tablets PO bid for patients $40-60$ kg). Doxycycline is no longer recommended as monotherapy due to higher relapse rates.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Occult Abscess Screening**: Any patient who is blood culture-positive for *B. pseudomallei* must undergo abdominal CT or ultrasound. Finding splenic/hepatic abscesses extends the IV intensive phase and dictates careful monitoring.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Intrinsic Resistance**: *B. pseudomallei* is intrinsically resistant to many common empiric sepsis regimens: penicillins, early cephalosporins, macrolides, and **aminoglycosides** (gentamicin/amikacin). Never use routine empiric regimens if melioidosis is suspected.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Abscess Drainage**: Small liver/spleen abscesses do not require surgery and resolve with antibiotics. Large prostatic abscesses or accessible large liver abscesses should be drained percutaneously.
