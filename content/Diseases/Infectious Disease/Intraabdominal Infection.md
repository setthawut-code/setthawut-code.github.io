# 🦠 Intraabdominal Infection (การติดเชื้อในช่องท้อง)

> **Definition**: Infections within the peritoneal cavity, categorized into **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP / Primary)** and **Secondary Peritonitis** (perforation/abscess).

---

## 🦠 1. Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)

* **Definition**: Infection of the ascitic fluid without an identifiable intraabdominal surgically treatable source of infection.
* **Pathophysiology**: Classically occurs in patients with end-stage liver disease (portal hypertension/cirrhosis) or nephrotic syndrome. Portal hypertension causes bowel mucosal edema $\rightarrow$ **bacterial translocation** from gut lumen into mesenteric lymph nodes $\rightarrow$ hematogenous seeding of ascitic fluid. Impaired humoral immunity (low complement in ascitic fluid) prevents clearance.
* **Microbiology**: **Monomicrobial** (usually a single organism).
  * ***Escherichia coli*** (most common).
  * *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.
  * *Streptococcus pneumoniae* or *Enterococcus* spp.
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * Fever, chills.
  * Diffuse abdominal pain or tenderness.
  * Worsening of ascites, development of hepatic encephalopathy, or unexplained acute kidney injury.
  * *Note: Classic peritoneal signs (rigidity, rebound tenderness) are often mild or completely absent in SBP because the fluid separates the visceral and parietal peritoneum.*
* **Diagnosis (Ascitic Fluid Analysis)**:
  * Perform **diagnostic paracentesis** before starting antibiotics.
  * **Diagnostic Criteria**: **Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC/PMN) in ascitic fluid $\ge 250$ cells/$\text{mm}^3$**.
  * Culture: Inoculate ascitic fluid directly into blood culture bottles at the bedside (improves yield).
* **Management**:
  * **Antibiotic Therapy**: **Ceftriaxone** 2 g IV qd (or Cefotaxime 2 g IV q8h) for **5 days**.
  * **Hepatorenal Syndrome Prevention**: Administer **IV Albumin** ($1.5$ g/kg within 6 hours of diagnosis, and $1.0$ g/kg on Day 3). Reduces mortality and renal failure in patients with bilirubin $>4$ mg/dL or creatinine $>1$ mg/dL.
  * **Prophylaxis (Secondary)**: Once SBP occurs, recurrence rate is $70\%$/year. Start long-term oral prophylaxis with **Norfloxacin 400 mg daily** OR **TMP-SMX DS** 1 tablet daily.

---

## 🦠 2. Secondary Peritonitis

* **Definition**: Inflammation of the peritoneum resulting from a primary, localized intraabdominal pathology (usually rupture or perforation of a hollow viscus).
* **Causes**: Perforated peptic ulcer, acute appendicitis with perforation, perforated diverticulitis, ischemic bowel necrosis, or penetrating trauma.
* **Microbiology**: **Polymicrobial** (mixed aerobic Gram-negatives + anaerobes).
  * *E. coli*, *Klebsiella* spp., plus ***Bacteroides fragilis*** (anaerobe).
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * Acute, severe, localized abdominal pain that rapidly becomes generalized.
  * High fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension (sepsis/septic shock).
  * **Peritoneal Signs**: **Board-like abdominal rigidity**, guarding, and severe **rebound tenderness**.
* **Investigations**:
  * **Upright Chest X-ray / CT Abdomen**: Shows **pneumoperitoneum** (free air under the diaphragm; pathognomonic for hollow viscus perforation).
  * Blood cultures, CBC (marked leukocytosis), renal panel.
* **Management**:
  * **Step 1: Resuscitation & IV Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum empiric coverage of Gram-negatives and anaerobes:
    * **Ceftriaxone** 1–2 g IV qd + **Metronidazole** 500 mg IV q8h OR
    * **Piperacillin-Tazobactam** 4.5 g IV q6h (covers Pseudomonas/Enterococcus) OR
    * Ertapenem 1 g IV qd.
  * **Step 2: Emergency Surgery (Source Control)**: Laparotomy or laparoscopy is mandatory to repair the perforation, resect necrotic bowel, drain abscesses, and perform extensive peritoneal lavage.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Differentiating SBP from Secondary Peritonitis**: If ANC/PMN is $\ge 250$ cells/$\text{mm}^3$ but secondary peritonitis (e.g., microperforation) is suspected, check ascitic fluid parameters (**Runge Criteria**). Perforation is highly likely if at least 2 of the following are met:
  > 1. Total protein $> 1$ g/dL.
  > 2. Glucose $< 50$ mg/dL.
  > 3. LDH $>$ upper limit of normal for serum.
  > *Secondary peritonitis requires urgent surgery; SBP is treated medically. Operating on an SBP patient has high mortality.*
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Anaerobic Coverage**: Ceftriaxone has zero anaerobic coverage. Never treat secondary peritonitis or hepatobiliary infections with Ceftriaxone monotherapy; always add **Metronidazole** to cover *Bacteroides* and other gut anaerobes.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Primary Prophylaxis for SBP**: Indicated in cirrhotic patients with acute gastrointestinal bleeding (give IV Ceftriaxone or oral Norfloxacin for 7 days to prevent SBP/bacteremia) or low-protein ascites (protein $<1.5$ g/dL) with renal/hepatic impairment.
