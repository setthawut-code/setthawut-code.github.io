# 🐛 Intestinal Parasitosis (โรคพยาธิและโปรโตซัวในทางเดินอาหาร)

> **Definition**: Infections of the gastrointestinal tract caused by pathogenic protozoa or helminths (nematodes, cestodes, trematodes), highly prevalent in developing countries and tropical regions.

---

## 🦠 1. Pathogenic Protozoa

Unicellular organisms, fecal-oral transmission.

### A. *Entamoeba histolytica* (Amebiasis)
* **Pathophysiology**: Ingestion of cysts. Trophozoites invade colon epithelium, producing cytotoxins that digest tissue $\rightarrow$ **flask-shaped ulcers**. Can enter portal circulation to cause liver abscess.
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * **Amebic Dysentery**: Gradual onset of bloody/mucoid diarrhea, abdominal pain. *Unlike bacterial dysentery, fever is mild/absent.*
  * **Amebic Liver Abscess**: High fever, RUQ pain, tender hepatomegaly. Aspirate shows **"anchovy paste"** (chocolate brown, odorless fluid; contains necrotic liver tissue, no PMNs). Abscess usually single, in right lobe.
* **Diagnosis**: Stool exam (trophozoites with ingested RBCs or cysts with 4 nuclei), serology (positive in liver abscess), liver ultrasound.
* **Management**: **Metronidazole** (or Tinidazole) to eradicate tissue trophozoites, followed by **Paromomycin** (or Diloxanide furoate) to eradicate luminal cysts (prevents relapse).

### B. *Giardia lamblia* (Giardiasis)
* **Pathophysiology**: Trophozoites adhere to duodenal/jejunal mucosa via ventral sucking discs, causing malabsorption of fats.
* **Clinical Manifestations**: Foul-smelling, **greasy/fatty stools (steatorrhea)**, flatulence, abdominal bloating, weight loss.
* **Diagnosis**: Stool exam (trophozoites with "old man face" look, 2 nuclei and flagella; or cysts), stool antigen test.
* **Management**: **Metronidazole** 250 mg PO tid for 5–7 days.

### C. *Cryptosporidium parvum*
* **Clinical**: Watery diarrhea. Self-limiting in immunocompetent. **Chronic, life-threatening diarrhea in HIV** (CD4 $<180$).
* **Diagnosis**: **Modified Acid-Fast Stain** of stool shows red-pink oocysts.
* **Management**: **Nitazoxanide** (in immunocompetent); antiretroviral therapy (ART) to restore immune function (in HIV).

## 🐛 2. Helminths (Worms)

Multicellular organisms, often associated with **peripheral blood eosinophilia**.

### A. Nematodes (Roundworms)

* ***Strongyloides stercoralis***:
  * **Transmission**: Filariform larvae in soil penetrate bare skin $\rightarrow$ lungs $\rightarrow$ swallowed into GI tract.
  * **Pathophysiology**: **Autoinfection** (rhabditiform larvae in gut transform to filariform, penetrate perianal skin/colon to re-enter circulation). Can cause **hyperinfection syndrome** in immunocompromised patients (steroids, HTLV-1 infection) leading to massive dissemination of larvae to brain, lungs, skin carrying enteric bacteria (causes Gram-negative sepsis/meningitis).
  * **Clinical**: Larva currens (rapidly moving, pruritic linear rash on buttocks/abdomen), epigastric pain (mimics peptic ulcer), malabsorption.
  * **Diagnosis**: **Rhabditiform larvae in stool** (rarely eggs). Agar plate culture (larvae leave tracks of bacteria).
  * **Management**: **Ivermectin** 200 $\mu$g/kg/day PO for 1-2 days (longer in hyperinfection).
* **Hookworms** (*Ancylostoma duodenale* and *Necator americanus*):
  * **Transmission**: Larvae in soil penetrate skin $\rightarrow$ lungs $\rightarrow$ intestine.
  * **Pathophysiology**: Adult worms attach to duodenal mucosa and suck blood.
  * **Clinical**: Ground itch (pruritic maculopapular rash at site of entry), **microcytic hypochromic iron deficiency anemia**, hypoalbuminemia.
  * **Management**: **Albendazole** 400 mg PO single dose.
* ***Enterobius vermicularis* (Pinworm)**:
  * **Clinical**: Intense **perianal pruritus, worse at night** (female worm migrates to perianal skin to deposit eggs).
  * **Diagnosis**: **Scotch tape test** (press tape to perianal skin in morning, view eggs under microscope; eggs are asymmetric, D-shaped).
  * **Management**: **Mebendazole** 100 mg PO single dose, repeat in 2 weeks. *Treat all household members concurrently.*
* ***Ascaris lumbricoides***:
  * **Clinical**: Loeffler's syndrome (transient eosinophilic pneumonitis during larval lung migration), mechanical intestinal obstruction (mass of worms in ileum), biliary colic/cholangitis (worm migrates up ampulla of Vater).
  * **Management**: **Albendazole** 400 mg PO single dose.

### B. Cestodes (Tapeworms)

* ***Taenia saginata* (Beef tapeworm) & *Taenia solium* (Pork tapeworm)**:
  * **Intestinal Taeniasis**: Ingestion of undercooked beef/pork containing larval cysticerci $\rightarrow$ adult worm in gut $\rightarrow$ passes proglottids in stool. Treatment: **Praziquantel** 5-10 mg/kg PO single dose.
  * **Cysticercosis**: Occurs **only with *Taenia solium*** when humans **ingest eggs** (fecal-oral from human feces, NOT from eating pork!). Eggs hatch, oncospheres penetrate gut wall and encyst in tissues.
    * **Neurocysticercosis**: Cysts in brain parenchyma or ventricles. Most common cause of acquired seizures in developing countries. Brain MRI shows multiple cystic lesions with an eccentric scolex ("hole-with-dot" sign) or calcified lesions.
    * **Management of Neurocysticercosis**: **Albendazole** + **Dexamethasone** (steroids mandatory before antiparasitics to prevent brain edema from inflammatory response to dying larvae).

### C. Trematodes (Flukes)

* ***Opisthorchis viverrini* (Southeast Asian Liver Fluke)**:
  * **Transmission**: Ingestion of raw/undercooked freshwater fish (e.g., *Koi-pla* in Northeastern Thailand) containing metacercariae.
  * **Pathophysiology**: Adult flukes inhabit the biliary tract. Chronic mechanical irritation and immunopathological response cause chronic cholangitis and adenomatous hyperplasia.
  * **Complication**: Strongly associated with development of **Cholangiocarcinoma** (bile duct cancer).
  * **Diagnosis**: Stool exam showing small, melon-seed-shaped, operculated eggs with a small knob at the abopercular end.
  * **Management**: **Praziquantel** 25 mg/kg PO tid for 1-3 days.

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

* > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Strongyloides & Steroids**: Never initiate high-dose corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy in patients from endemic areas (e.g., rural Thailand) without screening for *Strongyloides* or giving empiric **Ivermection**. Steroids trigger hyperinfection syndrome by accelerating autoinfection, leading to fatal dissemination and Gram-negative bacteremia.
* > [!WARNING]
  > **Amebic Abscess Aspiration**: Routinely avoiding aspiration in amebic liver abscess. Indications for aspiration are limited to: large size ($>10$ cm) with high risk of rupture, left lobe abscess (risk of rupture into pericardium), or failure to respond to Metronidazole after 72 hours.
* > [!TIP]
  > **Eosinophilia Rule**: Protozoan infections (*Entamoeba*, *Giardia*) do **NOT** cause peripheral blood eosinophilia. Tissue-invasive helminths (*Strongyloides*, hookworms during migration, *Trichinella*) cause significant eosinophilia.
