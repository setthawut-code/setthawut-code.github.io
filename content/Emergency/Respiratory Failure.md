# 🫁 Respiratory Failure (ภาวะหายใจล้มเหลว)

> "Respiratory failure is a life-threatening emergency in which the respiratory system fails in one or both of its gas exchange functions: oxygenation ($PaO_2 < 60$ mmHg) or carbon dioxide elimination ($PaCO_2 > 50$ mmHg with $pH < 7.35$)."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Severe hypoxemia or hypercapnic acidosis leads to rapid cellular dysfunction. Organs with the highest oxygen demands—the brain and heart—fail first, causing cardiac arrhythmias, hypoxic encephalopathy, coma, and cardiac arrest.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Check for upper airway obstruction (stridor, gurgling, snoring).
* If airway is compromised, perform head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw-thrust. Suction secretions. Insert oral/nasopharyngeal airway.
* Prepare for immediate endotracheal intubation if the patient cannot protect their airway or has agonal breathing.

### Breathing
* **Look**: Check respiratory rate (tachypnea $> 30$ or bradypnea $< 8$ breaths/min), chest expansion symmetry, accessory muscle use (sternocleidomastoid, intercostal retraction), nasal flaring, and central cyanosis.
* **Auscultate**: Listen for wheezing (bronchospasm), crackles (fluid/pus), or **silent chest** (no air movement; a pre-terminal sign in asthma).
* **Measure Oximetry**: Check $SpO_2$. Note: Pulse oximetry does *not* measure ventilation/carbon dioxide levels.
* **Initiate Oxygen Therapy**: Titrate oxygen based on etiology (see below).

### Circulation
* Assess blood pressure and heart rate.
  * *Early hypoxemia*: Tachycardia and hypertension (sympathetic drive).
  * *Late/Severe hypoxemia*: Bradycardia, hypotension, and arrhythmias (indicates impending arrest).
* Check for signs of right heart strain (elevated JVP, peripheral edema).

---

## 🔍 Classification of Respiratory Failure

Respiratory failure is divided into two primary types based on arterial blood gas (ABG) findings:

```
Respiratory Failure Types
└── Type 1 (Hypoxemic)
    ├── PaO2 < 60 mmHg with normal or low PaCO2
    └── Pathophysiology: V/Q mismatch, Intrapulmonary Shunt
└── Type 2 (Hypercapnic)
    ├── PaCO2 > 50 mmHg with pH < 7.35 (Acidosis)
    └── Pathophysiology: Alveolar hypoventilation (pump failure)
```

---

## 📊 Type 1 vs. Type 2 Respiratory Failure

| Feature | Type 1 (Hypoxemic) | Type 2 (Hypercapnic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arterial Blood Gas** | **$PaO_2 < 60$ mmHg**, $PaCO_2$ normal or low ($<35$ mmHg). | **$PaCO_2 > 50$ mmHg**, $PaO_2 < 60$ mmHg, **$pH < 7.35$** (acute). |
| **Primary Mechanism** | V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion limitation, low $FiO_2$. | Alveolar hypoventilation (bellows failure). |
| **A-a Gradient** | **Elevated** ($> 15 - 20$ mmHg) | **Normal** (if purely extra-pulmonary). |
| **Typical Causes** | • **[[Pneumonia]]**<br>• **[[Acute Pulmonary Edema]]**<br>• **ARDS**<br>• Pulmonary Embolism | • **COPD Exacerbation** / Severe Asthma<br>• Drug overdose (opioids, sedatives)<br>• Guillain-Barré / Myasthenia Gravis<br>• Obesity Hypoventilation |
| **Clinical Presentation**| Tachypnea, cyanosis, anxiety, tachycardia. | Somnolence, confusion, asterixis, warm extremities (due to $CO_2$ vasodilation). |

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Type 1 (Hypoxemic)**: Pneumonia, pulmonary edema, pulmonary embolism, ARDS, pneumothorax, pleural effusion.
* **Type 2 (Hypercapnic)**: COPD, severe asthma, opioid overdose, brainstem stroke, Guillain-Barré syndrome, Myasthenia Gravis crisis, severe kyphoscoliosis.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: The essential diagnostic tool. Differentiates Type 1 from Type 2 and measures acid-base balance.
  * **Alveolar-Arterial (A-a) Oxygen Gradient**:
    $$A\text{-a Gradient} = PAO_2 - PaO_2$$
    $$PAO_2 = \left(FiO_2 \times (P_{atm} - P_{H_2O})\right) - \frac{PaCO_2}{R}$$
    *At room air at sea level*: $PAO_2 \approx 150 - (PaCO_2 / 0.8)$.
    * *Elevated gradient*: Pulmonary parenchymal disease (V/Q mismatch, shunt).
    * *Normal gradient*: Normal lungs, hypoventilation (CNS depression, chest wall deformity).
* **Chest X-ray (CXR)**: Look for diffuse infiltrates (ARDS/pulmonary edema), focal consolidation (pneumonia), or hyperinflation (COPD/asthma).
* **CBC**: Check for infection (leukocytosis) or severe anemia.
* **Troponin & BNP**: Rule out cardiogenic pulmonary edema.

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
Respiratory Failure Escalation Pathway
├── 1. Initial Oxygenation
│   ├── Type 1: High-flow oxygen (Target SpO2 92-98%)
│   └── Type 2 (COPD): Titrate oxygen (Target SpO2 88-92% to avoid hypercapnic respiratory drive suppression)
├── 2. High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC)
│   └── Delivers heated, humidified O2 up to 60 L/min, providing mild positive pressure (PEEP)
├── 3. Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV)
│   ├── CPAP: Preferred for cardiogenic pulmonary edema (Type 1)
│   └── BiPAP: Preferred for COPD exacerbation with acidosis (pH 7.25-7.35) (Type 2)
└── 4. Invasive Mechanical Ventilation (Endotracheal Intubation)
    └── Indications: Apnea, altered mental status (coma), severe acidosis (pH < 7.20), hemodynamic instability
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* Address the specific underlying etiology:
  * **Opioid Overdose**: Give IV **Naloxone** $0.4 - 2.0$ mg.
  * **COPD / Asthma**: Inhaled beta-agonists (Salbutamol) + anticholinergics (Ipratropium) + systemic corticosteroids.
  * **Pneumonia**: IV Antibiotics.
  * **Heart Failure**: IV loop diuretics + vasodilators.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Pulse Oximetry ($SpO_2$)**.
* **Serial ABGs**: Repeat 30-60 minutes after initiating or adjusting oxygen/NIV to monitor $PaCO_2$ clearance and pH improvement.
* **Mental Status**: Monitor closely for $CO_2$ narcosis (somnolence, loss of consciousness).
* **Respiratory Rate & Work of Breathing**: Track closely to anticipate respiratory muscle fatigue.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The COPD Oxygen Target (88-92%)**: In patients with chronic hypercapnia (like severe COPD), the respiratory center in the brainstem desensitizes to high $CO_2$ levels. Their breathing drive becomes dependent on hypoxemia (**hypoxic drive**). Administering excessive oxygen can suppress this hypoxic drive, worsen V/Q mismatch, and cause profound hypercapnic narcosis and coma. Keep oxygen targets strictly at $88-92\%$.
> **The Silent Chest Warning**: In a patient with severe asthma exacerbation, the disappearance of wheezing ("silent chest") is a sign of complete airway closure, not improvement. It indicates immediate respiratory arrest and requires emergency epinephrine and preparation for intubation.
