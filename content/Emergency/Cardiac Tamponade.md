# 🫀 Cardiac Tamponade (ภาวะบีบรัดหัวใจ)

> "Cardiac tamponade is a life-threatening cardiovascular emergency caused by the accumulation of fluid, pus, or blood in the pericardial space under high pressure. This compresses the heart chambers, restricts diastolic filling, reduces stroke volume, and leads to obstructive shock and death."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
The pericardial space normally contains $15 - 50$ mL of fluid. Rapid accumulation of as little as $100 - 200$ mL of fluid raises intrapericardial pressure above intracardiac diastolic pressure. This prevents venous return to the right heart, reducing cardiac output. Without immediate decompression, it leads to pulseless electrical activity (PEA) arrest.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess and secure.
> [!CAUTION]
> **Avoid Positive Pressure Ventilation (Intubation/PEEP)**: Positive pressure ventilation increases intrathoracic pressure, which decreases venous return (preload). In a patient with cardiac tamponade, preload is already critically compromised. Intubation or CPAP can cause immediate cardiovascular collapse and cardiac arrest. If airway management is mandatory, perform pericardiocentesis first.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and dyspnea.
* Auscultate: **Lungs are typically clear to auscultation** (unless there is a co-existing pulmonary pathology). Clear lungs with profound hypotension and elevated JVP is a classic signature of tamponade (differentiating it from left ventricular cardiogenic shock).

### Circulation
* **Measure Blood Pressure**: Look for hypotension and a narrow pulse pressure.
* **Assess Jugular Veins**: Elevated JVP with a **prominent x descent and a abolished y descent** (due to compression of the ventricles preventing diastolic filling).
* **Auscultate Heart**: Check for **muffled or distant heart sounds** (fluid acts as an acoustic barrier).
* **Assess for Pulsus Paradoxus**: An abnormally large decrease in systolic blood pressure ($> 10$ mmHg) during inspiration.
* **Fluid Challenge**: Administer a rapid IV fluid bolus ($250-500$ mL of crystalloid) immediately. This increases venous pressure above pericardial pressure, temporarily improving diastolic filling as a bridge to decompression.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Clinical Diagnostic Triads & Signs)

### 1. Beck's Triad (Classic but present in only 30-40% of acute cases):
* Hypotension (with narrow pulse pressure).
* Jugular Venous Distension.
* Muffled or distant heart sounds.

### 2. Pulsus Paradoxus:
* *How to measure*: Inflate manual BP cuff above systolic pressure. Deflate slowly. Note pressure when sounds are heard only during expiration. Deflate further. Note pressure when sounds are heard during both inspiration and expiration. The difference is the pulsus paradoxus. A difference **$> 10$ mmHg** is abnormal.

### 3. ECG findings:
* **Low QRS Voltage**: Universal decrease in QRS amplitude across all leads.
* **Electrical Alternans**: Alternating amplitude or axis of QRS complexes from beat to beat, caused by the heart physically swinging back and forth in the large volume of pericardial fluid.

## 🦠 Causes

```
Pericardial Effusion & Tamponade Causes
└── 1. Traumatic (Hemopericardium)
    ├── Penetrating chest trauma (stab, gunshot wound)
    ├── Retrograde aortic rupture (Stanford Type A Dissection)
    └── Post-procedural (cardiac catheterization, pacemaker lead perforation)
└── 2. Inflammatory / Infectious
    ├── Acute pericarditis (viral, tuberculous, purulent bacterial)
    └── Uremic pericarditis (end-stage kidney disease)
└── 3. Neoplastic
    └── Metastatic malignancy (lung cancer, breast cancer, lymphoma)
└── 4. Post-Myocardial Infarction
    └── Left ventricular free wall rupture (occurs 3-14 days post-MI)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition                      | Distinguishing Clinical Features                                                               | Key Investigations                     |
| :----------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| **Cardiac Tamponade**          | Clear lungs, muffled heart sounds, JVP elevation, pulsus paradoxus, electrical alternans.      | Bedside Echocardiography.              |
| **[[Cardiogenic Shock]]**      | **Diffuse crackles** (pulmonary edema), abnormal ECG (ischemia), poor contractility.           | Echocardiography (low EF, dilated LV). |
| **Massive Pulmonary Embolism** | Acute dyspnea, hypoxemia, JVP elevation, clear lungs, right ventricular strain on echo.        | CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA).       |
| **[[Tension Pneumothorax]]**   | **Absent unilateral breath sounds**, hyperresonance, tracheal deviation, respiratory distress. | Clinical exam, Chest X-ray.            |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Gold Standard Diagnostic**:
  * **Echocardiography (Transthoracic / Bedside POCUS)**: The primary diagnostic investigation. Key signs of tamponade include:
    1. **Diastolic collapse of the Right Ventricle (RV)** (specific).
    2. **Late systolic collapse of the Right Atrium (RA)** (earliest sign).
    3. **Plethoric IVC** (dilated, with $<50\%$ collapse during inspiration).
    4. Respiratory variation in transvalvular Doppler flow velocities ($>25\%$ decrease in mitral flow on inspiration).
* **ECG**: Low voltage QRS, electrical alternans, or PR depression (pericarditis).
* **Chest X-ray**: Enlarged, globular **"water-bottle" cardiac silhouette** (only seen in slow, chronic accumulations of $>200-500$ mL; acute traumatic tamponade of 100 mL will show a normal-sized heart).

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management: Decompression

Emergency decompression is the only definitive treatment for cardiac tamponade:

```
Tamponade Management Protocol
├── 1. IV Fluid Bolus (Give 500 mL crystalloid rapidly as temporary preload bridge)
├── 2. Avoid Positive Pressure (No CPAP/BiPAP; avoid intubation if possible)
├── 3. Emergent Pericardiocentesis (Needle drainage)
│   ├── Target: Ultrasound-guided subxiphoid or apical needle insertion
│   ├── Aspiration: Draw fluid to relieve intrapericardial pressure (10-20 mL resolves shock)
│   └── Indication: Hemodynamically unstable non-traumatic tamponade
└── 4. Surgical Decompression (Pericardial Window / Thoracotomy)
    ├── Indication 1: Traumatic hemopericardium (stab wound - requires thoracotomy)
    └── Indication 2: Purulent or recurrent malignant effusions
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* Manage the underlying cause: surgical repair for aortic dissection or free wall rupture, initiation of urgent dialysis for uremic tamponade, or starting antituberculosis therapy for TB pericarditis.
* Leave a soft indwelling pericardial catheter in place for 24-48 hours until drainage is $<25$ mL/day.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Intra-arterial Blood Pressure (Arterial Line)**: Look for normalization of pulse pressure and resolution of pulsus paradoxus post-procedure.
* **ECG Telemetry**: Watch for ventricular arrhythmias from catheter irritation of the myocardium.
* **Drainage Volume**: Quantify and record pericardial catheter output hourly.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **"Clear Lungs" Clue**: When evaluating a patient in profound shock with distended neck veins (high JVP), listen to the lungs. If the lungs have diffuse crackles, think Left Ventricular Cardiogenic Shock. If the lungs are **completely clear**, think Cardiac Tamponade, Right Ventricular Infarction, or Tension Pneumothorax.
> **Volume is a Bridge, Not a Cure**: Fluid boluses temporarily buy time by increasing venous pressure above pericardial pressure. However, this is a short-lived bridge. Decompression via needle or surgery must be performed immediately.
