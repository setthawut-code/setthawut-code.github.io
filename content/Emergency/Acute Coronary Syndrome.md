# 💔 Acute Coronary Syndrome (กลุ่มอาการโรคหัวใจขาดเลือดเฉียบพลัน)

> "Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) is a spectrum of clinical conditions ranging from Unstable Angina (UA) to Non-ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI) and ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI), caused by sudden coronary artery thrombosis secondary to atherosclerotic plaque rupture."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
STEMI represents a complete occlusion of a major coronary artery. Without immediate reperfusion, transmural myocardial necrosis progresses rapidly, leading to irreversible loss of contractile function, [[Cardiogenic Shock]], mechanical complications (papillary rupture, VSD), malignant ventricular arrhythmias (VF/VT), and sudden [[Cardiac Arrest]].

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess and secure. Prepare for airway control in cases of cardiogenic shock, severe altered consciousness post-cardiac arrest, or respiratory failure from severe pulmonary edema.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use.
* Auscultate: Check for bilateral basal crackles or cardiac wheezing (indicates acute heart failure / [[Acute Pulmonary Edema]] secondary to myocardial dysfunction).
* Measure $SpO_2$. **Administer supplemental oxygen only if $SpO_2 < 90\%$** or if the patient is in respiratory distress (hyperoxia causes coronary vasoconstriction and increases free radical damage).

### Circulation
* Measure blood pressure and heart rate.
  * *Hypotension + Tachycardia*: Suggests cardiogenic shock or mechanical complications.
  * *Bradycardia*: Suggests inferior wall MI with vagal stimulation or heart block (RCA ischemia involving the AV node).
* Assess peripheral perfusion: cold clammy skin, capillary refill.
* Check JVP: Elevated JVP with clear lungs suggests **Right Ventricular Infarction** or cardiac tamponade.
* **ECG Acquisition**: Obtain and interpret a **12-lead ECG within 10 minutes** of medical contact.

---

## 🔍 Recognition & Diagnostic Classification

ACS is classified based on ECG findings and cardiac biomarkers (Troponin):

```
ACS Classification
└── 1. ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)
    ├── ECG: ST-elevation >= 1 mm in >= 2 contiguous leads (or new LBBB)
    └── Biomarkers: Elevated Troponin
└── 2. Non-ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI)
    ├── ECG: ST-depression, T-wave inversion, or normal
    └── Biomarkers: Elevated Troponin
└── 3. Unstable Angina (UA)
    ├── ECG: Normal, ST-depression, or T-wave inversion
    └── Biomarkers: NORMAL Troponin
```

### Contiguous Lead ST-Elevation Criteria:
* **Leads V2-V3**:
  * *Men $< 40$ years*: $\ge 2.5$ mm.
  * *Men $\ge 40$ years*: $\ge 2.0$ mm.
  * *Women*: $\ge 1.5$ mm.
* **Other leads**: $\ge 1.0$ mm in at least two contiguous leads.

## 🦠 Causes

```
ACS Pathophysiology
└── Type 1 Myocardial Infarction (Classic ACS)
    └── Atherosclerotic plaque rupture or erosion with occlusive thrombosis
└── Type 2 Myocardial Infarction (Supply-Demand Mismatch)
    └── Severe anemia, tachycardia, shock, sepsis, or coronary vasospasm
└── Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection (SCAD)
    └── Non-atherosclerotic tear in coronary wall (predominantly young women)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Key Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Coronary Syndrome** | Substernal pressure, radiation to left arm/jaw, diaphoresis. ST-changes on ECG. | ECG, serial Troponins. |
| **[[Aortic Dissection]]** | Ripping back pain, BP difference between arms, pulse deficits, wide mediastinum. | CT Angiography (CTA). |
| **Acute Pericarditis** | Sharp chest pain, **relieved by sitting forward**, aggravated by lying flat. Diffuse PR depression, ST-elevation. | ECG (diffuse concave ST elevation). |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Pleuritic chest pain, sudden dyspnea, tachycardia, hypoxia. | CTPA. |
| **Gastroesophageal Reflux** | Burning pain, acid regurgitation, relieved by antacids. | Diagnostic trial of antacids. |

## 🧪 Investigations

* **12-lead ECG**: The primary tool. Obtain immediately and repeat every 15-30 minutes if initial ECG is non-diagnostic but clinical suspicion remains high.
* **Cardiac Troponin (High-Sensitivity)**: The gold standard biomarker for myocardial necrosis. Check at presentation and repeat at 1-3 hours (depending on assay protocol).
* **Echocardiography**: Check for regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMAs) matching the coronary territory. Rule out mechanical complications (VSD, mitral regurgitation, tamponade).
* **Chest X-ray**: Exclude other causes (e.g., widened mediastinum in dissection, pneumothorax) and assess for pulmonary congestion.
* **Electrolytes & Creatinine**: Evaluate kidney function before coronary angiography and monitor potassium/magnesium (must keep $K^+ > 4.0$ mEq/L and $Mg^{2+} > 2.0$ mEq/L to prevent arrhythmias).

## 🏥 Immediate Management: The Emergency Bundle

All suspected ACS patients must receive immediate antithrombotic therapy:

```
ACS Immediate Pharmacotherapy Bundle
├── 1. ASPIRIN: 162 - 325 mg non-enteric coated, CHEWED immediately (antiplatelet)
├── 2. P2Y12 INHIBITOR (DAPT loading):
│   ├── Ticagrelor (180 mg PO load) OR
│   ├── Clopidogrel (300-600 mg PO load; 600 mg preferred if going to primary PCI)
├── 3. ANTICOAGULATION:
│   └── Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) (bolus 60 U/kg, then infusion 12 U/kg/h) OR Enoxaparin
├── 4. NITROGLYCERIN:
│   ├── 0.4 mg SL tablet every 5 min (up to 3 doses) for ischemic pain
│   └── Note: Avoid if BP < 90 mmHg, HR < 50 bpm, or Right Ventricular MI suspected
└── 5. HIGH-INTENSITY STATIN: Atorvastatin 80 mg PO load immediately
```

## 🏥 Definitive Management (Reperfusion for STEMI)

STEMI is a time-sensitive emergency. Reperfusion must be initiated rapidly:

1. **Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)**:
   - The treatment of choice.
   - **Goal**: Door-to-balloon time **$< 90$ minutes** (at PCI-capable hospital) or **$< 120$ minutes** if transfer is required.
2. **Fibrinolytic Therapy** (e.g., Tenecteplase, Alteplase):
   - Indicated if primary PCI cannot be performed within 120 minutes of first medical contact and there are no contraindications.
   - **Goal**: Door-to-needle time **$< 30$ minutes**.
   - Administer Tenecteplase IV bolus (dose adjusted to weight) or Alteplase, followed by transfer to a PCI center (pharmacoinvasive strategy).
3. **NSTEMI / UA Risk Stratification**:
   - Do not perform immediate fibrinolytics for NSTEMI.
   - Perform coronary angiography within 24 hours (early invasive) for high-risk features (refractory chest pain, hemodynamically unstable, dynamic ECG changes).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Telemetry**: Crucial during the first 24-48 hours. Monitor for ventricular fibrillation (VF) and ventricular tachycardia (VT), which are common early causes of death.
* **Vital Signs**: Assess BP, HR, and $SpO_2$ every 15 minutes during the acute phase.
* **Chest Pain Recurrence**: Indicates failed reperfusion or stent thrombosis; requires urgent repeat ECG.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Right Ventricular MI Nitrate Contraindication**: In patients with inferior STEMI (ST-elevation in II, III, aVF), always obtain a **right-sided ECG (V3R and V4R)**. ST-elevation in V4R indicates Right Ventricular Infarction. These patients are highly preload-dependent. Giving Nitroglycerin or Morphine will cause venous dilation, drop preload, and precipitate profound cardiogenic shock.
> **Silent Ischemia in Diabetics and Elderly**: Diabetic patients and elderly patients often present with atypical symptoms ("anginal equivalents") such as dyspnea, nausea, fatigue, or acute confusion, rather than classic chest pain. Always obtain an ECG in these patients for any unexplained acute illness.
