# 💔 Cardiogenic Shock (ช็อกจากการทำงานของหัวใจล้มเหลว)

> "Cardiogenic shock is a state of critical systemic hypoperfusion caused by primary myocardial pump failure. It is clinically characterized by persistent hypotension (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg) with adequate intravascular volume, accompanied by signs of pulmonary congestion and tissue hypoperfusion."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Myocardial pump failure decreases cardiac output, lowering systemic blood pressure and coronary perfusion pressure. This exacerbates myocardial ischemia, leading to a downward spiral of worsening contractility, progressive metabolic acidosis, and death. Mortality rates historically exceed 50% without immediate revascularization.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Severe cardiogenic shock causes altered mental status, compromising the airway.
* *Intubation warning*: Positive pressure ventilation increases intrathoracic pressure, which decreases venous return (preload). In a patient with profound hypotension, this can cause immediate cardiac arrest. If intubation is necessary, perform slow, hemodynamically-neutral induction and have vasopressors running.

### Breathing
* Look for signs of severe respiratory distress (tachypnea, accessory muscle use) caused by **[[Acute Pulmonary Edema]]**.
* Auscultate lungs: **Diffuse, bilateral crackles** (crepitations) and cardiac wheezing.
* Measure $SpO_2$. Support with high-flow oxygen. Use CPAP/BiPAP cautiously: positive pressure reduces preload but also reduces LV afterload, which can assist the failing left ventricle, provided systolic BP is $>90$ mmHg.

### Circulation
* **Assess Vitals**: Look for profound hypotension (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg or MAP $< 65$ mmHg) and tachycardia (or bradycardia in heart block).
* **Assess Perfusion**: Cool, pale, clammy, or mottled skin; cold extremities; delayed capillary refill; weak, thready pulses.
* **Assess Volumetric Status**: **Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)**, hepatojugular reflux, and a third heart sound (S3 gallop) on cardiac auscultation.
* **Auscultate for Murmurs**: Listen for a new holosystolic murmur at the apex (suggests **papillary muscle rupture** causing acute mitral regurgitation) or parasternal border (suggests **ventricular septal rupture**). Both are lethal mechanical complications of MI.

---

## 🔍 Recognition & Diagnostic Criteria
* **Hypotension**: Systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg for $> 30$ minutes, or requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg.
* **Pulmonary Congestion**: Clinical or radiographic evidence of alveolar fluid overload.
* **Tissue Hypoperfusion**: Oliguria ($<0.5$ mL/kg/h), altered mental status, cold clammy extremities, and elevated **serum lactate $> 2.0$ mmol/L**.
* **Hemodynamic Profile** (if monitored):
  * **Cardiac Index (CI)**: $< 2.2$ L/min/$m^2$ (severe pump failure).
  * **Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure (PCWP)**: $> 15$ mmHg (elevated left-sided filling pressures).

## 🦠 Causes

```
Cardiogenic Shock Pathophysiology
└── 1. Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) - Most Common (>80%)
    ├── Loss of >40% of Left Ventricular myocardium
    └── Right Ventricular Infarction (Preload dependency)
└── 2. AMI Mechanical Complications
    ├── Acute Mitral Regurgitation (Papillary muscle rupture)
    ├── Ventricular Septal Rupture (VSD)
    └── Free Wall Rupture (Cardiac Tamponade)
└── 3. Non-Ischemic Myocardium Pathology
    ├── Acute Myocarditis / Stress-Induced (Takotsubo) Cardiomyopathy
    └── End-stage Dilated or Restrictive Cardiomyopathy
└── 4. Severe Valvular / Arrhythmic
    ├── Critical Aortic Stenosis
    └── Complete Heart Block, Ventricular Tachycardia
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Cardiogenic Shock**: High JVP, pulmonary crackles, low Cardiac Index, high PCWP, poor LV contractility on echo.
* **[[Hypovolemic Shock]]**: Flat JVP, clear lungs, low PCWP, hyperdynamic LV on echo, history of bleeding or fluid loss.
* **[[Septic Shock]]**: Early warm skin, bounding pulses, low systemic vascular resistance, normal or high Cardiac Index.
* **[[Cardiac Tamponade]]**: Muffled heart sounds, high JVP, pulsus paradoxus, clear lungs, pericardial fluid on echo.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Immediate Panel**:
  * **12-lead ECG**: Look for ST-elevation (STEMI), ST-depression (NSTEMI), or new Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) to localize ischemia.
  * **Echocardiography (Emergent Bedside)**: The most critical diagnostic tool. Assess LVEF, rule out mechanical complications (VSD, mitral regurgitation, free wall rupture), evaluate RV dysfunction, and rule out tamponade.
  * **Cardiac Troponin T or I**: Rule out acute myocardial necrosis.
  * **Arterial Lactate & ABG**: Monitor systemic perfusion and acidosis.
  * **CBC, Renal Function, Electrolytes, LFTs** (look for shock liver: elevated transaminases).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Chest X-ray**: Check for cardiomegaly and pulmonary edema.

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
Cardiogenic Shock Resuscitation
├── 1. Avoid Fluid Boluses (Standard rule: fluid overload worsens pulmonary edema)
│   └── EXCEPT: Right Ventricular Infarction (Requires fluids to maintain preload)
├── 2. Support Blood Pressure (First-line: NOREPINEPHRINE infusion)
│   └── Target MAP >= 65 mmHg to maintain coronary perfusion pressure
├── 3. Inotropic Support (If MAP is stable but hypoperfusion persists)
│   └── Add DOBUTAMINE infusion (start 2-5 mcg/kg/min; relaxes vasoconstriction, increases contractility)
│   └── Note: Monitor for tachyarrhythmias and hypotension (Dobutamine causes mild vasodilation)
└── 4. Emergency Revascularization
    └── Immediate Coronary Angiography and PCI (or CABG) for ischemic causes
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Revascularization**: Emergent coronary angiography and **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)** or Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) is the gold standard (SHOCK trial).
2. **Surgical Repair**: Urgent surgery for mechanical complications (mitral valve replacement for papillary rupture, VSD patch repair).
3. **Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS)**:
   - **Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)**: Reduces afterload and increases diastolic coronary perfusion. Best for mechanical complications.
   - **Impella** or **Venoarterial ECMO (VA-ECMO)**: Provides active hemodynamic support, offloading the left ventricle and maintaining systemic perfusion.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Arterial Blood Pressure (Arterial Line)**: Mandatory.
* **Continuous ECG Telemetry**: Monitor for malignant ventricular arrhythmias.
* **Hourly Urine Output**: Maintain target $>0.5$ mL/kg/h.
* **Serial Lactate**: Monitor every 2 hours to confirm adequate tissue perfusion.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Right Ventricular Infarction Exception**: Patients with inferior MI involving the Right Ventricle present with hypotension, clear lungs, and elevated JVP. They are **preload-dependent**. Do *not* give diuretics or nitrates (which drop preload and precipitate cardiac arrest). Instead, treat hypotension with **cautious IV fluid boluses** (Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate) to push blood through the right heart, alongside urgent revascularization.
> **Dobutamine Caution**: Never start Dobutamine alone in a patient with profound hypotension (systolic BP $< 80$ mmHg), as its beta-2 agonist properties cause peripheral vasodilation, which can drop blood pressure further. Always normalize BP with Norepinephrine first, then add Dobutamine.
