# 🩸 Massive Gastrointestinal Bleeding (ภาวะเลือดออกในทางเดินอาหารรุนแรง)

> "Massive gastrointestinal bleeding (GI bleeding) is a life-threatening emergency characterized by rapid, high-volume blood loss from the GI tract. It presents as hematemesis, melena, or hematochezia, and quickly leads to hemorrhagic shock, organ failure, and death."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Massive GI bleeding causes rapid intravascular volume depletion. This leads to hemorrhagic shock and coronary hypoperfusion (precipitating myocardial infarction). In patients presenting with active **hematemesis** (vomiting blood), there is a critical risk of **aspiration pneumonia** and asphyxiation, which can cause death before hemorrhagic shock does.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Massive hematemesis with altered consciousness or active shock is a high-risk scenario for aspiration.
* **Early Airway Protection**: Perform endotracheal intubation early in patients with ongoing massive hematemesis, severe agitation, or altered consciousness. Use a large-diameter endotracheal tube (to facilitate later passage of an endoscope if needed) and have high-volume suction ready.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use.
* Monitor $SpO_2$. Maintain target $>92-94\%$.

### Circulation
* **Assess Vitals**: Look for hypotension (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg, MAP $< 65$ mmHg) and tachycardia (earliest sign).
* **Assess Perfusion**: Cool pale skin, delayed capillary refill, weak thready pulses.
* **Identify Bleeding Source**:
  * *Hematemesis*: Vomiting bright red blood or "coffee-ground" emesis (indicates Upper GI Bleed - UGIB).
  * *Melena*: Black, tarry, foul-smelling stool (indicates UGIB or slow right-sided LGIB).
  * *Hematochezia*: Bright red or maroon blood per rectum (typically indicates Lower GI Bleed - LGIB). *Caution: Massive, rapid UGIB with fast transit can also present as hematochezia, accompanied by profound shock.*
* **Obtain Large-Bore IV Access**: Insert two **14G or 16G peripheral IV lines** immediately.
* **Draw Type & Crossmatch**: Order 4-6 units of PRBCs immediately.

---

## 🔍 Upper vs. Lower GI Bleeding Differentiation

The anatomical boundary separating UGIB from LGIB is the **Ligament of Treitz**:

```
GI Bleeding Localization
└── 1. Upper GI Bleed (Proximal to Ligament of Treitz)
    ├── Clinical: Hematemesis, Melena (sometimes Hematochezia if massive)
    └── Diagnostic Clue: BUN / Creatinine Ratio > 30:1 (digested blood absorption)
└── 2. Lower GI Bleed (Distal to Ligament of Treitz)
    ├── Clinical: Hematochezia, normal nasogastric aspirate
    └── Diagnostic Clue: Normal BUN / Creatinine Ratio
```

---

## 🦠 Causes

```
GI Bleeding Pathophysiology
└── 1. Upper GI Bleeding (UGIB)
    ├── Non-Variceal: Peptic Ulcer Disease (most common), Mallory-Weiss tears, Gastric cancer
    └── Variceal: Esophageal and gastric varices (portal hypertension in Cirrhosis)
└── 2. Lower GI Bleeding (LGIB)
    ├── Diverticulosis (most common cause of massive LGIB)
    ├── Angiodysplasia (vascular ectasia)
    ├── Colitis (ischemic, infectious, Inflammatory Bowel Disease)
    └── Colorectal Malignancy
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Massive UGIB**: Hematemesis, melena, high BUN/Cr, history of cirrhosis or peptic ulcers.
* **Massive LGIB**: Hematochezia, normal BUN/Cr, history of diverticulosis.
* **Hemoptysis**: Coughing up blood, bright red frothy blood, acidic pH (if gastric) vs. alkaline (if pulmonary). *Lungs have crackles/wheezing; stomach is quiet.*

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Immediate Panel**:
  * **Type and Crossmatch**: The single most critical test.
  * **CBC**: Monitor hemoglobin and hematocrit. *Caution: Hgb/Hct may not drop immediately in acute, rapid bleeding until hemodilution occurs.*
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT, INR, aPTT)**: Crucial, especially in cirrhotic patients or those taking anticoagulants.
  * **Renal Function (BUN, Creatinine)**: Calculate BUN/Cr ratio. A ratio **$> 30:1$** is highly suggestive of an Upper GI source (due to absorption of digested blood proteins in the small intestine).
  * **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: Check albumin, bilirubin, and transaminases (assess for cirrhosis).
* **Diagnostic Imaging / Procedures**:
  * **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)**: Diagnostic and therapeutic gold standard for UGIB.
  * **CT Angiography of Abdomen/Pelvis**: Indicated in active, hemodynamic unstable LGIB to localize the bleeding site (requires active bleeding rate $>0.5$ mL/min).

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Resuscitate and initiate pharmacological therapies before diagnostic endoscopy:

```
Massive GI Bleed Resuscitation
├── 1. Protect Airway (Intubate early if massive hematemesis or altered mental status)
├── 2. Restrictive Transfusion Strategy (The Target 7 Rule)
│   ├── Target: Transfuse PRBCs to maintain Hemoglobin > 7.0 g/dL
│   ├── Exception: Target Hemoglobin > 8.0 g/dL in patients with active cardiovascular disease
│   └── Note: Liberal transfusion (>9 g/dL) increases portal pressures and worsens rebleeding
├── 3. Correct Coagulopathy (Keep platelets > 50,000/mcL; reverse INR with FFP/PCC)
├── 4. Pharmacotherapy for Suspected Non-Variceal UGIB (Peptic Ulcer)
│   └── Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI): PANTOPRAZOLE 80 mg IV bolus, then 8 mg/h continuous infusion
└── 5. Pharmacotherapy for Suspected Variceal UGIB (Cirrhotics)
    ├── A. IV Octreotide: 50 mcg IV bolus, then 50 mcg/h continuous infusion (reduces portal pressure)
    ├── B. IV Ceftriaxone: 1 g IV daily (mandatory prophylactic antibiotic; reduces mortality)
    └── C. Avoid over-transfusion (worsens variceal pressures)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD)**:
   - Perform **within 24 hours** of presentation (or **within 12 hours** if variceal bleeding is suspected and the patient is stabilized).
   - *Therapeutic options*: Variceal ligation (banding), hemoclip placement, epinephrine injection, or thermal coagulation of ulcers.
2. **Sengstaken-Blakemore Tube**:
   - A gastric and esophageal balloon tamponade device.
   - **Indication**: Refractory, life-threatening variceal hemorrhage failing endoscopic therapy. Serves as a temporary bridge (maximum 24 hours) to transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS).
3. **Angiographic Embolization or Surgery**:
   - For LGIB or non-variceal UGIB that fails endoscopic intervention.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Hemodynamics**: Continuous arterial line blood pressure monitoring.
* **Rebleeding Assessment**: Monitor for recurrent hematemesis, fresh hematochezia, or a sudden drop in blood pressure with tachycardia.
* **Hourly Urine Output**: Maintain target $>0.5$ mL/kg/h.
* **Serial Hemoglobin**: Check every 4-6 hours until stable.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Restrictive Transfusion Paradox**: In gastrointestinal bleeding, transfusing to a high hemoglobin target ($> 9$ g/dL) increases mortality. High-volume transfusion expands intravascular volume, raising portal venous pressures and blowing out clot seals on bleeding varices or ulcers. Target a hemoglobin of **$7.0 - 8.0$ g/dL** unless the patient has active myocardial ischemia.
> **Mandatory Antibiotics in Cirrhotics**: Every patient with cirrhosis presenting with GI bleeding must receive prophylactic antibiotics (**IV Ceftriaxone 1 g daily**). Antibiotics significantly decrease the risk of bacterial infections (like SBP), prevent rebleeding, and improve overall survival.
