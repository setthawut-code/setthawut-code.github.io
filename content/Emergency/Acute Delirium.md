# 🧠 Acute Delirium (ภาวะสับสนเฉียบพลัน)

> "Acute delirium is a transient, fluctuating disturbance in attention, awareness, and cognition, representing acute organic brain dysfunction. It is always secondary to an underlying medical illness, drug toxicity, or withdrawal, and is associated with high mortality."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Delirium is "acute brain failure." Hyperactive delirium puts the patient at immediate risk of self-harm, accidental falls, and accidental removal of critical medical lines (central lines, endotracheal tubes). Hypoactive delirium (somnolence, withdrawal) is frequently missed and carries a higher mortality rate due to delayed diagnosis of the underlying systemic crisis (e.g., urosepsis, myocardial infarction, or hypoxia).

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. (Typically secure, but compromised in hypoactive delirium with severe somnolence and aspiration risk).

### Breathing
* Look for breathing abnormalities.
* Measure $SpO_2$. **Hypoxia is a common, easily reversible cause of acute delirium.** Provide supplemental oxygen if $SpO_2 < 92\%$.

### Circulation
* Assess blood pressure and heart rate.
  * *Tachycardia + Fever*: Suggests sepsis (urosepsis, pneumonia) or **Delirium Tremens** (alcohol withdrawal).
  * *Hypotension*: Suggests septic shock or cardiogenic shock presenting atypically as delirium in the elderly.
* **Fingerstick Glucose**: Check immediately. Hypoglycemia can present as acute confusion or combativeness.
* **Assess for Urinary Retention / Fecal Impaction**: Palpate the suprapubic area. **A distended bladder or hard fecal mass are potent triggers of acute delirium in elderly patients.**

---

## 🔍 Recognition (CAM Diagnostic Criteria)

Delirium is diagnosed clinically using the **Confusion Assessment Method (CAM)**:

```
CAM Diagnostic Algorithm
├── Requirement 1: Acute Onset and Fluctuating Course
├── Requirement 2: Inattention (difficulty focusing or keeping track of conversation)
└── AND Either:
    ├── Requirement 3: Disorganized Thinking (incoherent speech, illogical flow) OR
    └── Requirement 4: Altered Level of Consciousness (hyperalert, lethargic, stupor)
```

*Diagnosis requires the presence of both 1 and 2, and either 3 or 4.*

---

## 📊 Hyperactive vs. Hypoactive Delirium

| Feature | Hyperactive Delirium | Hypoactive Delirium |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Clinical Presentation** | Agitation, hyperalertness, hallucinations, combativeness, restlessness. | Somnolence, lethargy, slowed speech, apathy, decreased responsiveness. |
| **Detection** | Easy to recognize; demands immediate attention. | **Difficult to recognize**; frequently missed or misdiagnosed as depression/dementia. |
| **Mortality** | Lower relative mortality (earlier intervention). | **Higher mortality** (delayed diagnosis of underlying cause). |
| **Common Causes** | Alcohol/Benzodiazepine withdrawal, drug toxicity. | Sepsis, uremia, hypercalcemia, hypoxia. |

---

## 🦠 Causes (The DELIRIUM Mnemonic)

```
Delirium Etiologies
└── D - Drugs (Anticholinergics, Sedatives, Opioids, Corticosteroids)
└── E - Electrolyte imbalances (Hyponatremia, Hypercalcemia) / Endocrine
└── L - Lack of drugs (Withdrawal: Alcohol [Delirium Tremens], Benzodiazepines)
└── I - Infection (UTI / Urosepsis in elderly, Pneumonia, Meningitis)
└── R - Reduced sensory input (Visual/hearing impairment) / Restraints
└── I - Intracranial pathology (Acute Stroke, Subdural Hematoma, Post-ictal)
└── U - Urinary Retention / Fecal Impaction (Crucial noxious stimuli in elderly)
└── M - Myocardial infarction / Metabolic (Uremia, Hepatic encephalopathy, Hypoxia)
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia | Acute Psychosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | **Acute** (hours to days) | **Insidious** (months to years) | Acute or gradual |
| **Course** | **Fluctuating** (worse at night) | Progressive, stable day-to-day | Stable |
| **Attention** | **Severely impaired** | Normal (until late stages) | Normal / Distracted |
| **Reversibility** | **Highly reversible** | Irreversible | Reversible with therapy |
| **Systemic Illness** | **Present** (underlying trigger) | Absent | Absent |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Immediate Resuscitation Panel**:
  * **Fingerstick Blood Glucose**: Rule out hypoglycemia.
  * **Pulse Oximetry / ABG**: Rule out hypoxia or hypercapnia.
  * **[[Urinalysis]] and Culture**: Screen for **Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)** (most common trigger of delirium in elderly).
  * **Electrolytes & Calcium**: Screen for hyponatremia and hypercalcemia.
  * **Renal and Liver Function**: Check Creatinine, BUN (uremia), and Ammonia (hepatic encephalopathy).
  * **ECG**: Rule out silent myocardial infarction (common atypical presentation in elderly).
* **Secondary Panel**:
  * **Non-Contrast Head CT**: Indicated if there is history of falls/trauma (subdural hematoma), new focal neurologic deficits, or unexplained fever/meningismus.
  * **Lumbar Puncture**: If meningitis/encephalitis is suspected.

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Treat the underlying cause and optimize the environment before using medications:

```
Delirium Stabilization Pathway
├── 1. Treat the Underlying Cause (First-line)
│   └── Stop offending drugs; give antibiotics for UTI; relieve urinary retention/fecal impaction
├── 2. Non-Pharmacological Interventions (First-line environmental care)
│   └── Frequent reorientation; family presence; daylight exposure; avoid physical restraints (restraints worsen agitation)
└── 3. Pharmacological Management (Only if patient is danger to self/others, or severe distress)
    ├── A. First-line: HALOPERIDOL: 0.5 - 1.0 mg IV / IM / PO (repeat every 30 min if needed; max 5 mg/day)
    │   └── Note: Monitor QTc interval on ECG (Haloperidol prolongs QT)
    ├── B. Atypical Antipsychotics: Olanzapine (2.5-5.0 mg IM/PO) or Quetiapine
    └── C. BENZODIAZEPINES WARNING: Avoid Lorazepam/Diazepam in general delirium (worsens confusion)
        └── EXCEPT: Alcohol withdrawal (Delirium Tremens) or Benzo withdrawal (here, benzos are first-line)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* Address the specific underlying pathology (e.g., surgical evacuation of subdural hematoma, antibiotics for sepsis, hormone replacement for thyroid storm).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **CAM Assessment**: Perform twice daily.
* **ECG Monitoring**: Monitor QTc interval if the patient is receiving haloperidol or atypical antipsychotics. (Hold if $QTc > 500$ ms).
* **Strict Fluid Balance & Bladder Scan**: Ensure the patient is voiding.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Restraint Paradox**: Physical restraints (tying the patient to the bed) should be avoided in delirium. Restraints increase the patient's panic, worsen agitation, increase the risk of aspiration and injury, and prolong the duration of delirium. Use one-on-one sitters instead.
> **The Benzodiazepine Trap**: Never give Lorazepam (Ativan) or other sedatives to an elderly patient with general delirium to "calm them down." Benzodiazepines cause paradoxical excitation, worsen confusion, increase fall risk, and prolong delirium. Haloperidol is preferred. The *only* exceptions are alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal states.
