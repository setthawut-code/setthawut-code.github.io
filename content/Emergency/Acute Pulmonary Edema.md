# 🫁 Acute Pulmonary Edema (ภาวะน้ำท่วมปอดเฉียบพลัน)

> "Acute pulmonary edema is a life-threatening emergency characterized by rapid accumulation of fluid in the alveolar spaces, leading to severe gas exchange failure, hypoxia, and respiratory distress. It is most commonly cardiogenic in origin, arising from acute decompensated heart failure."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Alveolar fluid barrier disruption blocks oxygen diffusion. This leads to rapid arterial hypoxemia, respiratory fatigue, acidosis, and death from asphyxiation or cardiogenic shock. Immediate intervention is required to reduce preload, afterload, and improve alveolar recruitment.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Pink, frothy secretions may pool in the upper airway; perform suctioning if necessary.
* Ensure airway is secure. If patient has agonal breathing or severe altered consciousness, prepare for immediate endotracheal intubation.

### Breathing
* Look for signs of severe respiratory distress: tachypnea (RR $>30$ breaths/min), accessory muscle use, nasal flaring, and intercostal retractions.
* Auscultate: Check for **diffuse, bilateral, coarse crackles** (crepitations) extending from the lung bases upward, and cardiac wheezing ("cardiac asthma").
* Measure $SpO_2$: Typically $< 90\%$ on room air.
* Initiate immediate **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV)** (CPAP or BiPAP) if patient is hypoxemic and cooperative.

### Circulation
* Measure blood pressure and heart rate immediately.
  * *Hypertensive ($BP > 140/90$ mmHg)*: Favorable prognosis; indicates high afterload precipitating edema. Treat with vasodilators.
  * *Hypotensive ($BP < 90$ mmHg systolic)*: Indicates **[[Cardiogenic Shock]]**. This is highly dangerous. Avoid vasodilators; initiate inotropes (Dobutamine).
* Assess volume status: Look for elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), hepatojugular reflux, and bilateral pitting lower extremity [[Edema]].

---

## 🔍 Recognition
* **Severe Dyspnea**: Sudden onset, worst when lying flat (**orthopnea**), requiring patient to sit upright.
* **Frothy Sputum**: Coughing up pink, blood-tinged, frothy sputum.
* **Physical Signs**: Diaphoresis, cyanosis, cold clammy extremities, tachypnea, tachycardia, and a third heart sound (S3 gallop).
* **Chest X-Ray Findings**: Bilateral perihilar infiltrates ("bat-wing" or "butterfly" pattern), cephalization of pulmonary veins, Kerley B lines, and cardiomegaly.

---

## 🦠 Causes

Classified into Cardiogenic and Non-Cardiogenic:

```
Pulmonary Edema Causes
└── 1. Cardiogenic (Hydrostatic Pressure Increase)
    ├── Ischemic: Acute Myocardial Infarction / Ischemia
    ├── Hypertensive Crisis (Flash Pulmonary Edema)
    ├── Valvular: Acute Mitral/Aortic Regurgitation
    └── Arrhythmia: Atrial Fibrillation with rapid ventricular response
└── 2. Non-Cardiogenic (Capillary Permeability Increase)
    ├── ARDS (Sepsis, Pneumonia, Pancreatitis)
    ├── Fluid Overload (Acute/Chronic Kidney Injury)
    └── Neurogenic (Intracranial Hemorrhage)
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** | Cardiomegaly, elevated JVP, S3 gallop, peripheral edema, high BNP. | Echo (low EF or diastolic dysfunction), CXR, BNP. |
| **[[Acute Asthma Exacerbation]]** | History of asthma, expiratory wheezes, prolonged expiration, normal JVP, normal BNP. | Clinical exam, peak expiratory flow. |
| **Severe [[Pneumonia]]** | Fever, purulent sputum, localized crackles/bronchial breath sounds, pleuritic chest pain. | CXR (lobar infiltrate), CBC (leukocytosis). |
| **[[Respiratory Failure]] (ARDS)** | Acute onset, non-cardiogenic trigger, normal PCWP ($<18$ mmHg), bilateral infiltrates. | CXR, Echocardiography (normal LV function). |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Emergency Panel**:
  * **12-lead ECG**: Look for ST-changes (acute myocardial ischemia/infarction) or tachyarrhythmias (AF with RVR).
  * **Chest X-ray (CXR)**: Look for pulmonary congestion, cardiomegaly, and pleural effusion.
  * **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Assess for hypoxemia, respiratory acidosis ($pH < 7.35$, elevated $PaCO_2$), or metabolic acidosis (indicates tissue hypoperfusion).
* **Cardiac Biomarkers**:
  * **Troponin I or T**: Rule out myocardial necrosis.
  * **BNP or NT-proBNP**: High sensitivity to rule out heart failure. (BNP $< 100$ pg/mL or NT-proBNP $< 300$ pg/mL makes heart failure highly unlikely).
* **Echocardiography (Bedside)**:
  * Assess Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF), regional wall motion abnormalities, valvular dysfunction (e.g., chordae tendineae rupture), and pericardial effusion.

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Use the classic mnemonic **L-M-N-O-P**:

```
Pulmonary Edema Emergency Protocol (L-M-N-O-P)
├── L - Loop Diuretics (Furosemide 40-80 mg IV; double the patient's daily oral dose)
├── M - Morphine (1-2 mg IV slowly; venodilator effect, reduces anxiety; avoid if hypotensive/altered)
├── N - Nitroglycerin (SL or IV infusion; reduces preload/afterload. Avoid if BP <90 mmHg or PDE5i use)
├── O - Oxygen & NIV (High-flow mask or CPAP/BiPAP to reduce work of breathing and push fluid out of alveoli)
└── P - Positioning (Sit patient upright with legs dangling over side of bed to reduce venous return)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Hypertensive Pulmonary Edema (Flash Pulmonary Edema)**:
   - Aggressive afterload reduction: **IV Nitroglycerin infusion** (start at $5-10$ $\mu$g/min, titrate up rapidly) or **IV Sodium Nitroprusside**.
2. **Ischemic Pulmonary Edema (STEMI/NSTEMI)**:
   - Perform urgent coronary angiography and **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)**.
3. **Valvular Pathology (Chordae Rupture / Valve Dysfunction)**:
   - Emergent cardiothoracic surgery consult for valve replacement or repair.
4. **Mechanical Ventilation**:
   - Intubate and initiate mechanical ventilation with positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) if patient has refractory hypoxemia despite NIV, worsening respiratory acidosis, or altered consciousness.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate**: Monitor every 5-15 minutes during IV vasodilator titration.
* **Continuous Pulse Oximetry ($SpO_2$)**.
* **Urine Output**: Insert Foley catheter. Monitor hourly urine output (Target $>0.5$ mL/kg/h post-diuretic).
* **Continuous ECG telemetry**: Monitor for ischemia recurrence or tachyarrhythmias.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Sit the Patient Upright Immediately**: The simplest, fastest ward intervention is positioning. Sitting the patient upright with legs dangling over the edge of the bed instantly pools blood in the lower extremities, reducing preload (venous return) and relieving pulmonary congestion.
> **Vasodilator over Diuretic in Hypertensive Edema**: In acute hypertensive pulmonary edema, the pathophysiology is sudden fluid shift into lungs due to high afterload, not absolute volume overload. Aggressive **IV Nitroglycerin** is the primary treatment; high-dose diuretics can cause intravascular dehydration once blood pressure is normalized.
> **NIV Contraindications**: Do not use CPAP/BiPAP if the patient is comatose, vomiting (aspiration risk), hemodynamically unstable (BP $< 90$ mmHg, unless caused by transient ischemia/arrhythmia), or has facial trauma.
