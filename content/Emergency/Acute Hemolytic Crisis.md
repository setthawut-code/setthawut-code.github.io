# 🩸 Acute Hemolytic Crisis (วิกฤตการสลายของเม็ดเลือดแดงเฉียบพลัน)

> "An acute hemolytic crisis is a hematologic emergency characterized by rapid, massive destruction of red blood cells (intravascular or extravascular). This leads to acute severe anemia, hemodynamic instability, hemoglobinuria, acute tubular necrosis, and life-threatening hyperkalemia."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Massive intravascular hemolysis floods the circulation with free hemoglobin. Free hemoglobin binds nitric oxide, causing systemic vasoconstriction. More importantly, it precipitates in the renal tubules, causing acute tubular necrosis (ATN) and acute kidney injury. In addition, rapid cell lysis releases massive amounts of intracellular potassium, which can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Severe tissue hypoxia and acidosis can cause altered consciousness.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use (respiratory compensation for tissue hypoxia and metabolic acidosis).
* Measure $SpO_2$. Provide high-flow oxygen.

### Circulation
* **Assess Hemodynamics**: Look for tachycardia and hypotension (shock secondary to severe anemia and nitric oxide depletion).
* **Assess Urine**: Look for **dark tea-colored, red, or black urine** (hemoglobinuria).
* **Assess Skin & Eyes**: Look for severe **pallor** and acute onset of **scleral icterus** (jaundice).
* **Obtain Large-Bore IV Access**: Start rapid IV crystalloid infusion to maintain renal perfusion.

---

## 🔍 Recognition
* **Acute Symptoms**: Sudden onset of severe back pain, abdominal pain, fever, chills, and headache.
* **Hemoglobinuria**: Red/brown urine.
* **Rapid Anemia**: Drop in Hemoglobin of $> 2$ g/dL within hours.
* **Physical Signs**: Scleral icterus, pallor, and splenomegaly (in extravascular hemolysis).

---

## 🦠 Causes

Hemolysis is categorized by the site of RBC destruction (intravascular vs. extravascular):

```
Hemolytic Crisis Causes
└── 1. Intravascular (Destruction inside blood vessels)
    ├── G6PD Deficiency Crisis (Triggered by Primaquine, Rasburicase, Infections, Fava beans)
    ├── Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA: TTP, HUS, DIC)
    ├── Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (ABO Incompatibility)
    ├── Infections: Clostridium perfringens sepsis, Severe Malaria (Blackwater fever)
    └── Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH)
└── 2. Extravascular (Destruction in spleen / liver)
    ├── Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA): Warm (IgG) vs. Cold (IgM)
    └── Drug-induced (e.g., Penicillins, Methyldopa)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Key Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Hemolytic Crisis** | Jaundice, tea-colored urine, high reticulocyte count, high LDH, low haptoglobin. | Low Haptoglobin, high Indirect Bilirubin, positive DAT (if AIHA). |
| **Acute Blood Loss** | Obvious trauma, GI bleed, or hematoma. Normal bilirubin, normal LDH early. | Normal LDH, flat JVP, visible blood loss. |
| **Aplastic Crisis** | Severe anemia. **Reticulocyte count is near zero ($<0.1\%$)** (often triggered by Parvovirus B19). | Low Reticulocyte count, bone marrow biopsy. |
| **Rhabdomyolysis** | Severe muscle pain, dark urine. Normal hemoglobin, normal bilirubin. | Markedly elevated CK ($>10,000$ U/L). |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Hemolysis Confirmation Panel**:
  * **CBC**: Severe anemia.
  * **Reticulocyte Count**: Elevated (typically $>3-5\%$), reflecting bone marrow compensation.
  * **LDH**: Markedly elevated (intracellular enzyme release).
  * **Indirect Bilirubin**: Elevated (unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia).
  * **Haptoglobin**: **Low or undetectable** (free haptoglobin is depleted as it binds free hemoglobin).
* **Etiology Testing**:
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear**:
    * *Bite cells & Heinz bodies*: **G6PD Deficiency** (Heinz bodies visible with crystal violet stain).
    * *Schistocytes*: **MAHA (TTP/HUS/DIC)**.
    * *Spherocytes*: **AIHA** or Hereditary Spherocytosis.
  * **Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT / Direct Coombs)**:
    * *Positive*: **AIHA**.
  * **[[Urinalysis]]**:
    * > [!IMPORTANT]
    * **The Dipstick-Microscopy Mismatch**: The urine dipstick detects heme and will show **strongly positive for blood**. However, microscopic examination of the sediment will show **few or no red blood cells**. This mismatch confirms **hemoglobinuria** (free hemoglobin) or myoglobinuria, rather than true hematuria.

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Resuscitation focuses on protecting the kidneys, correcting life-threatening anemia, and managing hyperkalemia:

```
Acute Hemolytic Crisis Emergency Protocol
├── 1. Aggressive Intravenous Hydration (First-line)
│   ├── Give Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate to maintain urine output > 100 - 150 mL/h
│   └── Goal: Flush free hemoglobin out of renal tubules to prevent ATN
├── 2. Urine Alkalinization (Optional but widely used)
│   └── Administer IV Sodium Bicarbonate to raise urine pH > 7.0 (prevents acid hematin formation)
├── 3. Manage Hyperkalemia (Lysed RBCs release potassium)
│   └── Monitor ECG; give Calcium Gluconate and Insulin/D5W if K+ > 6.0 mEq/L
├── 4. Cautious Transfusion (PRBCs)
│   ├── Indication: Hemodynamic instability or Hb < 5 - 6 g/dL
│   └── AIHA Alert: Cross-matching will show "pan-reactivity". Transfuse "least incompatible" blood if life-threatening
└── 5. Glucocorticoids (For suspected Warm AIHA)
    └── Administer METHYLPREDNISOLONE 1 - 2 mg/kg IV daily
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* **TTP / HUS**: Emergent **Plasma Exchange (PEX)**. Do not transfuse platelets in TTP (it fuels microvascular thrombosis).
* **G6PD Deficiency**: Discontinue all oxidative drugs (e.g., Primaquine, sulfonamides). Treat underlying infections.
* **Malaria**: Initiate IV Artesunate.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Urine Output**: Monitor hourly via Foley catheter (maintain target $>100$ mL/h).
* **Serum Potassium**: Measure every 4-6 hours during acute hemolysis.
* **Renal Function (Creatinine, BUN)**: Monitor daily.
* **Hemoglobin & Hematocrit**: Monitor every 6-12 hours until stable.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The G6PD Diagnostic Trap**: During an acute hemolytic crisis, a G6PD enzyme activity assay can yield a **falsely normal** result. This occurs because the older G6PD-deficient RBCs have already lysed, and the remaining circulation is rich in young reticulocytes, which have normal G6PD levels. Always repeat the G6PD assay 2-3 months after the crisis has fully resolved.
> **DAT-Negative AIHA**: In about 5% of autoimmune hemolytic anemia cases, the Direct Coombs test is negative due to low-affinity antibodies or IgA-mediated hemolysis. If clinical suspicion is high (spherocytes, high LDH, low haptoglobin), treat empirically with steroids.
