# 🤢 Acute Abdomen (ภาวะท้องด่วน / ท้องร่วงเฉียบพลัน)

> "Acute abdomen refers to a rapid-onset, severe, progressive abdominal pain (typically $< 24$ hours) requiring urgent diagnostic evaluation. The primary emergency goal is distinguishing surgical pathologies (perforation, obstruction, ischemia) from medical mimics."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Surgical causes of an acute abdomen (e.g., perforated viscus, mesenteric ischemia, ruptured ectopic pregnancy) can cause rapid death. Perforation leads to chemical and bacterial **peritonitis**, progressing to septic shock. Mesenteric ischemia leads to bowel gangrene and sepsis. Free internal hemorrhage from a ruptured aneurysm or ectopic pregnancy causes catastrophic hemorrhagic shock.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Secure the airway in patients with altered consciousness due to severe septic or hemorrhagic shock, or those with severe persistent vomiting.

### Breathing
* Look for breathing patterns. Patients with severe peritonitis have **shallow, rapid breathing** because deep diaphragmatic expansion aggravates abdominal pain.
* Measure $SpO_2$.

### Circulation
* **Assess for Shock**: Look for hypotension, tachycardia, delayed capillary refill, and cold extremities.
  * *Septic Shock*: Severe peritonitis (e.g., fecal peritonitis from colonic perforation).
  * *Hemorrhagic Shock*: Ruptured ectopic pregnancy, ruptured AAA.
* **Establish Access**: Insert two large-bore IV lines (14G or 16G) and start crystalloid resuscitation.
* **Keep Patient NPO (Nil Per Os)**: Suspend all oral intake immediately in anticipation of emergency surgery.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Surgical vs. Medical Abdomen)

Differentiating a surgical abdomen (requiring urgent surgery) from a medical abdomen (managed conservatively):

| Feature | Surgical Abdomen | Medical Abdomen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pain Onset** | Sudden, severe, abrupt ("like a light switch"). | Gradual, progressive, dull. |
| **Pain & Vomiting** | **Pain precedes vomiting**. | **Vomiting precedes pain**. |
| **Abdominal Exam** | **Peritoneal Signs**: Involuntary guarding, board-like rigidity, rebound tenderness, pain with coughing. | Diffuse tenderness; soft abdomen; no involuntary guarding or rigidity. |
| **Systemic State** | Hemodynamic instability common (shock). | Typically hemodynamically stable. |
| **Action** | **Urgent Surgical Consultation**. | Conservative medical management. |

## 🦠 Causes

```
Acute Abdomen Pathophysiology
└── 1. Perforation (Hollow Viscus)
    ├── Perforated Peptic Ulcer, perforated diverticulitis
    └── TREATMENT: Emergency Laparotomy
└── 2. Vascular / Hemorrhage
    ├── Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy, ruptured Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)
    └── Acute Mesenteric Ischemia ("pain out of proportion to exam")
└── 3. Obstruction (Mechanical)
    ├── Small Bowel Obstruction (adhesions, strangulated hernia)
    └── Large Bowel Obstruction (sigmoid volvulus, colon cancer)
└── 4. Inflammatory / Infectious (Surgical or Medical)
    └── Appendicitis, Cholecystitis, Pancreatitis (medical), Diverticulitis (medical)
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Surgical Abdomen**: Localized rigidity, pneumoperitoneum, unstable vitals.
* **Medical Mimics**:
  * *Cardiovascular*: Acute Inferior Myocardial Infarction (epigastric pain mimic).
  * *Endocrine*: **Diabetic Ketoacidosis** (can present with severe pseudoperitonitis and vomiting; resolves with insulin/fluids).
  * *Renal*: Acute Pyelonephritis, severe renal colic.
  * *Toxic/Other*: Lead poisoning, acute intermittent porphyria.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Urine pregnancy test ($\beta$-hCG)**: **Mandatory for all women of childbearing age presenting with abdominal pain.** Rule out ruptured ectopic pregnancy.
  * **Erect Chest X-ray**: Look for **pneumoperitoneum (free air under the diaphragm)**, which confirms hollow viscus perforation.
  * **Serum Amylase and Lipase**: Check for **Acute Pancreatitis** (typically $>3$ times normal; managed medically).
  * **Serum Lactate**: Elevated lactate indicates intestinal hypoperfusion and bowel necrosis (mesenteric ischemia).
* **Laboratory Panels**:
  * CBC (leukocytosis), electrolytes, renal function, liver function tests, coagulation profile, and Type & Crossmatch.
* **Definitive Imaging**:
  * **CT Abdomen and Pelvis with IV Contrast**: The most sensitive and specific tool. Delineates appendicitis, diverticulitis, bowel obstruction, ischemia, and intra-abdominal abscesses. *Avoid oral contrast in emergencies as it delays scanning.*

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Resuscitate and prepare for definitive diagnostic imaging or surgery:

```
Acute Abdomen Emergency Protocol
├── 1. Strictly NPO (Suspend all oral intake; stop IV/oral fluids if scheduled for EGD)
├── 2. Fluid Resuscitation (Correct volume deficit with warm crystalloids)
├── 3. Nasogastric (NG) Tube Decompression
│   └── Indication: Severe abdominal distension, persistent vomiting, or bowel obstruction
├── 4. Broad-Spectrum Intravenous Antibiotics (First hour)
│   ├── Target: Gram-negative bacilli and anaerobes
│   └── Regimen: Ertapenem, or Ceftriaxone (2 g IV) + Metronidazole (500 mg IV)
└── 5. Control Pain (DO NOT withhold analgesia)
    └── Administer IV Fentanyl (25-50 mcg) or Morphine. Opioids do not mask peritoneal signs
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* **Emergency Surgery (Laparotomy/Laparoscopy)**: Indicated for hollow viscus perforation, peritonitis, strangulated bowel obstruction, ruptured ectopic pregnancy, or acute appendicitis/cholecystitis.
* **Conservative Medical Care**: Managed in the ward/ICU for acute pancreatitis, uncomplicated diverticulitis, or pelvic inflammatory disease.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Vital Signs**: Monitor BP, HR, temperature, and respiration hourly.
* **Serial Abdominal Examinations**: Ideally performed by the **same clinician** every 2 hours to detect early progression from localized inflammation to generalized peritonitis.
* **Urine Output**: Target $>0.5$ mL/kg/h via Foley catheter.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Pain-Vomiting Sequence**: In a surgical abdomen, abdominal pain almost always precedes vomiting. In medical conditions (like gastroenteritis or food poisoning), vomiting typically precedes the onset of abdominal pain.
> **Pain Out of Proportion to Exam**: In an elderly patient with atrial fibrillation presenting with sudden, excruciating abdominal pain but a **completely soft, non-tender abdomen** on physical exam, suspect **Acute Mesenteric Ischemia**. The ischemia involves the mucosal layer first, so there are no peritoneal signs until transmural necrosis and perforation occur. Order an immediate CT Angiogram.
