# 🫁 Acute Asthma Exacerbation (หอบหืดกำเริบรุนแรงเฉียบพลัน)

> "An acute asthma exacerbation is a life-threatening respiratory emergency characterized by progressive bronchoconstriction, airway inflammation, and mucous plugging. This leads to increased airway resistance, dynamic hyperinflation, hypoxemia, and carbon dioxide retention."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Severe bronchospasm and mucous plugging increase the work of breathing, leading to respiratory muscle fatigue. If untreated, carbon dioxide accumulates, leading to respiratory acidosis, somnolence, cardiovascular collapse, and death from asphyxiation.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Severe bronchospasm does not affect the upper airway, but laryngeal edema or mucous plugging can compromise patency.
* Prepare for advanced airway management if the patient is comatose, exhausted, or has agonal breathing.

### Breathing
* **Look**: Check respiratory rate (tachypnea $>30$ breaths/min), accessory muscle use (sternocleidomastoid retractions), and ability to speak (phrase vs. single words).
* **Assess Position**: Look for **tripod positioning** (sitting upright, leaning forward, hands on knees to engage accessory muscles).
* **Auscultate**: Listen for diffuse, prolonged expiratory wheezing.
  * > [!WARNING]
  > **Silent Chest Warning**: The absence of wheezing in a severely dyspneic patient ("silent chest") is a sign of critical airway obstruction. Airflow is too low to generate a wheeze. This is a pre-terminal sign indicating immediate respiratory arrest.
* **Measure Oximetry**: Monitor $SpO_2$. Maintain target **$93 - 95\%$** ($92 - 95\%$ in pregnant patients).

### Circulation
* Measure heart rate (tachycardia $> 120$ bpm indicates severe exacerbation) and blood pressure.
* **Assess for Pulsus Paradoxus**: A drop in systolic BP $>10$ mmHg during inspiration is common in severe asthma due to large negative intrathoracic pressures.

---

## 🔍 Severity Classification

Resuscitation guidelines classify asthma exacerbations into three tiers:

| Feature | Mild / Moderate | Severe | Life-Threatening (Impending Arrest) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speech** | Talks in phrases | Talks in **single words** | Unable to speak |
| **Posture** | Prefers sitting to lying | **Sits hunched forward (tripod)** | Unable to maintain posture |
| **Mental Status** | Anxious | Agitated | **Somnolent, confused, or lethargic** |
| **Respiratory Rate**| Increased | **$> 30$ breaths/min** | Bradypnea / Agonal gasping |
| **Heart Rate** | $100 - 120$ bpm | **$> 120$ bpm** | Bradycardia / Hypotension |
| **$SpO_2$ (Room Air)** | $90 - 95\%$ | **$< 90\%$** | $< 90\%$ (despite oxygen) |
| **PEF / FEV1** | $\ge 50\%$ predicted | **$< 50\%$ predicted** | Unmeasurable / Silent chest |

## 🦠 Causes

```
Asthma Exacerbation Triggers
└── 1. Infectious
    └── Viral upper respiratory tract infections (Rhinovirus, Influenza)
└── 2. Allergen / Environmental Exposure
    └── Pollen, dust mites, animal dander, mold, tobacco smoke, air pollution
└── 3. Pharmacological / Toxin
    ├── Non-selective Beta-blockers (e.g., Propranolol)
    └── NSAIDs / Aspirin (Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease - AERD)
└── 4. Non-Adherence
    └── Abrupt cessation of inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Acute Asthma Exacerbation**: History of asthma/atopy, bilateral expiratory wheezing, normal JVP, normal BNP.
* **[[COPD Exacerbation]]**: Older age, extensive smoking history, productive cough, chronic dyspnea.
* **[[Acute Pulmonary Edema]] ("Cardiac Asthma")**: Elevated JVP, bilateral crackles, S3 gallop, history of heart failure.
* **[[Anaphylactic Shock]]**: Sudden onset, accompanied by urticaria, angioedema, hypotension, or allergen exposure.
* **Foreign Body Aspiration**: Sudden onset, localized unilateral wheeze, history of choking.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF)**: Perform if patient is cooperative. Essential to establish baseline severity and monitor response to therapy.
* **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Indicated in severe or life-threatening cases.
  * > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **The Dangerous "Normal" $PaCO_2$**: Early in an asthma attack, hyperventilation causes a low $PaCO_2$ ($< 35$ mmHg) and respiratory alkalosis. A **normal $PaCO_2$ ($35 - 45$ mmHg) or elevated $PaCO_2$** in a hyperventilating patient is a critical sign of respiratory muscle fatigue and impending respiratory failure. It warrants immediate preparation for intensive care admission and potential intubation.
* **Chest X-ray**: Only indicated if suspecting pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, or lobar pneumonia.

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Resuscitate using aggressive bronchodilator and anti-inflammatory therapy:

```
Severe Asthma Emergency Protocol
├── 1. Oxygen (Titrate to target SpO2 93-95%)
├── 2. Inhaled Short-Acting Beta-2 Agonist (SABA)
│   └── SALBUTAMOL: Nebulize 5.0 mg (or 4-10 puffs via spacer) every 20 minutes
├── 3. Inhaled Short-Acting Muscarinic Antagonist (SAMA)
│   └── IPRATROPIUM BROMIDE: Nebulize 0.5 mg (add to Salbutamol; repeat every 20 min for 3 doses)
├── 4. Systemic Corticosteroids (FIRST HOUR)
│   ├── Oral Prednisolone 40-50 mg OR
│   └── IV Methylprednisolone 40-80 mg (if vomiting or unable to swallow)
└── 5. Intravenous Magnesium Sulfate (For refractory severe cases)
    └── Magnesium Sulfate: 2.0 g IV infused over 20 minutes
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive & Intensive Care Management

1. **Intravenous Bronchodilators**:
   - If refractory to nebulizers and IV Magnesium, consider IV Terbutaline or IV Aminophylline (under close cardiac monitoring).
2. **Invasive Mechanical Ventilation**:
   - **Avoid intubation if at all possible**. Dynamic hyperinflation (air trapping) in asthma causes high intrathoracic pressures, leading to barotrauma (pneumothorax) and severe hypotension (decreased venous return).
   - *If mandatory (coma, cardiac arrest, refractory acidosis)*: Use low respiratory rate ($8-10$ breaths/min), small tidal volumes ($6$ mL/kg), and a long expiratory time (I:E ratio of 1:3 or 1:4) to prevent air trapping. Allow permissive hypercapnia.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **PEF**: Measure 1 hour after initial therapy. Discharge only if PEF is $>60-80\%$ of personal best or predicted.
* **Continuous Pulse Oximetry**.
* **Mental Status & Respiratory Effort**: Assess for somnolence or decreasing respiratory effort (muscle exhaustion).

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The Danger of Sedatives**: Never administer sedatives, anxiolytics, or antihistamines to a patient during an acute asthma attack. Their anxiety is driven by dyspnea. Sedatives will suppress the respiratory drive, causing rapid hypercapnia, respiratory arrest, and death.
> **Pulsus Paradoxus Mechanics**: Severe asthma causes hyperinflation. During inspiration, the massive negative pleural pressure required to pull air in increases right-sided venous return, bulging the interventricular septum to the left, which decreases left ventricular stroke volume and drops systolic BP ($> 10$ mmHg).
