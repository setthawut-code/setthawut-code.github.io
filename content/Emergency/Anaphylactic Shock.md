# 🐝 Anaphylactic Shock (ช็อกจากปฏิกิริยาแพ้อย่างรุนแรง)

> "Anaphylaxis is a severe, systemic, life-threatening IgE-mediated hypersensitivity reaction. Anaphylactic shock is the vasodilatory, distributive subtype characterized by profound hypotension and tissue hypoperfusion, accompanied by upper airway obstruction and bronchospasm."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Anaphylaxis causes massive, rapid release of histamine, leukotrienes, and prostaglandins from mast cells and basophils. This leads to systemic vasodilation, increased capillary permeability (causing up to 35% of intravascular volume to shift to third spaces within minutes), severe bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema. Airway occlusion or circulatory collapse can cause death in less than 10 minutes.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess for signs of impending upper airway obstruction: hoarseness, stridor, drooling, difficulty swallowing, or swollen lips, tongue, and uvula (**angioedema**).
* If stridor or airway compromise is present, prepare for **immediate endotracheal intubation** by an experienced clinician. Have a surgical airway kit (cricothyroidotomy) at the bedside.

### Breathing
* Look for signs of lower airway bronchospasm: tachypnea, severe chest tightness, dyspnea, expiratory wheezing, and accessory muscle use.
* Measure $SpO_2$. Administer high-flow oxygen via non-rebreather mask.
* Monitor for silent chest (loss of breath sounds indicating complete bronchial occlusion).

### Circulation
* Measure blood pressure and heart rate. Look for hypotension (systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg or drop $>30\%$ from baseline) and compensatory tachycardia.
* Assess skin: Diffuse hives (**urticaria**), flushing, pruritus, or angioedema.
* **Obtain IV Access**: Immediately establish two large-bore IV lines (14G or 16G) and initiate rapid crystalloid fluid resuscitation.
* **Positioning**: Keep patient **supine with legs elevated** (Trendelenburg position) to optimize venous return. *Caution: Do not allow the patient to stand or sit up abruptly, as this can precipitate fatal cardiovascular collapse ("empty-heart" syndrome).* If vomiting or severe dyspnea is present, elevate the head of the bed slightly.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Clinical Diagnostic Criteria)
Anaphylaxis is highly likely if **any one** of the following three criteria is met:

### 1. Acute onset (minutes to hours) of skin/mucosa involvement AND:
* Respiratory compromise (dyspnea, wheeze, stridor, hypoxemia) **OR**
* Reduced blood pressure or signs of end-organ dysfunction (collapse, hypotonia, incontinence).

### 2. Two or more of the following occurring rapidly after exposure to a *likely* allergen:
* Skin/mucosal involvement (urticaria, itch, flush, swollen lips/tongue).
* Respiratory compromise (dyspnea, wheeze, stridor).
* Reduced blood pressure or associated symptoms (syncope, hypotonia).
* Persistent gastrointestinal symptoms (crampy abdominal pain, vomiting).

### 3. Reduced blood pressure after exposure to a *known* allergen for that patient:
* *Adults*: Systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg or $>30\%$ decrease from baseline.

## 🦠 Causes

```
Anaphylaxis Triggers
└── 1. IgE-Mediated (Classic hypersensitivity)
    ├── Medications: Beta-lactam antibiotics (Penicillin), NSAIDs, Neuromuscular blockers
    ├── Foods: Peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, fish, milk, eggs
    └── Hymenoptera Stings: Bees, wasps, hornets, fire ants
└── 2. Non-IgE-Mediated (Direct Mast Cell Activation / Anaphylactoid)
    ├── Radiocontrast media (IV dye)
    ├── Medications: Opioids, Vancomycin (Red Man Syndrome)
    └── Physical triggers: Cold, exercise (exercise-induced anaphylaxis)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Key Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anaphylactic Shock** | Hypotension, wheezing/stridor, urticaria/angioedema, abdominal cramps. | Clinical diagnosis. |
| **[[Acute Asthma Exacerbation]]** | Severe wheezing, dyspnea. Normal BP. **No urticaria, angioedema, or hypotension.** | Peak expiratory flow, history. |
| **Vasovagal Syncope** | Fainting, **bradycardia**, pale cold skin. Normal airway. No hives/angioedema. | Orthostatic history, EKG. |
| **Hereditary Angioedema** | Recurrent angioedema (face, airway, bowel). **No urticaria, no pruritus, normal BP.** | Low C4, low C1-esterase inhibitor levels. |
| **[[Septic Shock]]** | Fever, warm skin early, source of infection. No sudden hives or airway swelling. | Lactate, blood cultures. |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Anaphylaxis is a clinical diagnosis. Treatment must never be delayed for laboratory tests.**
* **Confirmatory Testing (Retrospective)**:
  * **Serum Tryptase**: Mast cell degranulation marker. Draw blood **within 1 to 2 hours** of symptom onset. Normal levels do not rule out anaphylaxis (especially food-induced), but elevated levels confirm the diagnosis retrospectively.
* **Supportive Panels**:
  * **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Assess for acidosis or hypoxemia in respiratory distress.
  * **ECG**: Rule out myocardial ischemia (anaphylaxis can precipitate coronary vasospasm $\rightarrow$ Kounis syndrome).

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
Anaphylactic Shock Emergency Protocol
├── 1. IM EPINEPHRINE (1:1,000) - FIRST AND MOST CRITICAL STEP
│   ├── Dose: 0.3 - 0.5 mg IM (adult) in the anterolateral thigh (mid-outer thigh)
│   └── Repeat: Every 5 - 15 minutes if symptoms persist or worsen
├── 2. Aggressive Fluid Resuscitation (Give 1-2L warm crystalloids rapidly)
├── 3. Airway Support (High-flow oxygen; prepare for early intubation/cricothyroidotomy)
├── 4. Remove the Trigger (Stop offending IV drug infusion, remove stinger)
└── 5. Position Supine (Keep flat with legs raised; do not allow patient to stand/sit)
```

*Epinephrine Administration Rules*:
* **Concentration**: Use **1:1,000 (1 mg/mL)** for **Intramuscular (IM)** injection.
* **Site**: Always inject into the **anterolateral thigh** (vastus lateralis) because it yields significantly higher and faster peak plasma concentrations than deltoid or subcutaneous injection.
* **Intravenous Epinephrine warning**: Never administer 1:1,000 epinephrine intravenously. If the patient is in refractory shock despite multiple IM doses, use **IV Epinephrine infusion** using a **1:10,000 or 1:100,000** concentration (run at $0.1 - 1.0$ $\mu$g/kg/min, titrated to BP).

---

## 🏥 Second-Line (Adjunctive) Management

*Note: Second-line therapies do not treat airway obstruction or shock, and must never delay epinephrine.*

1. **Antihistamines** (Symptomatic control of pruritus and urticaria):
   - **H1 blocker**: Diphenhydramine 25-50 mg IV.
   - **H2 blocker**: Ranitidine 50 mg IV or Famotidine 20 mg IV (synergistic effect with H1 blocker).
2. **Systemic Corticosteroids** (To prevent **Biphasic Reactions**):
   - Administer **Methylprednisolone 1-2 mg/kg IV** or **Hydrocortisone 100-200 mg IV**. Biphasic reactions (recurrence of symptoms without re-exposure) occur in up to 20% of patients, typically within 8-24 hours.
3. **Inhaled Beta-2 Agonist**:
   - Nebulized Albuterol/Salbutamol (2.5-5.0 mg in 3 mL saline) for bronchospasm refractory to epinephrine.
4. **Glucagon (For Beta-Blocker Users)**:
   - Patients taking beta-blockers may have refractory shock and bronchospasm because beta-receptor blockade prevents epinephrine from working.
   - Administer **Glucagon 1-5 mg IV slowly over 5 minutes**, followed by an infusion of $5-15$ $\mu$g/min. Glucagon bypasses beta-receptors, directly stimulating adenylate cyclase to increase cardiac contractility.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Cardiorespiratory Monitoring & Pulse Oximetry**.
* **Observation Period**:
  * *Mild symptoms*: Observe for at least **4 to 6 hours** post-resolution of symptoms.
  * *Severe anaphylaxis or history of biphasic reactions*: Admit to the hospital and monitor for **24 hours**.
* **Discharge Action**: Prescribe an Epinephrine Auto-Injector (EpiPen 0.3 mg), verify patient technique, and refer to an allergist.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Glucagon in Beta-Blocker Patients**: If a patient with severe anaphylaxis is taking a beta-blocker (e.g., Metoprolol, Propranolol) and does not respond to multiple IM epinephrine doses, administer **IV Glucagon**. Glucagon has positive inotropic and chronotropic effects that are independent of beta-adrenergic receptors.
> **The "Empty-Heart" Collapse**: Standing or sitting up a patient during anaphylaxis can pool blood in the dilated venous system, causing an immediate drop in preload, ventricular collapse, and sudden death. Keep the patient completely flat with legs elevated.
