# 🩸 Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction - AHTR (ปฏิกิริยาการถ่ายเลือดแตกตัวเฉียบพลัน)

> "Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR) is a life-threatening immunologic emergency caused by the transfusion of incompatible red blood cells (most commonly ABO incompatibility). This triggers immediate, massive intravascular hemolysis, resulting in severe systemic inflammatory response, shock, DIC, and acute renal failure."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
AHTR is rapidly fatal. Pre-existing recipient antibodies (e.g., anti-A or anti-B IgM) bind to transfused RBCs, activating the complement cascade. This causes immediate intravascular lysis. The release of free hemoglobin, red cell stroma, and inflammatory cytokines leads to **anaphylactoid vasodilatory shock**, **[[Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation]] (DIC)**, and **acute tubular necrosis (ATN)**. Resuscitation must begin after as little as $10 - 50$ mL of incompatible blood.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Secure airway if the patient develops severe altered consciousness due to profound shock or airway edema.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea, dyspnea, wheezing (bronchospasm), and cyanosis.
* Measure $SpO_2$. Support with high-flow oxygen.

### Circulation
* **Assess Vitals**: Look for rapid onset of **hypotension** (shock), tachycardia, and **fever and chills** (present in $>80\%$ of cases).
* **Assess for Pain**: Patients often complain of severe **chest tightness, flank/back pain**, or pain at the infusion site.
* **Assess for Hemorrhage**: Check venipuncture sites and surgical wounds for oozing (indicates acute DIC).
* **Assess Urine**: Look for red/brown urine (hemoglobinuria).
* **Obtain IV Access**: Keep the current line open using a **completely new administration set and new Normal Saline**.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Transfusion Reaction Differential)

Differentiating AHTR from other transfusion-related complications is vital:

| Reaction Type | Key Features | Pathophysiology | Immediate Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Hemolytic (AHTR)** | **Fever, chills, hypotension, back/flank pain**, dark urine, bleeding/DIC. | ABO incompatibility (intravascular hemolysis). | **Stop blood immediately**. Aggressive IV hydration. |
| **Febrile Non-Hemolytic** | **Fever, chills** (mild temp rise). **BP normal**, no back pain, no hemoglobinuria. | Recipient antibodies against donor leukocyte cytokines. | Stop blood. Give Acetaminophen. Can restart slowly if stable. |
| **Allergic / Anaphylactic** | **Urticaria, pruritus, wheezing, stridor**, hypotension. **No fever.** | IgA-deficient patient receiving IgA-containing blood. | **Stop blood**. Give Epinephrine (IM) + Diphenhydramine. |
| **TRALI** (Acute Lung Injury) | Severe dyspnea, hypoxia, **bilateral infiltrates on CXR**. **Fever, normal JVP.** | Donor antibodies against recipient HLA/granulocytes $\rightarrow$ ARDS. | **Stop blood**. Support respiration (oxygen/NIV). No diuretics! |
| **TACO** (Circulatory Overload)| Dyspnea, hypoxia, **elevated JVP, bilateral crackles, hypertension**. | Volume overload. | **Stop blood**. Give IV Furosemide + Nitrates. |

---

## 🦠 Causes
* **ABO Incompatibility**: Almost always caused by **clerical errors** (e.g., misidentified patient, mislabeled pre-transfusion tube, or administering the wrong unit to the patient).

---

## 🧪 Investigations: The Emergency Transfusion Reaction Workup

If a transfusion reaction is suspected, perform the following steps immediately:

```
Transfusion Reaction Emergency Workup
├── 1. Stop Transfusion (Disconnect set immediately)
├── 2. Clerical Check (Verify patient ID band against blood bag label and transfusion form)
├── 3. Draw Post-Transfusion Blood Sample (Avoid hemolysis during draw):
│   ├── Tube 1 (EDTA): Centrifuge immediately. Look for PINK or RED plasma (indicates free hemoglobin)
│   └── Tube 2 (EDTA): Send to blood bank for Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) and repeat typing/crossmatch
├── 4. Collect Post-Transfusion Urine (Check for hemoglobinuria)
└── 5. Send Blood Bag & Tubing (Return the remaining blood bag and administration set to blood bank)
```

*Supportive Labs*: CBC (drop in Hb), Serum Haptoglobin (low), LDH (high), Indirect Bilirubin (high), DIC panel (low fibrinogen, high PT/PTT, low platelets), Electrolytes (specifically Potassium).

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management

Resuscitation focuses on stopping the antigen load and protecting renal function:

```
AHTR Emergency Protocol
├── 1. IMMEDIATELY STOP THE TRANSFUSION
│   ├── Disconnect the blood administration set from the IV cannula
│   └── Caution: Do NOT flush the remaining blood in the line into the patient!
├── 2. Keep the IV Line Open (Connect new tubing and run 0.9% NaCl or Ringer's Lactate)
├── 3. Aggressive Fluid Resuscitation (First-line)
│   ├── Infuse crystalloids rapidly to maintain urine output > 100 - 150 mL/h
│   └── Goal: Prevent hemoglobin precipitation in renal tubules (ATN prevention)
├── 4. Support Blood Pressure (If hypotensive despite fluids)
│   └── Initiate Norepinephrine infusion; avoid vasopressors that cause severe renal vasoconstriction
└── 5. Manage DIC & Hyperkalemia
    ├── Transfuse FFP/cryoprecipitate if bleeding from DIC occurs
    └── Treat hyperkalemia aggressively if potassium > 6.0 mEq/L
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* Supportive care in the ICU.
* If acute oliguric kidney injury develops, initiate renal replacement therapy (dialysis).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Vital Signs**: Monitor BP, HR, RR, and temperature every 15 minutes.
* **Urine Output**: Insert Foley catheter. Monitor hourly urine output (Target **$> 100 - 150$ mL/h**).
* **Serial Coagulation & Electrolytes**: Monitor potassium and fibrinogen/platelets every 4 hours.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Centrifuged Pink Plasma is Key**: A quick visual check of a centrifuged post-transfusion EDTA blood sample can confirm intravascular hemolysis at the bedside. Normal plasma is straw-colored; **pink or red plasma** indicates free hemoglobin ($>20-50$ mg/dL) released from lysed donor cells.
> **The Clerical Error Reality**: Over 90% of acute hemolytic transfusion reactions are due to administrative/clerical errors, not laboratory testing failures. Always perform a rigorous double-check of patient identity at the bedside immediately before spiking the blood bag.
