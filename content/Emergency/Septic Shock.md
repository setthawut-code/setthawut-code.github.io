# 🦠 Septic Shock (ช็อกจากการติดเชื้อ)

> "Septic shock is a subset of sepsis in which underlying circulatory and cellular/metabolic abnormalities are profound enough to substantially increase mortality. It is clinically identified by the requirement of vasopressors to maintain a MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg AND a serum lactate $> 2.0$ mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Septic shock leads to systemic vasodilation, endothelial barrier disruption (capillary leak), and microvascular thrombosis. This prevents delivery of oxygen to vital organs. For every hour of delay in administering appropriate antibiotics in septic shock, mortality increases by approximately 7.6%.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Severe sepsis often causes **Sepsis-Associated Encephalopathy** (altered mental status), which impairs airway protection.
* Intubate if there is refractory hypoxemia, severe work of breathing, or inability to protect the airway.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea (RR $> 22$ breaths/min) and accessory muscle use. Tachypnea is a sensitive early marker of systemic sepsis.
* Monitor $SpO_2$. Sepsis is a primary trigger for **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)**.
* Auscultate lungs for focal crackles (pneumonia source).

### Circulation
* **Measure Blood Pressure**: Look for persistent hypotension (systolic BP $< 100$ mmHg, MAP $< 65$ mmHg).
* **Assess Perfusion Cues**:
  * *Early Septic Shock (Hyperdynamic / Warm Shock)*: Warm, flushed skin, bounding pulses, rapid capillary refill, due to severe systemic vasodilation.
  * *Late Septic Shock (Cold Shock)*: Cold, clammy, mottled skin with weak pulses, due to myocardial depression and profound hypovolemia.
* **Obtain Large-Bore IV Access**: Two peripheral IV lines (14G or 16G) or establish central venous access.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Sepsis-3 Definitions)

Resuscitation guidelines base sepsis recognition on the SOFA and qSOFA scores:

### 1. qSOFA (Quick SOFA) - Bedside Screening (Score $\ge 2$ indicates high risk):
* **Altered Mental Status**: GCS $< 15$.
* **Tachypnea**: Respiratory rate $\ge 22$ breaths/min.
* **Hypotension**: Systolic BP $\le 100$ mmHg.

### 2. SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) Score:
* Sepsis is defined as an infection with an acute change in SOFA score **$\ge 2$ points** (representing organ dysfunction: $PaO_2/FiO_2$ ratio, platelets, bilirubin, Creatinine, GCS, MAP/vasopressors).

### 3. Septic Shock Definition:
* Sepsis + Persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg AND serum lactate **$> 2.0$ mmol/L** despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

## 🦠 Causes

Common sources of infection leading to septic shock:

```
Septic Shock Sources
└── 1. Pulmonary (Pneumonia)
    └── S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, atypical / viral pathogens
└── 2. Intra-abdominal (Cholangitis, Peritonitis, Abscess)
    └── Gram-negatives (E. coli, Klebsiella), Anaerobes (B. fragilis)
└── 3. Genitourinary (Pyelonephritis / Urosepsis)
    └── Gram-negative rods (E. coli, Proteus, Klebsiella)
└── 4. Skin and Soft Tissue (Cellulitis, Necrotizing Fasciitis)
    └── S. aureus (including MRSA), S. pyogenes (Group A Strep)
└── 5. Device-Related (Indwelling Catheters)
    └── Coagulase-negative Staphylococci, S. aureus, Candida
```

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Septic Shock**: Hyperdynamic state (warm skin, tachycardia) early, fever/hypothermia, elevated inflammatory markers, lactate elevation, positive cultures.
* **[[Hypovolemic Shock]]**: Cold skin, flat neck veins, history of bleeding or severe fluid loss, normal or low cardiac output.
* **[[Cardiogenic Shock]]**: Elevated JVP, bilateral crackles, poor LV function on echo, history of myocardial infarction.
* **[[Anaphylactic Shock]]**: Sudden onset after allergen exposure, urticaria, angioedema, wheezing/stridor.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Immediate Resuscitation Panel**:
  * **Serial Serum Lactate**: Measure immediately, and repeat every 2 hours if elevated ($>2.0$ mmol/L) to calculate clearance.
  * **Blood Cultures**: **Two sets** (one aerobic and one anaerobic from separate peripheral sites) prior to initiating antibiotics. *Do not delay antibiotics for $>45$ minutes to obtain cultures.*
  * **Urine and Sputum Culture, Fluid Tap Culture** (if applicable).
  * **CBC**: Leukocytosis (WBC $>12,000$) or leukopenia (WBC $<4,000$) with a left shift (bandemia). Thrombocytopenia is an early sign of organ dysfunction or DIC.
  * **Renal and Hepatic Function**: Creatinine/BUN elevation (AKI), bilirubin elevation.
  * **Coagulation Profile (PT, INR, PTT)**: Look for early signs of [[Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation]] (DIC).
* **Imaging**:
  * **Chest X-ray**: Rule out pneumonia or ARDS.
  * **Abdominal Ultrasound / CT**: Rule out intra-abdominal abscess, cholangitis, or cholecystitis.

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management: Surviving Sepsis 1-Hour Bundle

All components must be initiated within the first hour of recognizing sepsis/septic shock:

```
Surviving Sepsis 1-Hour Bundle
├── 1. Measure Lactate Level (Re-measure if initial lactate > 2.0 mmol/L)
├── 2. Obtain Blood Cultures (Prior to administering antibiotics)
├── 3. Administer Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics (Empiric IV antibiotics targeting likely source)
├── 4. Fluid Resuscitation (Give 30 mL/kg of IV crystalloid for hypotension or lactate >= 4.0 mmol/L)
└── 5. Vasopressors (Start NOREPINEPHRINE if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation to maintain MAP >= 65 mmHg)
```

*Fluid resuscitation note*: Isotonic crystalloids (Ringer's Lactate or Plasma-Lyte are preferred over 0.9% Normal Saline to avoid hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis).

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Source Control**: Identify and control the anatomic source of infection **within 12 hours** of diagnosis.
   - Examples: Percutaneous drainage of abscesses, surgical debridement of necrotizing soft-tissue infections, removal of infected central venous catheters, or biliary decompression for acute cholangitis.
2. **Corticosteroids**:
   - Indicated only in patients with septic shock who remain hemodynamically unstable despite adequate fluid resuscitation and vasopressor therapy.
   - Administer **Hydrocortisone 200 mg IV daily** (either 50 mg IV every 6 hours or continuous infusion).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)**: Maintain **$\ge 65$ mmHg**.
* **Urine Output**: Monitor hourly via Foley catheter. Target **$>0.5$ mL/kg/h**.
* **Lactate Clearance**: Monitor clearance until lactate is $<2.0$ mmol/L.
* **Hemodynamics**: If refractory, consider central venous pressure (CVP), central venous oxygen saturation ($ScvO_2$ target $\ge 70\%$), or echocardiography to assess for sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Source Control is Mandatory**: Antibiotics and vasopressors will fail in the presence of uncontrolled infection. An abscess, infected catheter, or necrotic limb must be drained, removed, or debrided immediately. Resuscitation will not succeed without source control.
> **Lactate Clearance Beats CVP**: Resuscitating patients to target a $>10\%$ clearance of lactate every 2 hours is superior to resuscitating based on static Central Venous Pressure (CVP) targets ($8-12$ mmHg), and is associated with reduced mortality.
> **Avoid Normal Saline in Large Volumes**: resuscitating with large volumes of 0.9% Normal Saline causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, which can worsen renal perfusion and exacerbate shock. Use balanced crystalloids (Ringer's Lactate).
