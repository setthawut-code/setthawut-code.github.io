# ⚠️ Shock (ภาวะช็อก)

> "Shock is defined as acute circulatory failure leading to inadequate tissue perfusion and cellular oxygenation. If unrecognized or untreated, it leads to anaerobic metabolism, lactic acidosis, multi-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), and death."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Shock is a state of cellular energy starvation. Without adequate oxygen delivery, cells switch to anaerobic glycolysis, producing lactic acid. Prolonged tissue hypoxia leads to cell membrane disruption, systemic inflammatory response, capillary leakage, irreversible tissue necrosis, and organ death.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Altered consciousness in deep shock impairs airway protective reflexes.
* Anticipate airway support. Intubation should be approached with extreme caution, as induction agents and positive pressure ventilation reduce venous return and can cause cardiovascular collapse.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use (respiratory compensation for metabolic acidosis).
* Measure $SpO_2$ and titrate oxygen to maintain $>92-94\%$.
* Assess lung sounds:
  * *Bilateral crackles*: Suggests [[Acute Pulmonary Edema]] or fluid overload (favoring cardiogenic etiology).
  * *Absent breath sounds unilaterally*: Suggests [[Tension Pneumothorax]] (obstructive shock).

### Circulation
* **Assess Hemodynamics**: Measure BP (look for systolic $BP < 90$ mmHg or Mean Arterial Pressure $MAP < 65$ mmHg) and heart rate. *Note: Shock can present with normal blood pressure initially due to compensatory vasoconstriction (cryptic shock).*
* **Check Perfusion Cues**:
  * Assess skin temperature: Cold, clammy skin (vasoconstriction in hypovolemic/cardiogenic shock) vs. warm, flushed skin (vasodilation in distributive/septic shock).
  * Measure Capillary Refill Time (CRT): Prolonged if $>3$ seconds.
  * Evaluate pulse volume (weak, thready vs. bounding).
* **Obtain Immediate IV Access**: Establish two large-bore peripheral IV lines (14G or 16G) or prepare for central venous access or intraosseous (IO) access.

---

## 🔍 Recognition
* **Hypotension**: Systolic BP $< 90$ mmHg, MAP $< 65$ mmHg, or a decrease in systolic BP of $> 40$ mmHg from baseline.
* **Altered Mental Status**: Confusion, agitation, lethargy, or obtundation due to cerebral hypoperfusion.
* **Oliguria**: Urine output $< 0.5$ mL/kg/h (early renal hypoperfusion).
* **Lactic Acidosis**: Venous or arterial lactate **$> 2.0$ mmol/L** (biochemical indicator of anaerobic metabolism).

---

## 🔍 Shock Classification & Hemodynamic Profiles

Shock is classified into four major categories based on pathophysiology:

```
Shock Classifications
└── 1. Hypovolemic Shock (Loss of blood/fluid volume)
    └── Hemorrhage, severe dehydration
└── 2. Cardiogenic Shock (Pump failure)
    └── Myocardial infarction, myocarditis, arrhythmia
└── 3. Distributive Shock (Pathological vasodilation)
    └── Sepsis, Anaphylaxis, Neurogenic
└── 4. Obstructive Shock (Physical barrier to blood flow)
    └── PE, Tension Pneumothorax, Cardiac Tamponade
```

| Shock Type | Preload (CVP / PCWP) | Cardiac Output (CO) | Afterload (SVR) | Mixed Venous $O_2$ ($SvO_2$) | Skin Temperature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypovolemic** | **Decreased** | Decreased | Increased | Decreased | Cold, clammy |
| **Cardiogenic** | **Increased** | **Decreased** | Increased | Decreased | Cold, clammy |
| **Distributive** | Decreased or Normal | **Increased** (early) | **Decreased** | **Increased** | **Warm, flushed** |
| **Obstructive** | Increased (or Dec) | Decreased | Increased | Decreased | Cold, clammy |

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

* **Hypovolemic Shock**: Hemorrhage, vomiting, diarrhea, burns.
* **Cardiogenic Shock**: Myocardial infarction, myocarditis, acute valvular regurgitation, severe arrhythmias.
* **Distributive Shock**: [[Septic Shock]], [[Anaphylactic Shock]], Neurogenic shock (spinal cord injury above T6).
* **Obstructive Shock**: Mass pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade.

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **First-line Screening**:
  * **Serum Lactate**: Crucial for diagnosis and monitoring. Check lactate clearance every 2 hours.
  * **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Check for metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation, and hypoxia.
  * **CBC, Renal Function, Electrolytes, LFTs, Coagulation Profile**.
  * **12-lead ECG**: Check for ischemia (STEMI), heart block, or PE signs ($S_1Q_3T_3$ pattern).
* **Bedside Imaging (POCUS - RUSH Protocol)**:
  * **R**apid **U**ltrasound in **S**hock:
    1. **The Pump**: Check Left Ventricular contractility, chamber sizes, and pericardial effusion.
    2. **The Tank**: Check IVC diameter and collapsibility (collapsed IVC indicates volume responsiveness). Perform eFAST for free fluid in abdomen/pleura.
    3. **The Pipes**: Check for aortic aneurysm/dissection and DVT.

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
General Shock Resuscitation Algorithm
├── 1. Oxygenation (Support respiration, target SpO2 92-98%)
├── 2. Fluid Resuscitation (Unless Cardiogenic Shock)
│   └── Give 30 mL/kg IV crystalloid bolus (Ringer's lactate or Normal Saline)
├── 3. Vasopressor Support (If MAP < 65 mmHg despite fluids)
│   └── First-line: NOREPINEPHRINE infusion (titrate to MAP >= 65 mmHg)
└── 4. Targeted Cause Interventions
    ├── Septic: Blood cultures + Early IV broad-spectrum antibiotics
    ├── Hemorrhagic: Blood transfusion (1:1:1 ratio) + Source control
    ├── Cardiogenic: Dobutamine + Revascularization
    └── Obstructive: Urgent decompression (needle/pericardiocentesis)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* Address the specific underlying cause (e.g., emergent coronary angiography for MI, chest tube for pneumothorax, emergent pericardiocentesis for tamponade, surgical source control for abscess).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)**: Target **$\ge 65$ mmHg** to ensure organ perfusion.
* **Urine Output**: Monitor hourly via Foley catheter. Target **$>0.5$ mL/kg/h**.
* **Lactate Clearance**: Serial monitoring. A clearance rate of **$>10\%$ decline every 2 hours** indicates adequate resuscitation and is associated with improved survival.
* **Mental Status**: Return of normal alertness.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Norepinephrine is First-line**: In shock requiring vasopressor support, **Norepinephrine** is preferred over Dopamine. Clinical trials show Dopamine is associated with significantly higher rates of tachyarrhythmias and increased mortality, particularly in cardiogenic shock.
> **Beware the "Warm Shock"**: Distributive (septic) shock presents early with warm, flushed extremities and rapid capillary refill due to vasodilation. Do not rule out shock simply because the patient's skin is warm. Check lactate and blood pressure.
> **Dynamic Fluid Assessment**: Instead of using static markers like CVP to guide fluid therapy, prioritize dynamic assessment of volume responsiveness (e.g., passive leg raise test, respiratory variation in IVC diameter, stroke volume variation).
