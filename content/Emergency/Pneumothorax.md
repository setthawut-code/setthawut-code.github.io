# 🫁 Pneumothorax & Tension Pneumothorax (ภาวะลมในโพรงเยื่อหุ้มปอด)

> "Pneumothorax is the accumulation of air in the pleural space, leading to partial or complete lung collapse. Tension pneumothorax is a critical, life-threatening emergency where a one-way valve mechanism traps air in the pleural space under high pressure, causing mediastinal shift, vena cava compression, and obstructive shock."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Tension pneumothorax is rapidly fatal. The progressive build-up of positive pressure in the affected hemithorax shifts the mediastinum to the opposite side, compressing the superior and inferior vena cava. This blocks venous return to the heart, causing an immediate drop in cardiac output (obstructive shock) followed by pulseless electrical activity (PEA) arrest.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Severe respiratory distress and tracheal deviation can compromise the airway.
* Prepare for needle decompression immediately *before* attempting intubation, as positive pressure ventilation will worsen a tension pneumothorax.

### Breathing
* **Look**: Assess for severe dyspnea, tachypnea (RR $>30$), cyanosis, and **asymmetric chest expansion** (decreased movement on the affected side).
* **Auscultate**: Check for **absent or decreased breath sounds unilaterally**.
* **Percuss**: Look for **hyperresonance** on the affected side.
* Measure $SpO_2$. Provide high-flow oxygen.

### Circulation
* Measure blood pressure and heart rate.
  * *Tension Pneumothorax Signs*: **Hypotension** (systolic BP $<90$ mmHg) and tachycardia, accompanied by **distended neck veins (JVP)**.
  * *Tracheal Deviation*: Palpate the trachea in the suprasternal notch; it will deviate **away from the affected side** (late sign).
* > [!IMPORTANT]
> **Tension Diagnosis is Clinical**: If a patient presents with severe respiratory distress, unilateral absent breath sounds, hyperresonance, and hypotension, **do not send the patient for a Chest X-ray**. This is a clinical diagnosis. Perform immediate needle decompression.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Tension vs. Spontaneous)

```
Pneumothorax Classifications
└── 1. Simple Spontaneous Pneumothorax
    ├── Primary: Tall, thin young men; rupture of apical subpleural blebs
    ├── Secondary: Underlying lung disease (COPD, TB, PCP pneumonia)
    └── Clinical: Unilateral chest pain, dyspnea, stable hemodynamics
└── 2. Tension Pneumothorax
    ├── One-way valve air entry; positive pressure build-up
    └── Clinical: Respiratory distress, hypotension, JVP elevation, tracheal deviation
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition                         | Distinguishing Clinical Features                                                                    | Key Investigations                    |
| :-------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------ |
| **Tension Pneumothorax**          | Hypotension, absent unilateral breath sounds, hyperresonance, tracheal deviation.                   | Clinical diagnosis. eFAST ultrasound. |
| **[[Cardiac Tamponade]]**         | Hypotension, JVP elevation, muffled heart sounds. **Bilateral breath sounds are normal and clear.** | Bedside Echocardiography.             |
| **Massive Pulmonary Embolism**    | Acute dyspnea, hypoxia, JVP elevation. Normal BP (or low), clear breath sounds.                     | CTPA.                                 |
| **[[Acute Asthma Exacerbation]]** | Severe wheezing, prolonged expiration. **Bilateral breath sounds present (though decreased).**      | Clinical exam, peak flow.             |

## 🧪 Investigations (For simple/stable cases only)

* **Bedside Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS - eFAST)**:
  * Highly sensitive ($>95\%$) and faster than Chest X-ray. Look for:
    1. **Absence of Lung Sliding**: The normal shimmering movement of the pleural line (sliding) is lost.
    2. **Barcode / Stratosphere Sign**: On M-mode, the normal "sandy beach" sign (indicating moving lung) is replaced by horizontal parallel lines (barcode).
    3. **Lung Point**: The boundary where the lung sliding disappears and the pneumothorax begins. **100% specific** for pneumothorax.
* **Chest X-ray (CXR) - Erect PA view**:
  * Look for a visible **white visceral pleural line** with a peripheral space devoid of lung markings.
  * In tension pneumothorax, CXR shows complete lung collapse, depression of the ipsilateral diaphragm, and shift of the mediastinum/trachea to the contralateral side.

## 🏥 Immediate Management: Decompression

Tension pneumothorax requires immediate mechanical decompression:

```
Tension Pneumothorax Decompression Protocol
├── 1. High-flow Oxygen (15 L/min non-rebreather; accelerates nitrogen resorption)
├── 2. Immediate Needle Decompression (Bridge therapy)
│   ├── Catheter: Large-bore angiocath (14G or 16G, at least 5 cm long)
│   ├── Site Option A (Preferred in modern guidelines): 4th or 5th intercostal space, anterior axillary line
│   ├── Site Option B (Classic): 2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line
│   └── Action: Insert needle over superior border of rib to avoid intercostal bundle; listen for rush of air
└── 3. Tube Thoracostomy (Chest Tube insertion)
    └── Perform immediately after needle decompression in the "safe triangle" (5th ICS, anterior axillary line)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Simple Spontaneous Pneumothorax**:
   - **Small ($< 2$ cm distance from chest wall to lung margin)**: If asymptomatic, manage conservatively with high-flow oxygen ($100\%$ $O_2$ creates a nitrogen gradient that speeds up resorption by 4-fold) and serial CXRs.
   - **Large ($\ge 2$ cm or symptomatic)**: Perform needle aspiration ($16-18$G catheter) or insert a small-bore chest tube/pigtail catheter.
2. **Chest Tube Management**:
   - Connect chest tube to a **three-chamber water-seal system** (with or without $-20$ $cmH_2O$ suction).
   - Check for bubbling (indicates active air leak). Clamp only under specialist direction due to tension recurrence risk. Remove tube once lung is fully re-expanded and no air leak is present for 24 hours.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Pulse Oximetry and Respiratory Rate**.
* **Chest Tube Water-seal Chamber**: Monitor for "tidaling" (fluid level moves with respiration) and bubbling.
* **Follow-up Chest X-rays**: Perform daily or after chest tube removal to confirm lung expansion.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Avoid Intubation Before Decompression**: Never intubate a patient with suspected tension pneumothorax before performing needle decompression. Positive pressure ventilation will rapidly force more air into the pleural space, accelerating venous compression, severe shock, and cardiac arrest.
> **The Rib Border Rule**: Always insert the needle or chest tube over the **superior border of the rib**. The intercostal artery, vein, and nerve run along the inferior border of each rib (V-A-N sequence). Inserting below a rib can cause massive hemothorax.
