# 🛑 Cardiac Arrest (หัวใจหยุดเต้น)

> "Cardiac arrest is the sudden, complete cessation of cardiac mechanical activity, resulting in total loss of systemic perfusion. Without immediate cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and defibrillation, irreversible ischemic brain injury and death occur within minutes."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Cardiac arrest represents the ultimate medical emergency. Oxygen delivery to the brain stops instantly. Neuronal death begins within 4-6 minutes. Success of resuscitation decreases by 7-10% for every minute without CPR and defibrillation.

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

Follow the **C-A-B** sequence of Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS), but assess through the template framework:

### Airway
* Assess patency. Open the airway using head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw-thrust (if cervical spine injury suspected).
* Clear secretions, vomitus, or foreign bodies with suction.
* Prepare for advanced airway placement (suction, endotracheal tube, laryngoscope, or supraglottic airway) without interrupting chest compressions.

### Breathing
* Look for chest rise and assess for apnea or agonal gasps (ineffective, gasping breaths that are a sign of cardiac arrest).
* Deliver 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions (if no advanced airway).
* Once an advanced airway is in place, deliver **1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min)** while continuous chest compressions are performed. Avoid hyperventilation (which increases intrathoracic pressure and decreases venous return).

### Circulation
* Check carotid pulse for **no more than 10 seconds** concurrently with checking for normal breathing.
* If no pulse or unsure, **initiate high-quality chest compressions immediately**:
  * *Rate*: 100-120 compressions per minute.
  * *Depth*: At least 2 inches (5 cm) but no more than 2.4 inches (6 cm).
  * *Recoil*: Allow complete chest wall recoil after each compression.
  * *Minimization*: Minimize interruptions in compressions ($<10$ seconds).
* Attach manual defibrillator/monitor or Automated External Defibrillator (AED) immediately to analyze cardiac rhythm.

---

## 🔍 Recognition
* **Unresponsiveness**: Patient does not respond to physical or verbal stimuli.
* **Apnea or Agonal Gasps**: No breathing or only abnormal gasping breaths.
* **Pulselessness**: No carotid pulse detected within 10 seconds.
* **EKG Rhythm**:
  * **Shockable**: Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) or Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT).
  * **Non-shockable**: Asystole or Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) (rhythm present on monitor but no palpable pulse).

---

## 🦠 Causes

Classify using the classic **"5 H's and 5 T's"**:

```
Reversible Causes (5 H's & 5 T's)
└── 1. The 5 H's
    ├── Hypovolemia
    ├── Hypoxia
    ├── Hydrogen ion (Acidosis)
    ├── Hypo- / Hyperkalemia
    └── Hypothermia
└── 2. The 5 T's
    ├── Tension Pneumothorax
    ├── Tamponade (Cardiac)
    ├── Toxins (Overdose)
    ├── Thrombosis (Pulmonary: PE)
    └── Thrombosis (Coronary: MI)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

Identify the arrest rhythm to guide management:

| Rhythm Category | EKG Description | Immediate Treatment | Common Causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)** | Chaotic, rapid, irregular waves without identifiable QRS complexes. | **Immediate Defibrillation** (high energy: 120-200 J biphasic) | Coronary thrombosis (MI), severe electrolyte abnormalities, drug toxicity. |
| **Pulseless VT (pVT)** | Wide, monomorphic or polymorphic QRS complexes at rapid rate; no pulse. | **Immediate Defibrillation** (120-200 J biphasic) | Ischemic heart disease, prolonged QT interval (Torsades). |
| **Asystole** | Flat line or minimal electrical activity on EKG. | CPR + Epinephrine. **Do NOT shock.** | Hypoxia, acidosis, massive infarct, end-stage cardiac failure. |
| **PEA** | Any organized rhythm on monitor without a palpable pulse. | CPR + Epinephrine. **Do NOT shock.** Investigate H's and T's. | [[Hypovolemic Shock]] (hemorrhage), [[Tension Pneumothorax]], [[Cardiac Tamponade]]. |

## 🧪 Investigations

* **During Resuscitation (Point-of-Care)**:
  * **Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS)**: Check for cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, severe hypovolemia (collapsed IVC), or pulmonary embolism (RV strain). Limit scan to $<10$ seconds during pulse checks.
  * **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Assess for severe acidosis, hypoxia, and hypercapnia.
  * **Electrolytes & Glucose**: Check potassium (hyperkalemia/hypokalemia) and blood glucose (hypoglycemia).
* **Post-Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC)**:
  * **12-lead ECG**: Look for ST-elevation (STEMI) or new LBBB indicating coronary thrombosis (warrants immediate coronary angiography/PCI).
  * **Chest X-ray**: Verify endotracheal tube placement, rule out pneumothorax, assess for pulmonary edema or rib fractures from CPR.
  * **Brain CT**: Indicated if patient remains comatose post-ROSC and cause of arrest is unclear.

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
ACLS Cardiac Arrest Algorithm
├── Confirm Arrest (Call for help, check pulse/breathing <10s)
├── Start CPR (Give Oxygen, attach monitor/defibrillator)
├── Analyze Rhythm
    ├── SHOCKABLE (VF / pVT)
    │   ├── Shock (Biphasic 120-200 J)
    │   ├── CPR 2 min -> Shock -> CPR 2 min + Epinephrine (1 mg IV every 3-5 min)
    │   └── Shock -> CPR 2 min + Amiodarone (300 mg IV first dose, then 150 mg) or Lidocaine
    └── NON-SHOCKABLE (Asystole / PEA)
        ├── CPR 2 min + Epinephrine (1 mg IV ASAP, then every 3-5 min)
        └── Search and treat reversible causes (H's and T's)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management (Post-ROSC Care)

1. **Optimize Ventilation and Oxygenation**:
   - Maintain oxygen saturation ($SpO_2$) between **92% and 98%** (avoid hyperoxia, which causes oxidative reperfusion injury).
   - Target $PaCO_2$ of $35-45$ mmHg (ventilate at 10-12 breaths/min).
2. **Hemodynamic Optimization**:
   - Maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (**MAP $\ge 65$ mmHg**) and systolic BP $\ge 90$ mmHg.
   - Use IV crystalloids and vasopressor infusions (Norepinephrine or Epinephrine) as needed.
3. **Coronary Reperfusion**:
   - Emergent coronary angiography / Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) for patients with ST-elevation on EKG or high suspicion of myocardial ischemia.
4. **Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)**:
   - Indicated for adults who do not follow commands after ROSC.
   - Target a constant temperature between **32°C and 36°C** for at least 24 hours using cooling blankets, intravascular cooling, or ice packs. Prevents secondary brain injury.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Quantitative Waveform Capnography (ETCO2)**:
  * Indicates CPR quality: Target **$ETCO_2 > 10$ mmHg** during compressions. If $< 10$ mmHg, improve compression depth/rate.
  * Detects ROSC: A sudden, sustained increase in $ETCO_2$ to **$> 35 - 40$ mmHg** is a highly reliable sign of ROSC.
* **Continuous Arterial Blood Pressure**: If arterial line is in place, monitor diastolic pressure. Target **diastolic BP $> 20$ mmHg** during chest compressions.
* **Continuous ECG Telemetry**: Detect recurring malignant arrhythmias.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Defibrillate Immediately**: For VF or pulseless VT, the single most critical intervention is rapid defibrillation. Do not delay shock to insert an endotracheal tube or establish IV access.
> **Avoid Hyperoxia post-ROSC**: High-flow oxygen post-ROSC creates free radicals that worsen reperfusion brain injury. Titrate oxygen down to maintain $SpO_2$ of $92-98\%$ as soon as ROSC is achieved and monitored.
> **Epinephrine Timing**: In non-shockable rhythms (Asystole/PEA), give Epinephrine **as soon as possible**. In shockable rhythms (VF/pVT), deliver Epinephrine after the **second shock** if VF persists.
