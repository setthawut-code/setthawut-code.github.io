# 💧 Anuria (ภาวะไม่มีปัสสาวะ)

> "Anuria is defined as complete or near-complete cessation of urine output ($< 100$ mL/day in adults). It represents a critical renal emergency, indicating either complete urinary tract obstruction, catastrophic renal parenchymal injury, or profound circulatory shock."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
The complete absence of urine output blocks the excretion of metabolic wastes, water, and electrolytes. This rapidly causes life-threatening complications within hours: **acute pulmonary edema** (from volume overload), **refractory metabolic acidosis**, and **severe hyperkalemia** (which causes fatal cardiac arrhythmias).

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess patency. Altered consciousness may occur secondary to severe uremic encephalopathy or shock.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use (indicates pulmonary congestion or Kussmaul breathing compensating for metabolic acidosis).
* Auscultate: **Listen for diffuse crackles** (indicates volume overload / pulmonary edema).
* Measure $SpO_2$.

### Circulation
* **Assess Hemodynamics**:
  * *Hypotension + Tachycardia*: Indicates profound circulatory shock (septic, cardiogenic, hypovolemic) causing renal hypoperfusion.
  * *Hypertension*: Often present in hypervolemic anuria.
* **Assess Volume Status**: Look for elevated JVP, gallop (S3), and peripheral [[Edema]].
* **Confirm Catheter Patency**: If the patient already has a Foley catheter, **flush it immediately** with sterile saline to rule out clot or mechanical occlusion of the catheter.

---

## 🔍 Recognition (Etiological Classification)

Anuria is classified into three classic categories based on the site of pathology:

```
Anuria Classifications
└── 1. Postrenal (Obstruction) - Most Common & Reversible
    ├── Bilateral ureteral obstruction (pelvic tumor, stones)
    └── Urethral / Bladder outlet obstruction (BPH, clot tamponade)
└── 2. Prerenal (Severe Hypoperfusion)
    └── Catastrophic shock (septic, hemorrhagic, cardiogenic)
└── 3. Intrinsic (Catastrophic Parenchymal Injury)
    ├── Bilateral acute cortical necrosis (severe obstetric hemorrhage/sepsis)
    └── Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN)
```

*Clinical Pearl: Absolute anuria (0 mL of urine) is highly characteristic of complete postrenal obstruction or vascular catastrophe (e.g., bilateral renal artery occlusion). Intrinsic AKI (ATN) typically presents with oliguria ($100-400$ mL/day) rather than absolute anuria.*

---

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Feature | Postrenal Anuria | Prerenal Anuria | Intrinsic Anuria (RPGN/ATN) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **History** | Pelvic malignancy, BPH, kidney stones, pelvic surgery. | Hemorrhage, vomiting, diarrhea, sepsis, heart failure. | Nephrotoxic drugs, systemic lupus, vasculitis. |
| **Bladder Scan** | **Bladder Volume $> 500$ mL** (distended bladder). | Empty bladder ($< 50$ mL). | Empty bladder ($< 50$ mL). |
| **Renal Ultrasound**| **Bilateral Hydronephrosis** (or hydronephrosis in solitary kidney). | Normal kidneys, no hydronephrosis. | Echogenic kidneys, normal size, no hydronephrosis. |
| **First Action** | **Catheterize / Relieve obstruction** | **Fluid challenge** (if hypotensive/dehydrated) | **Stop nephrotoxins**, consider biopsy/steroids |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Bedside Imaging (First Priority)**:
  * **Bladder Scan**: Immediately estimate bladder volume. If $>300-500$ mL, insert Foley catheter.
  * **Renal Ultrasound**: Mandatory if catheterization does not yield urine. Look for **bilateral hydronephrosis** or hydronephrosis in a solitary kidney (confirms postrenal obstruction).
* **Urgent Laboratory Panels**:
  * **Serum Electrolytes**: Specifically monitor **Potassium** (hyperkalemia risk).
  * **BUN and Creatinine**: Assess renal function.
  * **Arterial / Venous Blood Gas (ABG/VBG)**: Check for metabolic acidosis.
  * **[[Urinalysis]] & Urine Microscopy**: If any urine is obtainable, check for RBC casts (RPGN), WBC casts (interstitial nephritis), or muddy brown casts (ATN).

## 🏥 Immediate Management

```
Anuria Emergency Management
├── 1. Rule Out Postrenal Obstruction (FIRST)
│   ├── Action A: Insert Foley catheter immediately
│   └── Action B: If no urine, perform urgent renal ultrasound to look for hydronephrosis
├── 2. Address Postrenal Obstruction
│   └── Action: Consult urology for double-J (JJ) stent or Percutaneous Nephrostomy (PCN)
├── 3. Address Prerenal / Shock States
│   └── Action: Give fluids if hypovolemic; start Norepinephrine / Dobutamine if cardiogenic/septic shock
└── 4. Emergency Hemodialysis (If non-obstructive and meets AEIOU criteria)
    ├── A - Refractory Metabolic Acidosis (pH < 7.1)
    ├── E - Refractory Hyperkalemia (K+ > 6.5 mEq/L or ECG changes)
    ├── I - Intoxication (toxic alcohols, lithium)
    ├── O - Fluid Overload refractory to diuretics (acute pulmonary edema)
    └── U - Uremia (encephalopathy, pericarditis)
```

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management
* **Postrenal**: Urological stent placement or surgical bypass of obstruction.
* **RPGN**: High-dose corticosteroids (pulse Methylprednisolone 500-1000 mg IV daily for 3 days) and cyclophosphamide or plasmapheresis (consult nephrology immediately).
* **ATN / Cortical Necrosis**: Support with renal replacement therapy (dialysis) until renal recovery occurs.

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Urine Output**: Monitor hourly via Foley catheter.
* **Serum Potassium & VBG**: Check every 4-6 hours if potassium is elevated or renal function is rapidly declining.
* **Fluid Balance**: Strict fluid restriction (usually limited to insensible losses: $500 - 800$ mL/day plus measured output) if the patient is non-obstructive and anuric, to prevent pulmonary edema.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **Check the Foley Catheter First**: If a patient with a Foley catheter in place suddenly develops absolute anuria, do not assume renal failure. The most common cause is a kink in the tubing, catheter occlusion by blood clots/pus, or displacement. Always flush the catheter with $30-50$ mL of sterile saline before calling nephrology.
> **Absolute vs. Relative Anuria**: A urine output of exactly $0$ mL/day is rare in intrinsic kidney injury. If the urine output is absolute zero, suspect complete urinary tract obstruction, bilateral renal artery occlusion, or bilateral acute cortical necrosis.
