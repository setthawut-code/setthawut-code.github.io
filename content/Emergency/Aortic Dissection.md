# 🫀 Aortic Dissection (โรคหลอดเลือดแดงใหญ่ฉีกขาด)

> "Aortic dissection is a cardiovascular emergency caused by a tear in the aortic intima. Blood enters the media under high pressure, propagating along the aortic wall and creating a false lumen. It can lead to aortic rupture, cardiac tamponade, acute aortic regurgitation, or organ ischemia."

---

## 🚨 Why It Is An Emergency
Aortic dissection is rapidly fatal. Mortality for untreated Type A dissection is 1% to 2% per hour in the first 24-48 hours. Death results from aortic rupture into the pericardial sac (causing cardiac tamponade), free rupture into the pleural cavity (causing hemorrhagic shock), or occlusion of major branch vessels (causing myocardial infarction, stroke, or mesenteric ischemia).

---

## 🩺 Immediate Assessment

### Airway
* Assess and secure. Intubation is required if the patient is comatose due to cerebral malperfusion (stroke mimic) or hemodynamically unstable with respiratory arrest.

### Breathing
* Look for tachypnea and accessory muscle use.
* Auscultate: Check for decreased breath sounds on the left (suggests left hemothorax from leak/rupture) or bilateral crackles (pulmonary edema secondary to acute aortic regurgitation).
* Measure $SpO_2$.

### Circulation
* **Measure BP in Both Arms**: A blood pressure differential **$> 20$ mmHg** between the right and left arm is highly suggestive of dissection involving the subclavian artery.
* **Assess Peripheral Pulses**: Check carotid, radial, femoral, and pedal pulses. A **newly absent or asymmetric pulse** indicates branch vessel occlusion (pulse deficit).
* **Cardiac Auscultation**: Listen for a new early diastolic decrescendo murmur at the right sternal border (indicates **acute aortic regurgitation** due to aortic root dilation).
* **ECG**: Immediately obtain a 12-lead ECG. *Caution: Type A dissection can propagate retrogradely into the coronary ostia (typically the Right Coronary Artery), presenting as an inferior STEMI. Giving thrombolytics or heparin in this scenario is fatal.*

---

## 🔍 Recognition
* **Classic Pain**: Sudden onset of excruciating, **"tearing", "ripping", or "stabbing" chest pain** that radiates to the back (interscapular region for descending aorta; anterior chest for ascending).
* **Pulse Deficit**: Asymmetric or absent pulses in the extremities.
* **Neurological Deficits**: Stroke, spinal cord ischemia (paraplegia due to anterior spinal artery occlusion), or syncope.

---

## 🔍 Stanford Classification

Resuscitation and definitive care are dictated by the anatomical location of the dissection:

```
Stanford Aortic Dissection Classification
└── Type A (Ascending Aorta)
    ├── Involves ascending aorta, regardless of tear origin
    └── TREATMENT: Emergent Surgical Emergency
└── Type B (Descending Aorta Only)
    ├── Involves aorta distal to left subclavian artery
    └── TREATMENT: Primarily Medical (unless complicated)
```

## 📂 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing Clinical Features | Key Investigations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aortic Dissection** | Ripping back pain, BP discrepancy between arms, pulse deficits, diastolic murmur. | CT Angiography (CTA). |
| **[[Acute Coronary Syndrome]]** | Pressure-like chest pain, radiation to jaw/left arm, no pulse deficits, ECG ischemia signs. | ECG, Troponin. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism** | Pleuritic chest pain, sudden dyspnea, calf swelling, normal BP discrepancy. | CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA). |
| **[[Pneumothorax]]** | Sudden unilateral pleuritic chest pain, absent breath sounds, hyperresonance. | Chest X-ray. |

---

## 🧪 Investigations

* **Gold Standard Diagnostic**:
  * **CT Angiography (CTA) of the Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis**: The primary investigation of choice. Fast, highly sensitive ($>98\%$), and delineates the extent of the dissection, false/true lumens, and branch vessel involvement.
* **Bedside Evaluation (for unstable patients)**:
  * **Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE)**: Can be performed at the bedside in the ICU/ER for hemodynamically unstable patients who cannot be safely transported to CT.
  * **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE)**: Can identify aortic root dilation, aortic regurgitation, and pericardial effusion/tamponade.
* **Screening Tests**:
  * **Chest X-ray (CXR)**: Look for **widened mediastinum** (present in $80\%$ of Type A, but a normal mediastinum does not rule it out) or calcium sign.
  * **D-dimer**: Extremely high negative predictive value. A normal D-dimer ($< 500$ ng/mL) makes acute aortic dissection highly unlikely.

---

## 🏥 Immediate Management: Anti-Impulse Therapy

The goal of medical management is to reduce **wall shear stress ($dP/dt$)** and prevent propagation of the tear:

```
Anti-Impulse Therapy Protocol
├── Target 1: Heart Rate < 60 bpm (reduces rate of aortic wall shear stress)
├── Target 2: Systolic BP 100 - 120 mmHg (or lowest tolerated to maintain perfusion)
├── STEP 1: Intravenous Beta-Blocker (FIRST)
│   ├── IV Esmolol: Short-acting, easy to titrate (bolus 500 mcg/kg, then 50-300 mcg/kg/min)
│   └── IV Labetalol: 20 mg IV slow boluses or infusion
└── STEP 2: Intravenous Vasodilator (SECOND - ONLY after HR is controlled)
    ├── IV Nicardipine: 5-15 mg/h infusion OR
    └── IV Sodium Nitroprusside: 0.25-10 mcg/kg/min infusion
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Beta-blocker First Rule**: Never administer a vasodilator (Nicardipine/Nitroprusside) before achieving adequate beta-blockade (HR $< 60$ bpm). Vasodilation without beta-blockade triggers reflex sympathetic activation, causing tachycardia and increased cardiac contractility, which raises $dP/dt$ and propagates the dissection tear.

---

## 🏥 Definitive Management

1. **Stanford Type A**:
   - **Emergent Surgical Intervention**: Replacement of the ascending aorta with a synthetic graft, reconstruction of the aortic valve if regurgitant, and re-implantation of coronary arteries.
2. **Stanford Type B**:
   - **Medical Management**: Continue oral beta-blockers long-term.
   - **Endovascular Repair (TEVAR)**: Indicated for complicated Type B dissection (refractory pain, rapid aortic expansion, rupture, or end-organ ischemia due to branch vessel compromise).

---

## 📊 Monitoring

* **Continuous Intra-arterial Pressure (Arterial Line)**: Place in the arm with the higher blood pressure.
* **ECG Telemetry**.
* **Neurologic Assessment**: Check hourly for stroke signs or spinal cord ischemia.
* **Renal Function & Urine Output**: Monitor via Foley catheter (target $>0.5$ mL/kg/h) to ensure renal arteries are perfused.

---

## 💡 Clinical Pearls

> [!TIP]
> **The RCA inferior STEMI trap**: In about 1-2% of Type A dissections, the tear extends retrogradely into the right coronary artery (RCA) ostium, causing acute transmural myocardial ischemia. The patient presents with classic chest pain and inferior ST-elevation on ECG. Initiating antiplatelets, heparin, or thrombolysis in this patient is catastrophic, as it causes massive hemorrhage. Always check for pulse deficits and blood pressure asymmetry before giving thrombolysis.
> **Avoid Hydralazine**: Hydralazine causes significant reflex tachycardia and increases cardiac contractility ($dP/dt$). It is strictly contraindicated in acute aortic dissection.
